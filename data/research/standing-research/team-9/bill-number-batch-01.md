## 2. Research and payload for Team 2

Measured source statements from [Colorado Senate Republicans’ numbering explanation](https://www.coloradosenaterepublicans.com/bill-numbering-in-the-colorado-general-assembly/): House bills use HB, start at 1001 and have four digits; Senate bills use SB, start at 001 and have three digits. Numbers are assigned by nonpartisan chamber staff in received order. The same sequence is used each session; a bill number cannot carry over to another session. A two-digit year follows the chamber abbreviation. Since 2013, B identifies the first extraordinary session in the year; before 2013, S identified special-session bills. The explanatory page is institutional caucus educational material, not standing-rule text.

The official [HB25-1001 record](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb25-1001) corroborates the regular-session display. Payload: CO/HB firstNumber 1001, display width 4; CO/SB firstNumber 1, display width 3; regular display HB25-1001 or SB25-001; reset by session; first-extraordinary suffix B after the year for the stated modern period. Consecutive progression is supported by the explanation’s ordinal examples, so step 1 is an inference, not a separately quoted rule. Resolution starts, shared versus separate resolution namespaces, later extraordinary-session letters and reservations remain unverified. Do not extend these bill fields to every measure type.
