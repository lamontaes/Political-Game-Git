# Homelessness needs recorded consequences at the person level

Broad research supports service-cost accounting and checks on health, earnings and school continuity. It does not establish a universal causal multiplier from homelessness to each outcome. The implementation should record housing episodes and their actual consequences, preserving identity and history. This is a research handoff; no game behavior or watched-world result is delivered.

## Spending follows recorded service use

[HUD Family Options Study, three-year report](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/Family-Options-Study-Full-Report.pdf), executive summary page xxxi and Appendix G, reports costs in 2013 dollars. The study covers shelter-entry families across 12 U.S. communities.

| Service              | Observed monthly cost per participating family | Observed program range | Coverage                                                |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------: | ---------------------: | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emergency shelter    |                                   About $4,800 |          $1,900–$9,200 | 45 programs; services accounted for 63% of average cost |
| Transitional housing |                                   About $2,700 |          $1,300–$6,300 | 24 programs; services accounted for 42%                 |
| Permanent subsidy    |                                   About $1,200 |     No range extracted | Study programs                                          |
| Rapid rehousing      |                                     About $900 |     No range extracted | Study programs                                          |

These are average program costs, not marginal costs of one additional family. They exclude people who use no participating service. They are not per-person costs and cannot be applied to a single adult without another source. Accounting operates during recorded participation; the report establishes no universal delay between homelessness onset and service entry.

Suggested wiring: a canonical episode records shelter entry, exit, household size, service provider and payer. Actual service use creates the appropriate public or provider expense through the existing money writer. A provider's staffing, beds and fixed costs remain separate from variable costs. Capacity, family composition, local prices and service mix modify the accounting. The historical range may check broad starting costs after price adjustment; it must not replace exact contracts or turn total homelessness prevalence into an automatic charge.

## Mortality comparisons check totals; health records cause individual outcomes

[Meyer, Wyse and Logani, NBER working paper 31843](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31843/w31843.pdf), Table 4, links the national 2010 Census enumeration to deaths through March 2022. The comparison below concerns adults ages 18–54 in 2010.

| Cumulative mortality, April 2010–March 2022 | Homeless at enumeration | Poor housed | All housed |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------: | ----------: | ---------: |
| Unadjusted fraction who died                |                  15.75% |       6.19% |      3.85% |
| Adjusted for age and gender                 |                  16.20% |      10.37% |      5.03% |

The adjusted homeless-to-housed ratio is 3.22; homeless-to-poor-housed is 1.56. These are cumulative comparisons, not annual hazards or causal homelessness coefficients. Initial housing status does not establish later episode duration. The elevated comparison persists over 12 years; the paper does not identify an onset lag or recovery half-life. Poverty and homelessness comparisons overlap and must not be multiplied as independent effects. Conflicting hazard prose and confidence limits in the inspected PDF were excluded; Table 4 is the extracted evidence.

[White and colleagues, mortality systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13586615/), DOI `10.1093/ije/dyag129`, published September 18, 2026, includes 116 studies. The search runs through December 2024. Full text was read through [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13586615/fullTextXML).

- Pooled all-cause relative risk: 2.21, 95% confidence interval 1.91–2.57; heterogeneity was extremely high, with I-squared of 99.7%.
- Rough-sleeping subgroup: 7.63, interval 3.29–11.97. Hostel/shelter subgroup: 3.44, interval 2.10–4.77. These compare different study populations; they do not identify a causal shelter benefit.
- Cause-specific associations include overdose, injury and substance-use disorders. The U.S. national paper's adjusted sheltered/unsheltered comparison differs from the international subgroup results. Preserve that disagreement and its population limits.

The confidence intervals measure uncertainty in associations; they are not world-generation coefficient ranges. Individual consequences should arise from actual exposure, illness, injury, sleep, treatment access and existing health records. Age, prior health, disability, substance use, service access, weather and episode duration are relevant modifiers. No numeric weights for their person-level blend were identified here. Race or gender alone must not become a death cause, and no during-play mortality roll is authorized.

## Work and income reflect existing hardship and actual employment

[Meyer and colleagues, NBER working paper 28861](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28861/w28861.pdf), Tables 13 and 14, links national Census/ACS records to tax and administrative data. The 2010 cohorts include about 111,000 sheltered adults and 60,000 adults enumerated at food vans or soup kitchens. The latter is an unsheltered proxy with incomplete coverage. Earnings are in 2018 dollars.

| Administrative outcome in 2010               |                            Sheltered cohort |     Food-van/soup-kitchen cohort |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------: | -------------------------------: |
| Any positive annual earnings                 | 52.8%, standard error 0.2 percentage points | 40.4%, standard error 0.2 points |
| Mean annual earnings, including zero earners |                  $5,673, standard error $49 |      $5,953, standard error $126 |

The descriptive positive-earnings range is 40.4%–52.8% across these two sampled groups. It is not a monthly job probability. The dollar means are not wages of employed workers. ACS survey measures use a different frame: 39.40% of sheltered adults worked in the previous year, with standard error 0.93 points. Working respondents averaged 29.56 weeks a year and 32.97 hours a week. Do not average survey and administrative percentages into a fabricated midpoint.

Low earnings, poverty and disability often predate homelessness. Annual trajectories establish no immediate employment-loss lag. Suggested wiring reads the person's job, actual wage flows, health, schedule, contact and transport access, and employer decisions. Housing disruption may change those recorded inputs. The national observations check aggregate results and inform broad starting anchors within their population; they do not impose an automatic earnings cut. Existing transport owners retain their paths.

## Family subsidy effects bound school and household mechanisms

The HUD trial randomized 2,282 families already in emergency shelter. The three-year survey reached 1,784 families, 78.2%, at a median 37 months; follow-up ranged from 32 to 50 months. Exhibit ES-7 compares a permanent-subsidy offer with usual care.

| Estimated intention-to-treat outcome at 37 months |                   Change | Qualification                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -----------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Homeless/doubled-up/shelter composite             |  −21.1 percentage points | Confirmatory housing outcome; includes doubled-up housing |
| Number of schools attended since assignment       |            −0.15 schools | School continuity measure                                 |
| Psychological distress                            | −0.11 standardized units | Report marks p below .10                                  |
| Child behavior problems                           | −0.23 standardized units | Report marks p below .05                                  |
| Food security                                     |   +9.6 percentage points | Report marks p below .01                                  |

The school estimate concerns a subsidy offer, not the isolated causal effect of a homelessness episode. The measured follow-up is not an onset lag. The reported work estimate was −6 percentage points at 20 months, but the 37-month estimate, −0.9 points, had no significance marker. Housing stability therefore does not establish automatic job improvement. Income, supportive services and labor incentives also change with the intervention. Nonconfirmatory tests are not adjusted for all multiple comparisons.

Suggested school wiring reads actual moves, enrollment, travel and absences. Preserve the child's prior trajectory and avoid counting one eviction, move and homelessness episode as three independent penalties. No direct national homelessness-to-graduation coefficient or universal monthly lag was verified.
