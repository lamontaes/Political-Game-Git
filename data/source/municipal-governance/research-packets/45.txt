# Cross-tranche design conclusions


## 1. Government-building names do not prove institutional merger
Examples: Charlotte-Mecklenburg Government Center and Denver City and County Building. Canonical government-unit and legal-authority data determine whether systems are merged/consolidated/shared; environment data only says where people work/meet.


## 2. Same building != same scene
Savannah workshop Media Room vs Council Chamber; Mobile 9th-floor council offices vs ground-floor auditorium; San Diego Council Chamber vs Committee Room; Denver Chamber vs Parr-Widener room.


## 3. Renovations and civic construction need effective dates
Little Rock chamber renovation, Raleigh's new East Civic Tower, Portland's post-2025 governance regime, and existing statehouse swing-space cases demonstrate that both INSTITUTIONAL RULES and PHYSICAL ENVIRONMENTS need time-versioning.


## 4. Formal meeting body names should survive normalization
`Board of Directors`, `Mayor and Aldermen`, `Commission`, `Governing Body`, `City Council`, consolidated `Commission-Council`, etc. can map to internal legislative capabilities without erasing authentic public terminology.


## 5. Remote participation is a meeting state, not a new building
A physical chamber can remain the canonical scene while one or more members participate remotely. Store `attendance_mode` per participant/meeting rather than replacing the room with a generic videoconference UI.


## 6. Environment research can create gameplay without inventing mechanics
Real room/building patterns naturally create:
- hallway encounters;
- short same-building transitions;
- public/security bottlenecks;
- press waiting areas;
- committee vs floor dynamics;
- executive/legislative proximity or separation;
- district-office vs central-office differences;
- renovation moves and institutional disruption.
These opportunities should emerge from the physical-government graph rather than arbitrary travel timers.


# Visual-pack completion rule
For each environment, promote from `LOCATION VERIFIED` to `ART READY` only after collecting enough evidence for:
1. at least 2-3 usable contemporary interior references where practical;
2. room orientation/layout/dais/seating logic;
3. architectural materials/lighting/window conditions;
4. fixed institutional objects vs occupant-controlled decoration;
5. date/vintage;
6. source provenance;
7. contradictions/renovation flags.
Do not generate an environment from the written description alone when photos are available.

# New reusable municipal environment patterns
1. `SHARED CIVIC CHAMBER`: same room used by council + committees/boards (Albuquerque, OKC).
2. `MULTI-LEGAL-BODY SAME MEMBERS`: elected council reconvenes as financing/housing/redevelopment authority (Sacramento, Salt Lake City examples).
3. `VERTICAL GOVERNMENT`: different political functions occupy distinct floors of one building (Mobile, Tulsa, Orlando).
4. `CIVIC CAMPUS`: executive/legislative buildings adjacent but not identical (Phoenix and similar complexes).
5. `NON-CITY-HALL LEGISLATURE`: elected municipal legislature meets in another civic institution (Anchorage library).
6. `CONSOLIDATED CITY-COUNTY`: jurisdictional authority differs from normal nested city/county model (Augusta, Denver, Jacksonville, etc.).
7. `PERSISTENT HYBRID PUBLIC ACCESS`: physical meeting remains canonical while remote testimony/public attendance is layered onto it (Oakland, Albuquerque, Anchorage and others).
8. `ROOM-AS-INSTITUTIONAL-RESOURCE`: rooms can be scheduled by multiple bodies; who occupies a room at a given time is simulation state rather than permanently baked scene art.
