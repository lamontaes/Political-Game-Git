## Purpose
Make political places feel geographically and institutionally real. A Montana governor, California governor, Illinois senator, Virginia delegate, Tennessee legislator, U.S. representative, and Peoria council member should not inhabit the same generic office with a different flag.


This is an environment/reference backbone, NOT permission to regenerate the UI. The approved Political Game shell remains separate. Scene art should normally be generated/constructed cleanly and the locked UI layered deterministically afterward.


## Hard environment rules
1. Research the jurisdiction/building/era before rendering.
2. Distinguish WORKING OFFICE from CEREMONIAL/RECEPTION/PRESS/CHAMBER/COMMITTEE/DISTRICT/RESIDENCE spaces.
3. Do not infer a governor works every day in an ornate historic tour room merely because that room is called “Governor’s Office.”
4. Preserve temporary/swing-space conditions by year.
5. Architecture/fixed institutional furnishings are base assets. Generic personal decoration is not baked into the base.
6. Personal/local/career objects are modular and should come from actual character/geographic/history state.
7. Reuse geometry only when real environments belong to a genuinely similar family; do not erase distinctive buildings for asset convenience.
8. Photo research needs multiple references where practical: official floor plans/tours, government stills/video, archives/historical agencies, then reputable local journalism when government imagery is inadequate.


## Asset/reference taxonomy
### UNIQUE / MUST CUSTOMIZE
Architecture/layout is distinctive enough that a generic template would visibly fail. Examples: Hawaii State Capitol, Louisiana Capitol, Nebraska Capitol, New Mexico Roundhouse, North Dakota Capitol, Illinois chambers, Boston City Council chamber, major ornate civic halls.


### STATE-SPECIFIC VARIANT
Shares a broad archetype with other government buildings but local architecture/materials/layout are important enough to preserve.


### REUSABLE BASE FAMILY
Ordinary office/committee geometry may be shared when the real spaces are genuinely similar, but proportions, textures, fixed furniture, windows, state/institutional fixtures, and later personalization modules still vary.


### ERA / TEMPORARY OVERRIDE
Selected year changes where politics physically occurs because of renovation, construction, relocation or swing space. This overrides the “normal” building family for that period.


## Federal Washington backbone
### U.S. House member-office families
Do not use one “Congress office.” At minimum:
- Cannon House Office Building — oldest House office-building family; classical/Beaux-Arts institutional detailing; generally older/more compact lineage.
- Longworth House Office Building — interwar/neoclassical but relatively economical working-office language; historically member suites used greeting/private-room logic.
- Rayburn House Office Building — larger postwar/mid-century family with expanded offices/committee space.
Individual member identity belongs in modular district/state/family/career artifacts, not in a baked generic “politician décor” layer.


### U.S. Senate member-office families
- Russell — older Beaux-Arts/classical, richer traditional material/detail lineage.
- Dirksen — mid-century functional office/committee environment.
- Hart — modern office/atrium family, dramatically different spatial language.


### Federal scene backlog
Still requires dedicated visual packs for:
- each House/Senate member-office family;
- committee/hearing room families;
- Capitol chamber/lobby/corridor spaces;
- leadership offices where materially different;
- district offices (grounded by actual district/local building class rather than Washington styling);
- White House/EEOB/Cabinet/agency headquarters families if/when executive gameplay enters those locations.

# Cross-state conclusions from the matrix
## 1. “Governor’s office” is not one universal scene type
The state pass produced at least these patterns:
- historic Capitol working office;
- modern executive tower;
- separate executive office building;
- Capitol ceremonial/session office + separate everyday working office;
- temporary swing-space office during construction;
- multiple distinct reception/press/business/legislative rooms.
The game should therefore store `scene_role` and `building/location` separately from political office title.


## 2. “State legislator office” is equally nonuniform
Patterns include:
- offices inside historic Capitol;
- dedicated House/Senate office buildings;
- shared Legislative Office Building;
- underground Capitol Extension;
- annexes;
- modern new General Assembly building;
- multiple support buildings connected by tunnels/concourses.


## 3. Chambers are frequently the most unique assets
Even where offices can share base geometry, chambers like Illinois, Hawaii, Nebraska, New Mexico, Louisiana and major historic capitols demand dedicated layout/material reference packs.


## 4. Time must be part of the scene key
Minimum scene lookup should resemble:
`jurisdiction + institution + role/scene_type + building + effective_date_range`
Examples:
- California Governor / working office / 1021 O Street / 2026;
- Virginia legislator / member office / General Assembly Building / 2023+;
- Minnesota legislator / working office / temporary construction-era assignment / selected year;
- Oregon legislature / selected temporary/current space / CAMS construction period.


# Municipal-government backbone
## Canonical jurisdiction universe
Use Census Government Units Survey / 2025 Government Units Listing from Part 1 rather than relying on a hand-maintained city list or on EAVS reporting units. Reconcile Census governments with Census places/county subdivisions and state-specific government forms.


## Selection model for visual coverage
For every state, build at least three municipal reference targets:
1. MAJOR CITY — generally the largest or most politically/economically important municipal government in the state.
2. REPRESENTATIVE MEDIUM CITY — selected systematically from the middle of the state’s municipal population distribution, then checked for government-form and regional representativeness rather than hand-picked by fame.
3. DISTINCTIVE CIVIC HALL — an architecturally/historically/institutionally unusual city/county/municipal chamber worth dedicated art even if population is smaller.


Do not hard-code one city per tier forever. The underlying Census government universe means later content can expand to any real jurisdiction.


## Proposed systematic municipal selection algorithm
A. Start from active Census municipal/township governments for the selected state.
B. Crosswalk to Census place/county-subdivision population estimates where applicable.
C. Major city = top population/political center, with exception handling for unusual consolidated city-county systems or where the capital is especially gameplay-relevant.
D. Medium city = choose candidates around the state-specific 50th–75th percentile among meaningful municipal governments, then prefer one with accessible official chamber/office imagery and a government form not redundant with the major city.
E. Distinctive = search statewide historic-preservation/municipal/architectural sources for unusual civic halls/chambers, regardless of population.
F. Record mayor-council/council-manager/commission/town-meeting/other form separately from visual architecture.
G. Never infer government powers from chamber appearance; institutional authority comes from charter/state-law backbone.

# Modular office personalization — PROPOSED DIRECTION, NOT YET FULLY LOCKED
The base institutional environment should be comparatively neutral. Personal meaning is layered from persistent simulation history rather than fabricated decorative clutter.


### Layer 1 — fixed institutional room
Architecture, fixed/built-in furnishings, building-provided desk/seating, required flags/seals, permanent building art, functional equipment, windows/doors/lighting.


### Layer 2 — jurisdiction/local modules
District/state maps, locally plausible landscape/art/history/civic items, university/regional objects when actually relevant. Do NOT turn geography into clichés (Arizona=cactus; Florida=beach) automatically.


### Layer 3 — personal-life artifacts
Family/spouse/child photos, child school pictures that can age/change over years, graduation, military/service history, hobby objects, memorials, gifts, personal achievements.


### Layer 4 — political/career artifacts
Campaign photos/signs, signed laws, constituent gifts, front pages, photos with allies/leaders, awards, district/cause memorabilia, historically meaningful documents/events.


### Layer 5 — player display choices / character habits
Where appropriate, allow the player/character’s actual preferences and staff behavior to determine which owned artifacts are displayed. Offices can become visual biographies over time.


Hard rule: no generic books/plants/awards/photographs merely because an image generator thinks a politician’s office should look “busy.” Every nonfunctional object should have an institutional, geographic, personal or historical reason to be there.

## Environment production rule reinforced by this tranche
`office_role` is not enough to select an environment. Resolve at least:
`jurisdiction -> institution -> building -> room family -> era/effective date -> scene use`.


Then apply:
1. institutional base geometry/materials/fixtures;
2. current-year renovation/temporary-location state;
3. occupant personalization modules from canonical biography/geography/career;
4. people/working artifacts for the specific scene;
5. locked Political Game UI as a separate deterministic overlay.


Never infer that a photographed ceremonial/restored room is the current working office merely because it is called a Governor/Speaker/etc. room. Record `room_use_type` such as working office, staff office, reception/ceremonial, chamber, hearing room, committee office, visitor/historic restoration, press room or temporary/swing space.

## Municipal lesson from this tranche
The game should not expose a generic `city council` object with a universal member count, executive relationship, room or procedure.


Already-observed real differences include:
- Chicago: Mayor presides over a large aldermanic Council under term-specific rules.
- Springfield: Mayor/Aldermanic body, Robert's Rules fallback, Council Chamber doubles as administrative-hearing room.
- Detroit: strong-mayor + district/at-large Council + unusually prominent charter-created citizen/police oversight bodies.
- Grand Rapids: Mayor presides politically while a Council-appointed City Manager is chief administrator.
- Lincoln: home-rule Mayor-Council with a shared City/County building and joint city-county planning institutions.
- Minneapolis: executive Mayor + separate 13-member ward legislature.
- Saint Paul: municipal legislature embedded in a shared Art Deco city/county civic complex.
- Columbus: council structure itself changed in 2024 and must be time-versioned.
- Cincinnati: Mayor, Council and professional City Manager coexist as distinct actors within one historic City Hall.
- Cleveland: charter population thresholds directly changed the Council from 17 to 15 wards for 2026.


These institutional differences should affect staffing, appointments, agenda control, vetoes, oversight, constituent work, committee behavior and career progression—not merely visual labels.

## Cross-cutting findings from the consolidated/distinctive tranche
1. Consolidation is not one thing. Louisville, Lexington, Nashville and San Francisco all combine local-government functions differently. Preserve their legal structure instead of setting `is_consolidated = true` and assuming identical powers.

1. DC requires a federal-local legislative review dependency that should exist nowhere else unless a jurisdiction's law actually provides one.

## Campus topology lesson from this tranche
A state can be:
- tightly Capitol-centered (South Dakota, North Dakota, much of Montana/New Mexico);
- Capitol + dedicated extension/support complex (Wyoming, Arizona legislative buildings, Nevada support buildings);
- or Capitol-centered with specialized rooms that have institutional identities stronger than generic `committee room` (Colorado Old State Library/JBC, North Dakota named rooms).


Do not choose an asset family solely from state population or building age. Use actual selected-year campus topology and meeting location.
