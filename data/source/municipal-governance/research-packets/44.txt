# Next-Phase Municipal Governance and Environment Matrix — 2026-08-25


## Status
ACTIVE NATIONAL MUNICIPAL PASS. This document continues Part 2 at the municipal-government level. It is not a finished 50-state matrix yet.


## Purpose
Build a real municipal institutional and scene backbone from official sources. Each city entry must distinguish governance form from physical environment. The same label, such as “City Council,” can conceal materially different council size, district structure, mayor powers, professional-manager authority, veto/override rules, committee systems, public-comment rules, board/commission ecosystems, meeting locations, and office-building layouts.


This work follows the Part 1 Census Government Units backbone. Census supplies the governmental universe; current municipal charters, codes, budgets, meeting records, directories, public notices, planning documents, and official facility records supply the institutional and physical detail.


## Hard research rules
1. Prefer current official municipal sources, especially charters/codes, council rules, meeting agendas, budgets, clerk pages, facility pages, and planning/historic-preservation records.
2. Time-version institutional structures and locations. Do not apply a 2026 council size, building, or charter rule to an earlier start year unless it was then in force.
3. Treat governance form, elected-body structure, executive authority, professional administration, and physical scene family as separate fields.
4. A council chamber may be outside City Hall; a ward office may be geographically distributed; one chamber may serve several boards; a mayor may preside without voting; a professional manager may administer while a mayor remains politically prominent.
5. Do not infer room appearance from government form or government powers from architecture.
6. Separate permanent architecture from rotating exhibits, current-office decorations, temporary construction, and occupant-specific artifacts.
7. Never promote a candidate city to a production-ready visual pack until actual contemporary room/building imagery is sufficient.

# Tranche 1 cross-cutting lessons
- A municipal legislature may meet in a library (Anchorage), a special event/civic venue (Fort Smith), an auditorium separate from council offices (Mobile), or a shared city-county building (Denver and later examples).
- Professional administration and political executive authority can coexist in several distinct forms; “manager” does not imply identical mayor powers.
- Ward/district offices can be physically distributed across the city (Tucson, Denver), so constituent work should not always teleport to central City Hall.
- A physical chamber can support Council, planning, boards, hearings, budget sessions, or other procedural states; `scene_venue` and `active_institution` must be separate.
- Recent structural reforms must carry effective dates.

# Tranche 4 cross-cutting additions
- CONSOLIDATION does not necessarily abolish nested municipalities (Louisville/Jefferson County).
- MUNICIPAL LEGISLATIVE STAFF CAPACITY varies materially (Lexington research/budget staff; Shreveport Council analyst; Baltimore legislative infrastructure) and should affect what work elected officials can delegate.
- COUNCIL-MANAGER is a family, not one government type: Portland has a directly elected RCV mayor; Bangor and Cambridge select mayors from the council; Worcester directly elects a mayor but keeps professional executive management.
- ELECTORAL METHOD itself belongs in the institutional backbone: ranked-choice voting, proportional representation, ward/district/at-large mixtures and internal leadership elections create different career strategies.
- INDEPENDENT CITY, CONSOLIDATED CITY-COUNTY/PARISH, HOME-RULE CITY and ORDINARY INCORPORATED MUNICIPALITY are different legal-geographic relationships.
- EXTERNAL OR COUNTY-PROVIDED SERVICES must be explicit. A city government may not control schools, fire protection, libraries or other services residents associate with “the city.”
- NEW COMMITTEES, CHARTER REFORMS, COUNCIL EXPANSIONS and DISTRICT MAP CHANGES are historical institutional events with effective dates, not static metadata.
- A chamber can have meaningful connected subspaces such as a Members’ Lobby (Cambridge), and procedural access rules can shape scenes.

## Nevada


### Las Vegas — COUNCIL-MANAGER / MAYOR + SIX WARDS
Verified:
- Las Vegas explicitly identifies itself as council-manager government.
- Mayor and City Council are the governing body; Council appoints the City Manager and approves key managerial appointments. City Manager runs the professional administrative organization.
- Current organizational chart effective January 2026 places City Manager Mike Janssen beneath Mayor & City Council and above the operating departments.
- Mayor Shelley Berkley was elected in November 2024.
- Current Council geography uses six wards.
- City Hall is 495 S. Main Street; current 2026 events identify the Council Chambers on the second floor.
- Ward-level elected offices sponsor and attend neighborhood/community events outside City Hall, so constituent politics belongs in distributed venues as well as the chamber.
- Council also acts as the Redevelopment Agency and members sit on regional bodies including transit, flood-control and health institutions, generating cross-institutional roles.

### Reno — 2024 TRANSITION FROM FIVE WARDS + AT-LARGE SEAT TO SIX WARDS
Verified:
- Reno is council-manager government; Mayor is the chief elected/formal representative and City Manager selected by Council is chief administrative official.
- Current City Council has seven people total: Mayor plus six ward Council members.
- In 2024 Reno transitioned from five wards plus an at-large Council seat to SIX WARDS; the old at-large Council seat was eliminated. The sixth ward was created following state legislation and a City Charter Committee recommendation.
- Current 2026 election material confirms Wards 2 and 4 are up in 2026 and explicitly states the City transitioned from five to six wards in 2024.
- Regular Council meetings take place in City Council Chamber at City Hall, 1 E. First Street.
- Council also sits as Redevelopment Agency Board in some meetings.
Simulation implication: pre-2024 and post-2024 Reno have different electoral institutions, not merely a new district map.

### Carson City — CONSOLIDATED MUNICIPALITY / MAYOR + FOUR SUPERVISORS ALL ELECTED AT LARGE WITH WARD RESIDENCY
Verified:
- Carson City consolidated with Ormsby County in 1969.
- Governing body is a five-member Board of Supervisors: Mayor plus four Supervisors.
- All five are elected at large, but each Supervisor must reside in one of Wards 1-4. This is distinct from ward-only voting and ordinary at-large representation.
- Board as a whole is both legislative and executive branch; it appoints a City Manager, who serves as the administrative/operational arm under the council-manager framework.
- Mayor chairs meetings and is head of local government for ceremonial/emergency purposes.
- Regular Board meetings occur in the Robert “Bob” Crowell Board Room of the Community Center, not City Hall.
- City Hall is separately at 201 N. Carson Street.
Production implication: Carson City is a high-value exception where consolidated elected government meets in a community-center board room while City Hall is a separate administrative building.
Scene families:
1. ROBERT “BOB” CROWELL BOARD ROOM / COMMUNITY CENTER.
2. CITY HALL administrative offices, 201 N. Carson.
3. MAYOR + FOUR WARD-RESIDENCY/AT-LARGE Supervisor political family.
4. CITY MANAGER administrative family.
5. PLANNING and other Board/Commission procedural states in the community-center system.

https://www.carsoncity.gov/government/board-of-supervisors

### Manchester — MAYOR + 14 ALDERMEN / 12 WARDS + TWO AT-LARGE
Verified:
- Current 2026-2027 Board of Mayor and Aldermen is the legislative body.
- Board consists of Mayor plus 14 Aldermen: one from each of 12 wards and two elected at large.
- Aldermen serve two-year terms.
- Regular BMA meetings are in Aldermanic Chambers on the THIRD FLOOR of City Hall, One City Hall Plaza.
- The City Clerk prepares agendas for the Board, nine standing committees and special committees, creating a substantial legislative-support role.

- Manchester’s boards/commissions ecosystem includes Police Commission, Fire Commission, Planning Board, Retirement Board, Transit Authority and other bodies with mixed mayoral/aldermanic/ex-officio appointment structures.
Production implication: the elected government is only one part of Manchester’s formal power map; independent/semi-independent commissions and appointment coalitions matter.

- Nine Ward Aldermen are elected for two-year terms; six At-Large Aldermen are elected to four-year terms, with three at-large seats elected at each municipal election.
- Mayor is separately elected at large.
- Board is Nashua’s governing/policymaking body except where Charter provides otherwise.

# Tranche 6 cross-cutting additions
- AT-LARGE ELECTION WITH WARD RESIDENCY (Carson City) is distinct from ward-only election and ordinary citywide at-large seats.
- ELECTORAL REGIME TRANSITION can eliminate an office entirely (Reno’s at-large seat) while preserving the same total number of elected policy-makers.
- LARGE ALDERMANIC BODIES (Manchester, Nashua) coexist with boards/commissions that may hold real executive, appointment or service-control authority.
- CITY CLERK / LEGISLATIVE OFFICE capacity should be represented as institutional staff, not invisible menu plumbing.
- FORMAL COUNCIL, CAUCUS, PRE-MEETING CONFERENCE and PUBLIC TOWN HALL are different procedural states even when the same people/room are involved.
- SHARED CIVIC BUILDING does not necessarily imply consolidated government: Omaha’s shared city-county Civic Center and Carson City’s legally consolidated government are distinct institutional facts.
- NEW JERSEY FAULKNER-STYLE MUNICIPALITIES still require city-specific data: similar formal categories can produce different council geography, administrative practice, leadership selection and meeting culture.
