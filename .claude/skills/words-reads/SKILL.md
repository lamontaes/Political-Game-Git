# Words reads

**A words change is read as played, in drawn places, on a fresh draw, with the lines quoted, before it goes anywhere.** Tests say a rule holds. The read says what a player gets. On Nov 14 the conversation engine passed 15 of 18 test files and, read as played, had all 18 strangers in three towns say the same two questions.

## What the builder sends

1. The head's full sha and what a player now sees, in one sentence.
2. The gate with the train or main merged in: changed tests on the named seed and on a fresh seed, typecheck, `npm run guards`, `tests/english-parts-banks.test.ts`, `npm run words:rules` twice with a clean tree, `npm run words:composed -- --check`, zero-dice, `release:check --mode pr`. Say which reds are also red on the clean base. Never raise a baseline or lower what a test expects to pass.
3. The lines as played, whole, in order, with who speaks: at least three places drawn from all 56 on a seed nobody chose, the places passed over named, in a file under the shared project folder. For a screen, a picture at 1440 by 900 before and after. The change's own subject must appear: a year of papers with no endorsement in it is not a read of an endorsement brief.
4. Faults the builder saw while reading, left in.

## What the lead does

- Reads every line, not a sample, and counts where a count settles it (how many bodies repeat, how many talks ask a question).
- Rules in numbered rules a builder can check, in a file beside the lines, and says which reds are the branch's.
- Writes the lines itself when a builder lists the piece group, the facts a line may read, who speaks to whom and how many wordings are needed. A subject whose pieces are missing stays unoffered; a stand-in line is never written.
- Posts `Words lead: train-ready #N <full sha>` only after the gate and the read, quoting lines as played. A ruling from the owner that a cleared change breaks withdraws the line the same hour.
- A missing picture never holds words back (owner, Nov 14: "do not let pics hold it back. it can be generated later"). The scene plays on its fallback and is named in the wanted-pictures list.

## Rules 30 to 37: election result stories

30. A race for several seats is one story at each outlet, naming its winners together and the first who missed, with how far back.
31. A name in full once, then the last name; two people with one last name keep full names, in headlines too.
32. One name on the ballot is "ran unopposed" or "elected without opposition": no share, no "beat", no vote total. As many names as seats is unopposed.
33. A whole-number share only in a race for one seat with two or more on the ballot; under half, the field is said ("in a field of four"). Counts in figures.
34. Offices in lower case in a sentence; a district office in a headline is "District 5 commissioner". The body by its name as people say it, from the one name writer.
35. The runner-up comes from that race's own count.
36. Headline by last name; the body opens with the race, the count and the runner-up, turnout last.
37. No two outlets print the same body of one race. Wordings are picked by the outlet's order among those that printed it, and the test checks every place it draws.

## Rules 38 to 49: speeches

38. The belief leads an ask at most once in an address; the other asks come first, fold the belief in or leave it out. No address has two sentences opening "Because I".
39. Years are said once; counts under ten in words.
40. The body is named in the greeting and the first ask, then "this council".
41. The close does not repeat an ask.
42. A measure is named once, then "it".
43. An opponent names the measure plainly or their own way, never in its supporters' words.
44. The condition comes before its answer.
45. A figure is called trouble only where the record says so, is said once, and the line after it says what it means with no number.
46. A small amount is not offered as a remedy; with no record of the ask's size, no amount is said.
47. A bad term always shows, whatever kind the address is.
48. A good term says one good thing, the improved measure nearest the address's subject.
49. One "On {theme}," opener at most; the place word comes from the record ("across Guam", never "the state" in a territory); a person is thanked once.

## Rule 50: dialogue is only dialogue (owner, Nov 14)

"None of those count lines or anything like that should pop up in dialogue. It should literally just be dialogue." Nobody speaks a count, tally or progress of the player's own doing. A clerk says once what the office requires and by when; after that people say "that many". Numbers a player needs live on a screen. A clerk telling you your papers are in, or that three others are running, is talk and stays.

## Rules 51 to 61: conversation

51. A stranger does not know you: no name, one greeting, none of the lines for people who have met. Names come out by being given.
52. Nobody asks what they can see or already know. A stranger opens on why the two are in one place or by saying who they are.
53. One fact once in a talk.
54. The question fits the person's record (someone still in school is not asked where they went).
55. A talk never ends on the player's answer.
56. Six strangers in a day are six talks: what a person raises comes from that person.
57. People at home ask questions, each with a record behind it (the player's day, the calendar, something heard).
58. One opening line carries one thing.
59. A line about the President says which side the President took, then the speaker's view, as two parts (a lead and a stance) so the bank combines them; no line says "he" or "she" for the President unless it reads the record; no stock phrase stands in for the measure.
60. An answer answers the question; with no local link on record the speaker says so or the question is not offered.
61. One person in a room raises the address in an evening.

## Rules 62 to 73: door scenes

62. A scene has a middle: a door with a cause on record gets an exchange about the resident's own matter, with at least two replies that end differently.
63. One button is not a choice. No scene offers a single reply.
64. A name in narration comes from the household record, or nobody is named.
65. He, she or they comes from the person's record.
66. Narration states nothing the record does not hold: no pets, no other people, no objects that claim a fact. The door, the light, the hour, the dwelling kind and what the cause implies are free.
67. No narration and no reply plays twice in one outing; which wording a resident uses comes from that resident.
68. Nobody claims a past that is not on record ("I've been waiting for someone to ask").
69. The office and the day are said as people say them ("running for council", "Election Day").
70. Door endings have their own labels ("Promised you a vote", "Would not sign").
71. Progress is not said aloud ("That's one closer" is a count).
72. What a resident says matters to them is their own strongest question from their views record, with their side and a reason from their life; the player then holds it as a learned view. Stock answers for everyone are a fault.
73. After the opening line the town and the office are each said at most once more in a scene. A line meets the insert guard with a record that belongs in it, or is three words or fewer; never pad with the place name.

## Rules 74 to 81: conversation, second pass

74. A player's reply never says a slot's own name ("How long have you been with work?"); it says "there".
75. A stance must parse with its lead; mark which stances follow which leads in the pieces.
76. A follow-up fits the side: "plan" and "pass it" only when the President is for the measure.
77. One subject once per talk: nobody raises the same question under two headings.
78. A reply answers the line before it, and an opener says the stand, not its history ("always").
79. A figure is said once per talk; after that it is "that" or "the rent".
80. A goodbye is one goodbye: a core that is already a goodbye takes no closer.
81. A modest wording ("just a") fits the job; not for someone who heads a department or a body.

## Rules 82 to 90: the Journal

82. A past paragraph is one stretch of a life (growing up; first work and a partner; the children; the working years; the last few years), headed by its span of years.
83. Dates are said, never printed raw: a past paragraph by its years, a played entry by its day as the game says days.
84. A partner is named as what they are the first time ("my wife, Robin"), from the relationship record.
85. Children are told together in one sentence; a child gets its own sentence only when something else on record happened with it.
86. The town is named at most once per paragraph, and not at all where the chapter heading names it.
87. Nothing trivial (a spell of work under a season folds into the working years) and nothing about someone else alone.
88. Family is said the way people say it ("I had an older brother, Diego"), and a fact the record cannot explain is left out.
89. An event is told with its end: a filing says what it meant, election day is an entry, a race tells its result with the count once.
90. One entry per law that reached the person, by the law's said name, with the one largest change and where they learned of it; no entry for a change they could not notice.

## Rules 91 to 100: the reporter's call, the door, a seat said aloud, and what a reply must do

91. A bill is named once in a scene, by its said name; after that it is "the bill" or "it".
92. A reporter speaks for their own outlet and asks only about what a record put in front of them.
93. "Why?" gets a reason on record. A vote or a no with no reason on record is a fault in the record (a pool item), never something to word around.
94. Each follow-up question is about a new thing from a record, not the first question again.
95. Nobody is offered a lie about a roll that was watched.
96. A room holds only the people the records put there.
97. A stall is said once; a second one ends the call.
98. A count, where one is said at all outside a scene, is said once.
99. A door asks about what the office being run for can touch, and a candidate does not promise to act on a question above the office. The same holds at a desk and for a donor.
100.  A seat is said aloud with the area it covers ("Congress, the 3rd District", "the state Senate in the 17th District"); a statewide seat takes no place at the counter, and a town's own office takes nothing after it at its own counter.

Three rulings that go with these. The rules that a scene names its stake and that a promise names who, what and why judge a scene's path up to its ask, never one line or one pair of lines. An offered reply that nothing later reads is not offered: keep the offer as a fact a later talk brings up, or cut the reply. And before lines are written for a scene kind, it is played with the player in the right seat in three drawn places: a kind that never stages, or whose packet holds no facts, needs a producer or a fact first (skill `scene-producers`).

## Traps

- A test that counts questions or wordings going red after a rule change is usually telling the truth. Find which change emptied the supply before touching the test.
- A script that prints every wording of a story makes a paper look repetitive; say so in the lines file.
- `data/english/parts/` edits can be refused by the permission check for a builder. Do not work around it: list the lines in bank shape for the lead.
- The bank's limits shape every line the lead writes: a fixed line of four or more words needs a record slot (the line-inserts baseline only goes down) and a part is ten words at most. Write to both from the start, and never fill the slot with the town's name just to pass.
- When a count test wants many different lines, do not write near-twins: split the line into two parts the bank combines (a lead and a stance), and delete the whole-line pieces they replace.
- Read a builder's fault before ruling on it. A card that looked wrong ("Went to school together" in July) was right: the player had graduated in June. Ask whose card it is.
- A picture of one screen can hide the next one: a drafting card passed while the bill it produced showed one step of three. Ask for the screen the player lands on after pressing the button.
