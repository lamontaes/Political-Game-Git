# Surface / Register Matrix

Identify the surface before writing any text. The same canonical fact renders
differently per surface; a universal register is itself a defect.

## 1. Status / information UI

- Purpose: precise state at a glance. Length: fragment to one line.
- No dialogue, no personality. Numbers, designations, procedural stage;
  telegraphic legal: "HB 218 / Second reading complete / Floor vote tomorrow".
- May repeat verbatim when the same factual state warrants the same text.
- Failures: narrativizing UI; vagueness ("things aren't going well").
- Example: "You're down six points."

## 2. Menu / action choice

- Purpose: say what the player does. Length: usually 1–8 words.
- The action, plus only _certain_ intrinsic consequences. Casual wording for
  casual moments ("Sure. Go eat with them.").
- Silence, evasion, refusal, listening, and speaking are distinct actions when
  the distinction matters — never collapse them into "Don't answer".
- Failures: predicted reactions; bureaucratic wording; ambiguous labels.
- Example: "Vote no." (expectation exposed separately: "Your aide thinks
  leadership will take a no vote badly.")

## 3. Quick interaction

- Purpose: deliver a fact through a person, fast. Length: 1–2 sentences.
- Summarized dialogue, light personality, established relationship terms.
- Failures: inflating into a scene; quoting when the exchange doesn't matter.
- Example: "Your aide tells you the votes aren't there yet."

## 4. Narrative transition

- Purpose: move time/place. Length: 0–2 sentences — **deletion is valid**.
- Established seasonal/local texture; orientation toward known upcoming
  events.
- Failures: mechanical "A month later"; synonymized filler; invented
  festivals.
- Example: "Life in Kentucky carries on much as before."

## 5. Full scene

- Purpose: a moment worth playing. Length: short paragraph(s).
- Direct dialogue common; strongest legal personality coloration, still
  bounded by facts actually present.
- Failures: invented staging/arrivals; emotion-telling; suspense by default.
- Example: "Your aide closes the door. 'We're short.'"

## 6. Direct dialogue

- Use when the exchange itself matters, personality matters, or a player
  response is likely. Speaker voice; professional jargon legal in professional
  mouths.
- Failures: exposition dumps; re-explaining relationships in-line.
- Example: "I barely see you anymore." / "If you want it, you can start
  Saturday."

## 7. Summarized dialogue

- Use when the information matters more than the exchange. Keep attribution
  and established uncertainty ("your aide thinks...").
- Failures: losing attribution; over-quoting; false precision.
- Example: "The count isn't good. Your aide thinks you're still a few votes
  short in committee."

## 8. Memory / prior-decision moment

- Purpose: continuity from earlier choices. Usually one sentence; UI
  provenance markers carry the causal link.
- Salience-conditioned recognition; no perfect recall.
- Failures: "This reminds you of..."; melodramatic payoff for minor
  continuity.
- Example: "Your brother pays back the money you lent him."

## 9. In-world artifacts (not second person)

Second person is the default for character-facing narration only. In-world
artifacts keep their native register:

- **9a News article / broadcast copy** — third-person journalistic; headline
  conventions; attribution. Failures: second-person leakage; invented quotes
  or reactions; a quote from someone the facts say gave none.
- **9b Letter / correspondence** — sender's natural letter voice; formality
  per relationship and purpose; only what the sender knows and would write.
  Failures: home-narration casualness in formal mail; relationship
  re-explanation.
- **9c Legal / official document** — legal/document register; designation and
  procedural precision; state only established determinations (say no
  decision has been made when needed to avoid implying one). Failures:
  narrativizing; softening precise terms to be "human"; invented reasons or
  implied outcomes.
- **9d Memo / staff work product** — professional memo register; exact
  numbers; jargon legal here; no invented analysis or recommendation the
  facts don't supply. Failures: emotional coloration; second person
  narration; strategic inference beyond arithmetic.
- **9e Database / dossier** — factual reference register (extends surface 1).
  Failures: prose padding; vagueness.
