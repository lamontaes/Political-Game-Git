# A scene builder's first report

Part of the `scene-standard` skill. Until Nov 17, 2026 this was the `scene-first-report` skill; the CTO merged it here when the skills were capped (owner: "Let's go ahead"). What it covered: The first report a scene or story builder writes before any line: what stages the scene, how often it would in a played year, what facts the packet holds, what it writes back, and what the engine cannot yet do. Use at the start of any scene, conversation, story or "ask someone to" job. Not for writing lines (civic-prose, words-engine) or checking them (trusted-review-pass).

No lines come first. The Words lead reads this report and rules before a single line is written.

Report, for each variant of the scene:

1. **What stages it.** The record that makes it due, with its file and function, in the engine that owns it (`scene-producers`). One situation type with variants by data, never a path per office or place.
2. **How often.** Run a played year (`done-proof` (played-world-proof part)) in three drawn places and say how many times it staged and when. A scene that never stages is the finding.
3. **The packet.** Every fact the scene may say, from records (names, jobs, the tie to the player, the town, the office). Nothing else may be said: no he or she, no history, no "always" that no record holds.
4. **What it writes back.** Each ending writes a record a later scene or screen reads (`scene-standard`). List them.
5. **Who can be asked and why**, from records, as the existing list is.
6. **What the engine cannot do today.** A seat or event with no engine record is listed, not built; say what is missing.
7. **What the player's offered replies are** and how they differ by traits.

Then stop and wait for the ruling while other work continues. The owner's example in the ask is a generality (`crew-coordinator` (owners-words-to-threads part)): name the class of cases the scene serves.

## Before you start a design

Look in `/mnt/project-files/words-lead/words-machine/` and the story queue for a sibling thread doing the same work, and ask that thread before building a second one (`look-first`).

Name a record's new fields the way the engine's owner plans them (Elections' `channel` and `heardBy` on the announcement, not names of your own), so the report, the build and the owner's engine agree (CTO, Nov 18).

Before the report, trace the scene's engine path in three drawn worlds and print what it returns (lines, replies, the subjects offered and refused); a report that says "the engine says nothing" names the gate that stops it, by file and line, or the finding is not ready (Nov 18: the opening evening's housemate was stopped by `addressPoints`, not by missing lines alone).
