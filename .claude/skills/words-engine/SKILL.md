# The words engine and the director: the handoff

**Look first:** run the `look-first` search before building, fixing, filing or asking; a found ruling is built and cited, never asked again.

**Who does what (owner, Nov 10: "Split by role").** The Story session writes, changes and deletes every line under `data/english/parts/`: the rewrites of lines the rules pull, the new reply moves that make four replies four different answers, and the saved meaning-and-tone verdicts. Fable and its helpers build the machine: the rules, the checks, the run, the pulled list and the code that composes replies. Any other builder lists the lines and moves it needs on its item's pull request and Story writes them; one writer per bank file. Where a line below says "write" or "add a piece", read it as the Story session's job, or as the instruction for what a builder must list. Every note the owner gives becomes a rule in `data/english/line-rules.json` that a program tests (`npm run words:rules`); a rule a program cannot judge is judged once by a reviewer and saved (`data/english/judged/`).

**Lines ship per scene; real lives calibrate (owner, Oct 26-27; `scenes-from-life`).** Batch 22 was the Oct 15 lines; no batch after it, and no grading batch is owed. Every scene a pull request touches, new or old, lands with its own new lines written to `scene-standard` (several per speaker by trait and tie, up to four replies that are all different stances), and the Oct 15 lines it replaces are deleted in the same change; since Nov 10 the Story session writes and deletes those lines (see "Who does what"). New scenes come from real lives: one episode, a fact packet, the engine asked whether it fires, gaps turned into producers and lines (`scenes-from-life`). The home talks the owner heard on Oct 26 are rewritten first.

**SMART and look first (owner, Oct 26; `.agents/rules/smart-and-look-first.md`).** The standard is Wildermyth (`OWNER-DECISIONS.md`, Oct 26): a scene advances the story and hands the player back to the game with something changed; people are varied, with pasts from the world's records that surface in scenes. Research into real speech and records is allowed and expected.

## The target: scenes like Wildermyth (owner, Oct 24)

Scenes "that come out of what is actually happening in the world, narrated into a moment, played out with real dialogue and choices, the outcome back into the game; the current opening is the right shape, the words are wrong." That is the measure of every piece of this line: a record fires it, the narrator sets it, people with purposes play it out with four real replies, and what the player chose goes back into the records.

## The one rule that explains everything

Words come from the English engine; every bank part plays unless the owner's grades hold it or one of his rules pulled it (`data/english/pulled.json`), hand-written (`origin: authored`) parts included (owner, Oct 24; this replaces the Oct 20 "held until graded" hold, which no longer applies). Words come from the English engine's banks (`data/english/parts/`) and control names. The banks are built at development time: pieces are written to calibrated patterns, drawing on any source for the patterns (books, other games, transcripts, the internet), and copying no one's copyrighted text. At play the engine arranges the pieces; the director decides which moment is happening and who is in it. Neither types a line. Public-domain records remain a fine source of pieces, not a requirement. The old "two documents, one public-domain" attestation rule was never the owner's and is withdrawn (OWNER-DECISIONS.md, "The text rule", Oct 15, evening). Research and real data calibrate how pieces are arranged; they never decide a person's outcome.

## The three layers, and where each lives

1. **Situations (the director, `src/director/`).** `src/director/data/situation-types.json` is the catalog: 103 situation types (counted Nov 10; `src/director/director.test.ts` holds the count) (`departure`, `celebration`, `news-arrives`, `first-meeting`, `visit`, ...), each with roles (who fills it: `subject`, `counterpart`, `household`, `kin`, `present`, `classmates`, `coworkers`, `absent`, `authority`), the **moves** each role may make (`farewell`, `promise`, `comfort`, `tell`, `request`, `blame`, ...), what each role **wants**, a **bearing** (tender, solemn, ...), a setting, a timing, and **causes**: the record labels that fire it (a `money:job-started` record, a death, an eviction). A cause whose producer the core does not have yet names it in `awaitingProducer` and matches nothing. No type holds a sentence. The director (`src/director/scene.ts`, `scheduling.ts`, `ledger.ts`) watches the player's circle, fires a situation when a record matches a cause, stages it (home, work, town, the council room), and runs it as moves. `conversation.ts` runs talks: opens from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person in the ledger, keeps to that topic, chains to one related topic, closes when done; up to four replies ranked by the player's traits, lies as the same moves made false, "say nothing" beside them (`src/director/data/conversation-rules.json`). `journal.ts` writes the first-person "My life" memoir from records. `opening-story.ts` is the opening.
2. **Moves (the grammar).** A move is one thing a person does with a line: greet, deliver news, ask, answer, condole, congratulate, deflect, lie, agree, close, farewell, comfort, request, blame. Moves are situation-blind: a condolence serves a funeral, an assassination and a lost election. The director picks the next move from the situation's arc and the speaker's traits and relationship; the engine fills it. The move names in the catalog and the `kind`/`stage` names in the banks must agree; that mapping is the seam between director and engine and is the first thing to check when a line comes out wrong.
3. **Pieces (the banks, `data/english/parts/*.json`).** One bank per register (`ls data/english/parts` is the list; 21 files on Nov 10): `conversation.json`, `scene-talk.json`, `scene-words.json`, `journal.json`, `person-history.json`, `person-sketch.json`, `address.json` (floor, hearing, public comment, campaign, victory, concession), `opening-speech.json`, `speech-stages.json`, `minutes.json`, `legislation.json`, `bill-paper.json`, `notice.json`, `hearing.json`, `newscast.json`, `news.json`, `headlines.json`, `calendar.json`, `campaign-standing.json`, `bring-it-up.json`, `choice-names.json`. Each part is a sentence with slots, its move (`kind`, `stage`), its register, the record facts it needs (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the fact and is said only when the record holds it), and optionally a source note (document, date, address, license) or `origin: authored` with author and date. `src/english/english-composition.ts` (`composeAddress`, `composeFromBank`), `grounded-english.ts` and `speech-registers.ts` compose; `calibration.ts` and `move-fit.ts` weigh pieces by the speaker's traits, the register's length and the move before, never by a grade of the line; `english-grades.ts` applies the owner's grades (a failed part is never used; a conditional one only where its condition holds); `bank-parts.ts` (`playable`) is the one gate, grades and pulled list together; `runtime-text-origin.ts` was meant to guard that every shipped string has a bank origin, but its audit (`tests/e2e/runtime-text-audit.spec.ts`) is not on main and its baseline listed strings from `src/player`, which no longer exists (checked Oct 26 and again Nov 10). Nothing enforces it; check by hand with `player-screen-text`.

## The words machine: his notes become rules (owner, Oct 23 to Nov 10; `docs/design/words-machine.md`)

He will not grade thousands of lines ("im not grading 6000 lines and 800 conversations individually", Nov 8) and wants a grade to change the whole game ("every time I update a batch or something, it should update through the entire game", Oct 23). So a note of his becomes a rule, a program runs every rule over every line, and a line that breaks a rule stops playing until it is rewritten. There are no odds: the grade map, the reach table and the weights that favored ungraded lines are deleted. A line plays or it does not.

- **A rule is a note made testable**, one entry in `data/english/line-rules.json`: `id`; `says` (one plain sentence of what it forbids or requires); `from` (his exact words, at most 240 characters, with the date; a rule with only a research source has `research` instead and says so); `status`; `test`; optional `applies` (`banks`, `kinds`, `moves`, so a rule about speech does not touch a headline); `catches` (lines he rejected that the rule must flag); `spares` (lines he liked that it must not). The two lists are the rule's own test, and `tests/guards/english/line-rules.test.ts` runs them.
- **A rule about where a line is said reads the use's tags** (part 2 of the words machine, Nov 10). `applies.uses` takes `has` and `lacks`, each a map from a tag to a regular expression: the use's subject, role, purpose, step, `requiresFacts` and `excludesFacts`, plus the part's own `bearing`, `voice`, `addressKind`, `topic` and `device`, which `usesWithTags` copies onto every use. `applies` may be a list (any entry applies). A spot rule's `catches` and `spares` hold the line with its spot (`{ text, bank, use }`) so the case is tested where it is said. A rule that needs a fact no tag holds stays `awaiting-test` with the missing tag named in its `note`; never guess the context from the words. A line that is the only one some world has for its spot (a fallback said when a fact is missing) gets `rewrite`, not `enforced`: the spot count ignores facts, so pulling it could silence that world.
- **Who says a line, to whom and where, is on every use** (part 2 of the words machine, Nov 14). `usesWithTags` also adds `spotTags` (`src/english/spot-tags.ts`): `situation`, `speaker`, `speakerFill`, `listener`, `listenerFill`, `setting`, `stakes`, read from `src/director/data/situation-types.json` (and a variant's family through `arc-variants.json`) the way the director casts: the use's `role` asker or asked names the type's role, and where a type says `playerAsks` the asker's lines are the player's (`you`). `tie` uses the conversation engine's fact names (`stranger`, `acquainted`, `kin`, from the use's `requiresFacts`) plus `household` or `kin` where the role's fill fixes it. A purpose several types play gives lists. None of these is a spot field. A `judged` rule with `judges: "piece-in-context"` is judged in each spot a line is said (`spotSubjects`; id `verdictId([key, "spot:" + useSpotId(use)], [text, ""])`): a "no" in some spots goes to `pulledUses` in `pulled.json` and `admittedParts` (`bank-parts.ts`) leaves those uses out for every bank; a "no" in every spot pulls the line; a spot left with no other playable line waits instead. Sheets for such a rule have one row per spot with that use's tags.
- **Narrow a judged rule with `applies` before writing its first sheet** (part 7, Nov 14). A `judged` rule with no `applies` asks a reviewer about the whole bank (about 13,000 lines); give it the banks, kinds, moves or use tags it is about (`stake-is-named`: plea and bargain moves, 196 lines; `journal-names-no-feeling`: the journal, 340). A rule that judges a whole item, a generator's output or a writer's method (`look-up-how-real-people-say-it`, `business-names-sound-real`) has no bank line to judge: keep it without `applies` and say so in its `note`. Count a rule's subjects with `lineSubjects` before and after.
- **Three kinds of test.** The line's own words (`phrases`, `patterns`, `sentences`): a program reads the line. The line's place (`place`): whether a reply follows what came before, whether a person was introduced first; it needs the lines around it. The first, `reply-follows-the-line-before`, is built (next bullets). Meaning and tone (`judged`): a reviewer judges each line once and the verdict is saved with the line's exact text; the run reads it (next bullets). A `place` rule without its reader is `awaiting-test` and flags nothing, and so is a `judged` rule until its first verdicts; both keep his words and their catches and spares so the test has something to be built against.
- **One store for every rule a reviewer judges** (`src/english/judged.ts`, `scripts/words/verdicts.ts`). `data/english/judged/<store>.json` holds only the "no" verdicts, each with its keys and a one-sentence reason, the rubric word for word (the rule's `says`; the reply rule has its own, `REPLY_FOLLOWS_RUBRIC`), the count judged and a hash of the yes ids, which are in `<store>.yes.txt`. The store is named by the rule's `test.reads` (the reply rule's `reply-follows`) or else by its id. A verdict's id is a hash of the subject's keys and their exact words (`verdictId`): a line, a pair [line before, reply] or a set (part 3 builds set and exchange keys), so a changed line has no verdict until judged again. Only the fold writes the store. Nothing is judged while the game is played, and the run calls no model.
- **How a reviewer judges a sheet.** (1) `npm run words:pairs -- --rule <id> --out <dir outside the repository>`: for a rule about one line, every line it applies to with no verdict in its current words, with its bank, key, kind, move, the rubric and his words behind the rule (a rule about a line in its spot adds the tags of each use); for the reply rule, the pairs, one sheet per purpose (`--only-unjudged`, `--observed`, `--per-file`, `--sample`, `--seed`). (2) A reviewer, a different session from the builder, answers the rubric for each id and writes `{ rule, verdicts: [{ id, passes, why }] }` beside the sheet; a "no" needs its one-sentence `why`. Never judge a line you wrote. (3) `npm run words:verdicts -- --from <dir>` folds every file: it refuses an id the rule does not judge now (an old sheet or a changed line: write the sheet again), drops verdicts whose lines the bank no longer holds in those words, and changes nothing folded twice; with no `--from` it only drops what no longer applies. (4) `npm run words:rules`, twice: the second run changes no file.
- **What the run does with verdicts.** A `judged` rule flags every line judged "no" in its current words, and from there it is any other rule: `sample` lists the line for him, `enforced` pulls it, a line he graded good is never pulled and is listed under Conflicts, and a spot is never left with no line. A line nobody has judged passes and plays (LAW section 7: ungraded is not rejected). The report shows, per such rule, lines judged, judged bad and waiting for a verdict. A `judged` rule leaves `awaiting-test` for `sample` when its row has one line it must catch, one it must spare and its first verdicts folded (the English guard checks), and goes to `enforced` by the same step as any rule. **The backlog only falls:** `pulled.json`'s `awaitingVerdict` keeps, per `judged` rule past `awaiting-test`, the lines waiting for a verdict; `npm run words:rules` never raises it (it exits 1 naming the rule), and the English guard fails by the rule's name while the fresh count is higher. So a new or changed line comes with its verdicts for every such rule it falls under.
- **A place rule is judged pair by pair, and the picker reads the verdicts** (step 3; `src/english/reply-pairs.ts`, `reply-follows.ts`). Every pair (line before, reply) the director can produce is judged once against its rubric through the store above; a reply that does not follow some line is not pulled, it is never offered after that line (owner, Nov 9: fix the setup, keep the line). Judging a pair "no" lets the next closest reply into the pair list, so ask for `--only-unjudged` again until it prints no pairs. A line after which no reply follows is listed in `pulled.json` under `noReplyFollows` with the purpose and move: write a new reply for it (the rewrite queue). **A new reply line is judged before its pull request**: run the loop for the pairs it joins, and fold. Unjudged pairs play as before and are counted, never a failure (the reply rule is not held to `awaitingVerdict`: a "no" brings new pairs in, so its count can rise from judging).
- **Statuses.** `enforced` rules pull lines. `rewrite` rules never pull: the lines they flag keep playing and go on the rewrite queue (`waiting`), and a rewrite that passes the rules replaces them (CTO, Nov 10: the four journal work lines, whose first rewrites broke the journal's ten-word limit). `sample` rules never pull; use one where his own notes contradict each other ("I'm not going to talk about that" fine on Oct 15 and unnatural on Oct 16; "Alright" against "All right"), and the lines it flags go in his next small sample. `failsBuild` marks a rule that failed the build before the machine existed (the pattern bans); a bank line that breaks one fails the guard even when pulled.
- **Keeping line-rules green when you add or reword lines, or merge (account lead, Nov 14).** `data/english/pulled.json` counts the pairs each exchange rule still waits on (`awaitingVerdict`, keyed `<rule> (pairs)`), and `line-rules.test.ts` refuses a count higher than the committed one, so every new reply line makes pairs that come with their verdicts. Do it on your own pull request, never leave it for the train: (1) merge your base (main or the train) in, then `npm run words:rules`; if it exits 1 it names each rule that rose. (2) `npm run words:pairs -- --rule <rule> --since <ref> --out <dir outside the repository>` writes only the pairs that have a line new or reworded since `<ref>` and no verdict yet. Use `<ref>` = `git merge-base HEAD origin/<base>` taken after the merge; when the merge itself brought the new lines, use your head from before the merge. A ref older than needed only adds pairs to judge, never hides one. (3) Judge them with `npm run words:review -- --sheets <that dir> --out <batch dir outside the repository>` (account lead, Nov 14): it writes batches of about 160 with two planted entries that pass and one that fails from the fixed list in `scripts/words/review.ts` (add three for a rule that has none), with ids a reviewer cannot tell apart, the rule, the owner's words and what the rule does not judge on every batch, and the key kept apart in `<batch dir>.key.json`. Hand each `NN.md` to a fresh reviewer (Opus at medium) that reads only that file, never the builder's work or the key, and writes `NN.verdicts.json` beside it; then `npm run words:review -- --check <batch dir>` refuses by name any batch that missed a plant or skipped an entry (judge it again with a new reviewer) and writes `<batch dir>/verdicts/<rule>.json` with the plants dropped. A builder never judges its own lines. Tell the reviewer what the rule does not judge: for `reply-second-move-follows-the-first`, whether the reply fits the line before is a different rule (`reply-follows-the-line-before`); on Nov 14 a reviewer not told so failed 40 of 81 pairs on fit while its plants still came out right. (4) `npm run words:verdicts -- --from <batch dir>/verdicts`, then `npm run words:rules` twice (exit 0, the second run changes nothing) and commit `data/english/judged/` with `pulled.json`. A "no" bars that reply after that one line only once its rule is enforced; on a sample rule it is a list for the Words lead. Never lower or raise the count by editing `pulled.json` by hand, and never take the other side's `pulled.json` in a conflict without rerunning (`generated-file-merges`).
- **A scene written for one occasion must be held back from the others, by a fact the record holds** (campaign events builder, Nov 15). The rival's rally pieces played after a debate because nothing excluded them; the fix is `excludesFacts: ["debate"]` on each rally piece and `requiresFacts` on the debate pieces, never a reworded line. A line that names a time of day ("tonight") needs the daytime exclusion or it fails the banks test in a morning scene. Prove each placed line reaches a real place with `scripts/dev-tests/debate-rival-draws.ts <seed> <draws> <out.md>`, which stages the scene over drawn places and prints place, seed and lines, and delete a line no place reaches.
- **A tagged line must not thin its spot (Words lead, Nov 15).** `useFit` adds one point for each fact a use checks, and only the top score is offered, so tagging one line of a spot hides every untagged line beside it whenever the fact holds (#9433 left the reporter's call after a vote one wording at three spots). When you tag a line, give the plain lines at that spot that are still true a second use with the same facts, keeping their untagged use for when the fact is absent, so they tie. Where a plain line is not true with the fact, the spot needs a new line from the Words lead. Check with the spot's test or a probe of `fitting` with and without the fact. The class is `words-a-tagged-line-thins-its-spot-to-one-wording`.
- **Deleting a piece takes what only it reached (campaign events builder, Nov 15).** Search the bank for `"after": "<its key>"`: a piece whose only use row follows the deleted line is unreachable and goes with it (the concession call's "I need some air first." had a close that only it led to). The bank file does not round-trip through a JSON dump, so cut the object by text, not by loading and dumping it; then `words:verdicts`, `words:rules` twice, and show the scene's reply tree before and after at the exact sha.
- **Turning a note into a rule.** Look first: search `line-rules.json` for the rule (add his words to its `from` and the line to `catches` or `spares`), then the grades, then the rulings. Quote him exactly and short; never alter or paraphrase. Pick the narrowest test that is true to what he said. Three or more notes saying the same thing make a rule; one stray remark does not. A rule that needs a list of phrases takes only phrases his notes name.
- **A line faulted for its place is kept, not pulled** (owner, Nov 9: "the context is weird" means fix the setup and keep the line). When his words are about what came before, when the line played, who said it or a record it contradicts, the line goes in the place rule's `examples` (`line`, `before` when his words give it, `words`, `date`), the step 3 judging reads them, and the line stays in play: not pulled, not waiting, not sample. `rejected-by-voice` holds only lines his words fault as sentences, each with its own `from` entry in the same order.
- **A rule that flags a line he graded good is wrong.** Narrow it until it flags none (`runRules` lists each conflict, and the guard fails on one). Never drop a good line to keep a rule.
- **The run: `npm run words:rules`.** It reads the banks, the rules and the grade ledger, writes `data/english/pulled.json` (keys sorted: `pulled`, `waiting`, `sample`) and prints a report: for each rule its `says`, how many notes back it, how many lines it pulled, examples, then totals and every conflict. `--report <path>` also writes it as Markdown (do not commit a report that quotes his notes at length). A second run changes nothing. After a grading the update is one command, `npm run words:grade`, which runs `apply-grades.ts` then `words:rules` and prints the report's path and its Totals lines.
- **Pulled, waiting, sample.** Pulled: the line breaks an enforced rule and does not play. Waiting: it breaks one but every other line in one of its spots also breaks a rule, so pulling it would leave the spot with nothing; it keeps playing and heads the rewrite queue. Sample: a sample rule flagged it; it plays and goes in his next sample. A line he graded good is none of these. A spot is where a line is said: the bank plus the use's subject, role, move, choice, key, step, purpose, after and outcome, which are the fields the pickers match on.
- **The gate.** A new bank line that breaks an enforced rule fails `line-rules.test.ts` by name: "<key> breaks rule <id>: <says>". Fix the line; if the line should stay, the rule or the note behind it is what to question, with the owner's words.
- **What the desk visit taught about scene trees (account lead, Nov 15).** (1) An ask is offered only where the fact its answer reads is on record: give the ask the same `requiresFacts` its answers need, one use per fact where several answer it (`english-answerable-asks` fails "the scene ends on the question" otherwise). (2) One request once per talk: the replies that make it set a fact (`setsFacts`) and exclude that same fact, so it is never offered again in other words, and the other person's answer comes from their own lean. (3) A turn where only one reply fits offers no menu, and the talk just ends. Read every played path for an answer that is not followed by "You say:" when it should be. (4) A test fixture's facts must include every fact the producer always gives (the desk's cost or no-cost from `deskFacts`). A fixture without it lists paths no player can reach and hides ones they can. (5) Before asking for a stamp, list the new lines in your diff and find each one under "You say:" or spoken in the played file. A line no visit offers is deleted, not kept.
- **A reply that is only reachable after a return meeting needs the fact its asker's line needs (governor's briefing, Nov 15).** `words:pairs` pairs a reply with every request that could precede it by the facts alone; "Declare it. Bring me the order." (excludes `again`) still paired with a return request that did not require `again`, so one pair waited for a verdict. Give the later line every fact its meeting implies (`again` plus the return kind) and rerun `words:rules` twice; run `words:pairs --since $(git merge-base HEAD origin/<base>)` to see which pair is waiting, not only the lines you changed.
- **His notes and batch pages stay off main** (owner, Nov 9: "I don't know why like my notes and stuff need to be merged into main"). Only the rules (with their short quotes) and the grade rows go to main. A batch goes to him as an artifact page; his voice note is read at run time from where the CTO keeps it. A test uses a fixture in our own words.
- **A helper's claim that a guard passes is checked by running the guard** (CTO, Nov 10). Run `tests/guards/english` yourself before you say a bank change is clean; a helper's "it passes" turned main red twice.
- **Stacked bank branches are merged without re-running an old grade script** (CTO, Nov 10). A grade script re-run on an older branch counts a batch again. Merge by piece key (below), keep main's `part-grades.json`, then run `npm run words:grade` once, on the merged head.

## How a bank is made (authoring to patterns)

Full history of the existing mined banks: `docs/research/english-corpus/README.md`. The method now:

- **Authoring (the Story session's job since Nov 10).** The `civic-prose` skill is the process. A piece is drafted to a move, register, key and slots; reviewed for grounding (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the record fact it needs and is said only when the record holds it) and terminology; then added to the bank with its move tags. Mark new parts `origin: authored` with the author and date. Every part plays unless the owner's grades hold it or a rule pulled it: the ledger and `pulled.json` are the only gates, and a part carries no flag of its own. After adding or regenerating a bank, run `npm run words:rules`; if it lists your piece as pulled or waiting, the failure message names the rule and the owner's words behind it, so fix the line rather than the rule. Run `apply-grades.ts` only when a grade file changed.
- **Patterns.** Counts, shapes and wording come from any source: English books, other games, transcripts, council minutes, news, the internet (`scripts/english-corpus/shapes/<bank>.json`), copyrighted or not (owner, Oct 24: "There's plenty of stuff, even copyrighted, out there. You don't have to cite stuff."). Where wording came from never decides whether it plays, and no line names its source. No real politician's slogan.
- **Mining.** Where any record or text offers good pieces, mine them with `scripts/english-corpus/mine-shapes.py`. The `source` fields on a part are optional notes.
- **Tests.** `<bank>-bank.test.ts` composes a whole exchange through the real engine in a generated place (every record id resolving, no feeling words, both chairs where a chair exists). Add a bank by adding a shape or an authored set, a script and its test.
- **What "calibration" means.** Research sets how pieces are arranged and how often: the length of a public comment, how a concession opens and closes, which moves follow which. It never picks which line a particular person says: traits, relationship, the record and the owner's grades do.

## Grading: scenes with voice notes (owner, Oct 24; the batch tooling deleted Oct 28)

The owner does not grade batches. He plays or watches a scene or a scenario and reacts with a voice note; the session turns each note into grades on the parts the scene used and into fixes at the root. What he liked ships; what he rejected never plays again. The batch apparatus is deleted (owner, Oct 28, 18:15 UTC, `docs/coordination/OWNER-DECISIONS.md`; `docs/reports/words-director-plan-2026-10-28.md`, decision 4): no batch generators, no page builders, no table read, no files under `data/english/batches/`. Do not rebuild one.

- **One grade file per set of voice notes**, `data/english/grades/<set>.json` (`voice-notes-batches-11-19.json`, `voice-notes-oct15-28.json`): `{"batch": "<set>", "grades": [{"i", "grade", "by", "line", "note", "parts"}]}`. Every row carries the part keys of the line it grades (`parts`), so the file stands on its own. A voice note names a line by its label or number; the session writes one row per line.
- **`node --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/apply-grades.ts`** folds every grade file into `data/english/part-grades.json`, counting each grade once (by batch and row), so a part graded bad or fix stops playing everywhere. It no longer writes a map or a reach table. Run it after a grade file changes through `npm run words:grade`, which follows it with `npm run words:rules`: that is the whole update. `data/english/calibration.json` keeps only the trait, length and move-pair weights the bench grades set; no weight in it is a grade of a line.
- **The in-play grade control** (`src/bench/grade-line.ts`) writes a grade on a line the owner is looking at to `data/english/grades/play-grades.json`, naming the pieces that said it; `apply-grades.ts` folds it like any grade file.
- **A rewrite never deletes a line the owner graded good (owner, via the CTO, Nov 8).** Every key `data/english/part-grades.json` counts good must stay a piece in a bank, or be carried by a live piece through a grade row's `restoredFrom` (`data/english/grades/restored-liked-lines.json`, written by `scripts/dialogue-batch/restore-liked-lines.ts` from `restored-liked-lines.json`); `scripts/dialogue-batch/liked-lines-kept.test.ts` fails on any other graded-good key, and has no list of exceptions. Put a liked line where a scene reads it (a piece of that scene's purpose, with the grade moved onto it); a line no scene reads yet goes in a bank of its own kind (`address`, `hearing`, `legislation`, `minutes`, `notice`, each with a register class in `calibration.json`), under a stopgap `SG-WORDS-<bank>-no-reader` in `src/director/data/stopgaps.json` and a pool item for the scene that will read it. Before a rewrite, trim or bank deletion, run that test; when the new bank says the same job in other words, keep the old liked piece beside it in the same PR. The old pieces are in git: `git show <deleting commit>^:data/english/parts/<bank>.json`.
- **A scene shown to the owner answers why by record** (`ask-why`, `player-screen-text`): no raw id in a line, no body named by a member's title, no line repeated, no relative asked after with no recorded cause, no scene staged in the wrong household. Fix the engine defect at the root and replay in a fresh drawn place.
- **Proof scenes** are welcome: a situation the director does not yet fire may be composed from real records in a played world and shown, marked as a proof, so the owner reacts to what the game can say and about what. `scripts/dialogue-batch/scene-year.ts --seed <seed>` plays a drawn place's year and lists every scene with the record that fired it; the coverage table and the tooling that made it are gone; count empty cells from `scene-year.ts` and the type list instead.

## News stories: headline, lede, body and briefs (owner, Oct 29: "headlines and then a little bit of story. Nothing is actually on the paper")

- **One composer, one call.** A printed story's facts come from the source record by a builder in `src/bench/printed-words.ts` (`BUILDERS`, read through `Reader`). `composeNewsStory` (`src/english/english-newscast.ts`) words the headline, the lede, then one sentence per `context` role in `STORY_KINDS` whose facts are present. `wordPrintedStory` returns `{headline, lede, paragraphs, outlet}`, and the lane's paper prints exactly that (CREW.md seam): never a second composer on the page side.
- **Measure before writing:** `node --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/printed-kinds.ts --seed <seed>` plays a drawn place's year and lists every printed kind with its count, how many print a body, the unworded records and one example with its facts. A failing printed-words test's `GOLDEN_PATH_SEED` reproduces there.
- **A body sentence says something the record holds and the lede did not:** the bill's purpose, the sponsor, the chamber, what follows a veto or a committee vote, the national rate, the rate before. Add the fact to the builder from an engine record; never invent one. A sentence whose facts the lede already named is skipped by the composer.
- **Pieces live in two banks:** `data/english/parts/news.json` (14 words at most) and `headlines.json`, gated by `requiresFacts`. Words like passed, failed, vetoed and won add their own requirement (`variants()` in english-newscast.ts).
- **The paper's style:**
  - headlines in sentence case;
  - sums of a million or more rounded ("$228.7 million"), and a trillion in trillions ("$7.1 trillion", never "$7204.5 billion");
  - a law's measure named by the English engine's subject for its series (`storyTopicName`), never the key read as words ("snap maximum").
- **Measure what one reader meets.** `node --max-old-space-size=8000 --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/paper-year.ts --seed <seed> --draws 3 --played` plays the player's year and counts the sentences one paper prints more than three times in its year, the item's measure, and the same across all the town's outlets. A national story prints in every outlet, so the town-wide count says little: within one paper, Blunt, South Dakota went from 54 to 0 (#7022).
- **Print what the body did, not that it sat** (the CTO, Nov 1, under the owner's "real ones with some good fluff"). A sitting with business is told by its votes; one with nothing before it is a dated line in the week's meetings brief. A weekly line needs a date, or the same sentence prints every week.
- **The news picker takes any usable line, not the most specific.** `composeNewsStory` draws evenly among the lines whose facts are present, so a generic line plays beside a specific one. To keep a generic line out, set a different flag in its place: a repeated exposure sets `again`, not `conflict`.
- **Make a kind's pieces exclusive by flags.** Until the newscast reads `excludesFacts` (#7098), a piece that needs fewer facts still plays beside a specific one. So the builder sets exactly one flag from each set, and every piece of the kind requires one from each: the law pieces need `took` or `enacted`; `here`, `nationwide` or `federal-here`; and `up`, `down`, `same` or `new` (#7127). An old piece of the kind without the new flags stays eligible, so give it the flags or remove it.
- **A law is news only when it changed what stood, told at its own level** (owner's Nov 1 playtest, #7127). The archive keeps a law whose row differs from the row it replaced at its level and place (`rowReplaced`, laws). Its story tells that level's change, names the government that set it, and says whom it reaches from `amounts` in `data/english/bill-names.json`. The paper carries a law or a budget only while the news engine still calls it news (`stillNews`).
- **A word in a piece can add a hidden requirement.** `variants()` in english-newscast.ts makes "night", "tonight" or "evening" need an `evening` fact, "morning" a `morning` fact, and "won", "lost", "passed" and "failed" their outcome. A concession line that said "on election night" never played (Lane 3, Nov 3). Read that list before wording a news piece.
- **A body sentence needs a fact of its own in its words.** The composer drops a context sentence whose fact texts all appeared earlier. A sentence that names only the opponent after "{name} beat {opponent}." is taken for a repeat, so give its flag a text the sentence says ("concede"), not "yes" (#7151's follow-up, the concession in the race story).
- **A fact no piece reads is no fact.** Before counting on a builder's fact for a story's body, find a piece with its slot. The parent's death gave `relation` and `survivor`, no piece read either, and archive-words failed in three of five worlds (#7131).
- **Never print the outside account as an employer.** "Buyers and sellers outside the town" (an organization with `outsideFlow`) is the market's stand-in for work and trade out of town: say the person worked outside the town.
- **A government's treasury is not an employer either.** A public worker's work record names the organization `organization:treasury:<government>` (kind `government-treasury`, named "Arizona treasury"). Say the government they worked for, "the government of Arizona" or "the federal government", read from the budgets engine's governments row; a printed obituary said "had worked for Arizona treasury" (#7022's follow-up).
- **Briefs** (owner, Oct 29): the paper's side column carries short local stories for realism (a car stolen, a business opening, a student's win). A brief is a story kind marked `brief: true`, with a headline and one line only, not clickable. It comes from records the world keeps, chosen by the one press desk (`src/core2/press-desk.ts`, the Words session's per the CTO, Oct 29): by the outlet's space, the record's reach and its recency, never a roll.

## The owner's standing rulings on this line

- **Grades and rules change what plays, and no one is asked to approve lines one by one** (owner, Oct 24: "Quit worrying about my grades ... Get the English engine in the game. That should make every single piece of dialogue available. If I find pieces I don't like or I keep grading, you can keep updating it, and that will keep tuning the engine."). Every piece in every bank plays in ordinary play, scenes included, unless a grade holds it or a rule pulled it; a grade run and a rules run reach the whole game at once. Nothing waits for the owner to grade it, and nothing asks him to approve lines one by one.
- Scenes are the narrative: consequential dialogue happens in scenes; the day screen carries dealing. Day advances time; a scene stops it. A quiet day fires nothing.
- A talk starts from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person; one topic with side remarks, chaining to one related topic; news only as received; no cold "How is X?" about someone absent unless something recorded happened to X; the other person closes it; the listener remembers the last talk; the old subject loop (every known person in id order) is gone.
- Up to four replies, the scene sets the count, ranked by the player's traits; a short set (one, two or three) is allowed and is not a gap in the banks (owner, Nov 10, 2026: "it's also allowed to have less than 4 options sometimes... you might only have 1 option, but it's to make the player feel engaged. Or you may only have 2 options in the scene because you have to make a choice, a yes or no choice... So just up to 4.") It replaces "Four dynamic choices, always" (Oct 14). Every reply offered is a different answer, never a rewording of another, and each moves the talk somewhere; lies are the same moves made false about the player's own record; "say nothing" is the smaller control beside them. The player's choices are the player's: no auto-hire, no auto-vote.
- The narrator is settled: second person in scenes, first person in the memoir, grounded in records; the journal frames a childhood loss by age; school and work are chapters, not entries.
- A family scene is staged in the player's household from that household's records (a lost job, an eviction, a divorce); the people in the room show in the picture and the talk, never as a roll call of names.
- Knowing is a read: a person cites a law, a vote or an act only through a record of how they learned of it (news received, a talk, a notice), and the stance cites that source.
- No feeling words unless a record carries the feeling; grief has a cause record.
- **Scene PRs collide on four shared files; land on fresh main.** Every scene edits `data/english/parts/scene-talk.json`, `src/director/data/situation-types.json`, `src/director/purpose-scene.ts` and the situation-type count in `src/director/director.test.ts`, and mergers never hand-merge a bank. Right before marking ready, `git fetch origin main`, merge it, re-add your pieces by piece key and your count bump on main's files, and push within minutes; a PR that is stacked or held waits for its base to merge, then redoes this. Oct 27: #6201, #6296, #6298 (twice), #6303 and #6309 were each sent back for this one conflict.

- **Every line carries an insert (owner, Nov 11, 2026: "all the lines in the game need to be run through the English engine... a ton of different speech versions, a ton of different inserts").** No whole stock sentence is picked as it stands: a line names at least one slot in braces that the engine fills from the world's records, and a scene is composed from many versions. The opening comes first: the President's address and the evening after it.
- **What may stay fixed is his ruling, and only that (owner, Nov 11, call 12: "Replies of one to three words and action names stay; everything else is rewritten.").** Three kinds are excused, by rule: a line of one to three words ("Hello.", "You won."); the name of an action the player picks (the `choice-names` bank); and a piece the engine joins, when the sentence it makes carries an insert. Nobody widens that without a new ruling.
- **How a joined piece says so.** Give the part a `piece` field: `of` (the sentence it belongs to, one name for every piece of that sentence), `part` (which part it is: `opener`, `core`, `reason`, `closer` or the address's own move), and `required: true` when every such sentence has that part. The pieces of a sentence are excused when one of its required parts names a slot in every wording, because then no joined sentence can lack one. So put the insert in the part that is always there (the core), and the openers and closers around it may be plain.
- **The guard.** `tests/guards/english/line-inserts.test.ts` counts, in each bank, the lines with no slot that are not excused, against `tests/guards/english/line-inserts-baseline.json` (7,613 on Nov 11, after 605 excused). A count may only go down: a new line of four words or more with no slot fails by bank, and when you rewrite or delete such lines you lower the bank's count in the same change.

## The checks a Words session runs

Builders build; checkers check (owner, Oct 29; skill `checker`). A Words session and its helpers run only the tests they wrote or changed: the bank's own test, `tests/english-parts-banks.test.ts`, `tests/guards/english`, and the director's tests for changed files, each on a fresh draw and on any seed a report named. Never typecheck, the guards or the gate: open the PR ready, with a declaration in `docs/release/changes/`, and message the checker whose number is the PR number mod 5 (`docs/coordination/CREW.md`); from then the checker is the branch's one writer, so ask it before pushing there again.

- **Four checks before a PR leaves you (the CTO, Oct 30 and Nov 1).** `tests/guards/english/line-rules.test.ts` (and the rest of `tests/guards/english`) for any bank change, `src/bench/printed-words.test.ts` for a new public event or a press change, `npm run zero-dice` for any director change, and `tests/guards/families.test.ts` for any new import: a director file reaches another family only through its entry files (`life.ts` for the player's clock), and #6982's two direct imports turned main red (#7004). Name them in the PR's first lines.
- **A module table's rows are read-only: copy before sorting.** `table.query(...)` and the reader's `query` return the table's own frozen rows, so `.sort()` on them throws "Cannot assign to read only property". The tests passed and the paper-year measure crashed on an official's obituary. Write `[...rows].sort(...)`.
- **Never split a record id on colons.** Ids nest: an outlet owner is `person:press:outlet:newspaper:2:<jurisdiction>:owner:0`, so a letter's id `letter:<personId>:<date>` split on ":" gave "person" (the letters test, Northern Mariana Islands, #7050). Match the id's start with the known person id instead.
- **The press wire forgets.** `forgetOldWire` drops wire items a week past `storyFreshDays`, so a check over a longer span (a writer's last letter, a month back) reads the printed stories table (`byReporter`, `byOutlet`), never the wire.
- **A state's key is not a place.** `corePlaceByKey("US-NM")` and `corePlaceByJurisdictionId` of a state's id both return null; `coreStateJurisdiction(key)` resolves a state. A move whose destination is where it started names no place: say "moved away" (the family news talk, Nov 1).
- **Moving a function can move an import the families guard counts.** `tests/guards/families-baseline.json` counts each file's old imports past another family's entry. Moving `placeLabel` from `journal.ts` to `words.ts` carried its `core2/place-lookup` import, and the guard failed both "reaches no further" and "only shrinks". Export the helper from the file it lives in instead.
- **A scene whose purpose can never be worded still plays, empty.** `purposeScene` skips it ("no narration for filing-clerk can be worded from the records given"), and the bench then shows the scene with its plain narration: after the counter, a player who lived alone got a second, empty visit to the clerk (Monroe, Pennsylvania, #6990). When you add or change a type, compose it from a played record and check it is not skipped. A scene that follows a screen gets a purpose of its own, never the screen's lines.
- **A relative's answer takes its stage from their latest happening** (`familyNews` in `src/director/data/conversation-rules.json`). Every "How's your mother?" used to be the default stage, "She's doing well.", even after she died, so the illness lines never played. A line with no stages fits every stage, and the "well" answers load at the default stage only. Each news line cites the record it tells.
- **A fact that only keeps a line true goes in `onlyWhen`, not `requiresFacts`** (Words lead, Nov 14). `useFit` in `src/english/scene-talk-words.ts` adds `requiresFacts.length` to a line's rank and keeps only the top rank, so a truth guard there crowds out every sibling while the fact is set (a reporter's spot played one line). `onlyWhen` filters exactly the same way and adds nothing to the rank, so the guarded line ties with its siblings and the speaker's bearing picks. Rule of thumb: `requiresFacts` when the fact is what the line is about (it fills a slot or the line talks about it); `onlyWhen` when the fact only keeps the line from being false ("Why {bill}, and why now?" needs `bill`, and `introduced` only so it is not said about a veto). The spot tags and the name-padding check count a non-empty `onlyWhen` as placed, the same as `requiresFacts` (`src/english/only-when.test.ts`).
- **Hold a line back with `excludesFacts`, not `requiresFacts`.** The scene picker prefers the line that requires more facts (`fitting` in `src/english/scene-talk-words.ts` scores `requiresFacts.length`), so a required mark makes its lines the only ones that play while it is set. To keep "Thanks for your time tonight." out of the daytime, exclude `daytime`. The bank test lists every "tonight" line as held back or looking ahead (#6982).
- **A second ask of the same person reads the record, for every ask type at once** (Account lead, Nov 15, #9369). Each player ask sets `asked-before` from its own record: a sign-on from `declinedAsks` (`purpose-scene.ts`), a vote from `voteAskedBefore` (`legislature.ts`, read in `bargain-facts.ts`), a serve ask from the row's `askedBefore`, with `nothing-moved` (the no rests on the same reason) and `asked-more-than-once`. Every opening, request, refusal, stall and plea that reads as a first ask excludes `asked-before`, and the second-ask lines require it, so the most specific line wins with no tuned weight. A spot with no second-ask line is not offered (`backed` in `scene-talk.ts`), and a second no offers only the plain close (`secondNo`). A plea that claims a past act ("I backed you when it counted") is offered only where the record holds it: `read-favor-given`, the player's yes on a bill of theirs, from `favorsGiven` (`arc-variant.ts`); where it holds nothing, the not-yet gets a reply that keeps the ask open (move `defer`, `secondAsk` in `purpose-arcs.json`) beside the plain close. Prove it with a played file at three named places that shows the first ask beside the second and a third.
- **Every new line is seen played at the exact head, or it comes out** (Words lead, Nov 15). Variants of one spot are chosen by voice weights (`renderGroundedEnglish`), so a short twin can lose to every speaker a test draws. Take a line no played file shows out of the bank, list it to the Words lead with what would stage it, and if no speaker ever picks it, tell the Words lead and M4 that the pick may be the fault. A fold with `words:verdicts` also drops stale verdicts from stores you did not hand it; keep those drops after checking the dropped lines are gone from the bank.
- **Run the English guards on every bank change, lifted pieces included.** `tests/guards/english/` is quick and isn't in the changed-test list for a bank edit. `place-and-presence` refuses a sentence that only says where the player is or who is there (owner, Oct 27). A family-loss piece lifted from a reference build ended "{name}, who worked the campaign beside you, is waiting." and turned main red after #7151 merged (fixed in #7184).
- **A tie line has to beat the fact lines it competes with.** Tie-keyed pieces (`tie-friend`, `tie-rival`) require one fact, like the lead pieces, so a count with a lead played the lead line for friend and rival alike. Write the tie with the fact (`requiresFacts: ["lead", "tie-friend"]`), and test the two sides on one scene: warm, then cool, the tie through `api.relationship` and require different lines (`concession-received.test.ts`; Evart, Michigan, Nov 4).
- **Merge a bank file by piece, not by line.** When main is merged into a bank branch, `data/english/parts/*.json` conflicts in dozens of hunks, though the two sides rarely touch the same piece. Load the merge base, the branch and main, key each `parts` entry by `key`, start from main's list, and apply the branch's own changes. Its deletions go only where main left the piece alone, its changed pieces go in place, and its added pieces go after their neighbor. Union any top-level dictionary (`slots`). Write with `indent=2`, run prettier, and stop for a person only where both sides changed the same piece. Then check `git diff --name-only origin/main HEAD` lists only the PR's files. Lane 3 settled six bank merges this way on Nov 4.
- **A cause's `bind` overrides the role's own fill.** In `situation-types.json`, `"bind": { "member": "subject" }` casts the subject whatever the role's `fill` says, so a check written as a fill (`subject-on-body`) must be named in `bind` too. Salinas, Puerto Rico cast a resident who sat on no body until it was (#7218).
- **A listener's moment is on the feeling channel.** Whoever hears a speech as a witness gets `feeling:event:speech.heard`; the speaker's own is `cause:`. Match the label of the person the scene is for, and read the schedule's coverage reason (`no-type`) when a type never binds.
- **Run the director's numbers guard on director code.** `director.test.ts` "keeps numbers in data" refuses a bare `0` or `1` anywhere in `src/director`; use `P.zero` and `P.one` or the file's `zero` and `one`. A spacing loop in `ledger.ts` turned main red after #7199 (fixed in #7209). A comparator over `[id, votes]` pairs can't use `b[P.one] - a[P.one]` (indexing by a number loses the tuple's types and fails the typecheck): destructure the pairs, `([aId, aVotes], [bId, bVotes]) => bVotes - aVotes || aId.localeCompare(bId)` (#7258's runner-up sort turned main red; fixed in #7263).
- **A follow that ends a scene never asks.** A thank or an accept ends the talk (`terminalFollow`), so a question there hangs: "Then we start tonight. Where do you keep your contacts?" turned main red (#6980). The bank test refuses it now.
- **A later round needs two fresh replies.** Past the first round, the asker closes the talk when fewer than two replies of the arc's `again` moves can be worded. A move whose lines require a fact needs a second line with the same facts, or it drops out once its first line is said (the petition scene's leave lines, #6990).
- **A piece's key follows its words.** Rewording a line changes its key: update every `after` that names the old key, and run `npm run words:rules`.
- **One record, one line.** A journal row that tells a record another row already tells is a twin: the roots line ("My family came from Indiana.") repeated the birth line from the same record and is deleted (#6985). A wording rule lives in one helper the press and the journal share ("the District of Columbia", `stateInSentence`).
- **The Journal bank keeps only a line's words.** `journalDataFromBank` in `src/director/journal.ts` reads each journal piece's key and text and ignores its `requiresFacts`, so a line requiring "graduated" played for a senior without a diploma too. To choose lines by a fact, give the row a `stageFact` and `stageWords` (patterns on the line's text) in `src/director/data/journal-rows.json`. `stageOf` reads the fact from the event log, then from the moment's own facts (the school year's end, #7045).
- **A circumstance moment is the player's own only when `own` says so.** The Journal tells a moment as the player's line or someone else's by its cast, and a school year's end cast with the player's mother was hers, so no line was found. A record kind that is always the player's goes in `own`. A moment on two days (the last school day and the day after) is told once, with `oncePer` on a fact whose text is the same on both.
- **A test draws until its precondition holds, and names what it passed over.** A drawn place may have no qualifying case: a housemate's filing reaching the player, or a staged scene in a quiet town's first weeks (#6974, #6989). A death now stages a funeral at a venue, not a scene at home.

## Lessons of Nov 11 (the lead's review of the opening talk, #8736)

The first composed scene came back right in its build and wrong on the screen in two ways. Check both before you open a words pull request.

The insert rule asks for an insert in each line, not the same insert in every line. On one screen, no two replies carry the same insert: one names the point, one the town, one the other person, one a third party. The speaker's own line names the point once in full, then says "it".

## Lessons of Nov 11, second batch (the address and the filing counter)

- **Say a thing the way people say it.** "Another one for Council member in Mutual" and "U.S. Representative (District 2)" are a form's words. An office, a job, a bill or a place is said through one reader that gives its spoken form from its own record ("a seat on the Mutual Village Council", "mayor", "the District 2 seat in Congress"). Never a title-cased label in a sentence, never a parenthesis in speech.
- **An insert fits its sentence.** "They'll beat pay faster than any program could" was written for inflation and given pay. A wording that treats its subject as a problem (beat, fight, bring down) or as good news (the gains on, build on) says which it needs, and is offered only when the record says so. The same for grammar: Congress sends "a bill on {about}", not `{about}`.
- **A subject's word at most three times in one speech or scene.** The rule that every sentence carries an insert, with only a handful of facts to insert, gave an address that said "take-home pay" seven times. Past three, a move takes a wording that carries a different insert.
- **A lead-in does work or it goes.** Lead-ins that only announce the next clause ("Here's the thing:", "Let me be plain:") made every sentence of an address start the same way. Keep the ones that say who is spoken to, when or where.

## The composed checks (unit 5 of the composer, Nov 12)

A composed rule reads whole texts a player met, never one bank line: its row's test is `{ "kind": "composed", "check": [names], "applies": [kinds] }`, status `sample` (it reports, it pulls nothing), and `ruleFlags` returns false for it.

- **Run them:** `npm run words:composed -- --seed <seed> [--place-key <key>] [--draws N] [--days 14] [--report <file outside the repository>]`. Each draw plays a new life through the bench's own routes (`benchRequest`: the opening address and the evening after it, everyone at home who has something to say each day, Day pressed and every stop and staged scene played) and keeps every text as a `ComposedText` (`src/english/composed-text.ts`): speeches by level, talk at home and in town, scenes, narration, the Journal's first page and notes, news toasts and stories. A governor's or mayor's address the window misses is composed through `recordLocalAddress` at the end and marked `proof`. It prints per rule the texts read, breaks and three examples with place, seed and kind. A 14-day life takes under a minute; three draws in one process stay under 2 GB.
- **The count only falls.** `tests/guards/english/composed-baseline.json` keeps breaks per 100 texts per rule on recorded draws. `npm run words:composed -- --check` replays those draws and exits 1 naming any rule whose rate rose; the run is deterministic on a seed, so a rise is your change. After a change that lowers a count, write it again on the same draws with `--write-baseline` and the same `--seed` flags.
- **A words pull request quotes its before and after counts** from `--check` (or the table) for every composed rule its change touches, with the draws.
- **Add a check:** a pure function `(text, sample) => breaks` in `src/english/composed-checks.ts`, registered in `COMPOSED_CHECKS`; its numbers and word lists go in `data/english/composed-checks.json` (each number TUNABLE with a spread); name it in the rule's `test.check`; give it a catch and a spare in our own words in `tests/guards/english/composed-rules.test.ts`, which fails until every registered check is used by a rule and has fixtures. A check that cannot be sound without the telling or typed pieces is not guessed: leave that part named in the rule's `note`.
- **The talk's screen is one step of the talk box:** the other person's lines from one request plus the replies offered. A full name in the line and again in a reply counts twice on that screen, as the owner reads it.
- **A line is never changed only to pass a guard**, the unique-text check included. A duplicate text means delete your copy and use the existing piece. A builder never judges pairs on its own lines: the sheet goes to the planted review (`npm run words:review` once #9221 folds; `trusted-review-pass`).

## Outlets, talks and staging a line (builder M3, Nov 15)

- **An outlet is said as its record has it.** Read the outlet's kind with `outletMedium` (src/core2/modules/press.ts: paper, television, radio, null for online) and its name and article from `outletTelling`; `outletFacts` in src/director/conversation.ts sets `outlet`, `outlet-paper`, `outlet-television`, `outlet-radio`, `outlet-online` and `outlet-the` for every line. Never title-case a name by its shape ("WEST" is a word, "KKFX" is call letters): say it as the record has it. A line that names an outlet gates itself on those facts (paper is read in, television is seen on, radio is heard on).
- **`answers` are the player's lines.** In a conversation the part named `answers` is what the player can say back to the other person's last line (`offerChoices` in src/director/conversation.ts); `ask` is the other person raising a subject; `raise` is whoever opens one. A line with `role: answer` is never the neighbor's. Read which side speaks it before you stage it.
- **A line is reached only if a talk offers it.** Before you write or gate a line, open a talk the game itself opens (stage the cause through the engine's own route, such as `printStory(api, story, readers, atOnce)` with `kind: "press.printed"`, `topic` and `outletId`, then `openConversation` and `say` in a test pinned to a named place and seed) and read the "You say:" choices. A line no talk offers does not fold. Probe the wording engine directly with `wordConversationMove` and the subject's facts: a null line means the facts cannot fill the slots (a printed story carries `about`, a closed-place story carries `topic`).
- **A test never scans.** Pin the seeds and the place in the test file; name which seed gives which line. A loop over many seeds or people is a scan, not play.
- **Deleting a bank piece:** remove it from the part file, then run `npm run words:rules` and `npm run words:verdicts` (about five minutes; run it in the background) so the judged rows and yes lists lose its key; changing a piece's text and keeping its key is fine. Check the arc's replies and the situation type's `moves` too: a reply move with no piece left is a dead branch the player sees as a choice that ends the scene.
- **A frame that puts the question after "is" or "than" excludes `about-named`.** `questionOnce` says a question already named in the talk as "it", so "What matters to me is {about}." read "What matters to me is it." Give such a piece `excludesFacts: ["about-named"]`; do not reword it, and check every frame of that shape in the same move.
- **A row that says how it came out places no line that waits.** A story's row sets `story-event` and `story-decided` (`storyEvent` in conversation.ts, read from the row's own facts: a vote's yes and no, a floor failure, a law's id). Lines that look forward ("We'll see who's right", "once {decider} votes") exclude `story-decided`, and a fallback filler line is not written where no take fits.
- **A staged talk is staged on the opening day.** From the Train on, a played world holds natural printed stories within days, and the neighbor raises one of them; a test that stages its own story advances one day, not weeks, so the story it staged is the one raised. Name the place by its record id in the test and assert it.
- **Each new line is printed in the played file before the reader sees it.** Check three things for every new line: it is printed, the line after it follows from it, and it agrees with the story's row. A line that cannot be shown played comes out of the bank in the same PR.

A kind with no move named `plain` names its plain moves in `givesBack.plain`, and its give-back answers each act in its own stage through `givesBack.answers`.
- **Reuse a line from another subject by adding a `uses` row to the existing piece** ("I saw {name} {my_when}." serves both mutual subjects), never a second copy of the text; a give-back piece carries its own `piece` field, so it cannot reuse an answer piece and takes a text no other piece has. Two replies that say the same thing ("Not in a while." and "Nope. Not in a while.") are one: delete one. A denial ("I can't remember the last time.") is never offered when the record holds when. Dialogue writes "alright", not "all right" (line rule alright-in-dialogue). A new line of four words or more without a slot fails the line-inserts guard; keep such a give-back to three words ("Same, about then.").
- **Nobody asks what they would tell.** A question about someone is held back when the asker's own last contact is recent (`spoken-when.json` gap), and a question with no answer of the asker's own to give back is not asked.
- **When a bank's line count without inserts falls**, `tests/guards/english/line-inserts.test.ts` names the new number; lower `line-inserts-baseline.json` to it in the same commit.

## What a grade moves (Nov 15, the owner: "my grades should hit multiple parts of the engine at once")

- **A good or bad mark moves only its own piece.** `foldGrades` (scripts/dialogue-batch/apply-grades.ts) counts it against the piece's part keys, and `heldByGrades` holds or protects that piece wherever its uses reach; most scene-talk pieces have one use. Only 1-to-5 axis grades move `data/english/calibration.json` (move pairs, trait bearing, register length).
- **His reason is what spreads.** When he marks a line, find every piece with the same fault and turn the reason into a line rule that runs over every line. On Nov 15, "I wrote this one, so I'll say it plainly." (not good: the sponsor should say what the bill does) had the same fault as all 26 legislative state-tell lines, and "Yes, I'm with you." (too personal for the floor) as 10 floor-debate replies that address a colleague as "you". Report the count and the rule to the Words lead, not only the grade.

## Traps a talk-box builder met (Nov 15)

- **Two readers of one fact is the defect to look for first.** Acquaintance, kin and closeness were each worked out in the talk box, the scene packet and the Journal. They now come from `relationFacts(core, asker, asked)` in `src/director/relation-facts.ts` with one cutoff row (`tieSides.friendCloseness`). A new fact about how two people stand goes there, never into a scene or the talk box alone.
- **Order in the talk box is code.** `mainTopic`, `OPENING_ORDER` and the bring-it-up path decide what is said first; a fix to what a stranger says first must be proved with a test that fails without it (the introduction-first test), because a news path before `mainTopic` can bypass it.
- **`npm run words:pairs` sheets scene pairs only.** Home-talk bank pieces (`data/english/parts/conversation.json`) need a hand-written sheet for M5 from the played talks.
- **Test hygiene:** `buildBenchWorld` takes the seed as a string and about a minute to build, so a test that builds one sets its own timeout (say why in a comment); `npm run typecheck` needs `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=8192`; `release:check` needs a non-shallow clone.
- **A test red on the train is checked on the clean train before it is blamed on your change** (stash, run the one test, pop).

## What the scene reply record says, and bisecting a red (Nov 15)

- **A `scene.reply` record names the player first** in `personIds`; its `asked` fact is there only where the player asked, and its `move` is always the answerer's (the player's reply, or the colleague's answer where the player asked). `relationFacts(...).passed` reads those; read them there, never count replies in a scene or a variant.
- **A test cannot write an earlier day:** events enter on the current date, so a test that needs "before" writes the record, then `advanceCore`s a day.
- **A talk with no line is no talk:** the bench handler opens nothing when `openConversation` returns no lines (owner, Nov 1), so an empty talk in a test is by rule; a test that opens the same person twice in a day depends on a repeated introduction and must open each person once.
- **Bisecting a red across folds:** `git bisect start <bad> <good>` then `git bisect run` on one test works, but a merge-graph can name a commit that touches nothing the test reads (the celebration-ask count named an opening-landing commit); read the named commit's files before believing it, and file the item with the next step if they do not fit.

## Playing a relative's news for a words pull request (S3, Nov 15)

- **The proof script is `scripts/words/relative-news-proof.ts`.** `--stage death` starts a dev illness for the first relative who lives elsewhere and tells the death; `--cause <health cause>`, `--illness <condition or none>`, `--gender`, `--no-illness` stage what is too rare to wait for (firearm causes are under 1 in 100 deaths) and each prints a `STAGED` line; `--replays N` asks "How?" again on later days, because the wording the composer draws depends on the day. A played file says exactly what was staged.
- **The reader stamps only lines played at the exact head, in a file headed with that head's sha.** After merging the Train, rerun every staged run at the new head before posting it; a comment-only commit is a new head. Run the whole gate on that head, including eslint and prettier on every file changed against the Train.
- **A line placed on a fact needs a path to the fact.** Before writing a mark, read whether the engine ever produces it: every full person's death in health.ts is booked by a case with an onset event, so "nothing was wrong" was unreachable and its four lines were deleted (#9384). Ask M5 for the reach check on the fact, not only the wording.
- **A reach check is redone after merging the Train, and a deleted line is proved unreachable by composing the scene.** #9256 deleted three not-sure-yet proposal answers as unreachable at 0bf3e4f5; after Train b22cd350 merged, the asked person's typical tie rose (Rosebud PA 0.166 to 0.433) and the ten-place replay reached that answer six times, played as journal lines with nothing to say. Before deleting a line as unreachable, run the replay or walk at the head that will ship, after the Train merge, and name that head; compose the scene for 100 or more askers and see whether the line ever appears (a twin that always wins the pick, or an open line in a scene that has no open, are the two cases seen). A played file shows only lines that rendered, so list the deleted ones at its end.
- **JSON data files: never edit one with `sed` after prettier.** A quote inside a string broke `relative-news-words.json` and every proof run failed at start; parse the file after any scripted edit.

- **Folding verdicts rewrites files you did not judge (Nov 15).** `npm run words:rules` after `words:verdicts -- --from <dir>` rewrites every judged file and `pulled.json`. Keep the judged files of the rule you sent and revert the rest with `git checkout`; if the line-rules guard then fails on a count in `pulled.json` alone, change that one number by hand rather than keeping the whole rewrite.
- **A reply that belongs to a place (a meeting) is held off a scene held elsewhere with `excludesFacts`, and its twin is placed with `requiresFacts` on the fact the director sets (Nov 15: `outdoors` on a protest, so "Stay after, and let's sit down" is not said in the street).**
- **A moment on a person's ledger is not always theirs.** The director's ledger also holds what a person merely saw (every town race result reaches a watched resident). A line that speaks of the player's own filing, race or job reads the moment only when `moment.selfNamed` is true, as the journal's own-race split does; without it a player who never ran heard "I'm sorry about Council member" (`playerNews`, `src/director/conversation.ts`).

## A line that never plays: log the pick before you delete it (Nov 15)

When a line fits as well as another and still never plays, log the weights and the draw in `renderGroundedEnglish` before calling it unreachable. On Nov 15 two cause-invite lines had weight 1 each and the draw was 1 for all 23 askers: `stableHash` is FNV-1a, whose lowest bit is the parity of how many characters in the key have an odd code, and a moment key that holds the same two ids twice cancels them. Any `stableHash(...) % n` with even `n` is biased this way (item words-line-pick-hash-low-bit-is-character-parity; M4's pick PR fixes it). A line held out for this comes back once the pick is fixed.

## Lessons of Nov 15 (the reporter's call and the scandal call)

- **A scene that never composes looks fine on a page.** The scandal call had been skipped in every real game: a press record names its outlet by the press's outlet row (`outletId: outlet.id`), and purpose-scene.ts looked it up with `core.organizations.get`, so `{outlet}` never filled and the narration failed. Before trusting a scene's lines, stage its cause through the game's own path (the engine's function, then the director's schedule, then `purposeScene`) at a named place and seed, and read `result.kind` and `reason`. A hand-built fact packet hides a missing fact.
- **Place a line by the record, not by a guard on the line.** A reply that states a side, waits on a vote or says a bill works reads the asked person's own roll row (`src/director/vote-stand-facts.ts`: `acted`, `voted-yes` / `voted-no`, `bill-passed` / `bill-failed`, `reason-*`). A line about whether the vote is ahead reads `vote-held` (M4's #9399), which also covers a member who missed the vote. Before adding `requiresFacts` or `excludesFacts` to a bank line, diff the open words pull requests' scene-talk.json against the train head, so two pull requests never place one line two ways.
- **A line that claims a change of mind, or a record of the player's, has that record as its gate; an amount only weights (Account lead, Nov 15).** An official's no states the true reason from the record (the roll row's `reason-*` facts), never a reason the record does not hold.
- **The lie arc already exists.** A lie is caught when the packet holds `knows-truth` (purpose-arcs.json `lie.caught`); the asker's catch is a follow line with `after: "lie"` and the caught move, and the replies after it are placed with `after` set to that line's piece key. Set `knows-truth` from a record (every press exposure ran on the paper trail); never write a second catch mechanism.
- **`after` takes a move or a piece key; the piece key wins.** Use the key to hang a line on one exact line before it. A piece plays once per scene tree, so one reply that must follow two places needs two wordings from the Words lead.
- **A text is one part, case-insensitively.** If the line you need already exists in another scene ("Who told you that?"), add a use to that part; a second part with the same words fails the bank schema test.
- **After deleting lines:** `npm run words:verdicts` drops the verdicts about them and `npm run words:rules` rewrites data/english/pulled.json. Run both on your head and commit what they change. tests/english-answerable-asks-baseline.json only shrinks: remove your own deleted keys by hand; its rewrite script also adds other pull requests' lines.
- **A merge of main into a bank branch can silently lose a rule's fields.** A set rule (`judges: "set"`) needs `sets` and `applies` in `data/english/line-rules.json`; without them `words:verdicts` finds no subjects and drops every good verdict. After any merge that touches line-rules.json or the judged stores, compare each rule's good count before and after (`npm run words:verdicts` prints them); `tests/guards/english/line-rules.test.ts` fails when a stored set rule loses `sets`/`applies` or a judged rule has no good verdict. For a conflict in a judged store, take main's side, re-run `npm run words:verdicts -- --from <dir>` on the reviewers' files, then `npm run words:rules`, then prettier.
- **Showing a line said** (the second reader stamps only lines said at the exact head): press the offered reply, never write one. A scratch script plays a named place and seed, opens the talks, and presses a reply whose text is a target; every line is the engine's. The facts a reply needs (`requiresFacts`, a stance by relation and risk trait in `playerNewsFacts`) decide when it is offered, so scan seeds for the household first (a cautious parent for `stance-worry`), then fix that seed and name it; keep no draw loop. A reply that is one of several same-use variants (eight "disagree" wordings) may never be the one the engine picks; a line no play reaches comes out.
- **Never `git add -A` with scratch scripts in the tree**; keep them under /tmp and stage paths by name.
- **A goodbye that speaks to someone known needs a record of an earlier contact, and a talk with a stranger cannot play it.** The goodbye facts (`partingLine` in `src/director/conversation.ts`) read whether the pair met before this talk (`metBeforeAtOpen`, set when the talk opens, because the first reply stamps today as the last contact) and not the talk's own greeting; a relative who lives away is a husk that never plays one. Prove a known-goodbye line the same way as any line: stage the earlier meeting through the game's own call (`meet` with the visit cause) at a named place and seed, play the talk, and show the line said; a line no staged talk reaches comes out of the bank.
- **A reader of a record lives with the record.** The director may import a core2 family's entry files only (tests/guards/families.json); a reader of a press record goes in press.ts, not in a new director file that imports corruption.ts or contracts.ts. src/director files hold no numeric literal at all, a type index like `Parameters<typeof f>[0]` included; name the type.

## Facts a talk sets, and bank text (S2, layoff thread, Nov 15)

- **A talk's later pieces read one fact set once by the opener, never their neighbor line.** Put `setsFacts` on the opener's use (outlook-sure, outlook-unsure, outlook-plain) and `speakerKnows: true` on that use, so the composer treats a set fact as known to its speaker within one line; without the flag, other scenes whose pieces require a lead's facts shift their picks (the address talk did, Nov 14). Carry it into later turns with a function reading the told line's wording parts (`factsSetBy` in conversation-words.ts, used in the day-back branch of conversation.ts); one shared helper is pool item english-one-shared-facts-set-by-helper. Write a table of every piece with what it reads or sets, so a reader checks the table, not replays (the lost-job talk's second reader found two contradictions a turn later that the pair judge could not see).
- **A bank piece's text is unique across the whole file, lowercased.** A new line that repeats an existing text in another move fails the banks test: delete your copy or reword it by what the move says. A line that says less than its headline (no count) needs `excludesFacts` on the count facts so it is used only where the record lacks the count. A line that must not print for one kind of record is held off with a fact the record carries and `excludesFacts` on that use (the chamber story's `mid-term`, #9529), never by changing its text.
- **A played proof that needs the game's own long gap is a script, not a test (Nov 15).** The resident who is helped or turned away returns after the case gap (180 days), so the class H proof needs about 300 days of the world (ten minutes). It lives in scripts/words/relation-greets-replay.ts with a pinned place, seed and official and writes the played file at the head; the fact rule is checked by a fast test that says what it is (hand-written records, a check of the rule, not a played talk). A promise counts as help only once the case settled helped (`settled: help` on the reply record).
