# The words engine and the director: the handoff

**Look first:** run the `look-first` search before building, fixing, filing or asking; a found ruling is built and cited, never asked again.

**Who does what (owner, Nov 10: "Split by role").** The Story session writes, changes and deletes every line under `data/english/parts/`: the rewrites of lines the rules pull, the new reply moves that make four replies four different answers, and the saved meaning-and-tone verdicts. Fable and its helpers build the machine: the rules, the checks, the run, the pulled list and the code that composes replies. Any other builder lists the lines and moves it needs on its item's pull request and Story writes them; one writer per bank file. Where a line below says "write" or "add a piece", read it as the Story session's job, or as the instruction for what a builder must list. Every note the owner gives becomes a rule in `data/english/line-rules.json` that a program tests (`npm run words:rules`); a rule a program cannot judge is judged once by a reviewer and saved (`data/english/judged/`).

**Lines ship per scene; real lives calibrate (owner, Oct 26-27; `scenes-from-life`).** Batch 22 was the Oct 15 lines; no batch after it, and no grading batch is owed. Every scene a pull request touches, new or old, lands with its own new lines written to `scene-standard` (several per speaker by trait and tie, up to four replies that are all different stances), and the Oct 15 lines it replaces are deleted in the same change; since Nov 10 the Story session writes and deletes those lines (see "Who does what"). New scenes come from real lives: one episode, a fact packet, the engine asked whether it fires, gaps turned into producers and lines (`scenes-from-life`). The home talks the owner heard on Oct 26 are rewritten first.

**SMART and look first (owner, Oct 26; `.agents/rules/smart-and-look-first.md`).** The standard is Wildermyth (`OWNER-DECISIONS.md`, Oct 26): a scene advances the story and hands the player back to the game with something changed; people are varied, with pasts from the world's records that surface in scenes. Every bank item is SMART with a time box. Before any owner question, do the full reading (`OWNER-DECISIONS.md`, `DECISION-REGISTER.md`, `prior-rulings/`, `LAW.md`, a `docs/` search) and end it with the reading line. Research into real speech and records is allowed and expected.

## The target: scenes like Wildermyth (owner, Oct 24)

Scenes "that come out of what is actually happening in the world, narrated into a moment, played out with real dialogue and choices, the outcome back into the game; the current opening is the right shape, the words are wrong." That is the measure of every piece of this line: a record fires it, the narrator sets it, people with purposes play it out with four real replies, and what the player chose goes back into the records.

## The one rule that explains everything

Words come from the English engine; every bank part plays unless the owner's grades hold it or one of his rules pulled it (`data/english/pulled.json`), hand-written (`origin: authored`) parts included (owner, Oct 24; this replaces the Oct 20 "held until graded" hold, which no longer applies). Words come from the English engine's banks (`data/english/parts/`) and control names. The banks are built at development time: pieces are written to calibrated patterns, drawing on any source for the patterns (books, other games, transcripts, the internet), and copying no one's copyrighted text. At play the engine arranges the pieces; the director decides which moment is happening and who is in it. Neither types a line. Public-domain records remain a fine source of pieces, not a requirement. The old "two documents, one public-domain" attestation rule was never the owner's and is withdrawn (OWNER-DECISIONS.md, "The text rule", Oct 15, evening). Research and real data calibrate how pieces are arranged; they never decide a person's outcome.

## The three layers, and where each lives

1. **Situations (the director, `src/director/`).** `src/director/data/situation-types.json` is the catalog: 103 situation types (counted Nov 10; `src/director/director.test.ts` holds the count) (`departure`, `celebration`, `news-arrives`, `first-meeting`, `visit`, ...), each with roles (who fills it: `subject`, `counterpart`, `household`, `kin`, `present`, `classmates`, `coworkers`, `absent`, `authority`), the **moves** each role may make (`farewell`, `promise`, `comfort`, `tell`, `request`, `blame`, ...), what each role **wants**, a **bearing** (tender, solemn, ...), a setting, a timing, and **causes**: the record labels that fire it (a `money:job-started` record, a death, an eviction). A cause whose producer the core does not have yet names it in `awaitingProducer` and matches nothing. No type holds a sentence. The director (`src/director/scene.ts`, `scheduling.ts`, `ledger.ts`) watches the player's circle, fires a situation when a record matches a cause, stages it (home, work, town, the council room), and runs it as moves. `conversation.ts` runs talks: opens from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person in the ledger, keeps to that topic, chains to one related topic, closes when done; up to four replies ranked by the player's traits, lies as the same moves made false, "say nothing" beside them (`src/director/data/conversation-rules.json`). `journal.ts` writes the first-person "My life" memoir from records. `opening-story.ts` is the opening.
2. **Moves (the grammar).** A move is one thing a person does with a line: greet, deliver news, ask, answer, condole, congratulate, deflect, lie, agree, close, farewell, comfort, request, blame. Moves are situation-blind: a condolence serves a funeral, an assassination and a lost election. The director picks the next move from the situation's arc and the speaker's traits and relationship; the engine fills it. The move names in the catalog and the `kind`/`stage` names in the banks must agree; that mapping is the seam between director and engine and is the first thing to check when a line comes out wrong.
3. **Pieces (the banks, `data/english/parts/*.json`).** One bank per register (`ls data/english/parts` is the list; 21 files on Nov 10): `conversation.json`, `scene-talk.json`, `scene-words.json`, `journal.json`, `person-history.json`, `person-sketch.json`, `address.json` (floor, hearing, public comment, campaign, victory, concession), `opening-speech.json`, `speech-stages.json`, `minutes.json`, `legislation.json`, `bill-paper.json`, `notice.json`, `hearing.json`, `newscast.json`, `news.json`, `headlines.json`, `calendar.json`, `campaign-standing.json`, `bring-it-up.json`, `choice-names.json`. Each part is a sentence with slots, its move (`kind`, `stage`), its register, the record facts it needs (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the fact and is said only when the record holds it), and optionally a source note (document, date, address, license) or `origin: authored` with author and date. `src/english/english-composition.ts` (`composeAddress`, `composeFromBank`), `grounded-english.ts` and `speech-registers.ts` compose; `calibration.ts` and `move-fit.ts` weigh pieces by the speaker's traits, the register's length and the move before, never by a grade of the line; `english-grades.ts` applies the owner's grades (a failed part is never used; a conditional one only where its condition holds); `bank-parts.ts` (`playable`) is the one gate, grades and pulled list together; `runtime-text-origin.ts` was meant to guard that every shipped string has a bank origin, but its audit (`tests/e2e/runtime-text-audit.spec.ts`) is not on main and its baseline listed strings from `src/player`, which no longer exists (checked Oct 26 and again Nov 10). Nothing enforces it; check by hand with `player-screen-text`.

## The words machine: his notes become rules (owner, Oct 23 to Nov 10; `docs/design/words-machine.md`)

He will not grade thousands of lines ("im not grading 6000 lines and 800 conversations individually", Nov 8) and wants a grade to change the whole game ("every time I update a batch or something, it should update through the entire game", Oct 23). So a note of his becomes a rule, a program runs every rule over every line, and a line that breaks a rule stops playing until it is rewritten. There are no odds: the grade map, the reach table and the weights that favored ungraded lines are deleted. A line plays or it does not.

- **A rule is a note made testable**, one entry in `data/english/line-rules.json`: `id`; `says` (one plain sentence of what it forbids or requires); `from` (his exact words, at most 240 characters, with the date; a rule with only a research source has `research` instead and says so); `status`; `test`; optional `applies` (`banks`, `kinds`, `moves`, so a rule about speech does not touch a headline); `catches` (lines he rejected that the rule must flag); `spares` (lines he liked that it must not). The two lists are the rule's own test, and `tests/guards/english/line-rules.test.ts` runs them.
- **A rule about where a line is said reads the use's tags** (part 2 of the words machine, Nov 10). `applies.uses` takes `has` and `lacks`, each a map from a tag to a regular expression: the use's subject, role, purpose, step, `requiresFacts` and `excludesFacts`, plus the part's own `bearing`, `voice`, `addressKind`, `topic` and `device`, which `usesWithTags` copies onto every use. `applies` may be a list (any entry applies). A spot rule's `catches` and `spares` hold the line with its spot (`{ text, bank, use }`) so the case is tested where it is said. A rule that needs a fact no tag holds stays `awaiting-test` with the missing tag named in its `note`; never guess the context from the words. A line that is the only one some world has for its spot (a fallback said when a fact is missing) gets `rewrite`, not `enforced`: the spot count ignores facts, so pulling it could silence that world.
- **Who says a line, to whom and where, is on every use** (part 2 of the words machine, Nov 14). `usesWithTags` also adds `spotTags` (`src/english/spot-tags.ts`): `situation`, `speaker`, `speakerFill`, `listener`, `listenerFill`, `setting`, `stakes`, read from `src/director/data/situation-types.json` (and a variant's family through `arc-variants.json`) the way the director casts: the use's `role` asker or asked names the type's role, and where a type says `playerAsks` the asker's lines are the player's (`you`). `tie` uses the conversation engine's fact names (`stranger`, `acquainted`, `kin`, from the use's `requiresFacts`) plus `household` or `kin` where the role's fill fixes it. A purpose several types play gives lists. None of these is a spot field. A `judged` rule with `judges: "piece-in-context"` is judged in each spot a line is said (`spotSubjects`; id `verdictId([key, "spot:" + useSpotId(use)], [text, ""])`): a "no" in some spots goes to `pulledUses` in `pulled.json` and `admittedParts` (`bank-parts.ts`) leaves those uses out for every bank; a "no" in every spot pulls the line; a spot left with no other playable line waits instead. Sheets for such a rule have one row per spot with that use's tags.
- **Narrow a judged rule with `applies` before writing its first sheet** (part 7, Nov 14). A `judged` rule with no `applies` asks a reviewer about the whole bank (about 13,000 lines); give it the banks, kinds, moves or use tags it is about (`stake-is-named`: plea and bargain moves, 196 lines; `journal-names-no-feeling`: the journal, 340). A rule that judges a whole item, a generator's output or a writer's method (`look-up-how-real-people-say-it`, `business-names-sound-real`) has no bank line to judge: keep it without `applies` and say so in its `note`. Count a rule's subjects with `lineSubjects` before and after.
- **Three kinds of test.** The line's own words (`phrases`, `patterns`, `sentences`): a program reads the line. The line's place (`place`): whether a reply follows what came before, whether a person was introduced first; it needs the lines around it. The first, `reply-follows-the-line-before`, is built (next bullets). Meaning and tone (`judged`): a reviewer judges each line once and the verdict is saved with the line's exact text; the run reads it (next bullets). A `place` rule without its reader is `awaiting-test` and flags nothing, and so is a `judged` rule until its first verdicts; both keep his words and their catches and spares so the test has something to be built against.
- **One store for every rule a reviewer judges** (`src/english/judged.ts`, `scripts/words/verdicts.ts`). `data/english/judged/<store>.json` holds only the "no" verdicts, each with its keys and a one-sentence reason, the rubric word for word (the rule's `says`; the reply rule has its own, `REPLY_FOLLOWS_RUBRIC`), the count judged and a hash of the yes ids, which are in `<store>.yes.txt`. The store is named by the rule's `test.reads` (the reply rule's `reply-follows`) or else by its id. A verdict's id is a hash of the subject's keys and their exact words (`verdictId`): a line, a pair [line before, reply] or a set (part 3 builds set and exchange keys), so a changed line has no verdict until judged again. Only the fold writes the store. Nothing is judged while the game is played, and the run calls no model.
- **How a reviewer judges a sheet.** (1) `npm run words:pairs -- --rule <id> --out <dir outside the repository>`: for a rule about one line, every line it applies to with no verdict in its current words, with its bank, key, kind, move, the rubric and his words behind the rule (a rule about a line in its spot adds the tags of each use); for the reply rule, the pairs, one sheet per purpose (`--only-unjudged`, `--observed`, `--per-file`, `--sample`, `--seed`). (2) A reviewer, a different session from the builder, answers the rubric for each id and writes `{ rule, verdicts: [{ id, passes, why }] }` beside the sheet; a "no" needs its one-sentence `why`. Never judge a line you wrote. (3) `npm run words:verdicts -- --from <dir>` folds every file: it refuses an id the rule does not judge now (an old sheet or a changed line: write the sheet again), drops verdicts whose lines the bank no longer holds in those words, and changes nothing folded twice; with no `--from` it only drops what no longer applies. (4) `npm run words:rules`, twice: the second run changes no file.
- **What the run does with verdicts.** A `judged` rule flags every line judged "no" in its current words, and from there it is any other rule: `sample` lists the line for him, `enforced` pulls it, a line he graded good is never pulled and is listed under Conflicts, and a spot is never left with no line. A line nobody has judged passes and plays (LAW section 7: ungraded is not rejected). The report shows, per such rule, lines judged, judged bad and waiting for a verdict. A `judged` rule leaves `awaiting-test` for `sample` when its row has one line it must catch, one it must spare and its first verdicts folded (the English guard checks), and goes to `enforced` by the same step as any rule. **The backlog only falls:** `pulled.json`'s `awaitingVerdict` keeps, per `judged` rule past `awaiting-test`, the lines waiting for a verdict; `npm run words:rules` never raises it (it exits 1 naming the rule), and the English guard fails by the rule's name while the fresh count is higher. So a new or changed line comes with its verdicts for every such rule it falls under.
- **A place rule is judged pair by pair, and the picker reads the verdicts** (step 3; `src/english/reply-pairs.ts`, `reply-follows.ts`). Every pair (line before, reply) the director can produce is judged once against its rubric through the store above; a reply that does not follow some line is not pulled, it is never offered after that line (owner, Nov 9: fix the setup, keep the line). Judging a pair "no" lets the next closest reply into the pair list, so ask for `--only-unjudged` again until it prints no pairs. A line after which no reply follows is listed in `pulled.json` under `noReplyFollows` with the purpose and move: write a new reply for it (the rewrite queue). **A new reply line is judged before its pull request**: run the loop for the pairs it joins, and fold. Unjudged pairs play as before and are counted, never a failure (the reply rule is not held to `awaitingVerdict`: a "no" brings new pairs in, so its count can rise from judging).
- **Statuses.** `enforced` rules pull lines. `rewrite` rules never pull: the lines they flag keep playing and go on the rewrite queue (`waiting`), and a rewrite that passes the rules replaces them (CTO, Nov 10: the four journal work lines, whose first rewrites broke the journal's ten-word limit). `sample` rules never pull; use one where his own notes contradict each other ("I'm not going to talk about that" fine on Oct 15 and unnatural on Oct 16; "Alright" against "All right"), and the lines it flags go in his next small sample. `failsBuild` marks a rule that failed the build before the machine existed (the pattern bans); a bank line that breaks one fails the guard even when pulled.
- **Turning a note into a rule.** Look first: search `line-rules.json` for the rule (add his words to its `from` and the line to `catches` or `spares`), then the grades, then the rulings. Quote him exactly and short; never alter or paraphrase. Pick the narrowest test that is true to what he said. Three or more notes saying the same thing make a rule; one stray remark does not. A rule that needs a list of phrases takes only phrases his notes name.
- **A line faulted for its place is kept, not pulled** (owner, Nov 9: "the context is weird" means fix the setup and keep the line). When his words are about what came before, when the line played, who said it or a record it contradicts, the line goes in the place rule's `examples` (`line`, `before` when his words give it, `words`, `date`), the step 3 judging reads them, and the line stays in play: not pulled, not waiting, not sample. `rejected-by-voice` holds only lines his words fault as sentences, each with its own `from` entry in the same order.
- **A rule that flags a line he graded good is wrong.** Narrow it until it flags none (`runRules` lists each conflict, and the guard fails on one). Never drop a good line to keep a rule.
- **The run: `npm run words:rules`.** It reads the banks, the rules and the grade ledger, writes `data/english/pulled.json` (keys sorted: `pulled`, `waiting`, `sample`) and prints a report: for each rule its `says`, how many notes back it, how many lines it pulled, examples, then totals and every conflict. `--report <path>` also writes it as Markdown (do not commit a report that quotes his notes at length). A second run changes nothing. After a grading the update is one command, `npm run words:grade`, which runs `apply-grades.ts` then `words:rules` and prints the report's path and its Totals lines.
- **Pulled, waiting, sample.** Pulled: the line breaks an enforced rule and does not play. Waiting: it breaks one but every other line in one of its spots also breaks a rule, so pulling it would leave the spot with nothing; it keeps playing and heads the rewrite queue. Sample: a sample rule flagged it; it plays and goes in his next sample. A line he graded good is none of these. A spot is where a line is said: the bank plus the use's subject, role, move, choice, key, step, purpose, after and outcome, which are the fields the pickers match on.
- **The gate.** A new bank line that breaks an enforced rule fails `line-rules.test.ts` by name: "<key> breaks rule <id>: <says>". Fix the line; if the line should stay, the rule or the note behind it is what to question, with the owner's words.
- **His notes and batch pages stay off main** (owner, Nov 9: "I don't know why like my notes and stuff need to be merged into main"). Only the rules (with their short quotes) and the grade rows go to main. A batch goes to him as an artifact page; his voice note is read at run time from where the CTO keeps it. A test uses a fixture in our own words.
- **A helper's claim that a guard passes is checked by running the guard** (CTO, Nov 10). Run `tests/guards/english` yourself before you say a bank change is clean; a helper's "it passes" turned main red twice.
- **Stacked bank branches are merged without re-running an old grade script** (CTO, Nov 10). A grade script re-run on an older branch counts a batch again. Merge by piece key (below), keep main's `part-grades.json`, then run `npm run words:grade` once, on the merged head.

## How a bank is made (authoring to patterns)

Full history of the existing mined banks: `docs/research/english-corpus/README.md`. The method now:

- **Authoring (the Story session's job since Nov 10).** The `civic-prose` skill is the process. A piece is drafted to a move, register, key and slots; reviewed for grounding (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the record fact it needs and is said only when the record holds it) and terminology; then added to the bank with its move tags. Mark new parts `origin: authored` with the author and date. Every part plays unless the owner's grades hold it or a rule pulled it: the ledger and `pulled.json` are the only gates, and a part carries no flag of its own. After adding or regenerating a bank, run `npm run words:rules`; if it lists your piece as pulled or waiting, the failure message names the rule and the owner's words behind it, so fix the line rather than the rule. Run `apply-grades.ts` only when a grade file changed.
- **Patterns.** Counts, shapes and wording come from any source: English books, other games, transcripts, council minutes, news, the internet (`scripts/english-corpus/shapes/<bank>.json`), copyrighted or not (owner, Oct 24: "There's plenty of stuff, even copyrighted, out there. You don't have to cite stuff."). Where wording came from never decides whether it plays, and no line names its source. No real politician's slogan.
- **Mining.** Where any record or text offers good pieces, mine them with `scripts/english-corpus/mine-shapes.py`. The `source` fields on a part are optional notes.
- **Tests.** `<bank>-bank.test.ts` composes a whole exchange through the real engine in a generated place (every record id resolving, no feeling words, both chairs where a chair exists). Add a bank by adding a shape or an authored set, a script and its test.
- **What "calibration" means.** Research sets how pieces are arranged and how often: the length of a public comment, how a concession opens and closes, which moves follow which. It never picks which line a particular person says: traits, relationship, the record and the owner's grades do.

## Grading: scenes with voice notes (owner, Oct 24; the batch tooling deleted Oct 28)

The owner does not grade batches. He plays or watches a scene or a scenario and reacts with a voice note; the session turns each note into grades on the parts the scene used and into fixes at the root. What he liked ships; what he rejected never plays again. The batch apparatus is deleted (owner, Oct 28, 18:15 UTC, `docs/coordination/OWNER-DECISIONS.md`; `docs/reports/words-director-plan-2026-10-28.md`, decision 4): no batch generators, no page builders, no table read, no files under `data/english/batches/`. Do not rebuild one.

- **One grade file per set of voice notes**, `data/english/grades/<set>.json` (`voice-notes-batches-11-19.json`, `voice-notes-oct15-28.json`): `{"batch": "<set>", "grades": [{"i", "grade", "by", "line", "note", "parts"}]}`. Every row carries the part keys of the line it grades (`parts`), so the file stands on its own. A voice note names a line by its label or number; the session writes one row per line.
- **`node --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/apply-grades.ts`** folds every grade file into `data/english/part-grades.json`, counting each grade once (by batch and row), so a part graded bad or fix stops playing everywhere. It no longer writes a map or a reach table. Run it after a grade file changes through `npm run words:grade`, which follows it with `npm run words:rules`: that is the whole update. `data/english/calibration.json` keeps only the trait, length and move-pair weights the bench grades set; no weight in it is a grade of a line.
- **The in-play grade control** (`src/bench/grade-line.ts`) writes a grade on a line the owner is looking at to `data/english/grades/play-grades.json`, naming the pieces that said it; `apply-grades.ts` folds it like any grade file.
- **A rewrite never deletes a line the owner graded good (owner, via the CTO, Nov 8).** Every key `data/english/part-grades.json` counts good must stay a piece in a bank, or be carried by a live piece through a grade row's `restoredFrom` (`data/english/grades/restored-liked-lines.json`, written by `scripts/dialogue-batch/restore-liked-lines.ts` from `restored-liked-lines.json`); `scripts/dialogue-batch/liked-lines-kept.test.ts` fails on any other graded-good key, and has no list of exceptions. Put a liked line where a scene reads it (a piece of that scene's purpose, with the grade moved onto it); a line no scene reads yet goes in a bank of its own kind (`address`, `hearing`, `legislation`, `minutes`, `notice`, each with a register class in `calibration.json`), under a stopgap `SG-WORDS-<bank>-no-reader` in `src/director/data/stopgaps.json` and a pool item for the scene that will read it. Before a rewrite, trim or bank deletion, run that test; when the new bank says the same job in other words, keep the old liked piece beside it in the same PR. The old pieces are in git: `git show <deleting commit>^:data/english/parts/<bank>.json`.
- **A scene shown to the owner answers why by record** (`ask-why`, `player-screen-text`): no raw id in a line, no body named by a member's title, no line repeated, no relative asked after with no recorded cause, no scene staged in the wrong household. Fix the engine defect at the root and replay in a fresh drawn place.
- **Proof scenes** are welcome: a situation the director does not yet fire may be composed from real records in a played world and shown, marked as a proof, so the owner reacts to what the game can say and about what. `scripts/dialogue-batch/scene-year.ts --seed <seed>` plays a drawn place's year and lists every scene with the record that fired it; the coverage table and the tooling that made it are gone; count empty cells from `scene-year.ts` and the type list instead.

## News stories: headline, lede, body and briefs (owner, Oct 29: "headlines and then a little bit of story. Nothing is actually on the paper")

- **One composer, one call.** A printed story's facts come from the source record by a builder in `src/bench/printed-words.ts` (`BUILDERS`, read through `Reader`). `composeNewsStory` (`src/english/english-newscast.ts`) words the headline, the lede, then one sentence per `context` role in `STORY_KINDS` whose facts are present. `wordPrintedStory` returns `{headline, lede, paragraphs, outlet}`, and the lane's paper prints exactly that (CREW.md seam): never a second composer on the page side.
- **Measure before writing:** `node --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/printed-kinds.ts --seed <seed>` plays a drawn place's year and lists every printed kind with its count, how many print a body, the unworded records and one example with its facts. A failing printed-words test's `GOLDEN_PATH_SEED` reproduces there.
- **A body sentence says something the record holds and the lede did not:** the bill's purpose, the sponsor, the chamber, what follows a veto or a committee vote, the national rate, the rate before. Add the fact to the builder from an engine record; never invent one. A sentence whose facts the lede already named is skipped by the composer.
- **Pieces live in two banks:** `data/english/parts/news.json` (14 words at most) and `headlines.json`, gated by `requiresFacts`. Words like passed, failed, vetoed and won add their own requirement (`variants()` in english-newscast.ts).
- **The paper's style:**
  - headlines in sentence case;
  - sums of a million or more rounded ("$228.7 million"), and a trillion in trillions ("$7.1 trillion", never "$7204.5 billion");
  - a law's measure named by the English engine's subject for its series (`storyTopicName`), never the key read as words ("snap maximum").
- **Measure what one reader meets.** `node --max-old-space-size=8000 --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/paper-year.ts --seed <seed> --draws 3 --played` plays the player's year and counts the sentences one paper prints more than three times in its year, the item's measure, and the same across all the town's outlets. A national story prints in every outlet, so the town-wide count says little: within one paper, Blunt, South Dakota went from 54 to 0 (#7022).

## Lessons of Nov 11 (the lead's review of the opening talk, #8736)

The first composed scene came back right in its build and wrong on the screen in two ways. Check both before you open a words pull request.

- **One screen names a thing once.** The relative's line named the point twice and each of four replies named it again: "legal cannabis sales" six times in one box. The insert rule asks for an insert in each line, not the same insert in every line. On one screen, no two replies carry the same insert: one names the point, one the town, one the other person, one a third party. The speaker's own line names the point once in full, then says "it".
- **A reply that asks about the world gets an answer from the world.** "How would it land in {place}?" had eight answers and every one was a dodge ("I couldn't tell you yet"). An answer carries one fact the world keeps, cited to its record: the place's own law on the question, a figure about the town, an employer, a person. A dodge may close an answer; it is never the whole answer. Test it: every answer to a question carries a slot besides the one the question already named.
- **Read the scene in three drawn places before the pull request, as the owner would.** If all three are about the same thing, say why in the body by function and line, and tell the lead: the cause is usually upstream (here, what the President may raise), and it needs its own item the same night.

## Lessons of Nov 11, second batch (the address and the filing counter)

- **Say a thing the way people say it.** "Another one for Council member in Mutual" and "U.S. Representative (District 2)" are a form's words. An office, a job, a bill or a place is said through one reader that gives its spoken form from its own record ("a seat on the Mutual Village Council", "mayor", "the District 2 seat in Congress"). Never a title-cased label in a sentence, never a parenthesis in speech.
- **An insert fits its sentence.** "They'll beat pay faster than any program could" was written for inflation and given pay. A wording that treats its subject as a problem (beat, fight, bring down) or as good news (the gains on, build on) says which it needs, and is offered only when the record says so. The same for grammar: Congress sends "a bill on {about}", not `{about}`.
- **A subject's word at most three times in one speech or scene.** The rule that every sentence carries an insert, with only a handful of facts to insert, gave an address that said "take-home pay" seven times. Past three, a move takes a wording that carries a different insert.
- **A lead-in does work or it goes.** Lead-ins that only announce the next clause ("Here's the thing:", "Let me be plain:") made every sentence of an address start the same way. Keep the ones that say who is spoken to, when or where.

## Lessons of Nov 11, third batch (the Journal note, and five riders held off a train)

- **Say the government, not the office that pays.** "My brother Darius is a chief administrative officer at the Village of Athens treasury." Nobody works at a treasury; the treasury is the record that pays him. When an employer is a government's paying office, the one "said as" reader gives the government ("for the Village of Athens"), read from the record's government facts, never from a word in its name.
- **One fact, once, across the sentences of a paragraph.** "My father started at Michael's Market when I was 15" and "My father Matthew works as a stocker at Michael's Market" are one fact told twice. When the job someone started is the job they hold, one sentence says the start, the place and the work.
- **Name a person at the first mention, then "he" or "she".** "My father" opened three sentences and his name arrived in the last.
- **A sentence that leans on the one before it never opens a paragraph.** "A year and a half on, my father moved over to Mayer's Kitchen" came first, with nothing to be after and no place he left. A move names both ends or says "started at".
- **A fixed answer is not added beside a composed talk.** Once a talk is composed from the records, older pull requests that add fixed answers to the same question are not merged; their good pieces (a deflection, a change of subject) become parts of the composed answer, each offered only when its record holds, and the old pull request closes as replaced. "I work at {employer}. I live in {my_place}." answered a work question with an address.

## Lessons of Nov 11, fourth batch (the donor, the day and what a walk heard)

- **A bill is said by what it does, never by its number.** "May I ask you about Ordinance 2025-9?" is what a clerk says. A donor, a neighbor or a reporter says "the police oversight ordinance", and the next line says "it". Read the subject from the reader the bill's own page uses for its title. The same holds for a headline quoted in a talk.
- **Small talk loses to anything with a record.** The day at home ("Another work day, huh?") is the last thing a talk opens on, as the director's own comment says. Played, it outweighed every view, story and piece of family news, so every talk at home was about the day for months. A subject with nothing but the clock behind it ranks below every subject that has a record, and is not raised again with the same person that week while anything else is due.
- **Words know the hour and the person.** "Busy one at work today?" at 7:00 AM, to someone with no job, answered with "I'm beat. Just glad to be home." A line about a day is written for a part of the day and for whose day it was, and both come from records (the shift's own hours, the school day), never from the line's author assuming an evening.
- **Two people in one room do not say the same sentence.** On the morning after the address two parents gave the same second sentence word for word. Within one household on one day, the second speaker takes another point or another reason, and never a wording the player has already heard that day.

## The composed checks (unit 5 of the composer, Nov 12)

A composed rule reads whole texts a player met, never one bank line: its row's test is `{ "kind": "composed", "check": [names], "applies": [kinds] }`, status `sample` (it reports, it pulls nothing), and `ruleFlags` returns false for it.

- **Run them:** `npm run words:composed -- --seed <seed> [--place-key <key>] [--draws N] [--days 14] [--report <file outside the repository>]`. Each draw plays a new life through the bench's own routes (`benchRequest`: the opening address and the evening after it, everyone at home who has something to say each day, Day pressed and every stop and staged scene played) and keeps every text as a `ComposedText` (`src/english/composed-text.ts`): speeches by level, talk at home and in town, scenes, narration, the Journal's first page and notes, news toasts and stories. A governor's or mayor's address the window misses is composed through `recordLocalAddress` at the end and marked `proof`. It prints per rule the texts read, breaks and three examples with place, seed and kind. A 14-day life takes under a minute; three draws in one process stay under 2 GB.
- **The count only falls.** `tests/guards/english/composed-baseline.json` keeps breaks per 100 texts per rule on recorded draws. `npm run words:composed -- --check` replays those draws and exits 1 naming any rule whose rate rose; the run is deterministic on a seed, so a rise is your change. After a change that lowers a count, write it again on the same draws with `--write-baseline` and the same `--seed` flags.
- **A words pull request quotes its before and after counts** from `--check` (or the table) for every composed rule its change touches, with the draws.
- **Add a check:** a pure function `(text, sample) => breaks` in `src/english/composed-checks.ts`, registered in `COMPOSED_CHECKS`; its numbers and word lists go in `data/english/composed-checks.json` (each number TUNABLE with a spread); name it in the rule's `test.check`; give it a catch and a spare in our own words in `tests/guards/english/composed-rules.test.ts`, which fails until every registered check is used by a rule and has fixtures. A check that cannot be sound without the telling or typed pieces is not guessed: leave that part named in the rule's `note`.
- **The talk's screen is one step of the talk box:** the other person's lines from one request plus the replies offered. A full name in the line and again in a reply counts twice on that screen, as the owner reads it.
