# Words engine: dated lessons

Part of the `words-engine` skill. Lessons and traps from the Nov 11 and Nov 15 passes, each with its case.

## Lessons of Nov 11 (the lead's review of the opening talk, #8736)

The first composed scene came back right in its build and wrong on the screen in two ways. Check both before you open a words pull request.

- **One screen names a thing once.** The relative's line named the point twice and each of four replies named it again: "legal cannabis sales" six times in one box. The insert rule asks for an insert in each line, not the same insert in every line. On one screen, no two replies carry the same insert: one names the point, one the town, one the other person, one a third party. The speaker's own line names the point once in full, then says "it".
- **A reply that asks about the world gets an answer from the world.** "How would it land in {place}?" had eight answers and every one was a dodge ("I couldn't tell you yet"). An answer carries one fact the world keeps, cited to its record: the place's own law on the question, a figure about the town, an employer, a person. A dodge may close an answer; it is never the whole answer. Test it: every answer to a question carries a slot besides the one the question already named.
- **Read the scene in three drawn places before the pull request, as the owner would.** If all three are about the same thing, say why in the body by function and line, and tell the lead: the cause is usually upstream (here, what the President may raise), and it needs its own item the same night.

## Lessons of Nov 11, second batch (the address and the filing counter)

- **Say a thing the way people say it.** "Another one for Council member in Mutual" and "U.S. Representative (District 2)" are a form's words. An office, a job, a bill or a place is said through one reader that gives its spoken form from its own record ("a seat on the Mutual Village Council", "mayor", "the District 2 seat in Congress"). Never a title-cased label in a sentence, never a parenthesis in speech.
- **An insert fits its sentence.** "They'll beat pay faster than any program could" was written for inflation and given pay. A wording that treats its subject as a problem (beat, fight, bring down) or as good news (the gains on, build on) says which it needs, and is offered only when the record says so. The same for grammar: Congress sends "a bill on {about}", not `{about}`.
- **A subject's word at most three times in one speech or scene.** The rule that every sentence carries an insert, with only a handful of facts to insert, gave an address that said "take-home pay" seven times. Past three, a move takes a wording that carries a different insert.
- **A lead-in does work or it goes.** Lead-ins that only announce the next clause ("Here's the thing:", "Let me be plain:") made every sentence of an address start the same way. Keep the ones that say who is spoken to, when or where.

## Lessons of Nov 11, third batch (the Journal note, and five riders held off a train)

- **Say the government, not the office that pays.** "My brother Darius is a chief administrative officer at the Village of Athens treasury." Nobody works at a treasury; the treasury is the record that pays him. When an employer is a government's paying office, the one "said as" reader gives the government ("for the Village of Athens"), read from the record's government facts, never from a word in its name.
- **One fact, once, across the sentences of a paragraph.** "My father started at Michael's Market when I was 15" and "My father Matthew works as a stocker at Michael's Market" are one fact told twice. When the job someone started is the job they hold, one sentence says the start, the place and the work.
- **Name a person at the first mention, then "he" or "she".** "My father" opened three sentences and his name arrived in the last.
- **A sentence that leans on the one before it never opens a paragraph.** "A year and a half on, my father moved over to Mayer's Kitchen" came first, with nothing to be after and no place he left. A move names both ends or says "started at".
- **A line may not claim more than the record holds, and an offer that cannot work says so (Account lead, Nov 16).** An unplayed line comes out with an item that brings it back when a talk says it.
- **A fixed answer is not added beside a composed talk.** Once a talk is composed from the records, older pull requests that add fixed answers to the same question are not merged; their good pieces (a deflection, a change of subject) become parts of the composed answer, each offered only when its record holds, and the old pull request closes as replaced. "I work at {employer}. I live in {my_place}." answered a work question with an address.
- **A words pull request shows itself or it does not ride.** Six pictures under `docs/seen/<name>/` (three drawn places, the same seeds before and after), the line as played in each, and the owner's complaint it answers with its date. Where a scene never played before, say so and show the after. The gate desks send back a words pull request without them (skill `merger` (merge-trains part)).

## Lessons of Nov 11, fourth batch (the donor, the day and what a walk heard)

- **A bill is said by what it does, never by its number.** "May I ask you about Ordinance 2025-9?" is what a clerk says. A donor, a neighbor or a reporter says "the police oversight ordinance", and the next line says "it". Read the subject from the reader the bill's own page uses for its title. The same holds for a headline quoted in a talk.
- **Small talk loses to anything with a record.** The day at home ("Another work day, huh?") is the last thing a talk opens on, as the director's own comment says. Played, it outweighed every view, story and piece of family news, so every talk at home was about the day for months. A subject with nothing but the clock behind it ranks below every subject that has a record, and is not raised again with the same person that week while anything else is due.
- **Words know the hour and the person.** "Busy one at work today?" at 7:00 AM, to someone with no job, answered with "I'm beat. Just glad to be home." A line about a day is written for a part of the day and for whose day it was, and both come from records (the shift's own hours, the school day), never from the line's author assuming an evening.
- **Two people in one room do not say the same sentence.** On the morning after the address two parents gave the same second sentence word for word. Within one household on one day, the second speaker takes another point or another reason, and never a wording the player has already heard that day.
- **In a list of people, one sentence per person.** A paragraph that tells a family's work by date tells each person two or three times and names them last. Tell it person by person: who they are and their name, what they do now, where and since when; a job they left only inside that sentence.
- **`openConversation` is also the room's probe.** The bench asks it for everyone in a room to learn who has something to say (`sceneNow`, "read without writing anything"). Anything written inside it happens for every person in every room the player walks into. A write that belongs to a talk the player really opened (meeting someone, time spent) goes in the talk route, after the other person has a line (Nov 11: the first build of meeting people in a talk sat inside it and was sent back).

## Lessons of Nov 15 (asked about someone both know, #9374 and the no-against-the-record fix)

- **A reader stamps only lines played at the exact head, so every new piece must play.** Get each one played through the director's own scenes with a throwaway test beside the real ones: `stageWorldHeld({ id, seed, days: 120, onStaged })` from `scripts/dialogue-batch/stage-world.ts`, opening `openConversation` on each staged scene and saying a chosen move with `say`; write the talk and the offered replies to a file outside the repository. Run one process per seed and per player act (an env var picks the act), since a talk cannot be reopened the same day. Try a handful of fixed seeds and pin the ones that play; a piece that plays at none is deleted, not kept. Name the throwaway file outside `src` or delete it before `git add -A`, which otherwise commits it.
- **The played file lists the player's side** (rule 114): the talk, then `You could say:` with every offered reply, then one `You say:` line per act with what followed, headed with the sha, with the new or reused pieces in bold.
- **A reply is a choice only if the player has more than one honest act.** Taking away a reply that is false on the record (a plain no to "Have you seen Margaret?" from a player who saw her days ago) must leave other acts: the true answer, or the true answer with a question back ("How about you?", "Why do you ask?"). A decline ("I'd rather not...") is offered only where something reads it or the record holds a reason for it; otherwise it is not an act. The answer to a question that just named someone says their pronoun, not the name again, and a give-back that would repeat the player's words says it is the same instead. A question back rides after the plain answer (`afterPlain`, set in `src/english/mined/conversation-bank.ts` for the turn choices), never opening the reply (owner, Oct 29). A kind with no move named `plain` names its plain moves in `givesBack.plain`, and its give-back answers each act in its own stage through `givesBack.answers`.
- **Reuse a line from another subject by adding a `uses` row to the existing piece** ("I saw {name} {my_when}." serves both mutual subjects), never a second copy of the text; a give-back piece carries its own `piece` field, so it cannot reuse an answer piece and takes a text no other piece has. Two replies that say the same thing ("Not in a while." and "Nope. Not in a while.") are one: delete one. A denial ("I can't remember the last time.") is never offered when the record holds when. Dialogue writes "alright", not "all right" (line rule alright-in-dialogue). A new line of four words or more without a slot fails the line-inserts guard; keep such a give-back to three words ("Same, about then.").
- **Nobody asks what they would tell.** A question about someone is held back when the asker's own last contact is recent (`spoken-when.json` gap), and a question with no answer of the asker's own to give back is not asked.
- **When a bank's line count without inserts falls**, `tests/guards/english/line-inserts.test.ts` names the new number; lower `line-inserts-baseline.json` to it in the same commit.

## Traps a talk-box builder met (Nov 15)

- **Two readers of one fact is the defect to look for first.** Acquaintance, kin and closeness were each worked out in the talk box, the scene packet and the Journal. They now come from `relationFacts(core, asker, asked)` in `src/director/relation-facts.ts` with one cutoff row (`tieSides.friendCloseness`). A new fact about how two people stand goes there, never into a scene or the talk box alone.
- **Order in the talk box is code.** `mainTopic`, `OPENING_ORDER` and the bring-it-up path decide what is said first; a fix to what a stranger says first must be proved with a test that fails without it (the introduction-first test), because a news path before `mainTopic` can bypass it.
- **`npm run words:pairs` sheets scene pairs only.** Home-talk bank pieces (`data/english/parts/conversation.json`) need a hand-written sheet for M5 from the played talks.
- **Test hygiene:** `buildBenchWorld` takes the seed as a string and about a minute to build, so a test that builds one sets its own timeout (say why in a comment); `npm run typecheck` needs `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=8192`; `release:check` needs a non-shallow clone.
- **A test red on the train is checked on the clean train before it is blamed on your change** (stash, run the one test, pop).

## What the scene reply record says, and bisecting a red (Nov 15)

- **A `scene.reply` record names the player first** in `personIds`; its `asked` fact is there only where the player asked, and its `move` is always the answerer's (the player's reply, or the colleague's answer where the player asked). `relationFacts(...).passed` reads those; read them there, never count replies in a scene or a variant.
- **A test cannot write an earlier day:** events enter on the current date, so a test that needs "before" writes the record, then `advanceCore`s a day.
- **A talk with no line is no talk:** the bench handler opens nothing when `openConversation` returns no lines (owner, Nov 1), so an empty talk in a test is by rule; a test that opens the same person twice in a day depends on a repeated introduction and must open each person once.
- **Bisecting a red across folds:** `git bisect start <bad> <good>` then `git bisect run` on one test works, but a merge-graph can name a commit that touches nothing the test reads (the celebration-ask count named an opening-landing commit); read the named commit's files before believing it, and file the item with the next step if they do not fit.

## Lessons of Nov 17 (the bribe's offer and its pressing)

- **A kind of scene is staged only so often: `seasonCap` in `src/director/data/director.json` allows six of a kind to one person in 91 days.** A walk that makes offers back to back sees `bindings []` and a `null` scene after the sixth. `scripts/words/bribe-walk.ts` lets a season pass after five asks, and meets the lines the engine's own scene did not happen to draw through a probe copy of the cause record (a new moment id, the same facts), named as a probe in the printed file.
- **Print every `read-*` fact before placing a line by one.** The `public` read in `arc-variants.json` is `kind: setting` with no `is`, so an undefined setting equals it and `read-public` is true in every bribe scene: lines placed by it played in a private office. A read that no record can set (a complaint id the bribe's cause lacks) is a defect to file, not a fact to place by.
- **An offer for a bill needs the bill pending and the player seated at that body** (`billBribeScope` refuses "no vote or signature on this bill"); a state legislature's bills do not outlive its session, so a walk drafts a fresh bill for each offer. A contract offer needs an award row (the id ends in the day it was made). Dev mode's `forceBribe(... answer: "refuses")` makes the engine's pending offer and the player's reply is written through `replyInScene` along the tree's path to the ending, which is what the corruption engine reads.
- **A fact name is the slot name, so a read that means another thing gets another name.** The donor's `gift-weight` ranks a gift among a campaign's gifts; the bribe's envelope against the household's costs is `bribe-weight`.

## A line that never plays: log the pick before you delete it (Nov 15)

When a line fits as well as another and still never plays, log the weights and the draw in `renderGroundedEnglish` before calling it unreachable. On Nov 15 two cause-invite lines had weight 1 each and the draw was 1 for all 23 askers: `stableHash` is FNV-1a, whose lowest bit is the parity of how many characters in the key have an odd code, and a moment key that holds the same two ids twice cancels them. Any `stableHash(...) % n` with even `n` is biased this way (item words-line-pick-hash-low-bit-is-character-parity; M4's pick PR fixes it). A line held out for this comes back once the pick is fixed.

## Lessons of Nov 15 (the reporter's call and the scandal call)

- **A scene that never composes looks fine on a page.** The scandal call had been skipped in every real game: a press record names its outlet by the press's outlet row (`outletId: outlet.id`), and purpose-scene.ts looked it up with `core.organizations.get`, so `{outlet}` never filled and the narration failed. Before trusting a scene's lines, stage its cause through the game's own path (the engine's function, then the director's schedule, then `purposeScene`) at a named place and seed, and read `result.kind` and `reason`. A hand-built fact packet hides a missing fact.
- **Place a line by the record, not by a guard on the line.** A reply that states a side, waits on a vote or says a bill works reads the asked person's own roll row (`src/director/vote-stand-facts.ts`: `acted`, `voted-yes` / `voted-no`, `bill-passed` / `bill-failed`, `reason-*`). A line about whether the vote is ahead reads `vote-held` (M4's #9399), which also covers a member who missed the vote. Before adding `requiresFacts` or `excludesFacts` to a bank line, diff the open words pull requests' scene-talk.json against the train head, so two pull requests never place one line two ways.
- **A line that claims a change of mind, or a record of the player's, has that record as its gate; an amount only weights (Account lead, Nov 15).** An official's no states the true reason from the record (the roll row's `reason-*` facts), never a reason the record does not hold.
- **The lie arc already exists.** A lie is caught when the packet holds `knows-truth` (purpose-arcs.json `lie.caught`); the asker's catch is a follow line with `after: "lie"` and the caught move, and the replies after it are placed with `after` set to that line's piece key. Set `knows-truth` from a record (every press exposure ran on the paper trail); never write a second catch mechanism.
- **`after` takes a move or a piece key; the piece key wins.** Use the key to hang a line on one exact line before it. A piece plays once per scene tree, so one reply that must follow two places needs two wordings from the Words lead.
- **A text is one part, case-insensitively.** If the line you need already exists in another scene ("Who told you that?"), add a use to that part; a second part with the same words fails the bank schema test.
- **After deleting lines:** `npm run words:verdicts` drops the verdicts about them and `npm run words:rules` rewrites data/english/pulled.json. Run both on your head and commit what they change. tests/english-answerable-asks-baseline.json only shrinks: remove your own deleted keys by hand; its rewrite script also adds other pull requests' lines.
- **A merge of main into a bank branch can silently lose a rule's fields.** A set rule (`judges: "set"`) needs `sets` and `applies` in `data/english/line-rules.json`; without them `words:verdicts` finds no subjects and drops every good verdict. After any merge that touches line-rules.json or the judged stores, compare each rule's good count before and after (`npm run words:verdicts` prints them); `tests/guards/english/line-rules.test.ts` fails when a stored set rule loses `sets`/`applies` or a judged rule has no good verdict. For a conflict in a judged store, take main's side, re-run `npm run words:verdicts -- --from <dir>` on the reviewers' files, then `npm run words:rules`, then prettier.
- **Showing a line said** (the second reader stamps only lines said at the exact head): press the offered reply, never write one. A scratch script plays a named place and seed, opens the talks, and presses a reply whose text is a target; every line is the engine's. The facts a reply needs (`requiresFacts`, a stance by relation and risk trait in `playerNewsFacts`) decide when it is offered, so scan seeds for the household first (a cautious parent for `stance-worry`), then fix that seed and name it; keep no draw loop. A reply that is one of several same-use variants (eight "disagree" wordings) may never be the one the engine picks; a line no play reaches comes out.
- **Never `git add -A` with scratch scripts in the tree**; keep them under /tmp and stage paths by name.
- **A goodbye that speaks to someone known needs a record of an earlier contact, and a talk with a stranger cannot play it.** The goodbye facts (`partingLine` in `src/director/conversation.ts`) read whether the pair met before this talk (`metBeforeAtOpen`, set when the talk opens, because the first reply stamps today as the last contact) and not the talk's own greeting; a relative who lives away is a husk that never plays one. Prove a known-goodbye line the same way as any line: stage the earlier meeting through the game's own call (`meet` with the visit cause) at a named place and seed, play the talk, and show the line said; a line no staged talk reaches comes out of the bank.
- **A reader of a record lives with the record.** The director may import a core2 family's entry files only (tests/guards/families.json); a reader of a press record goes in press.ts, not in a new director file that imports corruption.ts or contracts.ts. src/director files hold no numeric literal at all, a type index like `Parameters<typeof f>[0]` included; name the type.
- **A played file never has anything written onto the player (Account lead, Nov 15).** A line that needs a filing, a job or money is played with what the game itself gives: the player accepts the one job offer the game makes (`jobOffersFor`, `acceptJobOffer`), is paid by the game, and files through `fileForOffice` with the fee from that pay. Cash set on the player, an offer or a filing record put in by hand makes the file unstampable. The header of the file says what was done, and "staged" is never the word for it.
- **A talk counts a fact once (Account lead, Nov 15).** A name, an employer, a place or a question the talk already said is not said, offered or raised again; read it through `nameOnce`, `alreadyAnswered` and `withoutSaid` in `src/director/conversation.ts`, never a second tracker. A line that says "work" or "there" in place of the employer is the second telling of the first.
- **A line placed by fewer facts is shadowed, and an unseen line comes out (Story lead, Nov 17, the donor's call).** The picker takes the most specific fit (the facts a line requires, and a variant's own piece beats the family's), and a composed request (a core and a reason) beats a standalone line, so a base request placed by two facts never plays where a three-fact line or a composed one fits. Write the line at the specificity of the lines it competes with, or put it under the variant's own purpose, then prove it said: `node --import tsx scripts/words/donor-walk.ts --seed <word> --label <a> [--state] [--unseated] --seen-out <file>` composes the donor's call at a drawn place for every variant and prints the tree with every reply pressed; a new line no run said comes out.
- **A scene with nothing to say about its record is not offered.** A donor's gift that names no bill stages no call: no reply can be worded without the bill, and the engine skips a scene whose menu has fewer than two repliable moves (`fewer than two replies ... can be worded`). Lines for the no-bill case can never play.
- **A lie's believed branch is worded from the scene's own facts.** The "if not" branch renders with the same facts as the caught one, so a line in it that excludes `knows-truth` is never said and leaves a gap; print both branches of a lie in a walk (`scripts/words/donor-walk.ts` does) or those lines are never seen.
- **A history read counts only what is dated before the cause's day.** `passedBetween` and the variant reads (`put-off-before`) skip a record dated today or later: stage the earlier scene reply, advance a day, then make the new cause. An event cannot be dated back (`Events enter on the current date`).
- **A fixture follows the packet.** `src/director/arc-variant.test.ts` hands a family the facts its scene builder sets (`donorFacts`); when a rewrite makes a reply need a fact the builder now sets (a stand that matches the donor's, for an agree), the fixture gains that fact; it is not loosened.
- An ask-vote agree reply the player gives requires the `stand` fact (ask-vote-paths.test.ts: an agree with no stand behind it is a red); a vote trade ("yours for mine") is a relent after the asker's bargain, not an agree (Nov 18).
- A test case that only asserts deleted lines is cut with the TypeScript parser script (never regex), its dead helpers dropped with a parser too, and eslint run on the file; a case that still checks a mechanic stays red and is named on the PR (CTO option 1, Nov 18).
- After a cut, a scene's facts can vanish while its record stays: `addressPoints` rebuilt the address's points from the sentences and found none when the speech only asked to be held to a question, though the outline's `opening.points` held the side. A talk reads the record the words were built from, never only the words (owner, Oct 28; Nov 18).
- Judging a red for the cut, read the failure's whole message from the json, never a line cut at 260 characters: "expected 's" hid "skipped", and a world error ("the player's clock held before a death") read like a line red and was deleted, then reverted (Nov 18).
- A loop over purposes that must skip the ones a cut emptied names them in the test (`CUT_PURPOSES`), from the replaced list, on a CTO line; "at least one composed" would hide a purpose that lost its lines by accident (CTO, Nov 18).
- An empty bank is still a bank the engine reads: the register counts every bank it reads, an empty one at zero, so the guard that wants every bank file registered holds through a cut (Nov 18).
- A bank's `maxWords` counts every word outside a slot, and a slot that ends a sentence leaves its period counted as a word ("...doesn't trust {president-object}." is 11); run `tests/english-parts-banks.test.ts` with the English guards, since the guards folder alone never reads the length (Nov 18).
- The most specific fitting wording wins, so one wording that needs one more fact beats every general one each time it fits (the stance-specific "Good. I think we should {to}." took every opening it could); give each general wording one use per case so they tie and bearing and seed choose (Nov 18).
- In a joined sentence only the opener names the subject; a core, closer or answer that names `{about}` again fails the address-talk test, and the speaker's own pieces never use `{name}`, which is what the player calls them (Nov 18).
- A conversation piece with two uses of the same subject and role becomes two variants with one key in `composedConversationBank`, and no line of that subject composes at all ("no housemate raised a point" in every drawn world); rank a general wording by the slot fact it names instead, one use per piece (Nov 18).
- A scene whose packet carries only the people and the town gets padded lines: the insert guard wants a slot in every line of four words or more, so writers tack `{first}` or "in {place}" on (167 of 204 lines on Nov 18). Before any line, check that `composeBinding` in `src/director/purpose-scene.ts` has a block for the scene's cause record that says what happened; if not, the block comes first (Nov 18).
