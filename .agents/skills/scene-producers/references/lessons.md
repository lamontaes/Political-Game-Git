# Scene producers: what later scenes taught

Part of the `scene-producers` skill. The damaged-home scene, the governor's briefing room, staging and asking, and a choice the engine must not make (Nov 9 and 15).

## 9. What the damaged-home scene taught (Nov 9)

- **A table is not a record.** A circumstance row reads logged events; an engine's module table (the disasters engine's `damaged` rows) calls nothing. Have the owning engine emit one event naming the household on the day it fills the row (as housing does for a notice), with the row's id in its facts, and word the scene from that event; the row stays the engine's only copy.
- **Do not set `topic` on such an event** unless it is a policy topic: the facts block turns any `topic` into the `cause` slot.
- **A scene that is logged but never staged:** read `ledger.circumstanceLast` for the player. The week between scenes (`lived: true` lifts it for the player's own life) and the cast window (`recast: true` lifts it for a household with one other person, whom a news scene may have cast lately) hide a scene without an error.
- **Pieces: only the top tier plays.** `fitting()` keeps only the pieces with the highest specificity, so a state that has pieces for a move (paid-up) silences the generic ones; a generic piece that presupposes the other state ("how do we pay for it") needs `excludesFacts`, and each state needs a piece for every move of every branch, or its tree loses a reply. Give the pieces for the heaviest state the most required facts (ties pick across both).
- **Money in a fact** goes through `moneySaid` ("about $4,000"): never write "the {loss}" or "toward the {left}"; write "come to {loss}" and "still owe {left}".
- **The director imports no module from another engine family** (the families guard counts it): name the record kind in a local constant beside `RALLY_EVENT`.
- A new scene type needs its picture row or a `wantedScenes` line (skill `scene-pictures`); the `scene-pictures` guard fails one that has neither. Never wait for a picture.

## 10. What the governor's briefing room taught (Nov 15)

- **A decision the player owns is a record, a type and a read of `scene.reply`.** The engine writes one record naming the player when the decision is due (`executive.emergency-offered`), a situation type with `stopsDay` stages from it, and the engine reads the scene's reply by `typeKey` and `ending` (the executive module for an emergency, corruption for a bribe, candidacy for a party's ask). The scene never writes the decision itself, and the old desk plate for it is deleted in the same change.
- **A scene that binds with a full cast and still plays as a journal line has no lines.** `registerInterrupts` in `src/director/ledger.ts` closes any interrupt whose purpose the bank cannot word (`speaks`, through `wordsPurpose`), silently. Read the schedule row (`interrupt: true`, roles filled, outcome `journal-line`) before looking for a binding fault; the Words lead's lines for the purpose are the missing piece, and they land with the type.
- **A test that plays a governor or any held office:** the controlled person is `BenchPlayer.personId` (the official's term id), not `core.playerId`; the director watches that id. Clear desk stops with `ordersView` and `ordersDecide(core, id, "decline")` before `resume`, or the day never ends. Play to a real disaster (Frankfort, Kentucky, 2128900: FEMA 3624 and 4860 land in February 2025, 28 days in) instead of inserting a record: the disasters engine's disasters begin on their own dates.
- **A data map of who is in the room** (incident type to cabinet post keys, cited) is a row file read by the cast fill, never a path per disaster; a state without a post has no one in that seat.
- **A scene reads facts the cause record kept when it was made, never a row that may have folded.** The rival-word scene is staged days after its message; the message row had folded by then in one of three played places and the reply "What are they saying" had no answer. Put the fact the scene needs (`how`, `side`) on the event's own facts when the event is written, and let a reply that needs a fact say so with `requiresFacts`, so no reply is offered that the record cannot answer (Nov 15).
- **A new bank placeholder must be declared in the `slots` of its bank file** (`tests/english-parts-banks.test.ts` fails with "to include '<slot>'"); a new `requiresFacts` name only needs the packet to set it. A situation type that is only worth staging while its race is open takes `liveWhile: "contest-open"` in `situation-types.json`, read in `scheduleMoment`.
- **A scene the player's own act causes never stops the day unless its type says so.** A host's town hall is `selfNamed`, and the scheduler's interrupt formula counts a self-named moment only when the type carries `"stopsDay": true` (the flag `took-office` carries); without it the scene is a Journal line the player never answers (Roy, Montana, meet-a1:1, Nov 14). Prove the stop with a played test that is red without the flag.
- **An offer the player owns needs an end the engine reads, never a stranded record (Nov 15, Account lead).** A `stopsDay` scene stage cannot be skipped by resuming the stop: the day holds until the player's first reply (any reply marks the unit answered and clears the stop, even one with no ending, such as asking a question). Only the endings that settle (`declared`, `let-pass`) answer the offer. Leave the rest standing, meet the player again on a calendar row (a new record under the same cause, so the same type stages again) and let the window close as a public record with its consequence (nothing declared, no aid asked for). Window days and cadence are a cited row in the data file, never a number in code. A held bill stop in a played governor's days is cleared with `leaveHoldsToCharacter(core)`, or the clock never reaches the date the test waits for.
- **Cast only the people who speak.** A role that has no turns in the arc is not seated for show; file the pool item that adds it when the machinery can seat more than one speaker.
- **Count a repeat by its cause record, never by its cast (governor's briefing, Nov 15).** A "same cast, same type" count read the press-interview, speech-afterward and donor-ask repeats of a played year as repeats, and every one named a different bill, speech or donation: the new record is the change, and the scene stages. Before saying a scene repeats, read each pair's cause records (`core.durableLog.get(causeId)`, kind and facts) side by side. A season cap or day window that held none of them back in a played year is inert; measure it with the cap on and off at fixed places before deleting or keeping it.

## 10. Staging and asking (Nov 15)

- **An ask that waits for the player's answer must not re-emit.** A row that says "asked" for both unanswered and put-off re-asks every campaign turn; a put-off is its own row value with its date, and only it comes back.
- **Stage a struck kind through the game's own path.** moved: `api.relocatePeopleV1` a household with no jobs; died: `api.updatePerson(id, {alive:false})`; barred: justice `sentences` and `cases` rows as `law-reach-felony-voting.test.ts` writes them. Stage "short with names struck" by letting every other signer die the day before the deadline (the check runs the morning after, so a strike after that day is too late). A place whose law asks no petition has no scene by design: a stage-by-draw test must skip it, and a kind no engine can produce (under-age signers) is a pool item, not a line.

## 11. A scene that offers the player a choice the engine must not make (Nov 15, petition-short)

OWNER-DECISIONS Oct 14: "No engine accepts a job, a home, a filing or any choice for the controlled person; the engine offers, the player decides." So an engine that sees the player falling behind writes a record once, the director stages it as a scene (a stop the player answers), and the engine acts only on the player's own yes, read from the `scene.reply` record's `typeKey`, `causeId` and `ending` facts (`answerPetitionShort`). Never do the work for the player between pauses (own-choice answers a choice pause only, never a scene).

- **One cause per setting.** The teller's kind picks the place: two `causes` in one type, each with `causeFacts` `equals` the kind and its own `setting` (the clerk at the venue, a helper at home). A lead line's `settings` must match.
- **`receive.counterpart: "apart"` leaves out housemates.** A teller who lives in the player's house needs `"any"`; with "apart" the moment quietly becomes a journal line and no scene is staged.
- **Journal lines for an ending** are keyed by its `keeps` value (`scene-ending:<keeps>`), not its id; an ending with `keeps: "none"` writes no journal line, so give each ending the player should see its own `keeps` name.
- **Reach a helper by play, never by the test.** A helper who walks the doors would fill the petition first, so a helper-told scene needs one who joined late. Find it by playing days and saying yes to the game's own offers (`manager-offer`, `offer-help`: Sherrodsville seed scan-7, offer June 8, told Aug 3); the test never calls `enlistVolunteer` itself. A seed where nobody offers is a clerk world.
- **Each teller measures by its own count.** The clerk reads the count the office requires (`needed`); someone on the campaign reads what it aims to gather. A line that calls a petition thin must be true against the number the player can read.
- **A journal line with a slot must name something the journal can fill.** The journal bank drops an unnamed wording whenever a named one exists, so an unnamed fallback beside a `{other}` line never plays; give the fact (`actFacts`, from the cause record) or delete the fallback. A new journal line needs a slot (the line-inserts guard) and 10 words or fewer.
- **One scene, one lead; one office wording.** The arrival names who is there, so a lead that names them again ("Eric is waiting. Someone wants a word") is wrong; a teller who lives in the house is never "at the door". Say the office once, from the same fact in the lead, the talk and the Journal.
- **Narration for the narrate step** is its own bank piece (`step: narrate`, `role: narrator`) and a purpose without one is skipped ("no narration for <purpose> can be worded"), whatever the lead lines say.
