**Look first:** run the `look-first` search before building, fixing, filing or asking; a found ruling is built and cited, never asked again.

Any other builder lists the lines and moves it needs on its item's pull request and Story writes them; one writer per bank file. Where a line below says "write" or "add a piece", read it as the Story session's job, or as the instruction for what a builder must list. Every note the owner gives becomes a rule in `data/english/line-rules.json` that a program tests (`npm run words:rules`); a rule a program cannot judge is judged once by a reviewer and saved (`data/english/judged/`).

## The target: scenes like Wildermyth (owner, Oct 24)

Scenes "that come out of what is actually happening in the world, narrated into a moment, played out with real dialogue and choices, the outcome back into the game; the current opening is the right shape, the words are wrong." That is the measure of every piece of this line: a record fires it, the narrator sets it, people with purposes play it out with four real replies, and what the player chose goes back into the records.

## The one rule that explains everything

Words come from the English engine; every bank part plays unless the owner's grades hold it or one of his rules pulled it (`data/english/pulled.json`), hand-written (`origin: authored`) parts included (owner, Oct 24; this replaces the Oct 20 "held until graded" hold, which no longer applies). Words come from the English engine's banks (`data/english/parts/`) and control names. The banks are built at development time: pieces are written to calibrated patterns, drawing on any source for the patterns (books, other games, transcripts, the internet), and copying no one's copyrighted text. At play the engine arranges the pieces; the director decides which moment is happening and who is in it. Neither types a line. Public-domain records remain a fine source of pieces, not a requirement. The old "two documents, one public-domain" attestation rule was never the owner's and is withdrawn (OWNER-DECISIONS.md, "The text rule", Oct 15, evening). Research and real data calibrate how pieces are arranged; they never decide a person's outcome.

## The three layers, and where each lives

1. **Situations (the director, `src/director/`).** `src/director/data/situation-types.json` is the catalog: 103 situation types (counted Nov 10; `src/director/director.test.ts` holds the count) (`departure`, `celebration`, `news-arrives`, `first-meeting`, `visit`, ...), each with roles (who fills it: `subject`, `counterpart`, `household`, `kin`, `present`, `classmates`, `coworkers`, `absent`, `authority`), the **moves** each role may make (`farewell`, `promise`, `comfort`, `tell`, `request`, `blame`, ...), what each role **wants**, a **bearing** (tender, solemn, ...), a setting, a timing, and **causes**: the record labels that fire it (a `money:job-started` record, a death, an eviction). A cause whose producer the core does not have yet names it in `awaitingProducer` and matches nothing. No type holds a sentence. The director (`src/director/scene.ts`, `scheduling.ts`, `ledger.ts`) watches the player's circle, fires a situation when a record matches a cause, stages it (home, work, town, the council room), and runs it as moves. `conversation.ts` runs talks: opens from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person in the ledger, keeps to that topic, chains to one related topic, closes when done; up to four replies ranked by the player's traits, lies as the same moves made false, "say nothing" beside them (`src/director/data/conversation-rules.json`). `journal.ts` writes the first-person "My life" memoir from records. `opening-story.ts` is the opening.
2. **Moves (the grammar).** A move is one thing a person does with a line: greet, deliver news, ask, answer, condole, congratulate, deflect, lie, agree, close, farewell, comfort, request, blame. Moves are situation-blind: a condolence serves a funeral, an assassination and a lost election. The director picks the next move from the situation's arc and the speaker's traits and relationship; the engine fills it. The move names in the catalog and the `kind`/`stage` names in the banks must agree; that mapping is the seam between director and engine and is the first thing to check when a line comes out wrong.
3. **Pieces (the banks, `data/english/parts/*.json`).** One bank per register (`ls data/english/parts` is the list; 21 files on Nov 10): `conversation.json`, `scene-talk.json`, `scene-words.json`, `journal.json`, `person-history.json`, `person-sketch.json`, `address.json` (floor, hearing, public comment, campaign, victory, concession), `opening-speech.json`, `speech-stages.json`, `minutes.json`, `legislation.json`, `bill-paper.json`, `notice.json`, `hearing.json`, `newscast.json`, `news.json`, `headlines.json`, `calendar.json`, `campaign-standing.json`, `bring-it-up.json`, `choice-names.json`. Each part is a sentence with slots, its move (`kind`, `stage`), its register, the record facts it needs (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the fact and is said only when the record holds it), and optionally a source note (document, date, address, license) or `origin: authored` with author and date. `src/english/english-composition.ts` (`composeAddress`, `composeFromBank`), `grounded-english.ts` and `speech-registers.ts` compose; `calibration.ts` and `move-fit.ts` weigh pieces by the speaker's traits, the register's length and the move before, never by a grade of the line; `english-grades.ts` applies the owner's grades (a failed part is never used; a conditional one only where its condition holds); `bank-parts.ts` (`playable`) is the one gate, grades and pulled list together; the runtime-text guard (`scripts/runtime-text/guard.ts`, baseline `data/runtime-text/baseline.json`) fails new fixed text on one fixed life's golden path; it reads one life, not every screen, so also check by hand with `player-screen-text`.

## The owner's standing rulings on this line

- **Grades and rules change what plays, and no one is asked to approve lines one by one** (owner, Oct 24: "Quit worrying about my grades ... Get the English engine in the game. That should make every single piece of dialogue available. If I find pieces I don't like or I keep grading, you can keep updating it, and that will keep tuning the engine."). Every piece in every bank plays in ordinary play, scenes included, unless a grade holds it or a rule pulled it; a grade run and a rules run reach the whole game at once. Nothing waits for the owner to grade it, and nothing asks him to approve lines one by one.
- Scenes are the narrative: consequential dialogue happens in scenes; the day screen carries dealing. Day advances time; a scene stops it. A quiet day fires nothing.
- A talk starts from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person; one topic with side remarks, chaining to one related topic; news only as received; no cold "How is X?" about someone absent unless something recorded happened to X; the other person closes it; the listener remembers the last talk; the old subject loop (every known person in id order) is gone.
- Up to four replies, the scene sets the count, ranked by the player's traits; a short set (one, two or three) is allowed and is not a gap in the banks (owner, Nov 10, 2026: "it's also allowed to have less than 4 options sometimes... you might only have 1 option, but it's to make the player feel engaged. Or you may only have 2 options in the scene because you have to make a choice, a yes or no choice... So just up to 4.") It replaces "Four dynamic choices, always" (Oct 14). Every reply offered is a different answer, never a rewording of another, and each moves the talk somewhere; lies are the same moves made false about the player's own record; "say nothing" is the smaller control beside them. The player's choices are the player's: no auto-hire, no auto-vote.
- The narrator is settled: second person in scenes, first person in the memoir, grounded in records; the journal frames a childhood loss by age; school and work are chapters, not entries.
- A family scene is staged in the player's household from that household's records (a lost job, an eviction, a divorce); the people in the room show in the picture and the talk, never as a roll call of names.
- Knowing is a read: a person cites a law, a vote or an act only through a record of how they learned of it (news received, a talk, a notice), and the stance cites that source.
- No feeling words unless a record carries the feeling; grief has a cause record.
- **Scene PRs collide on four shared files; land on fresh main.** Every scene edits `data/english/parts/scene-talk.json`, `src/director/data/situation-types.json`, `src/director/purpose-scene.ts` and the situation-type count in `src/director/director.test.ts`, and mergers never hand-merge a bank. Right before marking ready, `git fetch origin main`, merge it, re-add your pieces by piece key and your count bump on main's files, and push within minutes; a PR that is stacked or held waits for its base to merge, then redoes this. Oct 27: #6201, #6296, #6298 (twice), #6303 and #6309 were each sent back for this one conflict.

- **Every line carries an insert (owner, Nov 11, 2026: "all the lines in the game need to be run through the English engine... a ton of different speech versions, a ton of different inserts").** No whole stock sentence is picked as it stands: a line names at least one slot in braces that the engine fills from the world's records, and a scene is composed from many versions. The opening comes first: the President's address and the evening after it.
- **What may stay fixed is his ruling, and only that (owner, Nov 11, call 12: "Replies of one to three words and action names stay; everything else is rewritten.").** Three kinds are excused, by rule: a line of one to three words ("Hello.", "You won."); the name of an action the player picks (the `choice-names` bank); and a piece the engine joins, when the sentence it makes carries an insert. Nobody widens that without a new ruling. **The Nov 14 excuse for a line "the world places" is withdrawn (owner, Nov 16, on the Story thread #9750; asked whether to keep excusing the lines whose every use carries a fact, a caused purpose or a record-raised subject: "No. Count them again and rewrite them with inserts", with the note "either that or remove them and start fresh").** Those 5,339 lines (Nov 16: scene-talk 5,136, conversation 171, opening-speech 13) are back in the count and are rewritten with an insert from the record, most-heard scene first, or deleted; a fact in `requiresFacts` or `onlyWhen` places a line but does not excuse it.
- **How a joined piece says so.** Give the part a `piece` field: `of` (the sentence it belongs to, one name for every piece of that sentence), `part` (which part it is: `opener`, `core`, `reason`, `closer` or the address's own move), and `required: true` when every such sentence has that part. The pieces of a sentence are excused when one of its required parts names a slot in every wording, because then no joined sentence can lack one. So put the insert in the part that is always there (the core), and the openers and closers around it may be plain.
- **The guard.** `tests/guards/english/line-inserts.test.ts` counts, in each bank, the lines with no slot that are not excused, against `tests/guards/english/line-inserts-baseline.json` (7,613 on Nov 11, after 605 excused; 5,580 on Nov 16, when the placed lines came back into the count). A count may only go down: a new line of four words or more with no slot fails by bank, and when you rewrite or delete such lines you lower the bank's count in the same change.

- **Voice notes on a batch (Nov 9).** The owner grades by voice, not on a page (Nov 9, 8:20 a.m.: "It's all done in the voice notes again look how I did the previous ones"). His notes never go into a PR or onto main (Nov 9, 9:00 a.m.: "I don't know why like my notes and stuff need to be merged into main"): the CTO keeps each on its own branch, so read it at run time with `git show <cto-branch>:<note.md> > /tmp/note.md`, run `node --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/voice-note-reader.ts docs/coordination/batches/<batch>.json /tmp/note.md > data/english/grades/<batch>-voice.json`, then `apply-grades.ts`; only the grade file and the lines ship, and a test uses a fixture written in our own words. The reader follows how he talks: a line read aloud (no quote marks) then his judgment; "five point two", "scene four, the second one"; a part read back then judged; "rating that a three" (three: right sentence, wrong context); rules said in passing land in its `rules` list, which become entries in `line-rules.json`. **The click-through page's batch (`/mnt/project-files/words-lead/click-through/batch-latest.json`, Nov 14):** a moment is a scene (`description` is its title) and a line's id is `scene.wording.line`, or `scene.wordingLETTER.line` for a door (y agrees, m holds back, n declines); the reader matches written or spoken ids ("4.1m.5", "four point one m point five"), "scene four, wording one" with "the second one" inside it, and a title said in part ("the sick door", only when one scene fits). A line that is the same piece in several wordings is one grade row. A sentence that opens "Never say" or "Don't say" is a rule even when it quotes the line. Read the output against his note before folding it: a sentence of his that reads an unlisted line ends the line he was on, and only his first three judgments after a reading count.
- **Traps that return no line without an error (Nov 9).** A news or headline line is only said when its record has the outcome its words name: "won", "elected" need a `won` fact, "lost", "loses", "conceded" need `lost`, "passed", "approved" need `passed`, "rejected", "rejects", "failed", "vetoed" need `failed` (`english-newscast.ts`, `variants`), so "Senate rejects the nominee" never renders for a nominee and the story prints with no headline; say "turned down" or "voted down". A slot followed by a full stop counts the full stop as a word against the register's length (tests/english-parts-banks.test.ts strips the slot, not its punctuation): "I joined your ticket because you were for {about}. You moved." counts 11 in a 10-word bank. Count every line a lead sends by that rule before adding it, and send back every one that is over in one message. `maxWords` is the register's design; shorten the line, never raise the number.

## Lessons of Nov 16 and 17 (the Story lead's first night)

- **The conversation engine reads `requiresFacts` only; `onlyWhen` is scene talk's.** A stance gate written as `onlyWhen` on a `conversation.json` reply is ignored (`src/english/conversation-words.ts` never reads it): "Can't tell yet either" placed `onlyWhen: ["stand-unsure"]` played for a housemate who had just said "Count me out." In the conversation bank every fact a reply needs goes in `requiresFacts`; the rank it adds is the same for every sibling of the spot.
- **The padded-name check reads only the first-name slots.** `VOCATIVE` in `tests/guards/english/line-inserts.test.ts` matches `{first}`, `{their-first}` and `{player-first}`, so a line ending in `, {name}.` or `, {you}.` passes it. On Nov 17 the conversation bank held 9 agree answers ending in `{name}` and 121 lines ending in `{you}` (14 thanks or greetings that may keep it, 25 that keep another insert when the name comes off, 82 that then need a record insert written). Count them with a python pass over the bank before widening the regex; widening it raises `vocativeNames`, which needs the lines rewritten in the same PR.
- **The owner's Nov 14 excuse for a "placed" line is withdrawn (Nov 16: "No. Count them again and rewrite them with inserts").** The insert guard counts them again (5,580 on Nov 17); a rewrite deletes the fixed line or gives it a record insert, most-heard scene first. Measure "most heard" with `scripts/dialogue-batch/scene-year.ts` over several seeds, not by bank size: eleven played years as a new adult staged only 12 scene purposes and 118 purposes never staged, so a purpose a candidate or an officeholder meets is measured with `cap-year.ts` and its personas.
- **Proving a bank line in a composed talk:** `scripts/words/opening-talk-trace.ts` with `SEEDS=` plays the first evening and prints each talk's lines, replies and pieces. About one seed in two stages the address talk at home, and one reply wording plays per talk, so a spot with four stance combinations needs eight to twelve seeds; run four at a time with `--max-old-space-size=4096` when other helpers run (a 12-seed run was killed for memory). A line unplayed after that is deleted with its text kept in the PR body.
- **The Scenes page is fed from the engine.** `scripts/words/scene-samples.ts --seed <word> --days 240 --as resident|legislator|mayor|governor` writes one playable file per staged scene type under `docs/scenes/samples/` (new) (the opening, the ask, every reply with what it leads to, each line's tied wordings, the endings, the place, seed and record ids) and an INDEX.json; the CTO publishes them. A graded sample comes out of the next batch (owner, Nov 16: "remove those 14 and update or replace those 80, have them all be different"). Never write a sample by hand.
- **A branch from another account's thread is yours once the thread is gone.** The Words lead's played files live under `/mnt/project-files`, which a cloud session cannot reach; a read remakes the played file from a run at the head (a script in the helper's scratchpad, never committed) and the stamp rests on that run. Played, it outweighed every view, story and piece of family news, so every talk at home was about the day for months. A subject with nothing but the clock behind it ranks below every subject that has a record, and is not raised again with the same person that week while anything else is due.
- **Words know the hour and the person.** "Busy one at work today?" at 7:00 AM, to someone with no job, answered with "I'm beat. Just glad to be home." A line about a day is written for a part of the day and for whose day it was, and both come from records (the shift's own hours, the school day), never from the line's author assuming an evening.
- **Two people in one room do not say the same sentence.** On the morning after the address two parents gave the same second sentence word for word. Within one household on one day, the second speaker takes another point or another reason, and never a wording the player has already heard that day.
- **In a list of people, one sentence per person.** A paragraph that tells a family's work by date tells each person two or three times and names them last. Tell it person by person: who they are and their name, what they do now, where and since when; a job they left only inside that sentence.
- **`openConversation` is also the room's probe.** The bench asks it for everyone in a room to learn who has something to say (`sceneNow`, "read without writing anything"). Anything written inside it happens for every person in every room the player walks into. A write that belongs to a talk the player really opened (meeting someone, time spent) goes in the talk route, after the other person has a line (Nov 11: the first build of meeting people in a talk sat inside it and was sent back).

## The composer (owner, Nov 12: "change the engine", never one sentence at a time)

The design is `docs/design/words-composer.md`; read it before any words work. What it means for a session:

- **A complaint about a line is a complaint about a shared part.** Before touching a bank line, name which of the six parts let it through (the telling, said-as, the outline, the join, stance, the composed checks) and fix it there, so the fix holds for every speech, talk, scene, Journal page and news story. A pull request that rewords lines for one scene and changes no shared part is sent back (owner, Nov 12: "You're focusing too much on the president's speech when the problem is the engine").
- **No bank line holds a title, a full name or an employer's name as words.** A person, office, employer, bill or place is a slot, and the one said-as reader words it from what the text has already said.
- **An `{issue}` or `{said}` short name is a subject, never a side.** A line never puts a direction on it ("hold the line on {issue}", "give ground on {issue}"), because the short name of a question can read either way; the side is said through the speaker ("with me on {issue}", "stand where I stand on {issue}") (Words lead, Nov 15).
- **A composer is handed the telling or it does not compose:** the speaker and listener and their tie, who is in the room, the hour, what the listener knows, and what was said on this screen, in this household today and to this person this week. On Nov 12 the room's people were never passed to a talk and the hour was read only for the goodbye.
- **A skill line is not a fix.** "Small talk loses to anything with a record" and "words know the hour" were written here on Nov 11 and never built, and the owner met both the next night. A lesson goes in with the code or with a pool item that names the function.
- **A note about composed text becomes a composed rule,** read over whole texts in drawn worlds, with a count that only falls. Until its check exists, the rule's row says what it awaits; it is never marked done by rewording the lines he quoted.
- **Pre-grade before he sees it.** A session that did not build the change plays a new life, reads the screens, and grades at least a speech that is not the President's, the talk at home and the Journal as he would from his past notes. Only what survives goes to him.

## The composed checks (unit 5 of the composer, Nov 12)

A composed rule reads whole texts a player met, never one bank line: its row's test is `{ "kind": "composed", "check": [names], "applies": [kinds] }`, status `sample` (it reports, it pulls nothing), and `ruleFlags` returns false for it.

- **Run them:** `npm run words:composed -- --seed <seed> [--place-key <key>] [--draws N] [--days 14] [--report <file outside the repository>]`. Each draw plays a new life through the bench's own routes (`benchRequest`: the opening address and the evening after it, everyone at home who has something to say each day, Day pressed and every stop and staged scene played) and keeps every text as a `ComposedText` (`src/english/composed-text.ts`): speeches by level, talk at home and in town, scenes, narration, the Journal's first page and notes, news toasts and stories. A governor's or mayor's address the window misses is composed through `recordLocalAddress` at the end and marked `proof`. It prints per rule the texts read, breaks and three examples with place, seed and kind. A 14-day life takes under a minute; three draws in one process stay under 2 GB.
- **The count only falls.** `tests/guards/english/composed-baseline.json` keeps breaks per 100 texts per rule on recorded draws. `npm run words:composed -- --check` replays those draws and exits 1 naming any rule whose rate rose; the run is deterministic on a seed, so a rise is your change. After a change that lowers a count, write it again on the same draws with `--write-baseline` and the same `--seed` flags.
- **A words pull request quotes its before and after counts** from `--check` (or the table) for every composed rule its change touches, with the draws.
- **Add a check:** a pure function `(text, sample) => breaks` in `src/english/composed-checks.ts`, registered in `COMPOSED_CHECKS`; its numbers and word lists go in `data/english/composed-checks.json` (each number TUNABLE with a spread); name it in the rule's `test.check`; give it a catch and a spare in our own words in `tests/guards/english/composed-rules.test.ts`, which fails until every registered check is used by a rule and has fixtures. A check that cannot be sound without the telling or typed pieces is not guessed: leave that part named in the rule's `note`.
- **The talk's screen is one step of the talk box:** the other person's lines from one request plus the replies offered. A full name in the line and again in a reply counts twice on that screen, as the owner reads it.
- **A line is never changed only to pass a guard**, the unique-text check included. A duplicate text means delete your copy and use the existing piece. A builder never judges pairs on its own lines: the sheet goes to the planted review (`npm run words:review` once #9221 folds; `trusted-review-pass`).

## Outlets, talks and staging a line (builder M3, Nov 15)

- **An outlet is said as its record has it.** Read the outlet's kind with `outletMedium` (src/core2/modules/press.ts: paper, television, radio, null for online) and its name and article from `outletTelling`; `outletFacts` in src/director/conversation.ts sets `outlet`, `outlet-paper`, `outlet-television`, `outlet-radio`, `outlet-online` and `outlet-the` for every line. Never title-case a name by its shape ("WEST" is a word, "KKFX" is call letters): say it as the record has it. A line that names an outlet gates itself on those facts (paper is read in, television is seen on, radio is heard on).
- **`answers` are the player's lines.** In a conversation the part named `answers` is what the player can say back to the other person's last line (`offerChoices` in src/director/conversation.ts); `ask` is the other person raising a subject; `raise` is whoever opens one. A line with `role: answer` is never the neighbor's. Read which side speaks it before you stage it.
- **A line is reached only if a talk offers it.** Before you write or gate a line, open a talk the game itself opens (stage the cause through the engine's own route, such as `printStory(api, story, readers, atOnce)` with `kind: "press.printed"`, `topic` and `outletId`, then `openConversation` and `say` in a probe at a named place and seed that you do not commit) and read the "You say:" choices. A line no talk offers does not fold. Probe the wording engine directly with `wordConversationMove` and the subject's facts: a null line means the facts cannot fill the slots (a printed story carries `about`, a closed-place story carries `topic`).
- **A test never scans.** A loop over many seeds or people is a scan, not play. A test draws its place and waits for the case (`drawn-place-tests`), and names the place and seed that gave each line; pinning them in the file needs a CTO line on that pull request (`done-proof`).
- **Deleting a bank piece:** remove it from the part file, then run `npm run words:rules` and `npm run words:verdicts` (about five minutes; run it in the background) so the judged rows and yes lists lose its key; changing a piece's text and keeping its key is fine. Check the arc's replies and the situation type's `moves` too: a reply move with no piece left is a dead branch the player sees as a choice that ends the scene.
- **A frame that puts the question after "is" or "than" excludes `about-named`.** `questionOnce` says a question already named in the talk as "it", so "What matters to me is {about}." read "What matters to me is it." Give such a piece `excludesFacts: ["about-named"]`; do not reword it, and check every frame of that shape in the same move.
- **A row that says how it came out places no line that waits.** A story's row sets `story-event` and `story-decided` (`storyEvent` in conversation.ts, read from the row's own facts: a vote's yes and no, a floor failure, a law's id). Lines that look forward ("We'll see who's right", "once {decider} votes") exclude `story-decided`, and a fallback filler line is not written where no take fits.
- **A staged talk is staged on the opening day.** From the Train on, a played world holds natural printed stories within days, and the neighbor raises one of them; a test that stages its own story advances one day, not weeks, so the story it staged is the one raised. Name the place by its record id in the test and assert it.
- **Each new line is printed in the played file before the reader sees it.** Check three things for every new line: it is printed, the line after it follows from it, and it agrees with the story's row. A line that cannot be shown played comes out of the bank in the same PR.

## A line that never plays: log the pick before you delete it (Nov 15)

When a line fits as well as another and still never plays, log the weights and the draw in `renderGroundedEnglish` before calling it unreachable. On Nov 15 two cause-invite lines had weight 1 each and the draw was 1 for all 23 askers: `stableHash` is FNV-1a, whose lowest bit is the parity of how many characters in the key have an odd code, and a moment key that holds the same two ids twice cancels them. Any `stableHash(...) % n` with even `n` is biased this way (item words-line-pick-hash-low-bit-is-character-parity; M4's pick PR fixes it). A line held out for this comes back once the pick is fixed.

Before adding `requiresFacts` or `excludesFacts` to a bank line, diff the open words pull requests' scene-talk.json against the train head, so two pull requests never place one line two ways.

Set `knows-truth` from a record (every press exposure ran on the paper trail); never write a second catch mechanism.

- **A text is one part, case-insensitively.** If the line you need already exists in another scene ("Who told you that?"), add a use to that part; a second part with the same words fails the bank schema test.

Cash set on the player, an offer or a filing record put in by hand makes the file unstampable. The header of the file says what was done, and "staged" is never the word for it.

## Lessons of Nov 17 (the reporter's call, #9794)

- **Play the tree after the pairs are judged; the review reads pairs, not paths.** At Riverdale, North Dakota and Enterprise, Oklahoma the thank-you close followed a refusal, "runs without your side" followed an answer, and a believed lie closed on "you declined to talk": every pair read fine alone. A closing or follow line that depends on what the player did on the path needs such a fact; `after` alone reads only the last move.
- **A scene that waits for a rare record is proved through dev mode's own route.** 240 played days as a legislator staged no reporter's call; `DevMode.decideAt` (Hold the vote) on a colleague's bill, found by the seat's body office id from `seatView`, staged it the next day at the first drawn place.
