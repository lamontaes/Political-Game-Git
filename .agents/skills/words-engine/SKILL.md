# The words engine and the director: the handoff

Any session, on any model, can take this line over from this file. Read `AGENTS.md` first. Then this file. Then `docs/coordination/words.md` (the Words session's status and log, newest first) and `docs/coordination/OWNER-DECISIONS.md` (search "Oct 14 night playthrough", "Scenes carry the consequential dialogue", "batches grade content").

## The one rule that explains everything

No hand-written player text in screens or code. Words come from the English engine's banks (`data/english/parts/`) and control names. The banks are built at development time: pieces are written to calibrated patterns, drawing on any source for the patterns (books, other games, transcripts, the internet), and copying no one's copyrighted text. At play the engine arranges the pieces; the director decides which moment is happening and who is in it. Neither types a line. Public-domain records remain a fine source of pieces, not a requirement. The old "two documents, one public-domain" attestation rule was never the owner's and is withdrawn (OWNER-DECISIONS.md, "The text rule", Oct 15, evening). Research and real data calibrate how pieces are arranged; they never decide a person's outcome.

## The three layers, and where each lives

1. **Situations (the director, `src/director/`).** `src/director/data/situation-types.json` is the catalog: 34 situation types (`departure`, `celebration`, `news-arrives`, `first-meeting`, `visit`, ...), each with roles (who fills it: `subject`, `counterpart`, `household`, `kin`, `present`, `classmates`, `coworkers`, `absent`, `authority`), the **moves** each role may make (`farewell`, `promise`, `comfort`, `tell`, `request`, `blame`, ...), what each role **wants**, a **bearing** (tender, solemn, ...), a setting, a timing, and **causes**: the record labels that fire it (a `money:job-started` record, a death, an eviction). A cause whose producer the core does not have yet names it in `awaitingProducer` and matches nothing. No type holds a sentence. The director (`src/director/scene.ts`, `scheduling.ts`, `ledger.ts`) watches the player's circle, fires a situation when a record matches a cause, stages it (home, work, town, the council room), and runs it as moves. `conversation.ts` runs talks: opens from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person in the ledger, keeps to that topic, chains to one related topic, closes when done; four replies ranked by the player's traits, lies as the same moves made false, "say nothing" beside them (`src/director/data/conversation-rules.json`). `journal.ts` writes the first-person "My life" memoir from records. `office-scene.ts` is the office scene with a goal against one person (win one member's vote), whose outcome the engines carry through that person's traits and relationship, never a roll. `opening-story.ts` is the opening.
2. **Moves (the grammar).** A move is one thing a person does with a line: greet, deliver news, ask, answer, condole, congratulate, deflect, lie, agree, close, farewell, comfort, request, blame. Moves are situation-blind: a condolence serves a funeral, an assassination and a lost election. The director picks the next move from the situation's arc and the speaker's traits and relationship; the engine fills it. The move names in the catalog and the `kind`/`stage` names in the banks must agree; that mapping is the seam between director and engine and is the first thing to check when a line comes out wrong.
3. **Pieces (the banks, `data/english/parts/*.json`).** One bank per register: `conversation.json`, `journal.json`, `address.json` (floor, hearing, public comment, campaign, victory, concession), `interview.json`, `minutes.json`, `legislation.json`, `notices.json`, `meeting.json`, `hearing.json`, `newscast.json`, `news.json`, `headlines.json`, `newspaper-ledes.json`, `opening-speech.json`, `counter.json`, `filing-visit.json`, `scene-words.json`, `bring-it-up.json`, `choice-names.json`, `winning-losing.json`. Each part is a sentence with slots, its move (`kind`, `stage`), its register, the record facts it needs (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the fact and is said only when the record holds it), and optionally a source note (document, date, address, license) or `origin: authored` with author and date. `src/english/english-composition.ts` (`composeAddress`, `composeFromBank`), `grounded-english.ts` and `speech-registers.ts` compose; `english-calibration.ts` and `move-fit.ts` weigh pieces; `english-grades.ts` applies the owner's grades (a failed part is never used; a conditional one only where its condition holds); `runtime-text-origin.ts` is the guard that every shipped string has a bank origin.

## How a bank is made (authoring to patterns)

Full history of the existing mined banks: `docs/research/english-corpus/README.md`. The method now:

- **Authoring.** The `civic-prose` skill is the process. A piece is drafted to a move, register, key and slots; reviewed for grounding (a sentence that claims something about the speaker, "I'm a nurse.", names the record fact it needs and is said only when the record holds it) and terminology; then added to the bank with its move tags. Mark new parts `origin: authored` with the author and date, and `shippable: false` until the owner grades them; an ungraded authored part never ships, and a merger sends back one marked shippable.
- **Patterns.** Counts and shapes come from any source: English books, other games, transcripts, council minutes, news, the internet (`scripts/english-corpus/shapes/<bank>.json`). Copy no one's copyrighted text; short ordinary sentences are fine. No real politician's slogan.
- **Mining.** Where a public-domain record (federal works, 17 U.S.C. 105; congressional hearings; agency releases) offers good pieces, mine them with `scripts/english-corpus/mine-shapes.py`. The `source` fields on a part are optional notes.
- **Tests.** `<bank>-bank.test.ts` composes a whole exchange through the real engine in a generated place (every record id resolving, no feeling words, both chairs where a chair exists). Add a bank by adding a shape or an authored set, a script and its test.
- **What "calibration" means.** Research sets how pieces are arranged and how often: the length of a public comment, how a concession opens and closes, which moves follow which. It never picks which line a particular person says: traits, relationship, the record and the owner's grades do.

## Grading batches: the loop with the owner

- **The batch is the grading bench** (`scripts/dialogue-batch/grading-bench.ts`, Order 2 of `docs/work-orders/words-and-director-2026-10-15.md`), and grades feed `data/english/calibration.json`, which the engine reads.
- A batch is a Markdown page, `docs/grading/words-batch-N.md`, built by `npm run dialogue:coverage -- --id batch-N --seed batch-N --days 365` (`scripts/dialogue-batch/coverage-batch.ts`), from one played place (drawn by `drawPlace`, never chosen; draws go on until a year fires what the batch needs), built as the bench builds it (`buildBenchWorld`, two-level), watched by the director for a year. Every scene is one the engine composed, with how it was made (the record id that fired it, the function, the bank), and tests one thing: the wording of a bold line on one axis (formality, word choice, length) with alternatives; an opening and closing pair; or a conversation's choice set (four replies, the lies, say nothing). A phrase appears once in a batch. The batch JSON is `data/english/batches/batch-N.json`; the owner's grades go to `data/english/grades/batch-N.json` (`{"i","grade","prefer"}` or `{"i","choices":{...}}`) and `node --import tsx scripts/dialogue-batch/apply-grades.ts` folds them into the part ledger so a failed part never ships and a preferred alternative is promoted.
- The CTO screens every batch before the owner sees it and holds it when a scene cannot answer **why** by record (a raw id in a line, a body named by a member's title, a line repeated, a relative asked after with no recorded cause, a scene staged in the wrong household). A held batch names the engine defect; fix at the root and regenerate in a fresh place.
- **A batch grades content and situations, not only wording** (owner, Oct 15). Thirty different situations with their context. Proof scenes are welcome: a situation the director does not yet fire may be composed from real records in a played world and sent, marked as a proof, so the owner grades what the game can say and about what. Every batch also adds pieces to the engine (situation types, occasions, banks).
- The grading page the owner uses is built by the CTO as an artifact from the Markdown; the session only ships the Markdown and the JSON.

## The owner's standing rulings on this line

- Scenes are the narrative: consequential dialogue happens in scenes; the day screen carries dealing. Day advances time; a scene stops it. A quiet day fires nothing.
- A talk starts from the place and occasion, then the biggest recorded event of either person; one topic with side remarks, chaining to one related topic; news only as received; no cold "How is X?" about someone absent unless something recorded happened to X; the other person closes it; the listener remembers the last talk; the old subject loop (every known person in id order) is gone.
- Four replies always, ranked by the player's traits; lies are the same moves made false about the player's own record; "say nothing" is the smaller control beside them. The player's choices are the player's: no auto-hire, no auto-vote.
- The narrator is settled: second person in scenes, first person in the memoir, grounded in records; the journal frames a childhood loss by age; school and work are chapters, not entries.
- A family scene is staged in the player's household from that household's records (a lost job, an eviction, a divorce); the people in the room show in the picture and the talk, never as a roll call of names.
- Knowing is a read: a person cites a law, a vote or an act only through a record of how they learned of it (news received, a talk, a notice), and the stance cites that source.
- No feeling words unless a record carries the feeling; grief has a cause record.

## The checks a Words session runs

In-session, on changed files only: typecheck, eslint, prettier, the bank's own test (`scripts/english-corpus/<bank>-bank.test.ts`), the director's tests for changed files (`src/director/*.test.ts`), `npm run spelling` (American English; `tests/american-english-sweep.test.ts`), `npm run zero-dice`, `release:check --mode pr` with a declaration in `docs/release/changes/`. The played-world proof is the batch itself: a year in a drawn place with the record ids that fired each scene. One line at the top of the PR body with the result; the first lines of the PR body say what the owner can play now.

## Boundaries

- This line owns `src/director/`, `src/english/`, `data/english/`, `scripts/english-corpus/`, `scripts/dialogue-batch/`, `docs/grading/`, `docs/coordination/words.md`. It does not edit the engines (`src/core2/modules/*`): when a record is missing or wrong (a winner that is a seat-term id, a bill with no title), it writes the defect under **Needs from other sessions** in `words.md` and at the top of the PR body, builds against the smallest registered stand-in, and keeps going.
- Where a design is the owner's call and he has not ruled, build the simplest version that follows `AGENTS.md`, register an `owner-decision` stopgap, and write the question under **OWNER DECISIONS NEEDED** at the top of the PR body and in `OWNER-DECISIONS.md`.
