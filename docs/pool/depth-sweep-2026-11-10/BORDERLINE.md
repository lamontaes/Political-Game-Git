# The depth sweep: the few things you might want back

For the owner. Nov 10, 2026. From Fable B.

Every live pool item got your one test: where does the player see or feel this? Most answers were clear. The sweepers set 61 items aside as borderline. I read all of them and settled most myself. Four are yours, because they are about what you play. Each has a default, so no answer is needed for the work to go on.

## Four questions

### 1. A recount after a close race

Today a close race is counted once and certified. The loser can say he will not concede yet, and nothing comes of it.

- **Leave it out of early access (my pick).** A close race is certified as counted. No work. No cost in speed.
- **A thin recount.** When a race ends inside the state's recount margin, the count is run again, certification waits, and election night and the paper say so. About 14 hours: 8 for an Opus builder and about 6 of research for the recount law of all 56 places. No cost in speed: it runs only after a close race.
- **The full version.** A recount and a challenge the loser's side can file. It needs the courts, which come after early access. More than 20 hours.

Why I lean to leaving it out: nothing else waits on it, so it is cheap to add later, and you still cannot play the path end to end.

### 2. Laws you can pass that change nobody's life yet

Four laws are on the bill menu and reach no one, because the thing they act on is not in the game.

| Law                                           | What is missing                                       | Work                     |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Overtime pay                                  | Overtime and salaried pay in a paycheck               | 8 hours, Opus            |
| Universal preschool                           | A family's child-care bill, which the law would cut   | 8 hours, Opus            |
| A ban on discrimination in hiring and renting | A hire or a rental that records why it picked someone | 26 hours over four items |
| A town's own sanctuary policy                 | Each state's statute on it, read for about 17 states  | 8 hours, Sonnet          |

- **Build overtime and preschool only (my pick).** 16 hours. Both change a paycheck or a family's monthly bill that you can open. The other two stay passable and their bill cards say they reached no one. Speed: child care adds one payment a month for each family with a young child; overtime adds nothing each day.
- **Build all four.** 50 hours. The discrimination ban is the only one with a real cost in speed: every hire records its reasons.
- **Build none.** No work. All four stay passable and reach no one.

### 3. Town life: the fair, the parade, homecoming

A played town has no community events. The paper and the Journal have no ordinary town life to tell, and a candidate has no parade to walk in.

- **Leave it out of early access (my pick).** No work.
- **A thin version.** Three or four dated events a year in each town (a fair, the Fourth of July parade, homecoming). The paper mentions them and a candidate can campaign at one. About 8 hours, Sonnet. No real cost in speed: a few records a year for each town.
- **The full version.** Every kind of event, sized by the town, with who attends. 24 hours over four parts.

### 4. People's 14 values

The design gives every adult 14 values (limited government, personal liberty, public safety and so on), each with its reasons. Today people hold stances on issues, formed from their records, and no screen or talk reads values.

- **Leave it out (my pick).** People keep their stances as they are. No work.
- **Build the values and show them.** A person's card says what they value and why, and talks read it. About 16 hours: 8 to form the values and about 8 for the card and the talks. Cost in speed: paid once, when the world is made.
