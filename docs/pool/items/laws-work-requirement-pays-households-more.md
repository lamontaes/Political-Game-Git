**What.** A work requirement for assistance is meant to limit who draws a benefit, and in the game it pays households more. Measured in Marion, Mississippi (seed 7df73e53cd67): a county work requirement for assistance, passed with no rider and compared with a twin world with no bill, left households $94,000 higher. A further twin run traced the money to its payer: the United States treasury, $134,000 lower. The state version did the same. The sweep ran 120 days for local bodies and 180 for the state. In LaGrange, Wyoming (seed 63355aee286d) the county work requirement moved almost nothing, so the wrong-way result is not everywhere. The audit traced the result to the treasury that pays and no further; the code behind it is not traced. Find why turning the requirement on raises what the treasury pays out, and fix it at the cause for every assistance program the requirement touches, so that while it is law the adults it reaches draw only for its time limit and nobody draws more than they did without it. Seen: a household's income, $94,000 higher where a work requirement should limit it.

**Cause.** Found in code: `reconcileObligations` in src/core2/law-reach/group-b-money.ts (line 366). When a recipient's monthly amount changes, it ends the old standing payment and opens a new one due tomorrow (`first`, line 392; `firstDueAt: first`, line 427), whatever day the old one was next due. The treasury's monthly close runs food aid on the 1st, so every food aid payment normally falls due on the 2nd. The work requirement's change-day rerun (the `assistance-time-limit` case in src/core2/law-reach/group-b.ts, line 526, calling `payFoodAid`, line 970) runs food aid again on the law's effective date, in the middle of a month. The counted households' amount has drifted since the 1st, so their payment is reopened and paid the next day: a second payment that month. Measured: Marion, Mississippi (bench seed 7df73e53cd67, place key 2845160), county bill enacted with no added section, law row `law:county:28075:county:us-policy-positions:health-human-services.work-requirement-for-assistance:general:2025-02-19:2025-1`, effective 2025-02-19. In both worlds the federal treasury paid obligation `reach-b:benefit:snap-maximum:US|residents:jurisdiction_7fa9e484fb9a8bc7:2025-02-01` on 2025-02-02. In the law world it was reopened on 2025-02-19 at $113,002 and paid again on 2025-02-20, so its due day moved to the 20th. That obligation paid on 2025-03-20. Then the monthly close of 2025-04-01 reopened it at a new amount and it paid again on 2025-04-02. The law world made six payments to the counted households between 2025-01-21 and 2025-05-20; the twin made five. After 120 days the `reach-b` lines of the United States treasury were $111,294 lower and the town's counted households $80,666 higher. The state bill does the same: `law:US-MS:state:...:2025-03-21:SB 1`, paid 2025-03-02 and again 2025-03-22 at $118,485; after 180 days, federal `reach-b` was $116,166 lower and counted households $93,525 higher. The requirement itself changed nobody's limit. The federal starting row `law:US:federal:us-policy-positions:health-human-services.work-requirement-for-assistance:general:starting` (in force since 2024-07-01) is the first yes level, so `assistanceTimeLimit` (group-b.ts, line 259) reads the same row and the same start date in both worlds, and both stopped the same 78 heads on 2025-04-01. Probe: keeping the replaced obligation's next due date in `reconcileObligations` moved the county result to federal +$5,315 and counted households −$2,480, which is ordinary drift between the twins. The probe was undone.

**Fix.** In `reconcileObligations` (src/core2/law-reach/group-b-money.ts), when a recipient already has an open standing payment and only its amount changes, keep the date it is next due. Before ending the old obligation, read its `nextDueAt` from `accounts.obligation(row.obligationId)`. Then open the replacement with that date as `firstDueAt`, using tomorrow only when the old one has no later due date or the recipient had no payment before. The other way to do it is `accounts.setObligationAmount` on the open obligation, updating the `taken` row's `amountMinor`. This one function pays every benefit group B keeps (food aid, vouchers, preschool, child care, energy assistance, SSI and the welfare line's assistance), so the fix covers each program a mid-month law change reruns. Leave the change-day rerun in place: a law that changes who is paid should change it from its own date. Add a test that builds a drawn place, passes the county work requirement through the real body, runs 120 days, and asserts two things. First, no recipient of `benefit|snap-baseline` is paid twice within one month by the federal treasury (read the journal entries whose source id starts `reach-b:benefit:snap-maximum`). Second, the federal treasury's `reach-b` total is no lower than the twin's beyond the twin drift of the same run (the probe gave about $5,000 in Marion). When a drawn place already has the requirement in force, nobody's limit changes, and the result must be about zero either way.

**Time box.** 3 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** The chain is the acceptance: the rule is followed from the law row to the benefit payment that changed, to the named person it reached, and back to the treasury that paid, by record id. Ask why: the proof says, by record, why each household's benefit rose or fell, and a rise under a limit is a defect until its cause is named. One model, two tiers: counted households lose or keep the benefit by the same rule as full people, times their count. One rule, all 56 places: the result must point the same way in every place that has people the requirement reaches. Owner, November 8, quoted by the audit: "when it finds these errors or finds that these laws don't pass, I want them fixed. I don't just want a report of everything that was broken."
