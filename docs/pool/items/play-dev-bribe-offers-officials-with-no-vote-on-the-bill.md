**What.** Dev mode's Bribe panel on the Government screen offers a bribe for the vote on the bill last introduced in the Bills panel. Measured on screen in Ten Broeck, Kentucky (the audit gives no seed): the bill was before a five-member town council, and the bribe record the panel wrote names a senator from Kentucky and a House member from California as its two parties. Neither has a vote on a town council's bill, so the record is a bribe for a vote that does not exist. Causes, read in code on Nov 10. First, the panel's Official list is every member of every lawmaking body in the world, whatever bill is pending: `devBribePanel` (`src/bench/ui/dev-bribe.mjs`, line 24; the list at line 26) flattens the bodies from `lawmakingBodies` (`src/bench/dev-mode.ts`, line 696), which always adds the U.S. House and the U.S. Senate. Second, its Offerer list is the wealthiest living full people anywhere (`wealthiest`, `src/bench/dev-mode.ts`, line 1305), and full people include officials from every other place. Third, the engine never asks whether the official has a say: `considerBribe` (`src/core2/modules/corruption.ts`, line 235) checks only that both people are alive and the offerer has money. Fix both ends, for every bill and every body. The panel offers only the people with a say on that bill, read from the bill's own records (the members of each chamber it passes through, from `chambersOf`, and the executive who signs it, from `executiveOf`). The engine refuses an offer to an official who has no say over the subject, for every caller, and says why.

**Time box.** 4 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** Wire to the world: a bribe buys a vote, so it can only be offered to someone whose vote or signature the bill's own records show, and the money and the record must follow from that. One rule, all 56 places: who has a say on a bill is read from that body's roster and its executive in the place's data, never from a named body or state. No dice: whether it is offered and taken stays the two people's own decision in `considerBribe`; the fix only narrows who can be asked. Owner, Nov 8, 12:50 a.m.: "Making it happen should just trigger the code in the game that makes it happen."

**Done when.** A different session starts the bench (`npm run bench`, `/play`), leaves the Place field empty so the place is drawn (drawing again until the place has a town council), writes down the place and seed the title shows, and turns Dev mode on in Options. On Government it introduces a bill at the town council from the Bills panel. The Bribe panel's Official list then holds that council's members and the executive who signs its bills, and nobody else: the count equals the seats the Government screen shows for that council, plus one where an executive signs, and no member of Congress or of the legislature is in it. It introduces a second bill at the state's lower chamber, and the list changes to the members of the chambers that bill passes through and the governor. It sets Official's answer to Takes and presses Offer. The result line shows Offered, Taken, the dollars moved and a record id; the Trace of that record id names an official on the bill's own roster; and the dollars are followed on the two people's cards: the offerer's cash is lower and the official's higher by the same amount, as payment rows dated that day. It then asks the dev route directly (`POST /api/dev/bribe`) for an official from another body: the answer is the engine's refusal in words, no crime record is written and no money moves. A test in `src/core2/corruption.test.ts` or `src/bench/dev-bribe.test.ts`, in a drawn place with its seed named, asserts the refusal and fails when the fix is undone. It then repeats the screen steps in Ten Broeck, Kentucky (the audit's place; no seed was given, so the seed drawn is written down) and saves a screenshot. The owner plays it: with Dev mode on he introduces a council bill, and the Bribe panel lets him pick only that council's members and the executive who signs its bills.

What the audit found working, to keep: in Bigelow, Arkansas, a bribe called directly worked three ways ("left to the engine the resident chose not to offer; forced to 'takes,' $250 moved and the reasons were kept; forced to 'refuses,' the refusal was recorded"). The audit did not say whether the Ten Broeck bribe was taken or how much moved, and it named no cause in the code; the three causes above were read on Nov 10 by the session that filed this. `forceBribe` (`src/bench/dev-mode.ts`, line 1323) says the subject may be "a pending vote, a permit, a contract: any record id", so the engine's refusal has to hold for a contract award too (`gov-bribe-buys-a-contract`, done): the official must be the one who decides it. The Offerer list is a second question this item leaves alone: it lists the wealthiest full people anywhere, which is how a member of Congress can be the offerer (the audit does not say which of the two parties offered), and no ruling was found on who may offer; say so in the PR body if the fix narrows it. Nobody checked whether the background world's own bribes ever name an official with no say; the refusal in the engine covers that case either way.
