**What.** Four civic decisions weighed temperament at the general weight of 1 or at a size fitted to the wrong measure, so temperament settled them almost alone. A measure vote's trait term decided about half of all measure votes, nearly every identifier joined their party because the case was a fixed base, and most people a campaign asked to volunteer shared one total, so temperament alone sorted them. Give joining and volunteering each person's own circumstances (free time, travel, the reasons anyone backs a campaign, how closely they follow politics), then calibrate ballot-measures.vote, voting.back-incumbent, parties.join and campaigns.volunteer to cited studies. Give the employer's group join its nearest study and record why the lobbyist decision still has no base.

**Rules.** No dice: each case is the person's recorded free time, travel, warmth, agreement and engagement, never a roll. Research before building: every weight cites a study and its spread, and a decision with too few calls keeps a gap row naming why. Sliding scales: no base sits above zero for everyone, so temperament turns the close calls instead of all or none.

**Done when.** In the played election windows of six towns drawn from all 56, seeds named, the thirds' net or crossed odds for ballot-measures.vote, voting.back-incumbent, parties.join and campaigns.volunteer sit within the trait tests' bands of each study's size; ballot-measures.traits.test.ts, voting.traits.test.ts, parties.traits.test.ts and campaigns.traits.test.ts check them on a fresh drawn place; and the four gap rows come off data/content/trait-decision-gaps.json.
