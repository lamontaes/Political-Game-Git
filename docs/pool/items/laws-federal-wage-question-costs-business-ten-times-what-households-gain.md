**What.** A wage raise moves money from employers to workers, and under the yes-or-no wage question in Congress most of what businesses lose goes somewhere else. Measured in the law sweep in Marion, Mississippi (seed 7df73e53cd67), 180 days after passing, against a twin world with no bill: businesses $308,500 lower, households $30,500 higher, governments $194,000 higher and $82,000 gone to the outside. The audit says this question sets $15.00. It rode as a rider on 33 of the 40 bills Congress passed in the sweep, and 18 of the 40 show this one change, the same to within two dollars. So the wage question cost businesses ten times what households gained. For context, the audit also measured a clean raise of the minimum wage from $7.25 to $10.88 with no rider. Passed by the Mississippi House, households ended $18,200 higher and businesses $14,100 lower. Passed by Congress, households ended $4,200 lower, businesses $28,000 lower and governments $15,200 higher; by the payments' labels the town's businesses paid $19,000 more for goods from outside, since the raise applies everywhere, and the United States treasury took in $5,700 more from residents. The audit did not trace the wage question's result to a payer or to code. Trace each of the four totals by the payments' own labels and fix what is wrong at the cause, for a wage floor set at any level: the pay raised reaches the workers below the floor, governments gain only the tax on that extra pay, and any cost that comes from outside has a named cause.

**Time box.** 10 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** The chain is the acceptance: the floor is followed from the law row to a named worker's raised pay, to the employer that paid it, and to the taxes on it, by record id. Ask why: the proof names, by record, where each of the $308,500 went, and governments gaining more than the workers did is a defect until its cause is named. One model, two tiers: counted workers below the floor are raised by the same rule as full people, times their count. Sliding scales and causes: an employer's higher costs for goods from outside come from a price that moved for a recorded reason, never a fixed markup.
