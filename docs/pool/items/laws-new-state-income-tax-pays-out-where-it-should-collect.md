**What.** A new income tax pays out where it should collect. Measured in LaGrange, Wyoming (seed 63355aee286d): Wyoming has no income tax, and adopting one at 1% left households $349,000 higher and governments $138,000 lower after 180 days, against a twin world with no bill. The payments' labels show the Wyoming treasury paying residents $349,000 more, and its withholding line fell by $31,000 where it should have risen (traced to the payer). The Wyoming treasury itself ended $142,000 lower: it paid out the $349,000, the $31,000 and about $8,000 on smaller lines, and a reserve sweep brought in $246,000 from outside. Where a state already has the tax the direction is right: in Marion, Mississippi (seed 7df73e53cd67), raising the state rate from 4.4% to 6.6% took $479,000 from counted households and $7,000 from full people in town. So the fault is in adopting the tax where no value was in force. The audit traced it to the treasury that pays and no further. Find the payment that carries the $349,000 and the reason the withholding line falls, and fix it at the cause for every government that adopts an income tax where it had none: paychecks are withheld at the new rate, the treasury that levied the tax receives it, and no payment to residents grows because of it. Seen: the state treasury paying residents $349,000 when it adopts an income tax, and the paychecks it changes.

**Cause.** Found in code: `shapedRate` in src/core2/modules/legislature.ts (line 1761, called from the `law-amount` branch of `enact`, line 6133) treats a "no income tax" starting row as a graduated table. Wyoming's starting row `law:US-WY:state:income-tax:general:2025-01-01` has value 0, unit `percent-top-rate` and terms `{system: "none", schedules: {single: {brackets: []}}}`. Because `schedules` exists, the shape becomes "graduated"; there is no rated bracket to scale, so the 1% bill writes `law:US-WY:state:income-tax:general:2025-06-04:SF 1` with value 1, unit `percent-top-rate`, `system: "graduated"` and an empty bracket list. `withholdingFor` in src/core2/tax-withholding.ts then reads that row through `scheduleOf` (line 52): the schedule is not null, its top rate is 0, so it is returned unscaled, and `scheduleTax` on no brackets is $0 (line 145). The unit is neither `percent-flat` nor `percent`, so the flat branch never runs. The new tax withholds nothing from any paycheck, full or counted. The same starting shape is in src/core2/data/laws/starting-law.json for all nine states with no income tax (AK, FL, NH, NV, SD, TN, TX, WA, WY). Measured: LaGrange, Wyoming, seed 63355aee286d, place key 5643745, 180 days from 2025-01-20. Most of the "$349,000 higher" is the twin, not the bill: in the world with no bill, Wyoming's legislature adopts an income tax by itself through the policy question (`law:US-WY:state:income-tax:general:2025-06-04:adopt:law:US-WY:state:us-policy-positions:fiscal.adopt-income-tax:general:2025-06-04:HB 3`, 4.25% `percent-flat`, from `incomeTaxAct` in src/core2/law-reach/group-b-money.ts), and paycheck `withholding:work-payroll:2025-06-05:work-commitment:work-relationship_5f8d58b3f7b859fb:9` (person_ab9056c0792a1aae, $2,397.60 paid) withholds $101.90 to Wyoming. In the bill world, `SF 1` is in force from the same day, so `incomeTaxAct` sees a tax already in force (`hasTax`, line 1933) and adopts nothing, and `SF 1` itself collects $0. By the journal's own labels, the bill world's Wyoming treasury takes in less than the twin's: $322,471 on `work.unplaced-residents`, $30,518 on `budgets:withholding`, $16,056 on `work.count-payroll` and $11,762 on `owner-profit-tax`. That money stays with counted households and full people, and the reserve sweep (`reserve-sweep`) brings in $236,148 more from outside to cover it. No payment from the treasury to residents grows. The "pays residents" reading was withholding that never left their pay.

**Fix.** In `shapedRate` (src/core2/modules/legislature.ts), take the "graduated" shape only when the current row has at least one schedule with a rated bracket. A row with no rated bracket (a `system: "none"` starting row, or no row at all) adopting a positive rate becomes a flat tax: unit `percent-flat`, terms `system: "flat"`, the same shape `incomeTaxAct` writes when the policy question adopts one. This holds for every level and every place; no state is named. Keep the current behavior for rows that have rated brackets (Mississippi's raise from 4.4% to 6.6% already scales them). Add a test that drafts and enacts a 1% state income tax on a drawn place whose state's starting row has `system: "none"`. Search draws for one and skip with the counts if none is found, per the dev-tester skill. Assert that the enacted row is `percent-flat`, and that `withholdingFor` on a $2,000 two-week paycheck under it returns a state income line of $20 that names the new row's id. Do not touch `scheduleOf` or `withholdingFor`; they read a correct row correctly.

**Time box.** 3 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** The chain is the acceptance: the tax is followed from the law row to the amount withheld from a named worker's paycheck, to the treasury that received it, by record id. One rule, all 56 places: adopting a tax where none is in force follows the same rule as raising one that is, with no place named in the logic. One model, two tiers: counted households are withheld by the same rule as full people, times their count. Ask why: the proof names, by record, the payment behind each dollar the treasury paid out.

**Notes (cause finder, Nov 10).** Ran twin worlds from `buildBenchWorld("63355aee286d", gameStartDate(), {twoLevel: true, placeKey: "5643745"})`, one with the 1% state bill passed the dev-tester way (every member yes, chair hears it, governor signs; enacted, no added sections) and one left alone, for 180 days to 2025-07-19. A temporary probe in `postJournal` tallied every posting by owner and label; it was removed and not committed. Then a probe in `shapedRate` read a row with no rated bracket as flat. With it, `SF 1` became `percent`, and the Wyoming treasury took in $75,881 on `work.unplaced-residents` against the twin's $322,471 (0.235, and 1/4.25 is 0.235). So the bill collects at 1%, and the rest of the gap is the twin's own 4.25% adoption. The probe was undone. Checked and ruled out: a 2026-01-01 starting row overriding an enacted rate (enacted rows always outrank starting rows in `lawRowIsLater`, src/core2/laws-in-force.ts line 180), the reserve sweep (it only covers the shortfall), and every other reader of the income tax law (`counted-moves.ts`, `country-law.ts`, `outside-budgets.ts`; all go through `withholdingFor` or a rate ratio that is 1 when the opening rate is 0). Still unknown: whether the policy question adopting an income tax on its own in under five months is the intended pace (the twin trap belongs in the dev-tester skill), and whether dev mode's Law control should offer "flat" or "graduated" when a state adopts its first income tax.
