**What.** Measured Oct 20, 2026 on a 4-core cloud container with nothing else running: npm run speed:year -- --seed m7b --exclusive, a played year in Long Hill, Connecticut, takes 194, 200 and 214 seconds in three runs, over the 120-second limit in AGENTS.md. The same command on seed m7a, Discovery Harbor, Hawaii, takes 52 seconds, so the cost follows the town's size. A CPU profile of the 251 seconds shows no single hotspot: garbage collection is 52 seconds, and the largest named functions are 3 to 6 seconds each. This umbrella item is done when the parts filed beside it, found by that profile, bring the year under the limit.

**Rules.** Speed: a town's game year must run in under two minutes and no change may make a year more than 20 percent slower than its base, measured before and after on the same machine, one process at a time. Never scan the whole world or a whole history every day; use indexes or act on the day something changed. One rule, all 56 places: the check draws its place from all 56 and names it and the seed.

**Done when.** npm run speed:year -- --seed m7b --exclusive on the same container finishes the played world's year in Long Hill, Connecticut in under 120 seconds in three runs, and seed m7a stays within 20 percent of its 52 seconds; the receipts from before and after go in the PR body with the profile's top functions.

Live-heap profile Oct 21 (claude; same scratch script without the collected-object flags, so only what survives to the end; Long Hill, seed m7b, days 1 to 150, 81 s): 0.25 GB of survivors allocated during those days, led by `set` 30 MB (12 percent), `plainCopy` in `module-data.ts` 22 MB (9), `trim` 19 MB, `personInput` in `counted-groups.ts` 17 MB (7), `scoreOffers` 9 MB, `put` in `module-state.ts` 9 MB, `hear` in `news.ts` 7 MB, `kindLean` 7 MB, `copy` in `work-payroll-cash.ts` 7 MB. So the days themselves add little that stays; the roughly 900 MB heap is the opening world built before the profile starts. The 28 s of garbage collection is therefore mostly the cost of tracing that large, long-lived opening (major collections walk all of it) on top of 9 GB of short-lived allocation per 60 days. Two ways to attack it that no single-function edit covers: freeze or pool the opening's long-lived data so major collections skip it (fewer, larger objects: columns instead of per-person records; the counted store already does this), and cut the allocation volume across many functions at once. Both are structural; each needs its own item before more profiling. Claim released.
