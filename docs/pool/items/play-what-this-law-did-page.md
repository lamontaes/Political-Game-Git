**What.** A player can pass a law and never see what it did. Read by the audit on main as of Nov 9: after a law passes, no screen shows the people it reached. Measured by the audit in Davenport, North Dakota (three seats taken through Advanced, Play as; no seed given): none of the Your office screens shows it. Checked by me on Nov 10's main (commit 8ac2262853), read only: a law's page (`lawPage`, src/bench/play.mjs:5059) shows what is in force at each level, the statute, the measure that enacted it, what is coming, what changed and how members voted; nothing on it reads a reach record. The only place the game counts who a law reached is dev mode's reach control (`reach`, src/bench/dev-mode.ts:515), which reads the law-reach and raise records that name the law and returns a count of records, full people and counted people, under stopgap SG-PLAY-dev-law-reach-full-only. The audit ran that control on the 147 state laws its sweep enacted and it named a full person for 9 laws and a counted person for 2, which is why this item waits on the reach reading every effect record. Build one "what this did" section on the one law page, also opened from the bill's page once the bill is law. It is built from the same records dev mode's reach reads: the people the law reached, named for full people and by their count for counted groups, each with what it changed for them in dollars from the payments on the journal, and a repeal shown the same way (what stopped, for whom, how much). It is a view only. It computes nothing and stores nothing of its own.

**Time box.** 8 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** An effect is a read, not a record: the page shows a person only through the reach record and the journal payment that name them, by record id, and a law with no such record shows that it has reached no one yet, not an estimate. One model, two tiers: a counted group is shown by the same rule as a full person, times its count, and clicking it fills a person in who matches the record. The chain is the acceptance: one dollar is followed from the bill to the law in force, to the payment, to a named person, and back out through the repeal. No hand-written player text: names, dates and sums are worded labels and rounded money, with no raw kind, id, ISO date or unrounded number (the player-screen-text skill). AGENTS.md (owner, Oct 28): "A view that computes or stores its own version of a fact is a duplicate." Owner, Nov 8, 2026: "Most of this stuff should not be new coding. It should just be reading the code and then just displaying it in different ways." Owner, Nov 7, 2026: "these should all be able to be reversed."

**Done when.** A different session starts the bench (`npm run bench`, play at /play), begins a new game with the Place field empty so it is a drawn place, writes down the place and seed, and through Advanced, Play as takes a council seat. It drafts a change to the town's property tax rate, passes it with dev mode's bill card (the vote held, the executive's signature), and runs until the next collection. On the law's page it opens "what this did" and takes a screenshot: the people reached, each with dollars, and the totals for full people and for counted households. It then checks the page against three other readings and all must agree to the dollar: dev mode's reach and trace for the same law (the same record ids), the journal payments by account for three of the named people, and a twin world without the bill (`scripts/dev-tests/twin.ts`, same seed) for those same accounts. It repeals the law (a bill at the same body), runs to the next collection, and the page shows what stopped, for the same people, the same way. It repeats the pass and the page in the audit's sweep town, Marion, Mississippi (seed 7df73e53cd67), raising the town's rate from 0.921% to 1.381%, where the audit's twin measured $1,900 from full people in town, $31,100 from its counted households and $197,700 from officials elsewhere; the page's groups are set beside those three figures and any gap is explained by record. A bench test fails when the page shows a person or a sum that dev mode's reach does not hold, or when the page's code writes a record. The owner plays it: after his tax bill passes he opens the law and sees the households that now pay more and how much, and after he repeals it he sees the same people pay less.

**Notes.** From the Fable audit, Pillar 2 ("After a law passes, no screen shows the people it reached") and its fix list: "Medium: after any law passes, one 'what this did' page built from the same records, per person and in dollars, with the repeal shown the same way. The Marion figures are from the audit's law sweep (Pillar 2, "The property tax result repeats"): all 170 laws in dev mode's list at four bodies in Marion, Mississippi, seed 7df73e53cd67, each bill compared with the same world without it. The audit says the result was the same to the dollar whether the town board or the county board passed it, and that the first place drawn had no town board, so Marion was the second. The audit did not say how many days after passage it read these three figures; ask the audit's author or read them at the first collection after the bill.
