**What.** A counted person who finds work in the wider region (startRegionalWork in src/core2/modules/residents.ts, called from the regional find in src/core2/modules/jobs.ts, about line 2545) adds to their household record's unplacedWorkers, and the same household is added to again and again. In South Fork, Missouri (place key 2968762, world seed a499835260b786a6f03656c7b468096d095bdd4534cd0d04275bc3186d34d28f) on main at 9478f160ca: the small counted place beside the town (140 households, weight 1 each, about 1.3 people a household) holds 74.5 unplaced workers at the opening and 161.8 a year later, with one household record at 6.18; the rest-of-county place (17,126 households in 238 records, 33,877 people) goes from 2.1 on its records at the opening to 1,023.7 after one year and 4,544.4 after two, with one record at 35.09. Read the way a household's income reads it (householdIncome: unplacedWorkers times the pay per worker, per household, then times the record's weight), the three counted places together hold 312 workers at the start, 6,622 after two years, 33,036 after eight (more than their 29,935 adults) and 49,697 after twelve, while the people stay near 34,000. Median household income by the dev tester's vitals goes from $74,467 to $129,214 in eight years and the poverty share from 99 percent to 48. Two causes to settle, read in the code and not yet proved one by one: (1) the engines disagree about the unit of unplacedWorkers: householdIncome, unplacedShare and the head's work status read it per household and multiply by the record's weight, while startRegionalWork adds the record's whole weight to it, stopRegionalWork takes the weight off, jobs.ts line 917 divides it by the weight and counted-moves.ts line 1544 sums it unweighted; (2) even at weight 1 a household of one or two people reaches six, so the same person is found regional work more than once without the earlier one ending (the regional table keeps one row a person, so only one is ever taken back).

**Rules.** One model, two tiers (owner, Oct 14): a counted group is read by every engine with the same rule, times its count, so the field has one unit everywhere. Sliding scales and causes: a household's workers come from its people, never more than its working-age members. An effect is a read: the fix is proved by what the income, the seekers' count and the moves engine read afterward. No dice.

**Done when.** A second session, on main in a drawn place with a seed nobody chose, runs ten years in dev mode and reads the Metrics tab and Trace on three counted households of the surrounding county: none holds more out-of-town workers than it has working-age members, and the county's workers stay under its adults in every year. A test in src/core2 holds both in a drawn played world for three years, and one line in the pull request says which unit unplacedWorkers has, with every reader and writer listed. The 20-year game in South Fork, Missouri (the seed above) ends with the county's workers under its adults.

**Notes (Fable, Nov 10, read further in the code).** The unit at the opening is per household: openingUnplaced (src/core2/modules/residents.ts) gives each record its own eligible members times the pool's share, unweighted, and every reader of income multiplies by the record's weight. startRegionalWork then adds the record's whole weight to that per-household number (`h.unplacedWorkers += workers`, where `workers` is `weightOf(h)`), and stopRegionalWork takes the weight off. In the rest-of-county place the records carry about 72 households each at the opening, so one counted member finding regional work puts about 72 workers on one household record. The monthly pass that decides who looks for work does skip people already in regional work (the `abroad` set in jobs.ts), so the pile is more likely this unit than one person hired twice; in the small place where every record has weight 1 the number rose from 74.5 to 161.8 in the first year and then stopped at about its 167 adults. Also seen and not explained: that place's household records went from 238 to 1,318 to 3,113 in two years and its households (the weights added up) from 17,126 to 18,650, while its people stayed at 33,800; newcomers from the moves engine arrive as new records with their template's weight (counted-moves.ts, about line 1376) and take a share of the unplaced workers there too. The fix has to say what one counted member finding work means for a record that stands for many households, and make the number of workers the same whichever way it is read.
