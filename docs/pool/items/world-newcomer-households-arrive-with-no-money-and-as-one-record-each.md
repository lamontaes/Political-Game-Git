**What.** Measured on the workers-fix branch in South Fork, Missouri (place key 2968762, world seed a499835260b786a6f03656c7b468096d095bdd4534cd0d04275bc3186d34d28f), twelve months in, in the rest-of-county place. The 140 opening records that stand for 15,902 households (31,691 people, 20,219 of working age) hold 11,074 out-of-town workers, none of their members is in regional work and none is looking; their members' money covers their costs many times over. The 1,126 newcomer households (1,289 people, 1,081 of working age), each a record of its own, hold 615 workers by the place's share, 360 members already in regional work and 348 more looking, and their members' money covers zero days of their costs. The monthly review that decides who looks for work (reviewCountedEntry in src/core2/modules/jobs.ts) reads a household's days of money from its members' own cash (liquidMinor) over their living costs; a newcomer's members carry none, so every free member looks, and the regional find hires them. Two effects over a long game. First, the county's workers climb while its people stay flat: 12,305 of 27,866 adults at the opening, 19,150 after five years, 26,796 of 29,938 after eleven, as households at about 55 percent in work are replaced by households at 100 percent. Second, records grow without bound: the place's household records go from 238 at the opening to 1,356 after twelve months and 2,428 after eighteen while its households go from 17,126 to 18,303, because a newcomer is admitted as a new record of weight one (admitCountedHousehold from counted-moves.ts) and never joins a like record. Every monthly and daily pass over counted households walks all of them, so each year of a long game is slower than the last (43 seconds for year 1, 94 for year 10 in the same run, on a busy machine).

**Rules.** One model, two tiers (owner, Oct 14): a counted household's money is its share of its group's money, and a rule that reads it reads the same number for an opening household and a newcomer. Sliding scales and causes: who looks for work follows the household's real cushion, never an empty field. Speed (owner, Oct 29 and 30): records kept hold flat over a long save; old or like records fold. No dice. An effect is a read: say which engine reads the money a newcomer brings.

**Done when.** A second session, on main in a drawn place with a seed nobody chose, runs ten years in dev mode and reads the Metrics tab for the surrounding county: the share of adults at work stays within a few points of where it opened, and dev mode's records count for counted households is no more than twice its opening count. A test in src/core2 plays three years in a drawn place and holds both: newcomer households' members look for work at the rate opening households' members with the same money do, and the count of counted household records grows no faster than the count of households. The 20-year game in South Fork, Missouri (the seed above) ends with the county's workers under its adults and year 20 no more than 1.2 times as slow as year 4. The pull request says what money a newcomer household brings and where it sits, and how a newcomer joins a like record.

**Files.** src/core2/modules/counted-moves.ts, src/core2/modules/jobs.ts, src/core2/modules/residents.ts, src/core2/counted-groups.ts, src/core2/counted-money.ts

**Notes.** The probe that printed the figures is a scratch script, not in the repository; the counts are of counted households by place, by whether the record existed at the opening and by whether it stands for more than one household. Also seen there: the town's own opening counted records (weight one) read zero days of money too, so the same rule sends their free members looking; and the opening's many-household records read an enormous cushion because their members carry no living cost. Both point at the same reader: days of money should come from the household's share of its group's fund (householdCash in residents.ts), not from the members' own fields. **Notes (Fable, Nov 10, the money half is built; the design for the records half).** The money half is on branch claude/fable-newcomers-money, on top of the workers fix: the monthly review reads a counted household's days of money from its share of its group's fund (`countedDaysOfMoney` in src/core2/modules/jobs.ts), not from its members' own cash. In South Fork after 12 months the 1,174 newcomer households then hold 631 workers by the place's share, with none in regional work and none looking (before: 360 and 348). Test: src/core2/counted-days-of-money.test.ts (Dakota Ridge, Colorado: 3 of 25,536 newcomer members in regional work or looking). The records half is not built. Its design, read from the code: an arrival today builds a whole new record (`newcomers()` then `admitCountedHousehold` in src/core2/modules/counted-moves.ts, about line 1375), a copy of a template household with the template's weight and the template members' own cash, and a departure removes a whole record (`departCountedHousehold`). So records only ever multiply. The counted population is counts, so a move should change a count: (1) an arrival of one household shaped like a template raises that template record's weight by one household (a new store method beside `releaseWeight` in src/core2/counted-groups.ts, which already raises a record's weight, keeps each job's heads and marks its group changed), brings its share of the group's fund in from the outside account at the group's money per person, raises the place's unplaced-workers flow by the template's out-of-town workers at the pool's pay (the other open item, world-movers-change-unplaced-workers-not-the-pool-pay), and scales the record's rent flow (`rent:<household id>`) and income contracts by the new weight over the old; (2) a departure lowers a record's weight by the households that leave, with the same four things in reverse, and removes the record only when its weight reaches zero; (3) the moves table keeps one row per arrival or departure with the record's id and the people moved, so news and the Metrics tab read what they read today; (4) a record that the player's circle or a named person belongs to is never the target (it stays one household), as `releaseWeight` already refuses a household without a home. What must hold, as tests in a drawn place over three played years, each against the same world on main: people by place and age band within one household of main's every month; the money journal balances every day; the place's households (weights added up) equal main's within one; the count of records never exceeds the opening count plus the records of households the player has met; and housing's count of occupied homes equals the households (read how src/core2/modules/housing.ts counts a record of many households before changing anything). Not known yet: whether housing gives each record one home, which decides how an arrival finds a vacant home when it joins a record.

**Notes (Fable, Nov 10, the records half: what the code already does, read by a helper with file and line, and the design that follows).** Facts. (a) Money follows weight by itself: a record's rent, retirement awards and recorded wages are kept per household or per job and multiplied when paid (`weighFlow` in src/core2/counted-money.ts, lines 247 to 263: rent by `weightOf(h)`, wages by the job's heads), and the cache behind it is keyed on the store's `version`, which `releaseWeight` bumps. (b) Housing is not weight-true: a record holds ONE dwelling row and one unit whatever its weight (`dwelling:<household id>`, src/core2/modules/housing.ts about line 764; occupying takes one vacant rental per record, src/core2/housing-occupancy.ts line 777, releasing gives one back, line 182), while the opening's rental stock and the homeless counts are weighted. (c) A newcomer record is admitted with tenure `other`, no dwelling and no rent flow (src/core2/modules/residents.ts line 3085); only `houseUnassignedHouseholds` (housing-occupancy.ts line 949) later houses it, one unit a record, if the place has a vacant rental and the group's money covers the rent. (d) These caches hold weights and are keyed on `membersVersion` or the date, which a weight change does not bump: `countedResidents` (src/core2/electorate.ts line 201), `townConcernShare` (modules/meetings.ts line 570), `electorateSides`, `countedByDay` and `countedById` (modules/voting.ts lines 1580, 1287, 1410), and the press's weekly and monthly resident counts (modules/press.ts line 2912). (e) The moves table's readers use date, direction, people, places, reasons and the row's id; none needs one record per household (school-outcomes.ts line 185 reads householdId for "moved in since"). Design, replacing the sketch above. Folding a newcomer into its template would give it the template's home and mortgage out of thin air, so newcomers fold into records of their own kind: for each template, at most one record of households that have arrived and are waiting for a home (tenure `other`) and one of arrived households that are housed. An arrival raises the waiting record's weight by one household (creating it the first time, as today). `houseUnassignedHouseholds` moves as many households from the waiting record to the housed one as the place has vacant rentals and the money covers, taking that many units, so housing becomes weight-true for these records. A departure lowers a record's weight by one household and gives its unit back; the record goes when its weight reaches zero. The store's new weight change bumps `version` and `membersVersion` (so the caches in (d) refresh) and scales each recorded job's heads in proportion on the way down. Records then stay within three times the opening count however long the game runs. Not in this slice: making the opening's many-household records weight-true in housing (one unit a record today).
