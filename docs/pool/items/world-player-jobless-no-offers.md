**What.** Four drawn towns from the start of the game (the controlled person is a jobless 18-year-old; the seed is `off:0` to `off:3`): Addison (Webster Springs), West Virginia and Fort Laramie, Wyoming made the player no job offer in a whole year; Winchester, Kentucky made 3 by day 240 and 7 by day 365; Falmouth, Maine made 1 by day 56 and 6 by day 365. The player is never a registered seeker (the engine never answers for the controlled person), so an offer comes only when an employer's open position picks them. In a town where positions open rarely or fit poorly, a player who wants work gets nothing to accept. Find why employers in those towns never pick the player (openings, fit score, age band) and give the player a way to find work that is not luck: the same open positions every other jobless adult is matched to should reach the player as offers on the same rule.

**Rules.** The player is never auto-answered, but the engine may offer. One rule for every jobless adult: the seeker's fit and the position decide. No dice. One rule, all 56 places.

**Done when.** In a drawn place and a seed the builder did not choose, a jobless adult player gets at least one offer within 12 weeks whenever a position they fit is open in town, shown by position and offer ids; `src/core2/jobs-player-offers.test.ts` waits only the weeks that takes.

**Files.** src/core2/modules/jobs.ts

**Notes.** Found making `jobs-player-offers.test.ts` reach its case: it used to return silently when no offer came in 8 weeks (it did so in all 8 draws tried), so it never checked that an acceptance is what hires the player. The test now waits up to a year and draws further towns.

**Cause found by reading the code (Oct 17, claude), not built.** The player is considered: each week `runWeek` (`src/core2/modules/jobs.ts`) lets the jobless, working-age player apply to every open position in their place, and `offerToPlayer` fires when they are an opening's best-placed applicant. An employer ranks applicants by `fit`: `jobsFitExperienceWeight` times years in that occupation, plus `jobsFitReliabilityWeight` times reliability, plus a referral term. A new adult has no years in any occupation, so any seeker with experience in the role beats them for that opening, and a town with many counted seekers fills each posting from them. The player gets an offer only when a position opens that no experienced applicant applies to. That is the rule applied evenly (no dice), so the town data shows no defect to fix at the matching step. What is open is a design question for the owner: how a first-time job seeker finds work. The register says the world offers plausible entry-level work and the engine offers, the player decides (`DECISION-REGISTER.md`, job availability and the player's choices). Two ways forward, simplest first: rank an applicant's experience against what the position asks for, so an entry-level posting does not go to the most experienced seeker, or let the player look for work by choosing an employer, which is a play screen. Needs a ruling before a builder changes the fit rule, since it moves every hire in the world.

**Looked at Oct 17 (claude/player-jobless-offers), not built; the finding for whoever does.** Probed four drawn towns (seeds `off:0` to `off:3` through `drawPlace(seed, "player-job-offers")`: Kupreanof, Alaska; Utica, Oklahoma; Celada, Puerto Rico; Hampton, New Hampshire) for a year of weekly matching, bookkeeping off. Three causes, in the order they bite. (1) The player is under 18 at the start in most draws (born 2007), and `openToOffers` returns nothing before the working-age floor, so no offer can come in the first months. Kupreanof's first offer came the week the player turned 18 (the one opening there stood open all year because nobody else in town applied). (2) Openings are rare: Kupreanof had none until April and one all year, Utica none; that is `world-hires-below-jolts`. (3) Where openings are plentiful the player still loses: in Celada, Puerto Rico, 58 positions opened and filled in 140 days (the 1st of each month, filled at the next weekly pass), 0 to the player, because the employer's pick (`runWeek`, `fit`) is the best fit and a person with no experience loses to any counted seeker with a work history, so a first-time job seeker is never chosen in a town with a pool of counted seekers. Hampton, New Hampshire, with the same rule, made the player 9 offers in the year. The rule is the same for everyone, so this is not a bug in the player's path; it is that `fit` rewards experience without regard to what the position asks. A fix that serves the player and every first-time seeker is for the position to want the experience of the worker it replaces (`replacesPersonId` is on the row), so an entry-level opening goes to the best-placed entrant; that changes everyone's hiring and needs the JOLTS and employment checks re-run, so it is a Words/World decision for the owner, not a patch to the player's path. OWNER QUESTION: should an opening ask for the experience of the worker it replaces (fit = nearness to that, not more is better)?

**Read from the code, not yet measured (claude, Oct 17).** The player is an applicant to every open position near them each week (`openToOffers` in `runWeek`, `src/core2/modules/jobs.ts`), so the offer goes to whoever fits best among all applicants. Two things keep the player out. (1) A position with no schedule on record for a full person (`roleTemplate`) takes counted applicants only, so in a town whose openings are mostly such roles the player is filtered out before the pick. (2) Fit is experience times `jobsFitExperienceWeight`, plus reliability, plus a referral's standing; an 18-year-old has no experience, so each opening goes to a counted applicant with more, and the player never wins `pick`. Both are by design: the same rule decides every applicant. To give the player a path that is not luck without a special case, the rule itself has to let a first job exist: an entry-level opening that asks for no experience should rank applicants by reliability and referral only, and an official's or relative's word (`jobsFitReferralWeight`) should be something the player can earn. To measure first: per week in Addison, West Virginia (place 5400364) and Fort Laramie, Wyoming (5628230), the openings that have a role template, the number of applicants and the winner's fit against the player's.

**Measured (claude, Oct 17).** In Addison, West Virginia (place 5400364, seed off:0, `buildBenchWorld`, 365 days from January 20, 2025, the player born March 7, 2007 with no job) the player was an applicant to only four positions in the whole year: two on March 3 (32 and 33 applicants), one on April 7 (32) and one on April 21 (32), each of them a role with a schedule on record, 8 or 9 of the applicants counted and the rest full people. After April 21 no opening reached the weekly pick with the player in it. So the town opens few positions and each draws about 32 applicants; the player is one of 32 with no experience. The item's earlier reading holds; what is left to settle is whether a first job should rank applicants by experience at all.

**Measured (claude, Oct 19, branch claude/player-offers-s10).** The cause is not the player's fit. Weeks to the player's first offer in a played year from the start (`buildBenchWorld`, seed off:0, the 18-year-old jobless player): Addison, West Virginia (5400364) week 11; Fort Laramie, Wyoming (5628230) none in 52 weeks; Glenwood, North Carolina (3726560) week 19; Orient, South Dakota (4647460) none. I capped a seeker's experience credit at what the opening asks for (O*NET Job Zone years): the four numbers did not change, so experience was not what kept the player out. In Fort Laramie the jobs engine opened no position at all in the year (the weekly match returns at once on "no open positions"), with 27 businesses and 7 separations (2 layoffs, 3 quits, 2 left-work). So there was nothing to offer, and the player's rank never mattered. The code change was dropped, not shipped. The open cause is filed as jobs-no-openings-in-small-towns; this item waits on it for the towns with no openings, and its Addison-style case (few openings, many applicants) needs the first-job rule discussed above.
