**What.** The crew has two proof tools that answer "who did this law reach", and they count different things. Neither names the people whose lives the law changed. Measured on the same 147 state laws in Marion, Mississippi (seed 7df73e53cd67): dev mode's reach control gave a number above zero for 23 of them, named a full person for 9 and a counted person for 2. The crew's harness "searches every record that names the law. It found 175 people for 120 of the laws and three more for the other 27." On the wage law the lead looked the three up: "They are the witnesses named at the bill's committee hearing, two of them from the played town. They are not people the law reached." The House has 122 members and the Senate 52, and there is one governor: 175. So the harness is counting the people who voted, to the person (the audit's inference from the sum). The audit's reading of the crew's own count: "'147 of 178 laws work' means 147 laws have a reach record. It does not mean a player can watch 147 laws change a life." Read in code on Nov 10. `citing` in `scripts/dev-tests/law-run.ts` (line 57, called at line 124) walks the whole log and counts every record whose facts or id contain the law row's id or the bill's id. That takes in the roll calls, the signature and the committee hearing, whose record names the committee's members and its witnesses (`committeeStage`, `src/core2/modules/legislature.ts`, line 4274; the names at line 4410). It returns counts of records by kind, not a list of people. Dev mode's `reach` (`src/bench/dev-mode.ts`, line 515) reads three modules' rows by law id. A third reader was found that the audit does not name: the wiring matrix behind `docs/reports/law-wiring-table.md` ("Laws wired: 147 of 178") grades its reach link with its own `reachOf` (`scripts/law-wiring/check.ts`, line 267), which counts rows in the two law-reach stores by policy key; it counts reach records, not voters. The core has a fourth, `reachedBy` and `reachOn` (`src/core2/law-reach/host.ts`, lines 325 and 260), which the press desk and `src/bench/reached.ts` read. Fix it at the cause: one reader in the core for "who did this law reach", used by dev mode, by the harness and by the wiring matrix, that names the people whose money, eligibility or price changed, full people and counted groups alike, with the dollars. A voter, a signer or a witness is reached only when the law changed their own money, eligibility or price.

**Time box.** 10 hours for one session (sized for Sonnet; the lead is Fable). When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** Replace, never layer: the one reader replaces `citing`, the matrix's own `reachOf` and dev mode's own count in the same change, and the PR body counts the readers removed. An effect is a read, not a record: a law reached a person when another engine read it as it paid, priced or marked that person eligible, so a record that only mentions the bill proves nothing about reach. One model, two tiers: the reader counts a counted group by the same rule as a full person, times its count. The chain is the acceptance: the proof follows one dollar from the law to a named person and back out, by record id. Rule 0 of the repository guide: find the engine that does it and wire through it. Owner, Oct 27: "Sessions should not be building their own shit." Owner, Oct 13, standing rule: an effect is a read; a record nobody else reads is a placeholder.

**Done when.** A second session, in dev mode in a drawn place: it starts the bench (`npm run bench`, `/play`), leaves the Place field empty so the place is drawn, writes down the place and seed the title shows, and turns Dev mode on in Options. On Government it introduces a minimum wage raise at the state legislature from the Bills panel, checks "Chair hears the bill", presses Pass with the executive set to Signs, runs until that law takes effect and then 90 days more, and opens the law's reach on the screen. The people it names are workers whose pay rose and the employers who paid it, each with dollars. The number is not the size of the two chambers plus the governor. A member of the legislature is on the list only if their own pay or tax changed, and each named person's card shows the changed pay or payment row, by record id. It then runs the harness on the same place and seed (`scripts/dev-tests/law-run.ts <seed> state` with that policy) and the harness prints the same reached people, full and counted, that the screen showed. The dollars are followed in a twin world, the same place and seed with and without the bill, cash compared by account: the people on the list are the people whose accounts differ, and the bill's sections are read first so a rider is not mistaken for the law. It then runs the named repro: in Marion, Mississippi on seed 7df73e53cd67, the state wage raise from $7.25 to $10.88. The harness no longer answers 175; it names the people whose pay moved, beside the audit's figures (households $18,200 higher, businesses $14,100 lower, four people named by dev mode's old count). A test in a drawn place with its seed named passes a bill that changes nothing for anyone and asserts that the reader answers zero while the bill's voters number more than zero; it fails when the fix is undone. A search finds one reader: no `citing` in `scripts/dev-tests`, no private `reachOf` in `scripts/law-wiring`, and `reach` in `src/bench/dev-mode.ts` calls the core's function. `docs/reports/law-wiring-table.md` is regenerated, and its first line gives, beside the laws with reach records, the measured number of laws for which the reader names at least one person. A screenshot of the reach on screen is saved with the place and seed under it. The owner plays it: with Dev mode on he passes a law, opens it, and sees the workers, taxpayers or shoppers it reached and the dollars, never a list of the lawmakers who voted for it.
