**What.** Bring a game year in the slowest places under 120 seconds (the AGENTS.md rule). The item that found it (`core-all-places-year-speed-oct16`) measured Lewiston, Maine at 135 s and Washington, D.C. at 121.5 s with two workers. Re-measured alone on a slower 4-core container (about 2.5 times slower than that machine) with the same run seed (23072baac0b7207c), Lewiston took 332.6 s and Washington, D.C. 211.5 s, so the 120 s line needs re-measuring on the Mac first. A CPU profile of Lewiston's year (364 s under the profiler, `scripts/core2-year/profile-top.mjs`) shows no single hot function: of 302 s in `advanceCore`, 116 s is `activate` in `life.ts` (people choosing their daily acts: `availableActs` 33 s inclusive, `scoreOffers`, the drives `(anon)` 37 s), 89 s is `runModuleCalendar`, 40 s is posting the cash journal, and the garbage collector alone is 77 s (16 percent of all time). Cut allocation in the daily choice (`availableActs`, `scoreOffers`, `life.ts activate`) and in journal posting, and act on the day something changed instead of scoring every person every day.

**Rules.** Speed: a town's game year runs under two minutes and no change makes it more than 20 percent slower; measure before and after on the same base and machine, one process at a time; never scan the whole world or a whole history every day; use indexes or act on the day something changed. No dice. An effect is a read. One rule, all 56 places.

**Done when.** On the Mac or another machine whose speed is stated in the PR body, a one-year run of the played world (buildBenchWorld), one process at a time, in the drawn places Lewiston, Maine and Washington, D.C. (`node --import tsx scripts/core2-year/all-places.mjs --years 1 --seed 23072baac0b7207c --only US-ME --workers 1`, then `US-DC`) takes under 120 seconds a year, with every dollar still balancing and every all-places check passing. The PR body gives before and after seconds, and the `profile-top` self-time lines for `activate`, `availableActs`, `scoreOffers` and the garbage collector.

**Files.** src/core2/life.ts, src/core2/choice.ts, src/core2/modules/drives.ts, src/core2/journal-state.ts, src/core2/module-calendar.ts

Of the cheap wins, two stand out for a Core session: `parameter` (1.3 s) is a lookup per call that could be hoisted out of the hot loops, and the 8 s of garbage collection tracks the per-row copies in `module-state.ts` `insert` and `indexKey`.

Measured Oct 19 (claude/year-speed-activate), not changed. On a 4-core, 15 GB cloud container, `node --cpu-prof --import tsx scripts/core2-year/all-places.mjs --years 1 --seed 23072baac0b7207c --only US-ME --workers 1` took 448.5 s for Lewiston, Maine (seed 63949dda3113, heap 2.52 GB, every check passing). The worker's profile, 538.8 s in all of which 377 s is `advanceCore`: garbage collector 83.3 s self (15.5 percent); `runModuleCalendar` 123.6 s inclusive; `activate` 78.7 s inclusive (`perform` 56.2 s, `performPlanned` 35.5 s, `availableActs` 6.3 s self, `scoreOffers` 5.1 s self); `postJournal` 74.4 s inclusive, of which `postCashJournal` 72.6 s (20.7 s self) and `projectCashJournal` 26.6 s inclusive (21.6 s self); `transferSplit` 45.2 s; `releaseResidentHousehold` 14.7 s self; `buildBenchWorld` itself 67.6 s (opening kin 28.7 s). So the top self-time cost is not the daily choices but the cash journal's per-posting validation (BigInt arithmetic and set and map building in `projectCashJournal`, `src/core2/journal.ts` lines 137 to 420) plus the garbage it makes. AGENTS.md says validation is a debug switch, run in tests and the bench and not in play, so the next step that follows the owner's rule is a switch on `projectCashJournal`'s per-posting checks (keep the balance sum and the write), measured before and after with the same command, rather than tuning `activate`. The "every dollar balances" check stays as the bench's own guard. The 120 s line still needs measuring on the Mac: this container ran the year at 448.5 s, about 3.7 times the line.

First cut, Oct 19 (claude/year-speed-activate): `projectCashJournal` skips its structural re-checks (identities, duplicate sets, source-posting matching, owner index, dated sources) when `bookkeeping()` is off, as in play; the sequence, source, balance and overflow checks always run, and a test shows the same projection both ways and an unbalanced set still throwing. Same command, same container, same seed: the year went from 448.5 s to 405.7 s, `projectCashJournal` from 26.6 s to 14.0 s inclusive, `postCashJournal` from 72.6 s to 53.5 s, the garbage collector from 83.3 s to 73.7 s; money, ended jobs, seats and ballot checks all pass. Two runs on a shared container are not a controlled comparison, so read the 9.5 percent as an upper bound. The item stays open: 405.7 s is still about 3.4 times the 120 s line on this container, the Mac has not run it, and `postCashJournal` (16 s self), `transferSplit` and the garbage collector remain.
