**What.** A player who wins an executive office cannot send a bill to the body that votes. Read by the audit on main as of Nov 9: a council member can draft from 32 town policies and a state legislator from 152, and a mayor or governor has no bill menu at all, because only bodies with two or more seats get one. Measured by the audit in Davenport, North Dakota, three seats taken through Advanced, Play as (no seed given): the council member's Your office screen has a Draft a bill list; the mayor's shows "Office: Mayor" and "Pay: $4,262.00 a year" and nothing else. Checked by me on Nov 10's main (commit 8ac2262853), read only: `bodiesOf` (src/bench/legislature.ts:54) returns the two federal chambers and any office the person holds with two or more seats (`office.seats < 2` is skipped, line 93); `seatView` (line 191) builds the draft menus from that list; and `seatAct` (line 258) refuses anyone else with "You don't sit on that body." The engine for an executive's bill already exists and the player's screen never calls it: `proposeAsExecutive` (src/core2/modules/legislature.ts:3431) asks the member most inclined to carry the bill, by the office's right in src/core2/data/legislature/executive-proposal.json (president, governor, county executive and mayor are all "by-request"), and its only caller is the background world (src/core2/modules/world-legislating.ts:594). Build the screen for the class: any single-seat executive whose office has a proposal right gets the same draft card, for the body that votes on their government's bills, and drafting goes through `proposeAsExecutive`, the way real mayors and governors send bills. The member asked decides as anyone asked would; the player sees who was asked and what they said, and follows the bill from there.

**Time box.** 8 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** One rule, all 56 places: who may bring an executive's bill and to which body is read from the office's row and the place's own records (the proposal-right table, stopgap SG-GOV-local-executive-proposal where it is an estimate), never from a named town or state. Replace, never layer: the executive uses the one draft card, the one bill catalog and the one bill engine a member uses; no second menu, no second bill writer, no executive-only bill path. No dice: the member asked carries or refuses from their own position on the bill and their tie to the executive, and the screen shows that recorded reason. No hand-written player text: every label is a control name and every line about the ask comes from the English engine. AGENTS.md, early access: "local and state offices playable (council, mayor, county, state legislature, governor), including the executive role." The audit's root cause 7: "A mayor or governor cannot propose a bill."

**Done when.** A different session starts the bench (`npm run bench`, play at /play), begins a new game with the Place field empty so it is a drawn place with an elected mayor (it names any places it drew and passed over for having none), writes down the place and seed, and through Advanced, Play as takes the mayor's office. Your office shows a Draft a bill card with the town's own catalog and what is in force today beside each item. It drafts one bill and takes a screenshot of the result: the member who was asked, their answer, and, when they agree, the bill on the council's agenda marked as brought at the mayor's request (the bill row's requester, by record id). With dev mode's bill card it holds the vote; the bill comes to the mayor's own desk to sign or veto, and after signing the law page shows the new value. It does the same as a governor in a drawn place. Then, in the audit's place, Davenport, North Dakota (typed into the Place field; the audit gave no seed), the mayor's Your office shows the draft card and not only "Office: Mayor" and the pay. A member who refuses is shown with the reason, and no bill appears. A bench test fails when a serving single-seat executive with a "by-request" right gets an empty menu. The owner plays it: as mayor he picks a policy, sends it to the council through a member who agrees to carry it, and signs it when it passes.
