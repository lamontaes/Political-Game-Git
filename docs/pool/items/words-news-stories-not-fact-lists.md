**What.** Owner, Nov 1 playtest of v0.73.0, on the front page: "Stanton's Kitchen closes... 16 people worked here. The owner was retiring. That's not... none of this is a story." The closure printed four separate field sentences (people who worked there, the owner, retiring, the industry), and the rest of the page was race results ("Again, it's all just winning races"). A closing told as news says who, what it meant to the town and why, in sentences that connect, from the same records. The opening's front page also needs more than election results: the everyday kinds of words-paper-and-journal-everyday-life on its first day.

**Rules.** Scene standard and civic prose: lines connect and tie to the reader, never a field per sentence. No hand-written player text: every sentence comes from the bank and cites the record it tells. Wire to the world: the opening's first paper prints the everyday kinds the world already records.

**Done when.** In three drawn places (named with seeds), the opening's front page carries at least three kinds of story, and a closure story's body reads as connected sentences from its records, quoted in the PR before and after; the printed-words test fails on the field-per-sentence shape.

**Time box.** 4 hours; if not done, push what exists, open the draft PR, write the handoff.

**You will see.** A business closing reads like a news story, and the first morning's paper has more than election results.
