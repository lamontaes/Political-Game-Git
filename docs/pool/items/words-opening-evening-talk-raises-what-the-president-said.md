**What.** A new life opens on the President's address, and that evening a relative talks about it. Today the relative says one of 23 fixed sentences ("I've heard those promises too many times.") and the player picks from four fixed replies, the same in every town and whatever the President said. The owner ruled that this is the demonstration of the game: the relative raises a real thing the President said in this world's address, and where they stand on it from their own views, job and town, composed by the English engine in many versions with inserts from the records. Seen: the first evening at home, in the talk box, after the President's address.

**Cause.** Found in code: `addressTake` in src/director/conversation.ts returns only `for` or `against`, from the sign of the person's lean against the President's party, and `subjectsBetween` in the same file raises `address-view` with that stage and no other fact. The 23 `address-view-*` parts in data/english/parts/conversation.json are whole sentences with no slot. What the President said point by point exists only in the director's ledger: `Ledger.opening.address.sentences` (`OpeningSentence` in src/director/opening-story.ts), where a priority's sentences carry `values.about` and `values.to` and the source id `question:<question>`. A hearer's knowledge of the address (`ADDRESS_FACT` in src/director/opening-facts.ts) holds only the remembered line and the whole text, so the evening's talk cannot name a point. Nothing the player answers is written back except that the topic was talked over (`say` in conversation.ts).

**Fix.** Build it in this order; each step is small.

1. **The address's points, as one reader.** Add `addressPoints(opening)` to src/director/opening-facts.ts: from `Ledger.opening.address.sentences`, one row for each policy question the President spoke on (the sentences whose `sourceRecordIds` name `question:<q>`): the question, `about` (the worded subject, from `values.about`), `to` (what the President would do, from `values.to`), whether the President is for or against (the `stance-for` or `stance-against` fact) and the sentence's own source ids. Rows keep the address's order. The address often holds few policy points: in two drawn worlds on Nov 11 (Duneland Beach, Indiana and Riverside, Washington) the President named one, the same in both (no federal law on legal cannabis sales), because most federal questions give him no stand yet (pull request 8589). So a point is also each thing the address said about the country's year: a sentence whose `facts` name a claim on work, pay, prices, rent or growth, with its figure in `values`. Every address then has at least two points, and the reader says which kind each is (`policy` or `year`). A `year` point has no stance to stand against: the speaker stands on it by whether their own life bears it out (their job, their money, the town's own figure from `townMetrics`).
2. **Which point this person raises, from their own life.** Replace `addressTake` with `addressPointFor(core, speakerId, points)`. No chance decides it. In order: a point on work or pay when the speaker lost a job this year or is out of work (`lastEndedJob`, already in conversation.ts); a point on prices, rent or housing when they are in money trouble (`hardshipStress` in src/core2/trait-decisions.ts, or an eviction subject); otherwise the point they hold the strongest view on (`viewOn` in src/core2/modules/views.ts, by size); otherwise the President's first point. The row records which of the four reasons picked it (`work`, `money`, `view`, `first`).
3. **Where they stand on it.** Their view on that question (`viewOn`) against the President's stance on it: `with` when the signs agree, `against` when they differ, `unsure` when their view is near none (use the views engine's own threshold for "no view", not a new number). Party no longer decides it alone.
4. **The subject and its facts.** `subjectsBetween` raises `address-point` in place of `address-view`, with stage `with`, `against` or `unsure` and these facts for the slots: `about`, `to`, and one fact from the speaker's own life chosen by the reason: `employer` (their job record, or the employer they lost), `place` (the town, with `townMetrics` in src/core2/modules/world-metrics.ts deciding whether the town's unemployment or rent backs the line), or none for `view`. Declare any new slot in the bank's `slots` map; `{about}`, `{to}`, `{employer}` and `{place}` are the ones this needs.
5. **The line is composed, not picked.** Word the raise with `composeGroundedLine` (src/english/english-composition.ts: opener, core, reason, closer), so a spoken line is two to four parts joined by the engine. Write the parts in data/english/parts/conversation.json for subject `address-point`: at least 12 openers that name the point (`{about}`), at least 12 cores for each stage, and at least 8 reasons for each of `work`, `money`, `town` and `view`, in both a plain and a careful voice. Every part carries at least one slot or is a part that cannot stand as a line alone. Keep each joined line within the bank's word limit, or raise the limit for this subject in the bank's own data and say so. The `civic-prose` skill is the process; no feeling word without a record.
6. **The replies.** Four different moves, each about the point: agree, disagree, ask what it would mean here (`{place}`), and ask what they would do instead. Each has at least 8 wordings with `{about}` or `{place}`. `rankChoices` already keeps one per move and at most four; `expectUpToFourDifferent` (src/director/reply-set-check.ts) must pass on the set.
7. **What the reply writes back.** `say` records that the player agreed or disagreed with this person on this question (an observation the relative holds, keyed by the question), so a later talk or scene can bring it up. The relative's answer to each reply is composed the same way and names the point.
8. **The ask before it.** The opener "Did you see the President's speech?" is graded good and stays, but it gains versions that name the day or the place it was heard (`{place}`), and the replies to it (`agree`, `doubt`, `hope`, `turn`, `plain`, `missed`) are rewritten to carry a slot. The two `address-ask-seen` parts graded bad are deleted.
9. **Delete what this replaces, with every reference.** All `address-view-*` parts, the parts graded bad in data/english/grades/2026-11-10-nov8-playtest-rejected.json for this scene, their rows in data/english/part-grades.json, the `address-view` entries in src/director/data/conversation-rules.json (`tells`, `replyMoves`), `addressTake`, and the scene-talk pieces whose `after` is `address-view:*`. A good-graded line is never deleted (scripts/dialogue-batch/liked-lines-kept.test.ts). Then `npm run words:grade`, and `npm run words:rules` twice.
10. **Lower the guard's counts** for `conversation` and `scene-talk` in tests/guards/english/line-inserts-baseline.json to what the test asks for.
11. **Tests.** Rewrite src/director/conversation-address-talk.test.ts on drawn places: in each, the relative's line names an `about` that is in that world's address; two relatives with opposite views on the point take opposite stands; someone who lost a job this year raises a work point when the address has one; the reply set passes `expectUpToFourDifferent`; and over 200 openings in one world at least 40 different raise lines are produced. Across five drawn worlds the points raised are not all the same point. Run tests/guards/english and the bank tests yourself.

**Time box.** 8 hours for one Opus session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the item's state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** No hand-written player text in code: every word is a bank part, composed by the engine. No dice: which point and which stand come from the person's own records, never a draw. Ask why: the relative cites the address only because their knowledge holds it, and the line's source ids name the address and the record behind the reason. One rule, all 56 places: nothing names a place, and the tests draw their places. An effect is a read, not a record: what the player answered is read by a later talk, named by record id in the pull request.

**Done when.** A second session, in the running game on main at 1440x900, starts three new lives in drawn places it writes down with their seeds. In each, on the first evening it talks to a relative and sees them name something the President said that is in that life's address (it reads the address in the journal or the news to confirm), with a stand and a reason from their own job, money or town; the three talks do not share a sentence. The replies are at most four, each a different move; each names the point, the town, the President or the person, and no two carry the same insert. The answer to the reply about the town says one thing that world keeps about the town or its own law on the question, never only that it is too soon to say. It answers, opens the same person's card or a later talk, and finds the answer on record. `git grep -n "address-view" -- src data` returns nothing, and tests/guards/english passes with the lowered counts.
