**What.** A counted household's work outside the town's firms is kept as a share, a number such as 0.4, tied to no particular adult (`unplacedWorkers`). The same adults are also treated as jobless and looking. When one of them takes a real job, the job is added and nothing takes the share away, so the household holds both. Arrivals make it compound: a household that moves in is handed its new place's share per open adult on arrival, and its adults then go looking and take real jobs as well. Measured in Wickliffe, Oklahoma (seed 8832429ff1e53841): the crew's vital-statistics tool read 5.7% of adults at work at the opening, 125% after eleven years and 200% after eighteen, and median household earnings went from $40,665 to $120,056 in eleven years. In year one 1,865 counted adults took a real job, and after one year 1,576 of the 3,651 household records held more jobs than working-age adults. In one of the world's three place records the rate was already 1.5 after one year: 1,738 jobs for 1,124 adults. After three years the arrivals held 9,629 jobs for 5,586 working-age adults, 1.7 each and rising. The audit then tested the cause without changing game code: in a second run of the same world it cleared every household's share once a month, leaving only the jobs its members really hold. After three years the arrivals held 0.5 jobs per adult, and only 64 of 7,025 household records held more jobs than adults, against 5,149 of 7,254 in the game as it is. The same test in Frankfort, South Dakota (seed cd7e15c74e5f7692) gave the same answer: arrivals held 1.4 jobs per adult after three years as it is, and 0.6 with the share cleared. Fix it at the cause, for every counted household in every place: tie each share to an adult, or drop that adult's share the day they take a real job (the audit's call), so one adult never holds two jobs and is never paid for both.

**Time box.** 8 hours for one Sonnet session. When it runs out, push what passes, set the state honestly with a one-line note, and stop.

**Rules.** One model, two tiers: a counted adult holds at most the jobs a full person could, by the same rule times the count, and a share that no adult holds is a second population of workers. Wire to the world: the share pays wages from the place's outside account, so removing it must stop that pay on the journal the same day the real job starts. Sliding scales and causes: a household's work changes because an adult was hired, laid off or moved, never because a per-place rate was handed to it on arrival. Owner, November 8: "ranges aren't targets"; this is not a pull toward a real employment rate, it is one adult holding one job. Owner, November 8: twenty years in seconds is fine "if the result makes sense".

**Done when.** A second session, not the builder, runs this in the running game. Start the bench (`npm run bench`, play at `/play`) with the Place field empty so the place is a drawn place, and write down the place and seed. With dev mode's Run on the Calendar, advance three years, 366 days at a time. After each year read the Metrics tab's People at work for the town and dev mode's Watch panel: the jobs held by counted households never exceed their working-age adults, in total and for the households that moved in (the builder adds that one line to Watch from the same records if no line shows it; a screenshot each year). Then the two named repros, Wickliffe, Oklahoma, seed 8832429ff1e53841, and Frankfort, South Dakota, seed cd7e15c74e5f7692, three years each: arrivals hold at most one job per adult (the audit's cleared-share test gave 0.5 and 0.6, against 1.7 and 1.4 as the game was), and the household records with more jobs than adults are near the audit's 64 of 7,025, not 5,149 of 7,254. Follow the dollars in a twin world with and without the fix, by account: the place's outside account pays a household less on the payday after its adult takes a real job, by record id, and the journal balances. A test in `src/core2` draws a place, plays a year and fails if any counted household holds more jobs, share included, than working-age adults. The owner plays it: ten years in, the Metrics tab shows fewer people at work than adults in town, and typical earnings that have not tripled.

**Notes.** From the Fable audit, Pillar 7. Measured there: a 19-year run of Wickliffe, Oklahoma with readings each year, the same world's jobs split by household, its first year again with every household record open, then the world run twice for three years (as it is, and with the share cleared monthly), and the same pair in Frankfort, South Dakota. The audit did not separate how much of the growth in household records (2,339 at the opening to 3,651 after a year) was arrivals and how much was regrouping, and did not look up which place each of the three place records is; its call is that neither changes the finding. It changed no game code; its call on the fix is the one above, and "the two-world test in pillar 7 is the acceptance check, and it is Sonnet work". Read on main, Nov 10 (helper): `hireCounted` calls `addCountedJob`, which adds the job and the wage and never touches `unplacedWorkers`; `startRegionalWork` adds the record's whole weight to the share for one hire; `arrivals` sets a newcomer's share from `unplacedRates`, the place's share per open adult that day, so a place already above one job per adult hands that rate to everyone who moves in. The tool that reads these counts (`scripts/dev-tests/vitals.ts`) is the one that showed the owner "more people working, less poverty" on November 8, so its long-run readings are not evidence until this lands. Related: `world-county-opens-almost-jobless-in-the-counts` (the opening side of the same counts), `people-unplaced-outside-employer` (open: gives commuting workers an employer on the books, which would also tie each share to a person), `core-counted-household-jobs-in-step` (done: the household's job list follows hires).
