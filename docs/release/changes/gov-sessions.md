id: gov-sessions
impact: minor
section: Changed
title: Legislatures sit through their sessions instead of passing a year's bills in a day
---

The state legislature and Congress now convene on their real dates, take bills in through the session's opening weeks in both chambers, and vote them week by week once committee has had them; bills the floor never reaches die at adjournment. A bill you put on a body's calendar goes to its next real sitting: the council's next meeting, or the legislature's next floor week, instead of two weeks out.

A recall needs a resident moved enough to organize it, and one organizer brings one petition, against the member they hold most to account, instead of petitions against every member the same day. Where a state allows a recall only for cause, a vote on an ordinance is no ground for one, and no petition starts early in a term or again after one failed.
