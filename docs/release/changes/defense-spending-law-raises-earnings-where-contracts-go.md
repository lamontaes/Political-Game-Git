title: A federal law that grows defense spending raises earnings in the states that draw the contracts.
---

When Congress answers yes to "should defense spending grow faster than
inflation?", defense contracts grow each year at the pace they grew in the
years they beat inflation, for as long as the law stands and for up to five
years. Each state gets extra contracts in proportion to what it draws today,
and its earnings rise by about one and a half times the share of its output
those contracts add. A state with big bases and shipyards gains most; a state
with few gains little. A later law that answers no ends the build-up the day it
takes effect and earnings go back.
