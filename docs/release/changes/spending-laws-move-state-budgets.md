title: A law that raises the juvenile court age or funds parks now moves the state's budget
---

Before, a state could raise the age at which teenagers go to juvenile court,
lower it again, or dedicate money to its parks, and its budget spent exactly
what it had planned.

Now the state's corrections or parks line changes from the month the law
takes effect, and the next budget the state adopts builds the change in.
Raising the juvenile court age costs about $12.58 a resident each year,
from New York's Raise the Age appropriation; lowering it saves the same.
Dedicated parks funding adds about $11.21 a resident each year, the average
of Missouri's parks sales tax share and Minnesota's Parks and Trails Fund.
Both are marked as estimated from those states' figures.
