title: Town and township boards elect their seats on the terms their state sets
---

A town or township board (a selectboard, board of selectmen, town board, township committee or board of supervisors) now elects its seats on the term its state's law sets. Where the law staggers the seats, as in Vermont's one selectboard member a year for three years, only that year's seat is on the ballot, so you run for one seat against that seat's field and the count, the seating and the call afterward all follow that one race. Where the law elects the whole board at once, every seat is still one "vote for N" race. Before, every seat came up every year and was counted together, so a candidate who lost their own seat's race could be seated while an unopposed one was left off. A sitting member no longer runs for a second seat on their own board.
