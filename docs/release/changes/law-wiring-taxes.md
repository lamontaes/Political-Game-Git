section: Improved
title: Tax laws reach the people who pay them
---

When a government changes an income tax, a property tax or a corporate tax, the new rate is now what comes out of paychecks, home tax bills and company profits, for named residents and for the wider population alike, and each payer's record names the law that took the money. A graduated income tax follows a changed top rate. Businesses owned by a corporation now pay the state's tax as well as the federal one: a company short of cash owes each government its own share, and still pays a state whose tax was repealed. A higher state corporate rate now lowers pay a little in the town. An estate settles under the death taxes in force and names who paid them, for a resident of the wider population as well as a named one. When no one inherits and the state takes the estate, it pays the state's inheritance tax only where that state's own statute taxes a government heir; each state with the tax carries its statute's wording. A town council's draft of an income tax is checked against the state's grant of that power in force. Each month, as it closes its books, a treasury reads its balanced-budget, pension and reserve rules before it pays, and the pension rule names the public workers whose pensions it funds.
