id: town-boards-make-local-law
impact: minor
section: Changed
title: A place inside a town or township gets its local law from that town's board
---

A census-designated place inside a New England or New York town, or inside a
New Jersey, Pennsylvania or Midwest township, has no government of its own. Its
local government is the town or township it lies in, and that board now makes
its law. It is seated at the size its state's statute sets, read for 15 states,
meets every two weeks, and votes on the ordinances its members file. The
ordinances govern the place unless a state law on the same question outranks
them. Before, these places took their county's board, or had no local body at
all where the county has no government.
