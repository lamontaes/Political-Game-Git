title: A city council no longer passes state laws
---

A town council used to take up questions that only the state can settle,
such as whether the state should have an income tax, how many terms state
legislators may serve, or who draws the legislature's district lines. It
now votes only on questions that are its own to decide, such as its budget,
its transit, its zoning and its police oversight board. The same rule holds
in every state, in the District of Columbia and in the territories. An
ordinance a town passed on a state question in an older save stays on the
record but no longer counts as the law there.

Where a state has no law on a question and leaves its towns free to act, a
town's own ordinance now counts in that town. Where a state bars its towns
from acting, the state's rule still holds.
