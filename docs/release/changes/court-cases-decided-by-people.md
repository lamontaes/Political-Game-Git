title: A criminal case is decided by the people in it, not by chance
---

A criminal case no longer goes one way or another because a number came up.
A regulator sends a finding to prosecutors when the record shows the person
knew the rule: they had been found at fault for the same thing before, or they
denied what the finding showed. Prosecutors charge when the evidence would
probably convict.

The person charged then chooses between pleading guilty and going to trial,
from the evidence, the lighter sentence a plea brings, what a guilty plea
would do to a career in public life, and their own temperament. At a trial,
twelve people from their town who do not know them hear the case. Each juror
votes from the evidence, the reasonable-doubt rule and their own temperament,
and a juror who disagrees with most of the room usually comes around on the
second vote. A jury that still cannot agree brings a retrial, and after a
second one the charge is dropped.

After a plea or a conviction, one of the state's own trial judges chooses
between probation and jail. The judge weighs the plea, any earlier record, a
violent offense or an abuse of public trust, and their own view of firm
sentences. Every one of these choices is recorded with its reasons.

Asking for clemency and answering it no longer settle a close call by chance
either.
