title: The D.C. Council passes acts, and the Mayor signs or returns them
---

A life in Washington now opens with the whole Council of the District of
Columbia in its seats: the Chairman, four at-large members and a member for
each of the eight wards. The Council meets on its own every two weeks. A member
brings in an act on a question the District decides, such as consumer data
privacy, and the act comes back for a second reading at least two weeks later.
If it carries both times, it goes to the Mayor, who signs it or sends it back.
A signed act takes effect after Congress's review period. In a year of play,
about two dozen acts came in and roughly half became law.

A councilmember can do the same from the city screen. You bring in an act and
vote at each reading. The screen shows which reading comes next and the earliest
day it can be held. Once the act passes, it shows the day the Mayor must act by.
If you are the Mayor, you sign or return each act the Council sends you, and an
act you leave alone becomes law without your signature once ten weekdays pass.
If the Mayor returns an act, the Council has thirty days to reenact it, and that
takes two-thirds of the members voting.

The Council's own rules are not read yet, so for now any member can bring in an
act, there is no committee stage, and both readings are decided by a simple
majority. How often the Council meets, how each member votes, and when a Mayor
you do not play returns an act are marked stand-ins until they are researched.
