When a state's equalize-school-funding law is in force, the state books a pool of school aid and pays the town's city and county governments their shares through its treasury, more to a poorer region and less to a richer one; their schools lines rise by what they receive. The pool is 7 percent of the state's local school spending (Lafortune, Rothstein and Schanzenbach 2018, AEJ: Applied; the statewide conversion is provisional), and the same aid is built again from the Census base each fiscal year. School spending and graduation follow the law through the schools line.
