title: Medical bills and medical debt run in the background
---

Households in your town now pay their own doctor and hospital bills each month. A family with someone seriously ill pays more, and people with a health plan, Medicare or Medicaid pay a smaller share than people with no coverage. A bill a family cannot pay waits four months, then goes to collections as medical debt on its books, and a year later the bank sees it when the family asks for a loan. Expanding Medicaid now shrinks that debt: the adults it covers, in town households and in the wider population alike, get smaller bills, and the state and federal governments pay the town's hospital or clinic the rest of their care instead of sending the adults a monthly check. You take no step; it all happens in the background and shows in how families' money holds up.
