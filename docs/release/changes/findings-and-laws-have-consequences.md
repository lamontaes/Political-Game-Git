title: Ethics findings, laws and a governor's record now have consequences
---

A public ethics finding against a candidate now costs them support in any race
they are running, and a finding about campaign money they took for themselves
orders that money repaid to the committee. Their family, household, colleagues
and party organizers hear about it and decide for themselves whether to stand
by them or keep their distance, and a party chapter weighs it before agreeing
to help their campaign. A finding is remembered when they run again.

Laws now reach the economy through the money they actually move. When a
government pays out under an appropriation or a program, that state's own
economy picks up; when it collects a tax, demand comes out. Passing a law does
nothing to the economy until money moves.

A sitting governor who runs again starts ahead if unemployment fell on their
watch, and behind if it rose.

How large each of these effects should be has not been researched yet. The
game uses cautious placeholder sizes until it has.
