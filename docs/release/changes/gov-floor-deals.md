title: Members trade their votes for sections of their own, and opponents move poison pills
---

Before a chamber votes, a sponsor short of a majority now works the members nearest even: each asks for a section of their own cause in the bill, and the sponsor adds it where they, and everyone they have already dealt with, still back the bill with it. On a bill headed for passage, the member most against it may move a poison pill, a section of their own cause the bill's backers dislike, if it would pass and leave the bill short; whether they play it that way is their own temperament. Each amendment is a public record the press can take up.
