title: A bribe can buy a vote, and a scandal reaches voters through the news
---

A council member who takes a bribe on a bill now votes with that payment as one of their reasons, on that bill only. If the bribe comes out and someone takes it to the press, the local news carries it, and each voter holds it against the official only once the story has reached them, weighed by how much they trust the press.

Officials and candidates hold town halls when a question before them splits the town or their race is coming up, and each town hall records why it was called. Employers organize against any bill that binds their line of business, not only a minimum-wage raise. An official's open constituent cases sit on their desk, soonest deadline first.
