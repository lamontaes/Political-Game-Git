section: Improved
title: Candidates ask for volunteers and endorsements, and personality moves the answers
---

A candidate in a town with a race now asks the people they can reach to give their evenings and the people closest to them to put their names behind the campaign, and each person answers from their own record and temperament: open and outgoing people say yes more often, at about the rates real surveys find. Volunteers come to the campaign's events and an endorsement reaches the endorser's circle. Giving, answering the door, running for office, standing again and holding a party's line lean on personality by researched amounts instead of hand-set ones, and older officials are likelier to retire.
