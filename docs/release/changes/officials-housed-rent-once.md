title: Officials, the President's passed-over nominees and the Fed are no longer evicted in their first months.
---

Officials and the people around them keep their homes. Before, at the opening the housing engine wrote a lease for every household outside the town, including people no money moves for: the nominees a President passes over for the cabinet, the Fed's governors and the reserve banks' presidents. They had no pay and no costs, so they fell behind at once. Housed officials paid for their home twice, through the lease and again inside their living cost. Now a household no engine pays or charges keeps its home on record but owes no rent, and a housed official pays the home through its lease, with the rest of their living cost going to everything else. A cabinet secretary is paid by the federal government at the Executive Schedule's Level I rate ($250,600 in 2025), not by the played state as its treasurer. In a year of play at Washington, D.C., West Dennis, Massachusetts and Bennington, Vermont, removals of these households fell from 56, 66 and 68 to 6, 0 and 5. Those left are press outlet owners and one D.C. council member, whose living cost still uses up all their take-home pay.
