section: Changed
title: When someone passes on a story, your replies say what it has to do with you.
---

When a relative or a friend tells you about a story they saw, your replies come from your own life: where you stand on the question the story is about ("Count me in favor of a higher age to buy a handgun."), that you worked at the place that closed, that you saw it on the same station, or a question about what it means for your town. They answer from their own side and say when they heard it, and they remember where you stood. The stock answers ("I think it's terrible.", "What do you make of the news?") are gone.
