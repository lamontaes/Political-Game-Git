section: Changed
title: Hellos and goodbyes name you, the last time you met, and what you talked about.
---

When someone you run into says hello, they say your name if they know it, or when they last saw you ("The last time I saw you was a few weeks ago."). When a talk ends, they name what it was about ("Good luck at Brooks Public Schools. Take care.") or, if it was about nothing in particular, someone of yours they know. At home, a goodbye says the part of the day the clock shows ("We'll talk more later tonight."), never the morning in the evening. Each line is put together from pieces, so the same words rarely come twice. The stock goodbyes ("I've got to run.", "Have a nice day.") are gone.
