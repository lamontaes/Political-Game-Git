title: Errands get done, rent is due, officeholders are paid, and degrees can be applied for
---

Choosing "Get things done" now does the week's errands, and a new list comes a
week later. On the first of each month the person you play pays their share of
rent, food and bills. A person holding public office is paid a salary.
Associate's, bachelor's and master's degrees can be applied for and finished.

The errands take two and a half hours on the calendar and clear the list.
Before, the same list came back every week.

A life with something already waiting can be offered one new thing a day.
Before, one unanswered request stopped anything new from arriving.

The monthly cost is one placeholder figure of $1,500 for every state and town
until the research on real costs comes back. The first month you cannot cover
becomes a moment of its own.

A governor, legislator, judge or civil servant you play is paid every week.
Before, a governor's savings did not move in four years. The salary is one
placeholder of $60,000 a year for every office until real salaries by office
and state are researched.

The personal and tax screens now write money with a dollar sign.

A finished bachelor's counts for law school and graduate study. Until the
admissions research comes back, every applicant is admitted, and length and
tuition match the game's existing degree paths.
