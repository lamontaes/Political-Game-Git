section: Changed
title: A governor decides what the budget does with money a law gained or lost the state
---

Before, when a law added money to a state or took money away, the next
budget handled it the same way everywhere: extra money went to programs,
and a loss was cut only where a law required a balanced budget.

Now the governor decides when the next budget is adopted, from their own
principles. A governor who favors fiscal restraint saves a gain in the
rainy-day fund; one who favors public programs spends it. For a loss under a
balanced-budget law, the first cuts programs and the second draws on the
reserve above its required floor. Without that law, a governor who wants a
balanced budget cuts, and one who does not keeps programs and runs a
deficit. Each decision is recorded with the governor and the reason, and the
money it moves reaches the state's programs and the jobs they pay for.
