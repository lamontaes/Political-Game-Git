title: Governors you are not playing sign bills, and legislatures vote on overrides
---

A governor played by the game used to replay a veto written into the few
bills legislatures file on their own, so almost every bill that reached the
desk was vetoed and nothing became law. Every sitting governor now decides
each bill for themselves, the same way you do as governor. In test worlds
with no player governor, Nebraska and Alaska went from none of eighteen bills
becoming law in three years to nine of eighteen. How often a governor should
sign is still being researched, and the current rate is a placeholder.

When a governor does veto a bill, the legislature now votes on overriding
it, with each member deciding and the state's own override rule counting
the result. Before, nobody ever tried. A player who sits in the chamber is
never voted for; until they cast their own ballot they are recorded absent.

A governor's office history no longer tells the player that the game has no
bills written for their state; it says only that no bill reached the desk.
