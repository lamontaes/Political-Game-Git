section: Added
title: A council member's and a mayor's day in office
impact: minor
---

A town council or county board now meets on its own calendar, on a weekday at its hour, as often as bodies of its kind and size meet. When your own bill is on the agenda, the day stops at the meeting: you hear public comment, vote each item yourself or leave it to your character, and adjourn. A bill that passes waits on your desk the next morning to sign, veto or leave to your character. You choose what stops you; everything else still happens, and your character votes and decides by its own views instead of abstaining. A local seat is a job with its pay and hours: a part-time seat pays its stipend from the town's or county's treasury on the 1st. Office hours, a town hall, an interview, a meeting with a department head, a talk with a colleague about your bill and the state of the city are now choices in your day. The seat screen's Chamber, Ask, Favor and Schedule vote buttons are gone; drafting puts a bill on the next agenda.
