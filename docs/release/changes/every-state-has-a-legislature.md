title: Every state has a legislature now, not only the nine that were researched
---

Nine states had a legislature. The other forty-one and Puerto Rico had none at
all — no chambers, no seats, no floor vote — so a life lived in Texas or
California could reach a seat and find nothing to sit in and no bill to carry.
That is fixed: a state without a researched legislature now has one anyway.

A generated legislature has two chambers, a real number of seats in each, a
quorum, a passage vote, a governor with a veto and a threshold to override it.
The figures differ state to state, so crossing a state line changes what a
chamber looks like instead of finding the same one everywhere, and a given
state keeps its own forever, in every session and every save. Seat counts fall
anywhere in the range the researched states span; veto windows and override
thresholds are only ever ones a state actually enacted, because those are
choices a legislature writes rather than numbers that could be anything.

A senate is always smaller than its own house, which is the one thing American
bicameralism never gets wrong.

Where a state's own constitution has been read, that is what applies and
nothing here is consulted. Reading a state's real law replaces its generated
legislature entirely.

The District of Columbia is the exception and keeps none: it is legislated for
by a single Council rather than by a House and a Senate, and a generated
bicameral legislature would be a shape the District has never had.

Where a state's own constitution has been read for its veto override, that is
the threshold the game uses, even in a state whose legislature is otherwise the
game's own. Tennessee overrides on a simple majority, not the two thirds the
game would otherwise have picked; North Carolina on three fifths of those
present and voting; West Virginia on a simple majority for an ordinary bill.
Real law always wins over a generated figure.
