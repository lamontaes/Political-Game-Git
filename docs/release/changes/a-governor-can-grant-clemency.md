title: People serving a sentence can ask for clemency, and the law of their state decides who answers
---

Someone the game sentences to jail or probation can now ask for a pardon or a
shorter sentence. Whether they ask depends on their situation and their own
temperament, and they ask again when a new governor takes office.

Each state, D.C. and territory answers under its own real rule. In Oregon the
governor decides alone. In Texas the board must recommend it first. In
Alabama the board decides without the governor. In Massachusetts the
Governor's Council must also agree. In Arizona the board's yes is needed only
for crimes committed before 1994. A governor the game plays answers from their
own principles, how they know the person, whether the case was in the papers
and how close the next election is. When you are governor, the request comes to
your desk.

A granted pardon or commutation ends the sentence that day, so a jailed person
is free to campaign and hold office again.
