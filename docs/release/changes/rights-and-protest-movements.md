title: Rights and protest movements with leaders, marches, backlash and wins
---

Movements now form in a state from what has happened there. After unrest
driven by job losses lasts more than one season, someone in the state can
begin organizing a worker movement. When a state enacts a law that takes away
equal treatment or equal opportunity, someone can begin organizing a movement
to reverse it. The leader is an ordinary resident who usually holds no office.

A movement grows while anger in its state holds, marches in public when it is
strong, and draws organized opposition. It wins when a law answering its
demand is enacted in the state or nationally, and fades when it dwindles. When
its leader dies, someone else takes up the lead.

Under Who you are, a Movements section shows the movements in your state. You
can found one on any policy question and lead it, join one, oppose one, leave,
take up the lead when no one holds it, or speak in support, condemn it or call
for calm.

Movements do not yet spread between states, change what people believe, or
make their leaders targets of political violence, and unequal treatment in
daily life does not start one. Every rate is a placeholder until research
returns.
