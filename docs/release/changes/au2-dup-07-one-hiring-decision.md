title: Employers choose who they hire, the same way for everyone
---

Every hire in town now ends in the same choice. The owner of a business, or the person who runs the place, or the longest-serving employee, weighs each applicant against what the work usually asks of a new hire: the schooling and the experience behind them, whether someone who works there put them forward, and how well the employer knows them. How much each counts depends on the employer's own temperament. Your applications, your neighbors' applications, the town's rounds of hiring and the jobs a budget funds are all decided this way, and the open jobs on the listing board are the same ones for everybody.
