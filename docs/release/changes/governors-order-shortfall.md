id: governors-order-shortfall
impact: patch
section: Changed
title: A governor decides whether a mid-year shortfall is met by cuts or the rainy-day fund first
---

Before, a state that fell short mid-year always cut every program by up to
3% first, then drew its rainy-day fund, then cut again, whatever its
governor believed.

Now the governor decides from their own principles. One who favors keeping
a reserve cuts programs before touching it; one who puts public programs
first draws the reserve before cutting. A governor with no view on it, and
every county and city, draws the reserve first, since reserves are kept to
protect services through a temporary revenue shortfall. Each cut and draw
is recorded with who decided and why, in the order the money moved.
When the reserve runs out first, the cut that follows says how much the
reserve covered and how much was cut.
