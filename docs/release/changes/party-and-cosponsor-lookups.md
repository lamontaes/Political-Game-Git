section: Improved
title: Legislatures count their votes faster in later years
---

Every chamber vote asks two things about the bill and its members: who signed
on to the bill, and which party each member belongs to. Finding the signers
meant reading every event the world had ever recorded, and a member's party
was worked out again for every question the chamber weighed that day. The
signers are now kept by bill as events are written, and a member's party is
worked out once for each moment of the world. In South Fork, Pennsylvania,
game years one to five take 1.3, 4.7, 2.8, 6.2 and 5.5 seconds less. Every
vote comes out the same: the world after each of five years is exactly the
same as before.
