title: Firms in a big town stop closing for want of cash to pay their workers
---

In a town opened at its full Census size, shops, offices and local TV stations used to run out of cash and close, taking their workers' jobs with them. Their sales now grow with the town as their wages do, and a shop staffed by a big town's workers can serve as many customers as those workers stand for. A business short of cash on a payday draws the savings it holds at the bank before the payroll goes unpaid, and keeps enough there for its workers' full payroll. An owner no longer draws out the cash the next payroll needs, and a business owned by a resident of a big town pays that owner's draw and profit tax once, not once per household the owner stands for.
