title: Officeholders decide for themselves whether to run again
---

Members of Congress, state lawmakers, governors and town officials no longer
retire at a fixed age or on a random draw. Each one decides when the next
election comes up, from the office they hold, their own health record, their
own odds of living through the next term, the people they care for at home,
and their temperament. A town official deciding whether to resign mid-term
weighs the same things. The reason that weighed most is recorded with the
choice, so a governor who steps down says why.

A person the parties ask to run weighs their own health and age the same way,
so nobody past a set age is left off the list.

Sitting governors in a world nobody is playing now run again; before, only a
game with a played character let them.
