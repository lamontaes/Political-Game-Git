title: What happens in a person's own life now moves their chance of dying
---

A person's death hazard now reads their own records, not only the town's averages. A 55- to 64-year-old whose Medicaid coverage the state pays runs a quarter below the life table a year after it begins, and runs above it a year after it ends; the town-wide cut from the Medicaid expansion no longer lands on them a second time. A worker whose long job ended when the business closed or cut its staff carries a higher chance of dying that fades over twenty years. A worker who holds a job at 55 or more hours a week carries a higher chance of dying of heart disease or stroke for as long as they hold it. Each of these shows in the person's recorded reasons with the record it stands on.
