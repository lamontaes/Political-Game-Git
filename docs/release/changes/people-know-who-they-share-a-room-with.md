title: People know the council, congregation or club they belong to, and their children's classmates' parents
---

A person now knows the people they meet in the same place: the other members
of a town council, a congregation, a club or another group they take part in,
and the parents of their children's classmates. A class is the children at one
school who started kindergarten the same fall. Nothing is drawn; both people
have to be on record in the same place now.

A party registration is not a place people meet, so it makes no acquaintance.
Neighbors still come from the neighbor contacts already on record, because a
home has no place on a street yet.

Whom a person knows decides whom a mayor, governor or President can appoint,
and whom a person tells about how a law touched them.
