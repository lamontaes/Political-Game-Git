title: Screens say what they know, not how they came to know it
---

A person's card used to end by telling you what it had not been able to look
up — "their education and work history are not known to you here" — and an
unrelated person's opened with "No record establishes a relationship." The
economic figures came with the dates they were retrieved and the digests of the
files they came from. The charter screen carried publisher identifiers and a
link to the survey behind them. Standing for office offered to explain what the
game did not know about the seat, which unfolded into notes about which
documents had and had not been read.

All of it was true, and all of it was addressed to whoever builds the game
rather than to whoever is playing it. Those screens now say what they have to
say and stop: the figures without the paperwork, the charter as a document, the
directory admitting it does not list tuition the way a person would admit it.

Nothing became less careful. What the game declines to tell you, it still
declines to tell you — it simply no longer reads out the reason. The one place
that gained something is public office: whoever holds it now appears as holding
it on their card, whether or not you have ever met them, because that is a
public fact and waiting to be introduced to the governor was never how anybody
learned who the governor was.

Developers keep every line of it, on the routes built for that.
