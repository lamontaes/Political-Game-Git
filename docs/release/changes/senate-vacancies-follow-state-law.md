title: An empty Senate seat is filled the way that state's law says
---

An empty U.S. Senate seat is now filled the way that state's law says. In
states where the governor may not appoint, like Wisconsin and Kentucky, the
seat stays empty until a special election. In states that require it, like
Arizona and Oregon, the appointee shares the departed senator's party;
elsewhere the governor appoints someone of the governor's own party. Texas,
Massachusetts and eight other states hold a prompt special election instead
of waiting for November.
