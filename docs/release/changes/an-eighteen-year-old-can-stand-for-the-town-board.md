title: An eighteen-year-old can stand for the town, township or county board
impact: patch
---

A new adult could not file for the local board that governs them. A town board's age was copied from the state house, usually 21, every county commission seat was refused until its rules were read, and a village inside a town or township was offered no local seat at all. A seat on a town, township or county board now asks the qualified-elector age, eighteen, as a labeled estimate from state codes, until the place's own rule is read. In 52 of the 56 places a resident can file for it; Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands have no local board on record.
