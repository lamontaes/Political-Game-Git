title: Taking office is a swearing-in, with a speech where the office gives one.
---

The day you take an office, the day stops for the oath. The clerk who took your filing (or whoever the town's records name) gives it, your family and the other officeholders are in the room, and you answer in your own voice: a plain "I do," a pledge, a thank-you, or a question about how it goes. A mayor, a county executive, a governor or a president then gets the floor, and what you say is heard by the people who were there: it can move what they think and be told again at home. Your journal says who swore you in.
