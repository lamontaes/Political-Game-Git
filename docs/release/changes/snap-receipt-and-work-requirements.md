title: Each state now keeps how many of its residents receive SNAP, and a work requirement lowers it.
---

Every state, D.C., Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands now keep the share of
their residents who receive SNAP, starting from the fiscal 2024 count the
USDA publishes. A work requirement for assistance lowers it about 5% three
months after it takes effect: studies find such requirements cut
participation by about half among the adults they reach, with no gain in
work. The 2025 federal law puts that requirement in force everywhere, so
every place's share steps down from early 2026. Puerto Rico, American Samoa
and the Northern Mariana Islands run a different food program and record no
SNAP share.
