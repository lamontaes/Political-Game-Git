title: People a law costs band together, and residents contact officials and attend meetings
---

When a law costs at least six residents of a town a tenth of a month's pay or
more, a group forms there against it. Each person decides whether to join from
how hard the law hit them, their temperament, and whether they already know a
member through family or work. The group presses legislators to change the law and works
against the candidates its members blame in the next town election.

Each year, about a fifth of a town's adults contact an elected official and
about a quarter attend a meeting of the town's government. Older and
longer-settled residents, people with a job, people a law cost money, group
members, quick reactors and people with strong feelings about an official do
it more often. Where no local government sits, residents write to their
governor instead.
