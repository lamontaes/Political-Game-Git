title: Screens, boards and posters in the places you go show what is happening
---

The screens, boards, posters and papers painted into the places you visit
now show what is going on in your world that day, instead of sitting blank.

- In the Senate chamber, the panels on the wall list the latest votes and the
  bills filed. A county commission's screen shows the county's own latest votes.
- In a campaign office or a party office, the cork board carries posters for
  the people running in your races, and the whiteboard lists your own
  appointments for the week.
- At an office desk, a clipboard holds your appointments and a sheet on the
  board lists the bills filed in your town.
- The brochures at a clerk's counter and the notice board in the park
  describe how your town's public services are doing.
- The screens at a convention hall or an election-night party show a
  candidate or the evening news, and for a few days after a race is decided
  they show the count.
- A television in a living room shows your local station.

When there is nothing to show, the surface stays as it was painted. Nothing
is made up to fill it. At night, the paper and card on the walls dim with
the room while the screens stay lit.
