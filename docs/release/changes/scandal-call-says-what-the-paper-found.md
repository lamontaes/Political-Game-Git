title: A reporter's call about a scandal now plays, and says what the paper found
---

When a paper runs a story that you took a bribe or steered a contract to a firm you or your family own, the reporter now calls you. Before, that call never happened. The reporter says who paid you and for which vote, or which contract went to whose firm. Your answers fit the story: you can admit it, decline, ask who talked, or deny it, and if you deny it the reporter has the papers. The next day's story quotes what you said on the call, or says you declined. If you lie on a call where the other person has proof, they now answer and you can reply again; before, the call went silent after a lie. When a paper prints two stories the same day about your votes for the same firm, the first is no longer called the second.
