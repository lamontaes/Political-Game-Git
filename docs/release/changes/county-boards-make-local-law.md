section: Changed
title: A place with no town government gets its local law from its county board
---

A life in a place with no town government of its own, such as a
census-designated place or a Puerto Rico community, now has a local body that
makes law. The county board, parish council or municipio legislature meets
every two weeks, its members file ordinances on what their own principles press
them to, and they vote on each one. An adopted county ordinance governs the
place. Before, the board was seated but never met, so these places passed no
local law at all. A member with no view on an ordinance that would undo the
body's own law in force now votes to keep it, so a board no longer passes an
ordinance and its repeal back and forth.
