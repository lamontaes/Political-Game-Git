title: Each new character has a childhood of their own
---

When a new game summarizes a grown character's earlier life, two people in two
different states used to get the same childhood, line for line. Each one's family
moved when they were six, and each changed schools at seven. Each made room at a lunch table at
ten, and started as a store assistant at sixteen, a job that ended the day
they graduated. Every one of those things happened on their birthday.

Now each childhood is drawn for that person. School years start in late
summer and end in late spring. Some households move and some don't, and a
move within the same town no longer pulls the child out of school with
nowhere to go. Some teenagers hold a part-time job, and it ends when it ends.
The remembered moments are drawn from the situations the game can already
play: a falling-out with a classmate or a lunch table, a teacher's offer of
help, and one to three more. The classmate who was there
remembers what they saw, not the player's own memory of it.

A character who starts at eighteen before their final school year ends is
still in high school.

How often children move, when school years run in each state, and which
jobs teenagers hold are placeholders for now. They are filed as research
questions.
