title: A summarized childhood is no longer the same for everyone
---

When a new game summarized a grown character's earlier life, two people in two
different states used to get the same childhood, line for line. Each one's
family moved when they were six, and each changed schools at seven. Each made
room at a lunch table at ten, and started as a store assistant at sixteen, a job
that ended the day they graduated. Every one of those things happened on their
birthday.

Now the dates and events of a summarized childhood are drawn for that person.
School years start in late summer and end in late spring. Some households move
and some don't, and a move within the same town no longer pulls the child out
of school with nowhere to go. Some teenagers hold a part-time job, and it ends
when it ends. The classmate who was there remembers what they saw, not the
player's own memory of it.

A character who starts at eighteen before their final school year ends is
still in high school.

This does not change childhoods that are played. The remembered moments still
come from the same small set of situations, with the same wording in every
life. Some of those situations describe a new baby, an illness or money
trouble that nothing in the save holds. That is the next piece of work.

How often children move, when school years run in each state, and which
jobs teenagers hold are placeholders for now. They are filed as research
questions.
