title: Crime victims decide whether to call the police
---

Whether an ordinary local crime reaches the police is now each victim's own
decision instead of a fixed share. A victim weighs what was done to them
against the trouble of reporting, how close they are to the person who did it,
their own past with the police, their temperament and their age. Somebody
robbed by a stranger usually calls; somebody hit by a relative often keeps it
quiet. The same victim in the same circumstances always decides the same way.
