title: Running for office lists every office where you live, each with its own age
---

A character too young for every office nearby used to see one unlabeled
sentence under "Running for office" ("This office asks for a candidate to be
at least 24.") instead of the offices themselves. In Rapid City, South Dakota,
an eighteen-year-old now sees the state House and Senate seats, the city
council and the mayor, each with its own requirement beside its name, and
choosing one shows why it cannot be filed for yet.

Every minimum age now reads the same way: "You must be at least 21 to stand
for this office." The governor's section no longer explains that the game had
not read the state's rule.

The same held in Louisiana and Indiana, where the state House's "at least 24"
sat directly above the governor's "at least 21" with nothing to say which was
which. Each age now stays with its own office, and the governor's section shows
one age: the one a filing is held to.

"Who governs where you live" now uses the state's own word. Lake Charles lists
Calcasieu Parish and no longer mentions counties, and Anchorage speaks of
boroughs. The note about places that span several of them appears only when
yours does, and gives the actual number ("This place lies in two parishes, and
each is listed above."). A place with no separate county, parish or borough
government is told so plainly instead of citing a government listing.
