id: state-effective-date-rules-35-places
impact: patch
section: Changed
title: New state laws take effect on their own state's date in 35 places
---

A new law that names no date of its own now takes effect when its state's rule
says in 35 places, up from 5. Georgia's take effect July 1 or January 1 by the
half of the year they are signed, Virginia's and Vermont's the next July 1,
Connecticut's and Nevada's the next October 1, New York's 20 days after they
become law, and Colorado's, Delaware's, Guam's and the Virgin Islands' at once.
North Dakota, Iowa and Rhode Island use a date in the same year, or a set
number of days once that date has passed.

Kentucky's laws now take effect the day after 90 full days have passed since
the session closed, so a 2026 law takes effect July 15, 2026. Illinois counts
from the legislature's final vote, which each new law now records: January 1
for a bill passed before June 1, and June 1 of the next year for the rest.
California's take effect on the January 1 after 90 days, and first-year bills
on the next January 1.

The other 21 places count from the end of a session the game doesn't record,
from publication, or from Congress's review of D.C. laws, so they still wait
90 days until the game can date them.

West Virginia cities now start unable to set rent limits: the state lists
what its cities may do, and rent is not on the list.
