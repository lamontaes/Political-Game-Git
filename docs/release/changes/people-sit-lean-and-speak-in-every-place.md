title: People sit, lean and speak where the picture has room for them
---

Before, only 11 place pictures marked where people could stand, and everyone
stood facing you in a row, like models. A courtroom, the Senate floor or a
state capitol had nowhere to put anyone at all.

Now every place picture (all 50 rooms and street scenes and all 56 state
capitols) marks where people can be: open floor at several distances, the
chairs at a desk or table, a bench or a sofa, the lectern, and a wall or
doorway to lean on. Each spot knows which way a person there faces and how
large they are at that distance, and a person on a stage, a dais or the
capitol steps is sized for that raised floor.

The people on shift in a place now take those spots: the city clerk sits
behind the counter, office staff sit at their desks, others wait on the floor
or by a wall, and nearer people are drawn in front of farther ones. Waiting
people are set to fold their arms or rest a hand on a hip, and people at the
side to turn toward the room; they show standing and facing you until those
poses are painted. Each place also marks one spot for your own character, for
the title screen.
