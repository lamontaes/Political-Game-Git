title: Personality shapes civic life and news
---

Who turns out to vote, who stands up at a meeting, who joins a party or a group, who passes a story on and who believes the story they are told now follow each person's own temperament, sized from the research. Whether you believe a story depends first on who tells it and how close you are: a suspicious person still believes a trusted friend and doubts a stranger who argues against them. Whether a party member comes to a meeting depends on their free time and how far they live from it, and whether a resident joins a group depends on how hard the question pulls them and on whether their friends are already in it. Counted business owners now decide whether to join an employers' association, as named ones do. Open-minded, trusting people are moved further by an argument than closed, wary ones.
