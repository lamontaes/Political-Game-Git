---
id: elections-petition-own-electorate
impact: minor
section: Changed
title: A statewide or district race asks its own law's signatures, and you can pay circulators to gather them
---

A petition for a county, legislative, congressional or statewide office used to ask only your town's share of the law's signatures, so a U.S. Senate race in a small town could need a single name. It now asks the number the state's law sets for the whole race: a fixed count where the law gives one, or the law's formula (a share of the votes cast, of registered voters or of the party's voters) worked out from the voters of the race in your world. Your town's doors still count toward it, and on the Campaign tab you can now pay petition circulators, who gather the rest among the race's voters elsewhere in the state or district over the days the petition is out, paid from your campaign's money as the signatures come in. Rival candidates do the same with their own money, and a campaign that cannot pay for them may not make the ballot.
