title: A per-mile road charge and fix-it-first road spending change the world
---

A state's fuel tax now brings in a little less each year as cars burn less
fuel per mile. A law replacing it with a per-mile road charge stops that loss
from the first bill, two years after the law takes effect, so the state keeps
money it would have lost; a repeal sends it back to the shrinking fuel tax.
A fix-it-first law, which spends highway money on repair before new lanes,
leaves fewer roads in poor condition: about a fifth fewer after five years
and more than a third fewer later on. Smoother roads cost drivers less in
repairs, which lowers prices a little. Repealing the law undoes both.
