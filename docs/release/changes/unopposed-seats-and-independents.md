title: Some seats are unopposed, and some races have an independent
---

Every race used to have exactly one opponent. Now the other party does not
always field a candidate, so a town or state legislative seat is sometimes
unopposed. The campaign screen says so, and on election night the one name on
the ballot takes every vote. An independent sometimes runs as well, making a
three-way race. A governorship always has an opponent, and a sitting governor
who runs again is always on the ballot. How often each of these happens is a
placeholder until the real rates are known.
