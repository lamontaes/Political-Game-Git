title: A reporter's call about your vote now plays as a friendly beat reporter, a hostile one, a scandal chase or the national press
---

A reporter who calls about a vote no longer always asks the same way. A reporter who has covered you before and knows you asks plainly and leaves you room. One who is writing about a vote the town stood against asks why you went the other way. One chasing the allegations against you confronts you with them. One from a national outlet asks what it means for a place like yours. Which one calls is read from what the records show: where the town's shares stand against your vote, the talks you and the reporter already had, a quarrel between you, the stories of scandal against you, and the outlet the reporter writes for.
