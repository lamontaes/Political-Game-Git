title: States begin with their real budget, labor, energy, housing and police laws
---

Every state, D.C. and territory now begins the game with the law it really
had at the start of 2026 on twenty more questions. They cover whether public
workers may bargain as a union, whether power companies must sell more clean
electricity each year, whether carbon has a price, whether 17-year-olds go to
juvenile court, whether small loans have an interest cap, whether the budget
must balance, whether groceries are free of the state sales tax, whether
pensions must be paid in full, whether property tax bills can only grow so
fast, and whether the state must keep a rainy-day reserve. They also cover
whether public money can follow a student to a private school, whether social
media sites must check ages, whether duplexes are allowed on every house lot,
whether tenants facing eviction get a lawyer, whether police use of facial
recognition is limited, whether cities may pass laws the state hasn't reserved,
whether civilian boards may investigate police, whether former lawmakers must
wait before lobbying, whether the state sets a minimum teacher salary, and
whether new buildings must include homes below market price.

A law a place already had when its crime and school figures were measured is
part of those figures, and repealing it later moves them. A law passed after
that date, such as Maryland's 2024 limits on police facial recognition, counts
as a change from those figures from the start. Where a place's law couldn't be
confirmed, the game treats it as unknown rather than guessing.
