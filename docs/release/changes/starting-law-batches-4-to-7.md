title: States begin with their real budget, labor, energy, housing and police laws
---

Every state, D.C. and territory now begins the game with the law it really
had at the start of 2026 on twenty more questions. Where a place's law couldn't
be confirmed, the game treats it as unknown rather than guessing.

1. Work and money: whether public workers may bargain as a union, whether
   small loans have an interest cap, and whether the state sets a minimum
   teacher salary.
2. State budgets: whether the budget must balance, whether groceries are free
   of the state sales tax, whether pensions must be paid in full, whether
   property tax bills can only grow so fast, and whether the state must keep a
   rainy-day reserve.
3. Energy: whether power companies must sell more clean electricity each year,
   and whether carbon has a price.
4. Housing: whether duplexes are allowed on every house lot, whether tenants
   facing eviction get a lawyer, and whether new buildings must include homes
   below market price.
5. Police and courts: whether 17-year-olds go to juvenile court, whether police
   use of facial recognition is limited, and whether civilian boards may
   investigate police.
6. Schools and families: whether public money can follow a student to a private
   school, and whether social media sites must check ages.
7. Government: whether cities may pass laws the state hasn't reserved, and
   whether former lawmakers must wait before lobbying.

A law a place already had when its crime and school figures were measured is
part of those figures, and repealing it later moves them. A law passed after
that date, such as Maryland's 2024 limits on police facial recognition, counts
as a change from those figures from the start.
