title: Congress files, debates and passes federal bills
---

Congress now makes law while you live your life. On the first of each month
a member of each House files a bill on one of the federal issues they have
taken up, and members who work on the same issue sign on as cosponsors. The
bill goes to the committee for its field, then to a vote in each House. In
the Senate it first needs sixty votes to end debate, so a bill backed by
only one party usually dies at the filibuster. A bill that passes both
Houses goes to the President, who signs it or vetoes it, and two-thirds of
each House can override a veto. A tax bill has to start in the House. Every
member casts their own vote and each ballot records why. A federal law takes
effect the day it is enacted. What a federal law changes in the world is not
built yet; each bill says so plainly.
