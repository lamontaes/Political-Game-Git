title: People who helped you may call to ask for help back
---

Somebody who once helped you, still expects something back and now needs
help, whether with a race they are running or with finding work, may call to
ask. Whether they call is their own decision, from what they expect, what they
need and who they are; somebody who helped out of kindness never does. You can
say you will help, offer something smaller, say not now, or say no. A yes is a
promise the game remembers and judges by what you do next; a no strains things
between you. A person who still feels they owe you is also likelier to agree
to meet you, or to back your campaign when you ask.

More of what people say now counts as a promise. A neighbor who agrees to go to
the public meeting for you, or a coworker who offers to check a case, now owes
you that.

At your own campaign town hall, somebody may ask where you stand on a bill
still in play in the body you are running for. Saying you would vote for it or
against it is a public pledge, and your first vote on that question once you
are seated says whether you kept it.
