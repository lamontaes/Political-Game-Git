title: A borough or village has the council its state's law gives it.
---

A town whose own charter the game has not read used to get the same council everywhere: five members with four-year terms. Now it gets the council its state's law sets for its kind of town. A New Jersey borough has a Mayor elected for four years and six council members elected at large for three-year terms, two up each year. A Pennsylvania borough has seven members and a Mayor. A New York village has a Mayor and four trustees on two-year terms. Illinois and Wisconsin villages have a Village President and six trustees. Other kinds of town keep the typical council until their state's law is read.
