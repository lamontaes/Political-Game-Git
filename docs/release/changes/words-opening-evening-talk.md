title: On the first evening, your relative talks about something the President actually said.
---

After the President's address, the relative at home names one point from that speech, says where they stand on it, and says why, from their own life: a job they lost, money being tight, what rent or work is like in town, or a view they already held. The line is put together fresh each time, so two relatives, or two new lives, rarely say it the same way. You get up to four replies, each about that point: agree, disagree, ask what it would mean for your town, or ask what they would do instead. They answer what you said, and they remember it.
