title: A minimum wage raise no longer multiplies the pay of a town's appointed officers
---

When a state raised its minimum wage, a small town's department heads, city clerk and attorney could see their paychecks jump several times over, because their part-time stipend was counted as a full-time hourly wage. Elected officials, their staff and their appointed policy heads are now left out of the minimum wage, as the federal wage law leaves them out, so their pay stays what the town budgets for it.
