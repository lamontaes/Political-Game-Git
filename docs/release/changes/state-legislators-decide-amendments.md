title: A state's legislators decide whether to propose a constitutional amendment
---

When a governor is barred from another term, or has served three in a row, a
state's legislators now decide for themselves whether to amend the governor's
term limit. Each weighs the governor's party, how they get along with the
governor, and the higher bar of changing the constitution. The legislature
proposes only when every chamber would carry it. Before, a yearly draw decided
whether an amendment came up, and each chamber's vote was drawn.

A state now writes a policy into its constitution, or takes one out, when its
legislators' own principles would carry it, rather than on a drawn subject in
a drawn year. A question the voters turned down at the last general election
is a strong reason to leave it. The world no longer proposes changes to how
towns recall their officials, since nothing gives a legislator a reason to.
The voters' own share on a referred amendment is still drawn.
