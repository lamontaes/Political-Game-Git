section: Changed
title: A severe disaster lowers what homes are worth and pushes people to leave
---

When a disaster that FEMA found damaged many homes strikes a town, the town's homes are worth less, by the size research measured for a severe disaster (about 5 percent), and residents are more drawn to move away. Purchases and sales of homes take the lower value. A town with only minor disasters is unchanged.
