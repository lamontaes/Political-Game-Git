title: Prisoners no longer vote, run for office or answer the door in a prison town
---

In a town with a prison, the people held there were counted as voters, could be drawn to run for a local seat, and were visited by canvassers as if each cell were a house. Where the state's law bars people held in prison from voting, the town's electorate and its candidate draw now leave those rooms out, and the barred share applied to everyone else is cut by the prison part so no one is barred twice. In the states that let people vote from prison they stay voters. Canvassers no longer knock on prison cells or nursing-home rooms; dormitories and barracks are still doors.
