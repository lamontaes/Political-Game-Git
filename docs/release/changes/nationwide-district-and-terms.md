title: The District of Columbia has its own mayor, and short-term states keep short terms
---

A life in the District of Columbia now opens under the District's own chief
executive — a mayor, under the District's own Code, not a fifty-first governor.
The District is one government, so everything district-wide and everything
citywide names the same jurisdiction: one office, one public account, no
second mayor beside the first. A mayor elected in November takes office on
January 2nd, the day the District's Code names, rather than on the game's
usual first Monday.

New Hampshire and Vermont run two-year terms and elect every two years,
instead of inheriting a four-year default nobody had read. Vermont's and the
District's terms come from their own instruments where those fix a value, and
from the game's disclosed rule where they do not — Vermont's constitution sets
a two-year term but no calendar start date, so the term length is the state's
and the start day is the game's.

Every office now tells the player, plainly, how long the term runs, when it
begins and how often it is elected — and nothing else. Where a rule was read
from a law, which law it was is a record the game keeps, not a line on the
screen a candidate files from.
