title: A law can change how many judges sit on a court
---

A bill can now set the number of seats on a court: Congress for the federal
courts, including the Supreme Court, and a state for its own courts. A court
whose size is written into its constitution changes only by constitutional
amendment. On the day the law takes effect, a larger court gets new, empty
seats, and on the Supreme Court the President nominates for each one and the
Senate votes. A smaller court removes nobody: each sitting judge keeps the
seat until leaving it, and that seat is then retired instead of refilled.
The news records the change.
