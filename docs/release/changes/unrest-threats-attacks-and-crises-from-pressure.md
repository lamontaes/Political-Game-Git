title: Anger in a state can turn into unrest, threats and attacks
---

Anger now builds in a state when a governor or President fails a disaster
there, when national unemployment rises, and when a political figure is
attacked. It fades when nothing feeds it. When a state's anger runs high,
unrest can break out there. If the unrest lasts, a prominent political person
in that state can be threatened: the governor, a member of Congress or a local
party organizer, and that can be you. If the anger holds, an attack can
follow. It can injure or kill, and a death leaves the office empty by the
usual rules. Nothing happens on a timer, and nothing happens where anger never
ran high.

An international dispute the news has reported, such as shipping delays or
fishing-rights talks, can now become a crisis the President has to handle if
it stays open while unemployment rises and anger builds at home. One dispute
starts one crisis at most.

How high anger must run, how fast it fades and how likely each step is are
placeholders until research comes back.
It applies to lives started from now on; an older save does not change.
