title: Renters pay rent to a real landlord
---

A rented home in town had no rent behind it, and the only rent in the game
was one flat $900 inside your own monthly costs. Now every renting household
pays rent on the first of each month to a landlord on record: a neighbor who
owns their home, a realty or property-management firm, or the town's housing
authority. The rent comes from the county's federal Fair Market Rent for a
home with that many bedrooms, carried forward with prices, and a public
housing rent is 30% of the household's income. When your household rents,
your month's costs no longer include the flat $900; the real rent is paid to
your landlord. A household that falls two months behind can be taken to
court and evicted. Rent stabilization holds down yearly increases and makes
covered renters move less, an inclusionary housing law sets aside affordable
homes in new buildings, and a right to counsel gives tenants facing eviction
a lawyer, so fewer lose their homes.
