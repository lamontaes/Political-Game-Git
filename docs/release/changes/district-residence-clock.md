title: A life that has always lived somewhere has always lived in its district too
---

A character who had lived in the same town since childhood was treated as
having lived in that town's legislative district for no time at all, because
the district clock started on the day the life was written rather than on the
day the records say the life came to live there. The two clocks now agree:
both read the same household history, so a forty-year-old Sitka resident has
been in their house district for forty years, not for nothing, and does not
have to wait out a residence requirement they already meet.

Where a town is split across several districts — Duluth, Columbus, Lincoln —
the game still refuses to say which district its resident lives in, because it
genuinely cannot. What changed is what it says: it now tells you the district
is the thing it cannot establish, instead of saying it never recorded when you
came to live here, which was not true.
