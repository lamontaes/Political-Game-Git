title: Amendments change what a vote means
---

A vote used to count only for what a bill was filed to do. Now the game
keeps the bill exactly as it read at every recorded vote, so a work
requirement added on the floor is on the record of every member who voted
for the bill afterward, and on the law once it passes. Members of a seated
chamber now offer floor amendments of their own, each for a reason the game
records: to pass something they care about, to split a bill's coalition and
sink it, to put colleagues on the record, or to attach what they want to a
money bill that must pass. They weigh a part by their own principles and
judge the count from what colleagues are known to stand for, so a private
objection can surprise them. Each chamber starts under its real 2026
amendment rules: germane amendments only in almost every chamber, the U.S.
House floor open only to amendments a special rule allows, and each state's
single-subject rule. A chamber can change its own rules in play, but not
what its constitution holds. A governor with an item veto can strike a rider
from a budget they sign; the rider stays on every vote's record and never
becomes law. Each part of a bill is known by the plain question it answers,
such as "Work requirement for assistance".
