title: People bring up what is actually happening in your life, and a lie can come back.
---

The people around you now start real conversations about real things: a
housemate asks where you'll be on an evening you already have plans, a local
party organizer calls to invite you to an open meeting, somebody close reacts
when an election is decided, a friend or coworker asks a specific favor, a
staff member asks what to do about a pending bill, and a reporter asks about a
promise you made.

People you know can now be asked to meet, and they answer for themselves: yes,
no, or a different day that you can turn down in turn. Somebody you have not
seen for a long time may call you first. Agreeing puts the evening on the
calendar; it does not spend it.

When somebody raises something they asked of you long ago, you can tell them
the truth, say what you think you remember, refuse to get into it, or lie. An
honest mistake and a lie are not the same thing, and the person who was there
is the one who knows better.

A childhood is now lived before it is directed: in the early years the adult at
home decides and the child is left with the memory, and the choices become
yours as you grow.

Two people who live together can decide to have a family, and the day arrives
in its own time. After a death, the people who were told can say something to
each other about it; nothing is required.

Each of these comes in more than one situation. An organizer checks in after
you turn a meeting down, or asks whether you want to join after you went. The
person you live with asks whether you are really running once you file, and
marks your first day in office. A reporter asks why you are running. If you
promised someone a quiet evening at home and something else is on the same
night, they ask about it.

Some answers can now be a deliberate lie, clearly marked before you choose it.
Nobody finds out by magic. A lie is only discovered when someone who heard it
later learns otherwise, such as a housemate seeing you leave or a source
confirming your promise to a reporter, and then they bring it up with you.
Talking still takes no time.
