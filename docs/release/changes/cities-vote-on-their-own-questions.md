title: A city council can set its own wage floor, fairness rules, term limits and wards
---

A town council can now take up four questions that belong to the city
itself: a minimum wage above the state's, an ordinance barring
discrimination in housing and work on grounds state law does not cover,
term limits for its own members, and an independent commission to draw its
own council districts. Whether the state lets it is the state's answer. A
city can't set its own minimum wage where the state bars cities from doing
so, and a fairness ordinance can't stand where the state has barred local
ones. If a state later bars its cities, their ordinances stay on the books
but stop counting as the law from that day.

State legislatures can now also pass the two housing questions that states
have taken up in real life: allowing small apartment buildings where only
single-family homes were allowed, and approving housing that meets the code
without a drawn-out review.
