title: The District and the territories each have a Delegate you can name
---

The District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands,
American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands each send one member to the
U.S. House who speaks and sits on committees but casts no final vote. Before,
a life in any of those places read "No current record names who holds the
seat." Now every new game seats a fictional Delegate (or Puerto Rico's
Resident Commissioner) for each of them, with a party and a term.

The terms follow the law: two years, and four for Puerto Rico's Resident
Commissioner. When a term ends on January 3, a living member stands again and
returns if the place's leaning still favors their party; otherwise a new person
takes the seat. Each place's leaning comes from its own 2024 result, read one
way for all six. American Samoa's race is nonpartisan, and Puerto Rico's and
the Northern Marianas' parties or independents do not map cleanly to the two
House caucuses, so those three are marked ESTIMATED FROM AVERAGE in the code
with the source. The six members are not among the 435 voting seats.
