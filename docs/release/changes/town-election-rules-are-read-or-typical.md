title: A town's election rules no longer differ by a hidden draw
---

Before, a town nobody had researched got its council size, council terms,
counting rule, election season and recall rules from a hidden draw keyed to
its name, so two similar towns next door could have different councils for
no reason. The candidate filing deadline for every town race was a flat four
weeks.

Now a town's rules come from its own records where the game has read them.
Where it has not, every such town gets the most common rule in the country:
a five-seat council on four-year terms, and the counting and recall rule the
most states use. The candidate filing deadline is the national middle, 85
days before the first vote. A new campaign committee's first filing is due
in 10 days, the federal rule, wherever a state's own rule is not read.
