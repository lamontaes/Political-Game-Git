title: Newspapers, reporters who come back, and money questions with consequences.
---

The news now has names. Three national outlets exist from your first day, and a
state newsroom appears once state politics touches your life. Their reporters
keep their jobs, remember who they have covered, and come back to you. A
reporter can ask for your response before a story runs. You see the exact
words of every answer before you give them, you can decline, and if an answer
is a lie the game tells you so first. Staying silent is reported as silence,
not as guilt.

Sources agree ground rules before they say anything: on the record,
background with an agreed description, or off the record. Off-the-record
material never runs. Stories cite what actually happened. When the facts
change, the story gets a follow-up or a correction, and the original stays
on the record.

Campaign money can now become a scandal, whether the misuse is real or only
alleged. A rival can file a complaint about a payment that was
legitimate, and the complaint can be dismissed. A real misuse can stay hidden
unless someone who keeps the books finds it. Complaints follow the actual
steps of the body that hears them, such as the Kentucky Legislative Ethics
Commission or the Federal Election Commission. A complaint is never treated as
a finding. Where the game has no researched procedure, it says the inquiry is
simulated and cannot punish anyone.
