title: Councils no longer pass a law and repeal it two weeks later
---

A member who likes change now backs changing long-standing law and resists undoing a law the body just passed, and members hold to their own recent votes, so a board no longer passes and repeals the same law every meeting. A mayor, county executive, governor or president who is the player gets each passed bill on their desk to sign or veto, and a bill left there becomes law at the office's real deadline. Each level of government can pass only what that level may: a county can't impose a Medicaid work requirement and Congress can't change state sales tax. Delegates from Washington, D.C. and the territories, and Alabama's effective dates, now come from data.
