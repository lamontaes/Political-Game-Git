title: A household behind on rent pays what it can, keeping back only what living costs beyond its home; 28 states' rent grace periods are read from their statutes.
---

A household behind on rent now pays it when it has the money. Each month, a household that owes rent settles what it can from its cash, as before. But what it keeps back is now a month of what living costs beyond its home, never less than the everyday basket for its size. Before, it kept back a whole month of living, rent included, so a household holding what it owed often paid none of it. Each place's grace period, the days after rent falls due before it counts as late, was read from the statute for 28 states and territories, each with its statute, page and a quote. Four figures change: Hawaii and Maryland give no grace period (the game had 5 days), Nevada gives 3 days and Washington 5 (the game had none). The places whose statutes could not be read keep their earlier figure, still marked as an estimate.
