---
id: words-speech-hurt-terms
impact: minor
section: Changed
title: An address given in a bad term answers its own bad news, and its opening says what the address is about.
---

When a governor's, a town head's or the President's address opens on what went wrong in their term, the next thing they say is what they are doing about it, from their own record in that area: a bill they sponsored, a question or budget line they back, or another view they hold there. When their record holds nothing in that area, they say so plainly ("I have not yet put a plan before you on safety on our streets"). Only then do they turn to the rest of their speech, with a sentence that says they are turning ("There is other work before us as well, and it is the cost of a place to live").

The opening names the subject the speech starts on, so an address that opens on crime no longer announces that it is about rent. A speaker promises "one matter" only when the speech has one subject.

A speaker who has held office for under two years can now say their worst area's bad news with its own figure ("Since I took this office, we have seen reported crime rise by about 20 percent"), for rent, crime, schools, health, roads and the environment.
