title: Scenes with a purpose sound like real people and keep moving
---

A reporter changes the angle instead of repeating the question; a vote counter asks what would change your mind; a volunteer opens with something from the rally and your character answers first. After the first exchange the replies offer new moves, including a way out, and a lie can be owned with an edge. Every branch of a scene ends with the other person's closing line, never on an unanswered question. The lines are calibrated on real interviews, oral histories and casework reports, studied, not copied.

People say "have to", not "must"; a complaint says why the change hurts; a good argument keeps going instead of ending on a jab; and someone can storm off, told as a reaction rather than a "Good night." Filing for office happens at the place's own filing office, where the clerk mentions the rival who filed first. A friend who comes by after a baby is born asks how you're actually doing.

An officeholder's own records now bring scenes: signing or vetoing a bill brings the reporters who cover it and the aide who carried it to the desk, the sponsor hears from the executive who signed or vetoed, a town hall puts the residents who asked in front of its host, and a town post's appointer tells the appointee. Five bills signed in one afternoon are one press availability, not five. The narrator speaks between lines ("A second reporter drifts over, then a third."), and someone who keeps to themself skips the hello and says why they came.

The President's address is a scene at the seat of government, with the speech the English engine wrote. Some scene endings open a game screen: the desk, the filing office, your office, the meeting or the campaign. A remark at a council meeting is no longer mistaken for a campaign event, and an aide bringing a bill to the desk talks about what it costs and who voted for it instead of inventing changes nobody made.
