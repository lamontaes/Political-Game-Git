title: A law passed in the game can change the rules of office
---

A bill can now carry a clause that changes a rule of government: how many
seats a chamber has, how long a term runs, how old someone must be to serve,
or how long they must have lived in the state or district. A state
constitutional amendment can carry the same kind of change.

Nothing changes while the bill is only a bill. Once it becomes law, the new
rule applies from the day the act takes effect, and the old rule still governs
every day before that. Where the game does not yet know a state's own rule for
when its acts take effect, the change takes effect ninety days after the act is
recorded, and the record says that this is the game's default. Rules that no
law can change yet (term start dates, election calendars, the form of a
legislature, local charters) are refused with the reason, not recorded as if
they had acted.

The constitutional workspace now says in plain words which rule a ratified
amendment changes.
