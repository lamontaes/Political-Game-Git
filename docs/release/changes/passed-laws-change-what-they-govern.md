title: A law you pass changes what it governs, and its page says what changed
---

Before this change, whether a law did anything depended on the route it came
through. When you passed a bill, its tax took effect, but its spending never
became money the state could spend. When a legislature passed a bill without
you, it was the other way around. A city ordinance did neither.

Every passed law now goes through the same step, whoever passed it. A tax
becomes a tax that collects. An appropriation becomes money the governor can
commit and pay out.

A law's page now has a "What the law changed" list with a line for each
effect. It says what the law made available to spend, and how much of that
has been committed and paid out. It says when a tax took effect and what it
has collected so far. It also names each part of the law that nothing in the
world acts on yet, instead of letting you assume it worked. What those parts
should do, and who really bears each one, has been asked of the research
desk.
