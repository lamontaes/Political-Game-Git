section: Improved
title: Town councils make law for their members' own reasons
---

A town council and the Council of the District of Columbia used to take up a
random question with a coin-flip answer, and each member voted yes two times in
three. Now a member files an ordinance on the question their own principles
press hardest, where the town's law does not already say what they want, and
every member votes through the same engine as the legislatures. A member with
no view of their own no longer votes yes by default: they follow a trusted
colleague, the mayor's known position, the committee, or a clear lean among the
town's voters, and otherwise answer present. A council elected without party
labels, like the Nebraska Legislature, gives no party cue. An adopted ordinance
takes effect 30 days later where the charter's own rule is unread. Sixty-six
councils now have their real seat counts, read from their charters and codes.
