title: Renters across the whole town now buy homes when they have saved enough, so ownership and home prices no longer only fall
---

Until now only the handful of households you had met could go from renting to owning; the rest of the town rented forever while owners still sold when they moved or died, so a town's ownership rate only fell and its home prices leaned down. Now every renter household weighs buying once a year, on the month its lease began, by the same rule as the people you know: the home its rent stands for, what owning would cost over renting, its children, age and years in its lease, and whether its own savings cover the down payment. A household that has not saved enough keeps renting. A buyer stops paying rent, starts paying a mortgage to the town bank (or a lender outside town when the bank is full), and counts as a buyer in the town's housing market.
