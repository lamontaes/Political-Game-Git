title: Renters across the whole town now buy homes when they have saved enough, so ownership and home prices no longer only fall
---

Until now only the handful of households you had met could go from renting to owning; the rest of the town rented forever while owners still sold when they moved or died, so a town's ownership rate only fell and its home prices leaned down. Now every renter household weighs buying once a year, on the month its lease began, by the same rule as the people you know: the home its rent stands for, what owning would cost over renting, its children, age and years in its lease, and whether its own savings cover the down payment. Each household now holds its own share of its neighbors' pooled savings, so two families alike in income and age can have saved very different amounts, and households open with what families of their income and age typically keep in the bank. A household that has not saved enough keeps renting, and the renters it leaves behind pay nothing toward its down payment. A buyer stops paying rent, starts paying a mortgage to the town bank (or a lender outside town when the bank is full), and counts as a buyer in the town's housing market.
