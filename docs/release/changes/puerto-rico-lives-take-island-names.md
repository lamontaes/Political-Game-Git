title: A life in Puerto Rico is named the way the island names people
---

A new life in Mayagüez drew Douglas Pope from the random name button and grew
up with Alexander Rosado, Frances Patton and Waylon Rosado. Names came from the
national tables, which do not cover Puerto Rico. Once Puerto Rico is chosen,
the random name button now draws from the island's own names, and so does the
new game for the character, their family, and the neighbors, classmates and
meeting-goers they meet. Each person has a given name and two surnames, the
father's first surname and then the mother's, and each parent carries the
surname they passed on. The names come from the people the island's public
schools are named for, so they lean toward an older generation until a
birth-year source is found. Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, the U.S. Virgin
Islands and American Samoa have too few such schools to measure, so they keep
the national names for now. Saved games keep their names, and so does
everywhere else.
