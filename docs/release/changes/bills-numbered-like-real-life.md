title: Bills are numbered the way each legislature really numbers them
---

A bill's number now starts over at 1 when a new session opens, and the bill's
name says which session it belongs to, such as "HB 1 (2027 Regular Session)"
or "H.R. 1, 120th Congress." The session a life begins in still starts partway
up, because other bills were filed before you arrived.

Each state uses its own prefixes and chamber names: California and Nevada file
Assembly Bills, Minnesota and Iowa file House Files and Senate Files, New York
and New Jersey file A and S bills, and West Virginia's lower chamber is the
House of Delegates. States that sit in two-year sessions keep counting through
the second year. The District of Columbia numbers its bills by council period.

A bill you draft on the docket now shows the number it will actually be filed
under. Bills already in a saved game keep the numbers they have.
