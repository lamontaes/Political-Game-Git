title: Many more state laws now change life in the state, not just its budget.
---

A state that passes a right-to-work law sees fewer people vote in the years
after. Capping how fast property taxes can grow costs schools ground in math.
A mental health crisis team that answers calls instead of police trims crime,
and so does giving a civilian board power to investigate police complaints,
while limiting police use of facial recognition nudges it up. Raising teachers'
minimum pay helps a few more students graduate, easing licensing rules pulls a
few people out of poverty, and handing district lines to an independent
commission lifts turnout slightly. Some laws that people expect to matter,
such as term limits, raising the juvenile court age or exempting groceries
from sales tax, change none of these measures, and the game now holds them to
that. How big each effect is varies a little from one game to the next.
