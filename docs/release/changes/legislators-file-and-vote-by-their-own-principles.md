title: Legislators file bills and vote by their own principles
---

Sitting legislators and governors now hold political principles of their
own. On each bill day, a member of the state's legislature files a bill on
the question their principles press hardest, answering it the way they lean,
or repealing a law in force that they oppose. Members vote for a bill that
does what their principles call for and against one that cuts across them,
even when their party's member carries it, and a governor whose principles
bear on a bill signs or returns it on them.

The forty-one states whose legislatures are not yet researched now send
bills too. Their chambers have a standing committee, and are sized by the
state's own Census legislative districts. Where the rules say pending bills
die when the session adjourns, they now do.
