title: A city minimum wage ordinance now sets the wage where the state lets cities set one.
---

A city ordinance that answers yes to "should the city set its own minimum wage
above the state's?" now raises the wage in that city, by the average premium of
real city minimums over their state's rate (9.2 percent, from the 40 California
localities posted by the UC Berkeley Labor Center), from the ordinance's
effective date. Town jobs paid below it are raised on the first pay period
after. It counts only where the state's law lets cities set a wage, so a state
law on that question turns city ordinances on and off, and a later ordinance
that answers no takes the city back to the state's rate. Counties are not
modeled, and the answer to the research question on real city and county rates
replaces the average.
