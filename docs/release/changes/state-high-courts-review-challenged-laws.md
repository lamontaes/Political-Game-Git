title: A state's highest court can strike down a challenged law before it takes effect
---

A state or local law that answers a question the way laws that were really
challenged in court did is now reviewed the day before it takes effect. The
questions are a ban on new gas hookups, age checks for social media, public
money for private schooling, photo identification to vote, ending cash bail,
a permit to carry concealed, right to work, rent stabilization and
independent redistricting commissions. Each justice of the state's highest
court votes for their own reasons: the real rulings decided by that day, what
they themselves hold right, and the presumption that a statute is valid. A
law most of them vote to strike stays on the record but never takes effect,
so a bill that passes can still fail in court. Before, every law a
legislature passed took effect.
