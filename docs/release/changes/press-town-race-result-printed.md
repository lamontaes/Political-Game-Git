title: The local paper and stations now report who won the town's races the week after election day, in plain newspaper sentences
---

After an election, the town's paper often printed nothing about who won: a council race was weighed by its three or four candidates, so the week's council votes and new laws always took the paper's space. A race's result now counts everyone who could vote in it, as a vote or a law counts everyone it covers, and the reporter who files one of the night's races files the others counted with it. State ballot questions on the town's ballot and recall votes are weighed by the same rule.

The stories read like a paper's: a council race that fills several seats is one story naming its winners and who finished just out ("Eric Lowe finished fourth, five votes back"), a contested race gives the count ("34 votes to 12") and a whole-number share, an unopposed winner is "elected without opposition" instead of winning "with 100% of the vote", each person is named in full once and then by last name, offices are written in lower case in a sentence, and the turnout is the number of people who voted.
