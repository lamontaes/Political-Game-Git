title: A town council seat reads as an office everywhere, and councils follow national shares
---

A player who won a seat on their town's council used to read "You do not hold
a job or an office right now" just above the line naming the body they sat on.
The seat now counts as their role. Under Who governs, the members seated on a
town's governing body are named. A party host's advice about running for
office now includes the town's own council, where it used to say that no office
was known.

Counties read the way Americans say them: "Washington County", not "County of
Washington". "St." keeps its period, as in St. Louis County.

Towns whose own rules the game has not read now get their council size and
term from the national shares of American municipal councils. Five seats and
four-year terms are the most common. The screen still says these are typical
rather than the town's own.
