title: A town's ordinances open and number as its state's law says, and a phase-in can rise by a percent a year
---

An ordinance in a town or county now opens with the enacting clause its state's general law gives that kind of government, with the place named, and the District of Columbia's Council numbers its bills by Council Period, as its own rules say. Places whose statute was not read keep the national clause and the year-and-order numbering. A member drafting a bill can now phase an amount in by a percent a year as well as by dated steps: the bill carries one dated section a year, each the amount before it raised by the percent. A bill a body's lawyer would warn about names the lawyer by the body where the town seated no attorney.
