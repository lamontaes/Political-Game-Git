Add review dialogue about lease dates, rent collections and tenant testimony for state legislators debating rent stabilization.

Expand the repeal exchange with questions about renewal notices, partial payments, witness cards and charges during a signed rent freeze. Members opposing repeal can answer by walking through a lease, ledger, hearing record or bill notice.

Members debating enactment can ask how a departing tenant recovers an overpayment, how to hear a medical witness, how an older lease adjusts for costs and what a proposed tax break would buy.

Debaters describe what happens to tenants and owners in their own words. Local examples can use the other member's recorded town, general examples cover seats without one, and only the bill's sponsor can claim to have written it.

Members can agree or disagree with a specific point about a renewal, repair, overpayment or hearing witness. The speaker can acknowledge the reason or press the objection before the vote.

Members who want to keep the ceiling can explain what the renewal date means for a household budget, how rent debt carries forward, what a family witness asked for or why a signed freeze mattered on moving day.

Members arguing for repeal can challenge the ceiling on renewal dates, repair financing, a family witness’s request or the treatment of a signed freeze. The opposing speaker can press each objection on its own terms.

Members agreeing with an objection to a new ceiling can compare the full cost of two leases, ask when an overcharge will be repaid or examine whether a tax break lasts longer than the promised freeze.

A player defending a new ceiling can explain how a tenant proves an overpayment, ask an owner to document fuel costs or distinguish a rent limit from a proposed tax concession. The opposing member can challenge each remedy.

Members who support a new ceiling can pick up a colleague’s argument by name. The speaker can respond briefly about a specific document, date or payment or explain why it matters.

Members questioning a new ceiling can challenge the duration of a tax concession, the timing of a rent credit or the treatment of costs already charged under a lease. The speaker can answer with a brief question or press for a specific remedy.

When the opening speaker favors repeal, the player can distinguish a signed renewal from a pending offer, test whether repair money was actually collected, question a witness’s position or ask which charge was already due under a lease.
