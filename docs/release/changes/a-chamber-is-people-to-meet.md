title: A seat in a big chamber no longer makes every member someone you know
---

Before, taking a seat in a state House made every member somebody you knew
on the first day: People showed "Work 181", and the web drew all of them in
one ring of overlapping faces with names printed on top of each other.

Now, in a workplace or group with more than 20 other people, a colleague is
somebody you know once there is something between you on the record. The
rest are listed under Work or Politics as "Not met yet", and you can open
their card to reach them. A small workplace works as before: you know
everybody there.

The web also fits its drawing. It uses up to three rings sized to the space
and prints names only when they have room; a name that doesn't fit shows
when you hover or pick the face. Anybody who doesn't fit is counted, with a
button to see everyone in the list.

The size of 20 is a placeholder and is filed as a research question.
