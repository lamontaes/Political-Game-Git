title: Newspapers endorse candidates in the races they cover
---

A newspaper or magazine now endorses in a race it covers, the way groups and people already do. Its head reads their own views against every candidate's platform and backs the one they agree with above every rival, under the paper's name, or no one. The paper's followers hear it, and it moves how they feel about that candidate when they vote. Television and radio stations do not endorse, as in real life since the FCC's rule on broadcast editorials was repealed in 2000.
