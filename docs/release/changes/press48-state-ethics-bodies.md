title: An ethics complaint now reaches your own state's ethics body in twenty-four states
---

An ethics complaint against a legislator or legislative candidate used to reach a real commission only in Kentucky. Everywhere else it went to a simulated oversight review that said on its face it could not discipline anyone. In twenty-three more states and the District of Columbia the complaint now goes to that state's own body, named as the state names it — the Ohio Joint Legislative Ethics Committee, the Nevada Commission on Ethics, the Texas Ethics Commission — and the proceeding is called what that state calls it. The steps run from the complaint through notice, an answer period, an inquiry and findings, and the body stops there: whether anyone is disciplined is decided separately, and the game says so rather than inventing a punishment. States nobody has read yet still reach the simulated review, which is the honest answer, not another state's commission.
