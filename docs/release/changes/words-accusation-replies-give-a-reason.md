title: When someone confronts you about a complaint to the prosecutor, your answers give a reason
---

When someone who reported a bribe to the prosecutor confronts you about it, your answers no longer just promise to explain, deny or apologize. They say what the money was for and whether it changed hands, why you did it (how much the vote or contract mattered to you, being behind on bills, a tie to the official, or what you had heard), what you did not do, and how the person accusing you came to know. Later answers move the talk on instead of saying the first ones again, and the accuser can accept your reason.
