title: The filing counter speaks again: the clerk greets you, tells you about the race, answers your questions and takes your papers.
---

At the office that takes a place's filings, the clerk now greets you by the office they work for, lists the races you can file for, and tells you the race's dates, its term and what the office does. You can ask who else has filed, challenge whether a seat is already decided, or file. The clerk also says which rule stops a filing, if one does, and the next day tells you about the petition: how many signatures and by when, or that there is none. The first time you file, the clerk asks whether you have run a campaign before, and a no gets a short walk-through of signatures, door knocking and money, naming the town's paper where it has one. Before, the counter showed no clerk line and no race to pick, so a filing could not be started at it.
