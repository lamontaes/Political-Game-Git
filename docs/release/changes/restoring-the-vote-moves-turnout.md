title: Restoring the vote after a sentence changes a state's turnout
---

Where a state bars people from voting after their sentence, a law that
restores the vote at the end of a sentence now changes the state's turnout.
Restored voters join the eligible population but vote less often than others,
so the share of eligible people who vote falls while the number of ballots
rises. In Tennessee the turnout rate falls about 4 percent. The size depends
on how many people each state bars after their sentence. Where the vote
already returns by the end of a sentence, the law changes nothing.
