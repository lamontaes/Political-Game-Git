title: Every body that confirms an appointment weighs the nominee from each member's own lean.
---

Every body that confirms an appointment now votes by the cabinet's rule: each member weighs the nominee from their own lean, not their party. That covers a state senate confirming the governor's cabinet and posts, a town council confirming a department head, the Senate confirming a successor or a Federal Reserve governor, and the Fed's own board choosing a reserve bank's president. Before, each of those read a member only by party, so a body the appointer's party did not hold turned nominees down as a bloc.
