title: Every body that confirms an appointment weighs the nominee from each member's own lean.
---

Every body that confirms an appointment now votes by the cabinet's rule: each member weighs the nominee from their own lean, not their party. That covers a state senate confirming the governor's cabinet and posts, a town council confirming a department head, the Senate confirming a successor or a Federal Reserve governor, and the Fed's own board choosing a reserve bank's president. Before, every member of a party voted the same way. Now members split by their own views: at Tibes, Puerto Rico, the council confirmed the police chief 14-2 and the fire chief 13-3, where before every vote was 16-0. At Ryderwood, Washington and Avaio, American Samoa, the opening's votes came out the same as before.
