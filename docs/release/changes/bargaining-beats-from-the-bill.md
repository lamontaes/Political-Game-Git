title: Legislators bargaining with you talk about the bill, not made-up history
---

Before, a legislator in the bargaining room could bring up things that were
never true in your game: a garage that had sat on a replacement list for
years, a water bill of their own coming up, four votes they could hold, or a
session with "days, not weeks" left.

Now four kinds of remark speak only from the bill and the room: a member
pressing for something for home, a member saying they'll back you, a member
urging you to settle the bill before its next step, and a warning about the
next step itself. They name the section, the place, the amount and the next
step as the bill records them. A member says nothing in the bill is written for
their place only when that is true, and thanks you for a section only once it
is in.
