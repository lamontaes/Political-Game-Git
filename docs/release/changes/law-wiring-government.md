section: Improved
title: Election and government laws reach people and cost money
---

Mail voting, automatic registration, same-day registration and photo ID now reach every voter in town, named or counted, and the county or city that runs the election pays for ballots, registrations and IDs out of its administration budget. A treasury short of cash owes the rest and pays it as cash arrives, even after the law is repealed. When a council raises a staffing line (administration, police, fire, jail, schools), the employer posts the new jobs and any job seeker in town can be hired, and a cut lets the line's own hires go first. Local news now covers a budget line a council changes. A ban on lobbying after office keeps former officials from being hired as lobbyists and makes a council seat a little less attractive to newcomers. Term limits are applied when someone files, home rule when a council drafts a bill, and a state with an independent redistricting commission pays it every month. A public pay floor raises every public worker in town paid under it, and payroll pays them the raised rate; repealing it keeps the raises.
