title: The grocery tax exemption and sales tax changes show up on store prices
---

When a state, county or city exempts groceries from the sales tax, the town's stores lower their prices at their next monthly price review, and a household that could not afford all of its groceries buys more of them. Repealing the exemption raises those prices again. A change in the sales tax rate now reaches store prices the same way: the price before tax holds still and the tax moves what shoppers pay. The town's separate food-insecurity figure no longer moves on its own for the exemption, so the effect is not counted twice.
