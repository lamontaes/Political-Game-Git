title: People's principles form from their own lives
---

A person who meets a new law now forms their principles from what the game
records about their life, instead of holding none. What their parents held,
what the people they confide in hold, their party, a congregation or union,
the years they grew up in, their town's size, their work and home, a job they
lost and a crime against them each pull toward or away from a principle. They
hold it as firmly as the pulls add up, and a person pulled both ways is torn.
Reading a life again writes a new row only where the result changed, so
principles move with experience. A combative temperament holds a view harder
and a conflict-averse one gives way sooner, but temperament never picks the
side. The player's own principles are still the player's. Sitting
officeholders keep the principles drawn for them at the start until their own
lives are recorded.
