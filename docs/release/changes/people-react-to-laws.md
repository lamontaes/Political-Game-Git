title: People feel the laws that reach them, and it shows in votes and talk
---

When an enacted law takes money from someone, the game now records it, and
their spouse feels it too. A few days later they blame or credit whoever
signed the law and the legislators whose votes they know of. Close followers
of local news are likelier to know a vote, and people tell up to three others
they live or work with, who think it over as something a friend went through.

Town elections now weigh what residents think of each candidate's part in the
laws they felt, with the half year before the vote counting more. A legislator
who was blamed for a law leans toward a bill that changes it.

Ask an adult what they think of the people in office, and they will say who
voted for or signed the law that reached them and how it hit their household.
