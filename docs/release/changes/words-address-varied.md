The President's inaugural address no longer follows one outline. What it leads with comes from the President's traits and the year's record: the people they met, the country's long story, their mission, the year's work or the occasion itself. A talkative President says more, a terse one less, so addresses run from about seven paragraphs to eleven. The openings the owner turned down ("I'm from Wisconsin. I've been Vice President. Now I've been handed this job") are rewritten, and a President's home state is no longer called their birthplace.
