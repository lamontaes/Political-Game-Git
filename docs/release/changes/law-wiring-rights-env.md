title: Rights, privacy, environment, farm, park and road laws now reach people and money
---

When a council or legislature passes a clean air plan, a groundwater limit, an age check for social media, a facial recognition limit, dedicated park money or a shift of highway money to transit, the town's books now show it. Factories pay for the clean air plan, farms sell less under a groundwater cap, online platforms pay to check ages (whether or not they employ anyone in town), the police and parks budgets rise, and the transit line gets the moved highway money. A ban on discrimination keeps small employers from marking down older applicants, and a restriction on abortion raises the births the town counts. Repeal each and the effects stop. A law that sets a budget line, such as dedicated park money or the highway shift, is read again by the treasury each month and each new budget year, so it keeps its effect past the year it passed, and more highway spending lowers the share of roads in poor condition over time.
