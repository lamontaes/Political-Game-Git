section: Changed
title: When someone tells you where they stand, they name the question they care most about, and why.
---

A relative or friend who follows politics no longer just says "I lean conservative." They name the question they feel strongest about and which side they are on ("If it's a higher minimum wage, count me out."), and often why, from their own life: their job ("Working at the post office taught me that.") or money being tight in their town. You can agree or disagree on that question, but only on the side your own view is really on; ask them why; or keep your view to yourself. Their card then says what they care most about, and if you agreed or disagreed, they know where you stand.
