---
id: laws-small-dollar-lenders
impact: minor
section: Changed
title: Payday lenders now exist where state law allows them, and the loan rate cap reaches them
---

Towns in states that allow payday and other high-cost small loans now have a cash-advance lender, charging that state's real rate, which is several times a bank's. A household that is short of money and cannot get a bank loan can borrow from it against its documented pay. In a state that bans these loans there is no such lender. A consumer loan rate cap passed in a town that allows them now holds the lender's rate down to the cap, and the borrower's payments drop with it. Once a cap has been in force for a year, the lender also lends less to each borrower. A household that defaults on the lender is turned away for a year, and an open loan from it shows on the bank screen as a cash advance.
