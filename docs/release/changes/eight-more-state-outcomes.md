title: Every state now tracks hunger, reading, degrees, broadband, asthma, overdoses, earnings, transit, migration and borrowing costs.
---

Each state starts at its real recent share of households that go hungry,
eighth graders who read at grade level, adults with a college degree, homes
with broadband and children with asthma, and at its real overdose death rate,
typical earnings, amount of bus and train service, number of people moving in
or out, and what it pays to borrow. Each drifts over the years, sometimes with
the whole country, and is never pulled back to where it started. Laws now
reach them too: taking the sales tax off groceries eases hunger, naloxone
programs may cut overdose deaths, and letting towns build broadband brings more
homes online. A state that requires a balanced budget, funds its pensions on
schedule or keeps a minimum reserve pays less to borrow after a year or more.
Places with no published figure record nothing rather than a
guess.
