title: A state law dedicating revenue to parks moves the parks budget line and park access
---

Before, a state law on dedicating a share of revenue to parks changed nothing
and no place had a starting answer. Now every place starts with its real
answer: fifteen states already dedicate a tax, the lottery or royalties to
parks (Missouri, Minnesota, Oregon, Texas, Florida and Michigan read from their
own law, nine more from the Trust for Public Land's records of passed
measures), and the other 41 places start at no, marked as estimated.

A state that adds a dedication puts about $9.26 a resident a year on its parks
line, the average of Missouri's and Minnesota's dedicated sales taxes, and a
repeal takes it off. A year later the share of residents near a park or
recreation facility moves by about a tenth of a percent, either way, because
the research found access about zero against spending. Cities and counties
are not sized yet and start at their state's answer.
