title: Legislators vote for senators one by one, and old amendments come back only when something changes
---

Where an amendment has handed the choice of senators back to the state
legislatures, each legislator now votes for a candidate on their own: for
their party caucus's nominee, for a candidate they know and trust, against
one they have clashed with, and for one who shares their principles. A
sitting senator, including the player, is one candidate among the others,
and a legislature that cannot give anyone a majority leaves the seat empty.

Congress no longer proposes and rejects the same amendment every year: it
tries again only when its members, or how any of them would vote, have
changed. A state's application for a convention now stands until its
legislature votes to rescind it.

Empty Senate seats now follow the statutes read this week in 44 states.
