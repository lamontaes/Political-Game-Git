title: Office work and press exchanges use recorded choices instead of blank writing boxes
---

Office work and ordinary press exchanges now ask you to choose from what the
record already holds: the subject, what you are asking for, your stance, and
the exact wording shown before you commit. The reporter still owns the
question. If you look for a journalist covering public affairs, that search is
a contact in this life, not a developer construction.

On-background attribution comes from a recorded work title. A planned meeting
place names the arranged written or spoken channel; it does not invent a room.
An interview answer cites a recorded fact, or an attributed stance when no
correcting record is in hand. It does not treat the reporter’s question as
something already established. Older typed statements stay in the saved record.
Executive officeholding still has to be reached through a supported start; this
does not add a new way to take office.
