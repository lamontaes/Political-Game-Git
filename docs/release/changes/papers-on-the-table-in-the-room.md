title: The papers waiting on you are on the table in the room
---

What was waiting on you could be acted on, but only after two presses: open
the calendar, then find the day inside it. The room itself held nothing you
could touch. A room you can only look at is a backdrop, not a place.

The papers now sit on the table where the scene already has a place for them.
Resting on them says how many things are waiting, and nothing about what they
are; picking them up opens the list, and each line still leads to the record
that answers it, exactly as it does on the day. Putting them down, or pressing
escape, returns you to the room and to the paper you picked up.

Reading them spends nothing. The clock on the shell does not move when the
papers open, when a record opens from them, or when they go back down. When
nothing is waiting there is no object to pick up, and the table is the painted
one the scene has always shown.
