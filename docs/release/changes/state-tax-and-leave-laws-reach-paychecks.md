title: A state's income tax and paid leave laws now change what its workers take home.
---

When a state legislature repeals its income tax, its workers' paychecks stop
withholding it from the next tax year. When a state without one adopts it, or
a state switches between one flat rate and rates that rise with income, the
new tax is withheld from every paycheck and sent to the state. Bills do not
carry their own rates yet, so a new tax starts from the average of the states
that already have that kind of tax, and the paycheck record says it was
estimated from that average.

Workers in the states and D.C. with a paid family and medical leave program
now pay its premium from each paycheck, at the 2026 rate the program
publishes, from the date it began collecting. A state that creates a program
starts collecting a premium near the average of the others, and a state that
repeals its program stops.
