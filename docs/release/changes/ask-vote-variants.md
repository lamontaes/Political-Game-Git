section: Added
title: A colleague's ask for your vote now plays as a whip counting votes, a trade for a vote already given, a vote the town is watching or a friend's ask
---

A colleague who asks for your vote on a bill no longer asks the same way every time. One who holds a post in the body's leadership and shares your party is counting the caucus. One who voted for a bill of yours earlier names it and asks for the vote back. One asking about a bill the town has settled views on says what the town will see. A close friend asks as a friend. Which one asks is read from what the records show: the post the asker holds, the party you share, the earlier bills of yours they voted for, where the electorate stands on the questions the bill changes, and how close the two of you are. A scene where you do the asking plays as before.
