title: Legislatures stop passing laws that change nothing
---

A state legislature no longer passes the same law over and over in one session, or undoes a law it passed a few weeks earlier before that law took effect. A bill whose law is already on the books, or was passed earlier in the session and is waiting for its date, now ends on the floor without a vote and keeps a note of the law it repeated. Members also stop trading for a section that only repeats what the state already has, so the laws a session passes are the ones that change something.
