Add review dialogue for legislators debating whether a private-sector union fee can be required as a condition of employment. Members can open with a reason for their vote and hear a related question about the workers or contract at issue. Follow-up questions can come from a shift change, a transfer between plants, a bargaining request, or a vote on a contract. Members can press a bargaining point, ask for the agreement before advising a worker, or speak up for the worker left outside a vote. Floor answers can turn on the contract date, the plant named in an exception, the local’s ballot rules, or the authority for a payroll deduction. Members opposing a new ban can open with the state exception, a grievance, the store notice or a concession at the bargaining table.
A member can now ask what the vote changes for a clerk, a bargaining team or workers across a state line without reading the bill number aloud. Follow-up questions can refer to the member who spoke or ask how a worker in the questioner’s hometown would be affected. When a member’s hometown is missing from the record, the debate can still turn on the same paycheck, contract or vote at the union hall. Supporters of a new ban can open with a worker taking a transfer, a clerk reading the notice after hours or a nonmember left outside a contract vote.
