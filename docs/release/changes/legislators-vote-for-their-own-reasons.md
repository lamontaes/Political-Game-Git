id: legislators-vote-for-their-own-reasons
impact: minor
section: Changed
title: Every state legislator now casts their own vote
---

Where your state's legislature has been seated with real people, a vote on a
bill is no longer a count written in advance. Each member answers the
question: a sponsor votes for their own bill, a member of the sponsor's
party supports it, and a member of the other party has no reason to oppose
an ordinary bill but lines up against the sponsor's party on an override of
the governor's veto. A promise a member made about the question outweighs
those cues. Each
recorded vote says why the member voted as they did. An empty seat is a
vacancy rather than a voter, and a legislator who is the player is never
voted for. A legislative staffer now works for one of the members actually
sitting in the chamber, and bills the legislature files on its own are
carried by seated members, with the budget bill carried by the largest
caucus. Older saves keep their recorded votes.
