title: Personality shapes votes, petitions, campaigns and decisions in office
---

Voters, petitioners, campaign contacts, legislators, executives, staffers and party members now weigh their whole personality, at both ends of every trait, when they decide: whether to vote and for whom, whether to sign or start a recall, whether to open the door, give or volunteer, how a council member votes, whether a mayor signs or vetoes, whether an official helps a constituent, and whether someone accepts a press interview.

Turnout and door-knocking stay at real-world levels with personality counted: a person's sense of duty and reliability still weighs beside it, so about two in three named adults vote in a presidential November, a little under half in a midterm and about a quarter in an off-cycle local election, and a canvasser reaches about a quarter to a third of the doors knocked.
