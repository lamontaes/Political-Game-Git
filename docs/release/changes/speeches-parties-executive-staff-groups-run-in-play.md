Five parts of the town's political life were built but never happened unless you did them yourself. Now they go on around you. Residents who speak up at a council or board meeting are heard by the members and the rest of the room. Candidates' debates and speeches are heard as speeches. People who hear them may change their minds and repeat what they heard at home. Each party meets every month. The leader speaks to whoever shows up, and people who come of age or move to town pick a side. The governor, mayor or president records what they sign or veto. They replace a department head who dies or moves away, and a new governor names a new cabinet. Officials with a seat take on an aide from people they know or from applicants, and the aide briefs them before each vote. Residents who feel strongly about a bill form a group, recruit members and meet the lawmakers who will vote on it.
