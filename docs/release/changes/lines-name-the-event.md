title: Lines say what happened, in words a person would use
---

Several lines read like a form being filled in. A candidate now "filed to run
for Governor" instead of "filed as a candidate for a Governor", and a winner
"qualified to take office as Governor" instead of "Recorded qualification for a
dated executive term was entered". When time stops for a decision, the notice
says what the decision is ("Decide whether to request a federal disaster
declaration.") instead of "something came up".

Dates in these lines read the way people say them, "January 16, 2028", in
special-election notices, press deadlines, story threads, bill stages, term
notices, the district-residence panel, the life history under Who you are and
a judge's office history. The district panel also speaks to you
directly now: "You have lived in this chamber's district since …".

Choices that take no time no longer carry a "No time passes" label. "In this
game" is gone from the campaign and party screens. The political map no longer
names where its boundaries came from. A chief of staff's advice no longer has a
stray period in the middle of a sentence.
