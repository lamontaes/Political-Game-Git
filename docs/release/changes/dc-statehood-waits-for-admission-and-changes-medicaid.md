title: D.C. statehood seats and money follow admission, and Medicaid aid falls when the state certifies
---

Before, D.C. gained its two Senate seats and a voting House seat the day the
statehood law took effect, and no money moved. Now the District is admitted
180 days after the law takes effect, the days the bill allows for the
President's certification, the Mayor's proclamation and the President's
proclamation of admission, and the seats are filled then.

At admission no federal payment changes. Each year, when the new state adopts
its budget, its government decides whether it can certify to the President
that it covers five years of what ending the higher Medicaid match costs, and
writes down why. A state that certifies loses about $694 million a year of
federal aid from the next fiscal year: Medicaid falls from 70 to 50 percent of
$3.42 billion, and children's health insurance from 79 to 65 percent of $68.3
million. A state that cannot cover it keeps the higher match and asks again.
The numbers come from a data file.
