# Every state and town governs, and Congress passes real laws: the build plan

Laws lead, October 29. Today only Congress and the town's own state ever sit, and Congress passes no policy law. This plan makes every state, D.C., the territories and other towns govern, and Congress pass real laws, in eight pieces of work that each ship on their own.

**What the owner gets.** Every state legislature, D.C.'s council and the territories' legislatures sit on their real calendars and pass laws through the one legislature engine. Other towns' councils meet and pass ordinances. Congress passes policy laws, signed or vetoed by the president at the same desk every executive uses, and a federal law reaches named people in the town.

## Why it doesn't happen today

- **Only two sessions open.** The world opens sessions only for Congress and the town's own state (`world-legislating.ts:192-193`). The calendar data is already there: the session calendars have 2025-2027 rows for all 56 places, and effective dates, enacting clauses and starting answers cover all 56.
- **Other states have no chambers.** At the opening, the offices engine seats only the town's state. Other states get a sample of 8 officials per chamber with no office rows (`officials.ts:588-640`), so `chamber()` returns nothing and a session would file nothing. The builder that seats a whole state, `officesOfState`, already exists (`offices.ts:979-1018`), but only the Politics screen calls it, lazily.
- **A law from elsewhere would land as the town's own.** `billJurisdiction` returns the town's state for any state bill (`legislature.ts:1780-1790`). Effective dates, power checks, filer lean, settledness, interest groups and 13 event emits also read the town's chain.
- **Congress files only on the town's platform questions.** Its stances on federal questions are all zero, it reads federal law from a two-row file (`federal-answers.json`) instead of the researched starting law, and so it adopts only its budget (`SG-GOV-federal-proposal-coverage`).

## The one hard limit: speed

A town's year must stay under two minutes, and main is already at about 118 to 135 seconds (Long Hill, seed m7b). U1 adds about 7,400 officials as full people. On October 13, 1,614 such officials took a year from 97 to 175 seconds, though the biggest cause of that (daily drive predictions for everyone) is gone. So the budget for this whole item is about zero, not 20 percent. U1 is measured alone first. The cuts that change no behavior come first: town-only loops that skip officials, the by-body indexes, no town events for outside laws, interest groups only on the chain, and the floor agenda only for the player's bodies. Then the ruled lever is real bill volume (sessions today move 10 to 19 questions against about 6 real adoptions a state a year; `laws-session-moves-too-many-questions`). The CTO rules on the conflict before U1 lands.

## Seams with other sessions

- **Governing lead:** U1b edits `voting.ts` next to its campaign work; U2 edits `legislature.ts` next to its phases B and D. File order is agreed before each.
- **Words:** U2 moves outside events off the town's news and needs the press's `wireItem` change. C3 gives Congress and the president stances the opening address waits on.
- **Play-first:** the Politics screen calls `officesOfState`; after U1 that loads only the federal part, with no bench change.

## Found along the way (to file)

- A federal-and-state question's federal answer (the Medicaid work requirement, P.L. 119-21) gets no US starting row, so the town's law in force misses federal law.
- `officesOfState`'s federal part adds House seats that `racesIn` races again.
- `gov-federal-questions-admitted` has the same test file, stopgap and done-when as `gov-congress-passes-policy-laws` and should fold into it.

## Time

About 30 to 47 hours of builder time over eight PRs, plus a played-year test of about 6 minutes and a speed run of about 4 minutes per unit. U1 and the C2 research start October 29.

## Method

A read-only map of the code by four readers (other states, other towns, Congress, and a baseline run with the overlap search), then a planner and a completeness critic. The critic's corrections are applied above. The full map, plan and critique are in the JSON report beside this one, dated the same day.
