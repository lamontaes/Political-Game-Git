# Gap sheet, November 12, 2026: why the jobs, the history and the news read thin

A new life still shows a town of markets and kitchens, a Jobs screen with no openings and no way to take one, and a Journal with little to tell before the first day. Nineteen gaps were traced from the screen to the code, the open work and the owner's rulings. Seven need a builder, four are small and ruled, and eight need one named measurement first. None needs the owner.

## What was traced

Each row below follows the owner's three questions for a problem seen in play. A is the cause, with file and line. B is whether a fix or an item already exists. C is the ruling that answers it. Every claim is marked **measured** (a run or a count) or **inferred** (read from the code).

The three biggest gaps:

- **Row 1, jobs.** The town's jobs come from an authored list of 38 workplace kinds with about 100 role titles. The 852 real occupations in the repository are never read to build a town.
- **Row 3, no way to a job.** The Jobs screen shows no openings, and the player can never be a job seeker.
- **Row 4, the Journal.** No engine writes an accident, a hospital stay, an arrest, college or military service for anyone's past.

## 1. Jobs: "it's only the market and like craft shops. Why are there no real jobs?"

**Seen.** Owner, Nov 12: "even though I've been told multiple times that the job data is in the game ... It's only ever like tool places and dye places and markets." Wofford Heights, California, seed a9c85de3: the player stocks shelves at Sullivan's Market. The Travel list in a village of 128 people has three markets and three kitchens and no town office (`play-travel-in-a-village-lists-no-town-office`, audit Nov 9).

**A. Cause.**

- **Where the real job data is (measured).** `data/source/county-business-patterns/regional-corpus.json.gz` holds the county employment for 21 two-digit industry codes only (no deeper). `data/source/career-occupations/regional-corpus.json.gz` holds employment and wages for 852 detailed occupations in 584 states and metro areas, and `corpus.json` holds 1,016 occupations. The only readers are `scripts/source/regional-money/export-town-employment.ts` (state totals) and the pay table (`src/core2/reference/town-job-soc.ts`, which only prices a job). No engine builds a town's roles from the 852 occupations. There is no industry-by-occupation table in the repository.
- **What the town is built from (measured).** `src/core2/town-employment-mix.ts:143` and :213 map each of the 21 sectors to authored workplace kinds.
  - One to seven kinds per sector.
  - `src/core2/town-workplaces.ts:61` lists 38 kinds with two to four authored roles each (about 100 titles in all).
  - `opening-town-jobs.ts:511` (`fillTownJobs`) hires every working-age resident into the kind furthest below its share.
  - Each kind has one to three outlets (`town-workplaces.ts:50`, "outlets").
- **The weights are not the narrow part (measured).** `publishedTownWorkplaceWeights` in four drawn towns:
  - Amity, Missouri: 33 kinds (retail 22 percent, restaurant 9, wholesale 7).
  - Gray, Maine: 37 kinds (retail 10, hospital 7, manufacturing 7).
  - Wellton Hills, Arizona: 37 kinds (retail 15, restaurant 11, building services 9).
  - Springdale, Arkansas: 37 kinds (retail 10, regional office 10, manufacturing 9).

  Retail plus restaurants hold 17 to 31 percent. Hospital, clinic and bank weight is thrown away unless the town has a sourced institution (`opening-town-jobs.ts:620`), which empties the largest health and finance shares in a small town.

- **Why it reads as markets and kitchens (inferred, from the code and the names file).** The first kinds to fill in a town of a few hundred are the heaviest (retail, then restaurant, then wholesale, manufacturing). Each kind has three or four name styles in `src/core2/data/business/names.json`, so every retail door is "X's Market", "X Foods" or "Town Market", every restaurant "X's Kitchen", "County Diner" or "X's Cafe", and manufacturing's third style is "X Tool and Die" (the owner's "tool places and dye places"). A small town cannot spread a few hundred jobs across 37 kinds of one to three doors, so the same few words repeat.
- **What the screens show (measured, read).** The Jobs sheet (`src/bench/play.mjs:3676`) draws only the player's own job and past work. A card shows the person's own job. There is no list of the town's kinds anywhere.

**B. Open fix.** Item `core-town-jobs-are-mostly-markets-and-kitchens` (open, "Needs cause"; this row is its cause). `research-opening-jobs-sourced-parameters` (open) names `SG-P8-authored-job-mix` as the stopgap whose replacement is "a source-grounded open occupation/employer mix". `play-travel-in-a-village-lists-no-town-office` is a twin of the symptom. No open pull request builds the mix.

**C. Ruling.** OWNER-DECISIONS, Oct 27, 5 PM Eastern: "Businesses: already ruled Sept 22, 28 and 29 ... employers are generated to fit each town from calibrated business tables, no fixed list, no generic names ... The fixed 38-kind list in `src/core2/town-workplaces.ts` contradicts it and is a defect." Also "Business and roles database: private businesses may be simulated from a large calibrated database of business kinds and roles with real starting pay (public occupation and county business tables)" and DECISION-REGISTER, Sept 22: "Openings cover varied ordinary occupations".

**Fix.** Replace the authored role lists with roles drawn from the 852 occupations' employment in the town's state or metro area, weighted by the town's sector shares. Spread each kind over as many named employers as the town's size allows, using a worker count per establishment from the county size bands already in the CBP file. Delete `TOWN_WORKPLACES` roles and the name-style cap in the same change.

**Size.** An Opus day (the data join, the generator and the proof). **Heavy runs:** yes, the 24-month played-world proof and the three-town check (`labor-market-proof`); the measurement above needed none.

**Verdict: BRIEF A BUILDER** (Opus; rewrite item 1's Cause with the lines above, then build).

## 2. A housemate "works outside the town"

**Seen.** Wofford Heights, same life: a housemate's job reads "works outside the town".

**A. Cause (read; the count is not measured).** `src/core2/modules/residents.ts:3666` (`startCommuterJob`) gives the fill-in job title "Works outside the town" (line 3696) to a household member with no job when the household has a share of the place's unplaced workers (`residents.ts:2081`).

The unplaced pool is the county's employment rate times the counted adults, less recorded jobs (`src/core2/residents.ts:881`); the pay comes from the place's outside account, with no workplace, kind or occupation. So the title is the fill-in's one-size job for anyone the opening left jobless but the employment rate says should work.

`fillTownJobs` places every working-age resident into some kind. So the pool is whoever it could not place, plus the rate gap. Unplaced residents are those with a hospital, clinic or bank slot and no sourced institution, or a role with a minimum age.

**B.** Same item as row 1 names it (`core-town-jobs-are-mostly-markets-and-kitchens`, open). No open pull request. `world-jobs-elsewhere` style work (regional work, #8157, #8886) touches the same pool.

**C.** Standing rule: "One model, two tiers ... A rule that applies only to full people is an unregistered placeholder" and "Estimate, never UNKNOWN". The Oct 28 look-first audit (OD:192) ruled that a town with no open jobs offers "work elsewhere in the region (the outside employers)": that is the intended home of this job, but not as a title shown to a player on a housemate's card.

**Fix.** Show the outside employer by a worded kind and a real occupation drawn from the same table as row 1 (a regional occupation mix), so the card says "drives for a freight company in the next county", and keep the pay source as is. The count of how many get it is the one measurement: in three drawn towns, the share of filled-in adults holding a job titled "Works outside the town".

**Size.** A Sonnet afternoon after row 1's table exists. **Heavy runs:** the measurement is one short run; the fix is not heavy.

**Verdict: NEEDS MEASURING** (the share, in three towns; then it joins row 1's builder).

**Fix.** Split #8455. The screen lists positions from the position rows by record id, and that part stands.

## 7. Layoffs: full people at about 0.5 percent, counted workers at 5 to 8 percent

**Seen.** PR #8889's body (line "Pool note (needs an item, for the jobs lead)"): "the full people present at the opening are laid off at about 0.5 percent in six months, an order of magnitude below the counted books in every place ... Not traced here."

**A. Cause (read, not measured).** Both go through one function: `reviewTurnover` (`src/core2/modules/jobs.ts:2307`) builds a pull per worker from `layoffPerQuarter(...)` and lets the worker go when it reaches one. The two differ in three places:

1. A named person's pull adds their trait lean (`workLeans(...).layoffLean`, :2331); a counted one's is zero. A lean can only lower or raise the pull, so this is a possible factor of a few.
2. The worker chosen is the one `layOff` picks; for one job it picks that job, but `layOff` returns without ending the job when a full worker's last attendance record is today (`jobs.ts:1815-1821`: "c.work.lastAttendanceByJob.get(job.id)?.date === c.date"). The turnover pass runs in the monthly review, and the pull is kept and retried next month, so a worker who is on shift on review days is skipped every time. Counted jobs have no attendance record. This is the likeliest cause of a factor near ten (inferred).
3. `layOff` returns "over" for a counted job standing for more heads than the budget, which only slows counted workers, not full ones.

**B.** `people-residents-cadence-mechanic-part-2-counted-layoffs` (open, lead lives) claims the opposite direction ("counted tenure runs from the opening history ... so full workers are laid off first"). #8625 (open) fixes the measure it waits on (the turnover measure counts private jobs only). No item names the full-person rate.

**C.** Standing rule: "One model, two tiers (owner, Oct 14). The counted population is not a second simulation. A counted group is read by every engine with the same rule as a full person." No dice. `jobs.ts:2295` comment: JOLTS layoffs about 1.2 percent of employment a month.

**Fix.** If the attendance check is the cause, move the check to "the worker's day has not been played" for the controlled person only (its comment says so) and let every other full worker be let go on the review day. If the lean is the cause, report the lean's spread against JOLTS before changing it. One measurement settles it: in one drawn town for six months, count review-day returns of "undefined" from `layOff` by reason, and the leans, for full workers.

**Size.** A Sonnet afternoon. **Heavy runs:** yes, a six-month played run (give it to a builder on another machine).

**Verdict: NEEDS MEASURING** (the run named above; add the finding to the `part-2-counted-layoffs` item's Notes, do not file a twin).
