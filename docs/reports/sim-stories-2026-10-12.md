# Three years in two towns: people get by, but government never reaches them

Two random towns ran for three game years each, from October 12, 2026 to October 12, 2029: Greenfields, Pennsylvania, and Juliaetta, Idaho. Layoffs, unemployment checks, moves and one death reached named people. Government did not. Every election ran, but nobody new ever ran for office, no law changed, and no budget that passed changed a single dollar. Income tax and Social Security withholding stopped on January 1, 2027. The 2028 presidential winner never took office. No baby was born. Neither player character got past the golden path's first step. One call is yours: which missing modules (the outcome web, meetings, press and others) belong in early access. Each break below names the module behind it and a one-line job.

## The story in Greenfields, Pennsylvania

Each town has stories for the player character and three other people, then its government, then the town as a whole. Lines in quotation marks are the game's own words, copied exactly. News stories carry facts only, with no written headline, so no news text is quoted. Nobody steered the player characters; each lived on the game's own daily choices, the way a character lives through a skip.

Greenfields is an unincorporated place inside the Township of Bern, in Berks County. The game opened it with 61 named residents and a counted population of 9,941 people in 4,718 households. Its local government is the three-member Bern Township board of supervisors. The county has a seven-member board of commissioners and six row officers.

### Casey Ortiz, the player

Casey Ortiz was 18 on the opening day and turned 19 three days later, on October 15, 2026. She lived with her father, Mark Ortiz, 51. Neither of them had a job. Her mother, Brenda Sandoval, lived apart and sat on the Berks County board of commissioners. Casey had a brother, Alexander, and a grandfather, Tobias. She started with $828.30 in cash and had $0 by the first anniversary. She stayed at $0 for the next two years.

For three years Casey did two things. She rested, 1,096 times, and she got in touch with someone she already knew, 627 times. She contacted her brother 598 times, her mother 28 times starting January 18, 2027, and her father once, on October 18, 2026. She met nobody new. She knew three people at the start and the same three at the end. She never looked for work, filed for anything or applied for credit.

Casey's journal has two lines, both from childhood: "I started at Hereford El Sch." and "I started at Intermediate Sch." Nothing from the three years reached it.

She was on the rolls for 15 races and voted in none. Her ballot record shows a sense of duty and interest well above her cost of voting, yet she stayed home every time, because nobody voted for her (break 3). The game tied her to the Republican Party on the opening day. Her views are recorded as "not sure."

Laws reached her as eligibility only. Pennsylvania's Medicaid expansion made her eligible from November 12, 2026, at 138% of the poverty line. The state's rules on concealed-carry permits for young adults, juvenile court age, consumer-loan rate caps, abortion and automatic voter registration were all attached to her record on the opening day. No payment, service or coverage under any of them appears afterward. She heard 76 news stories, among them the governor's race result on November 7, 2026 and the Hoffman Academy closure on November 14, 2026.

## Where the story breaks

These are observed in both towns unless a town is named. Each says whether it was measured in the records or inferred, names the module behind it and gives a one-line job.

1. **Nobody ever decides to run for office.** (measured) In both towns over three years, every filing was "incumbent stands again" or an unnamed party challenger, and the candidacy engine recorded 0 decisions. No named person filed with a clerk, and no new face won anything. Module: `candidacy` (`src/core2/modules/candidacy.ts`). Job: have named adults weigh running when a local seat comes up, and record why.
2. **The golden path stops at step one.** (measured; the cause is in the code cited) Both players were 18 at the opening and turned 19 within three days. The bench picks the oldest 18-year-old (`src/bench/world.ts:78`), so turning 18 never happens in play. Neither player met a new person, looked for work or filed for office; their days were only resting and contacting relatives. Job: pick the resident closest to turning 18, and give an idle young adult work-seeking and civic choices.
3. **The player never votes on a skip.** (measured; cause in code) The voting engine casts no ballot for the player unless the player chose one (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:950`), so both players missed every election, including a presidential one. Job: on Election Day, stop the skip or let the character vote on its own reasons, as everyone else does.
4. **Income tax and Social Security stop on January 1, 2027.** (measured) The federal income tax, Social Security tax and state income tax rows end that day, with no rows after them. Withholding then drops to Medicare only (`src/core2/modules/budgets.ts:520` reads them through `lawInForce`). In Greenfields, Mark Abbott's 2027 pay of $80,954.88 had $1,173.94 withheld, all of it Medicare. Module: `laws` and its starting-law data. Job: carry tax rows forward until a law changes them.
5. **The 2028 presidential winner never takes office.** (measured; cause in code) The voting engine leaves the presidency to the national race engine (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1124`), and that engine (`world-races`) is not among the modules the played world installs (`src/bench/world.ts:256`). The winner has no name, and the 2026 president still sponsors bills in October 2029. Job: install the national race engine and name the major-party nominees.
6. **Passed budgets change nothing.** (measured) Idaho's state budget and Juliaetta's city budget passed every year with real cuts and raises, yet the adopted spending stayed at the opening figures. Module: `budgets` and `legislature`. Job: apply an enacted bill's line changes to the adopted budget.
7. **No law is ever proposed or changed.** (measured) Over three years and two towns, the only bills were yearly budgets; no ordinance, state law or policy bill was introduced. `world-legislating` is not installed in the played world. Job: let councils, legislatures and Congress introduce policy bills from members' positions.
8. **Officials keep serving after their terms end and after they move away.** (measured) In Greenfields, all six Berks County row officers' terms ended January 1, 2027, with no election until 2029, and the record keeps them serving. Richard Snyder, Cynthia Carson and Coroner Ryan Dominguez moved away in 2029 and stayed in office; Snyder filed for re-election after moving. In Juliaetta, Mary Wyatt and Christopher Fuller moved away and stayed in office, and Fuller's budget vote counted after he left. Modules: `offices`, `elections`, `moves`. Job: open a vacancy when an official moves out, and schedule an election when a term ends.
9. **Elections are scheduled wrong.** (measured; cause in code) Latah County voted on all nine seats in 2026 and on eight of them again in 2028 for terms that start in 2031 and 2032. Juliaetta's 2027 winners "took office" on Election Day for terms dated from then. Modules: elections and voting; the date a winner takes office is copied from the old term's end date (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1086`). Job: date each contest and term from the state's term rules.
10. **The counted population votes only in partisan races.** (measured) In every county, township and city race, the thousands of counted voters cast no votes, and races were decided by 2 to 34 named voters. In partisan races the counted vote split was identical across every contest on the ballot. In both states the Senate and presidential (or governor) totals matched exactly. Module: `voting`. Job: give counted voter groups local-race choices, and let ticket-splitting vary by office.
11. **Votes don't follow views.** (measured; cause inferred) Kelly Abbott ("very conservative") voted Democratic. Mary Wyatt and Christopher Fuller ("liberal") voted straight Republican. The ballot follows the party-tie lean, which can have the opposite sign from the views lean. Modules: `parties` and `views`. Job: reconcile the opening party tie with the person's views.
12. **No births, and a frozen counted population.** (measured) Across both towns, the 18 couples made 250 family-planning decisions over three years, and every one chose to wait. The 9,941 and 9,934 counted residents stayed exactly the same through three years, with no births, deaths or moves; they only aged. Modules: `core2-renewal-v1` (`src/core2/modules/renewal.ts:85`) and the residents counts. Job: let couples' circumstances lead to births, and age the counted population with births, deaths and moves.
13. **Moved-away people still count as residents.** (measured) Every person who moved away still has Greenfields or Juliaetta as their place in the records. Module: `moves`. Job: change the person's place and household when they move.
14. **Laid-off counted workers disappear instead of looking for work.** (measured; cause inferred) Employment fell by 685 in Greenfields and 367 in Juliaetta while unemployment fell. Module: `jobs` and the residents counts. Job: put counted workers from a closed employer into the job-seeker pool.

17. **Outcome measures are missing.** (measured) Crime, schooling, coverage and other outcome readings have no data because the outcome web module is not installed in the played world (`src/bench/world.ts:256`). The meetings, press, constituent cases, interest groups, ballot measures, recalls, staff and world panels modules are not installed either. Job: install the outcome web and decide which of the others belong in early access.
18. **Laws that reach no one.** (measured) Farmland protection, the balanced-budget rule, the pension contribution rule, the reserve-balance rule, the groundwater limit and the clean-air plan reached 0 residents in their towns. Module: law reach (`src/core2/law-reach/group-b-store.ts`); the same module switched two price rules on and off for Richard Snyder each fall and winter. Job: write "we moved" for a household move and add civic moments.
20. **Cash runs out for most people.** (measured; cause inferred) By the third year, 33 of 53 named Greenfields adults and 31 of 55 in Juliaetta had $0, including working people like Jace Rose. ## Method

Every fact above comes from the game's own records: its event log, its module tables, its journal and its news deliveries.

Both towns were built the way the bench builds the owner's game, two-level, with the economy on, opening October 12, 2026. Each place was drawn from a fresh random seed the same way the yearly speed script draws it. Each run advanced one day at a time for three game years with no character controlled. The story director watched the player and the chosen people so their journals were written. Snapshots were taken at the start and at each anniversary.

- Greenfields, Pennsylvania (place 4231038, seed `0a70fd61ecb59a33f5b27e9a5dd4bea1f907b99d`): build took 112 seconds and three years took 212 seconds, running alone. Two later runs with the director watching took 190 and 201 seconds and gave the same results. The heap reached 6.5 GB.
- Juliaetta, Idaho (place 1642130, seed `62767d576f09d8e54139acccb6b3511e5f1debbc`): build took 84 seconds and three years took 296 seconds, running alone. Election days were the slowest: November 3, 2026 took 10.9 seconds and November 7, 2028 took 8.6 seconds. The heap reached 7.7 GB. An earlier run sharing the machine with another run was stopped by the system for lack of memory in April 2029.
- No game errors were thrown. Memory grew by roughly 300 to 600 MB per game month in every run, which limits how long a game can run on a 16 GB machine.
