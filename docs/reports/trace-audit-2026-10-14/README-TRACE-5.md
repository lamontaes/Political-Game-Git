# A dead congresswoman won Virginia's governor race, and empty seats are refilled with no record or not at all

In ten places run for one game year, the state legislatures, budgets and Congress all ran. The town council or board met and voted in nine of the ten. Elections are the weak point. In Virginia a congresswoman died in July, won the governor's race in November and took office in January. In South Dakota a television reporter filled the mayor's seat and then appointed the city administrator. Across the ten places, seats emptied by a death or a move were refilled with no record, or stayed empty. Five fixes are listed at the end. Nothing needs the owner's decision.

## Defects, ranked by how much they break play

Each claim was measured from the year's record unless marked inferred. Each cause is one code location the verifier read and confirmed, unless marked otherwise.

1. **A dead candidate wins and governs.** Virginia, seed `a0b90e61b6e6`. Alice Foster (`official:term:seat:US-VA:us-house:08:2025-01-20`) filed for governor on April 8, 2025. Her House seat was vacated by her death on July 25, 2025 (`seat-vacated:seat:US-VA:us-house:08:2025-07-25`). She won `contest:seat:US-VA:governor:2025-11-04` by 812,737 votes to 785,614 and took office January 1, 2026. A second run on the same seed shows her not alive from July 26 on. Cause: the ballot keeps every filing marked on-ballot and never checks that the candidate is alive (`src/core2/modules/elections.ts:737`).
2. **A reporter holds the mayor's seat and appoints the town's staff.** South Dakota, seed `85748008adb3`. The mayor's seat was vacated February 1, 2025. On April 20, 2025 the city administrator was appointed by a television reporter, `person:press:outlet:television:2:jurisdiction_d6ec256b02df8147:reporter:0`, as the mayor's seat holder. The April 1 mayor race was won, unopposed, by `person_072fe54d456956ba`, whose term had not begun by April 20 (inferred from the term-start rule). Cause: the pool for an empty seat takes any living adult placed in the town, and press staff are placed there (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:256`). The same pool rule made the 18-year-old player character Public Works Director. She turned 18 on January 25, 2025, five days into the run, and 18 is the only test.
3. **Empty seats are refilled silently or not at all.** The ten worlds recorded 41 state and federal seats vacated, 3 to 6 per place. 17 fell under an appointment rule, and 24 under a rule that calls for a special election. Each appointment seat got a new serving term the same day with no appointment or took-office record (for example `seat:US-WV:lower:096:0`), because the appointment step writes the term but nothing to the log (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:861`). Three special elections were held, in Tennessee, Utah and Wisconsin. The other 21 seats that called for one had none by year end (for example `seat:US-TX:upper:006:0`). Cause: no special contest opens for a seat outside the town (`src/core2/modules/elections.ts:399`).
4. **No state legislative race where the town sits in no district.** Virginia, seed `a0b90e61b6e6`. The House of Delegates serves two-year terms, yet none of its 100 seats had a race on November 4, 2025. Mount Olive matched no district, so both chambers are marked as not covering the town, and contests open only for seats that cover it (`src/core2/modules/elections.ts:289`). The other nine towns each matched a district.
5. **Races run under the wrong office rules.** Wisconsin, seed `f872f1774f2b`. The superintendent office is titled "nonpartisan, spring election", but it held a party primary on August 12, 2025 and a general on November 4 (`contest:seat:US-WI:superintendent:2025-11-04`). Cause: every state office gets the November date (`src/core2/elections-calendar.ts:130`). Virginia's sitting governor also filed for a consecutive term, which Virginia bars. Cause: term limits apply only to the presidency (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1112`).
6. **Elections with one name or none.** Vermont, seed `7cf881828c25`. Selectboard seat 0 had no candidate, and its result (`contest:seat:gus2025:191738:board:0:2025-03-04`) names no winner. All 12 county party primaries on August 12, 2025 counted no votes, because a party primary with fewer than two names is skipped (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1317`). Washington, seed `0e4e6bf1f6bc`: each of eight county races had one candidate, and primary totals are fractions such as 3,261.54. The fractions come from adding the wider population's turnout without rounding (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1270`). South Dakota's three April 1 races each had one candidate. Cause: only the incumbent files for a town seat (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1003`).
7. **Legislatures vote in lockstep.** All ten places. Every bill goes to one committee, named "general", with the same members, and bills held there record 0 to 0. Floor tallies repeat: Wyoming's House voted 56 to 6 on 12 of its 16 recorded votes, and West Virginia's Senate voted 32 to 2 on 13 of 16. Cause: every bill is sent to the "general" committee (`src/core2/modules/legislature.ts:1768`).
8. **Congress is recorded as a town council.** Eight of ten places. On December 15, 2025 the U.S. House's leadership is written under "council-president", naming a member of the home delegation (Virginia `official:term:seat:US-VA:us-house:07:2025-01-20`). Cause: the leadership engine does not recognize the delegation's House office and falls back to the council layout (`src/core2/modules/leadership.ts:113`).
9. **Recalls and veto overrides end with no outcome.** South Dakota had two recall petitions, Washington three and Wisconsin seven, all filed March 6, 2025. None of the petitions has an outcome record, because the recall step changes the seat but writes nothing to the log (`src/core2/modules/recalls.ts:284`). Tennessee's council shows one bill "overridden" with no override record, because the override vote also writes nothing to the log (`src/core2/modules/legislature.ts:2188`).
10. **Very few deaths.** Each world holds about 1,850 people. Each lost 0 to 4 of them to death in the year (inferred from the count of people alive). The record counted 0 or 1 per world, because deaths outside the player's circle are not kept (`src/core2/state.ts:690`). The cause of the low death rate was not found.
11. **Three years ran over two minutes:** Tennessee took 127 seconds, Wisconsin 134 and Utah 222. Three worlds ran at once on four cores, so these times are not a clean measure. The cause was not found.

## The five things to fix first

1. Take a candidate off the ballot when they die or move away, and check again before seating a winner (`src/core2/modules/elections.ts:737`).
2. Fill empty seats and town posts only from residents, never from press or other placed staff (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:256`).
3. Write a record for every filled vacancy (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:861`), and hold special elections for state seats outside the town (`src/core2/modules/elections.ts:399`).
4. Hold state legislative races where the town falls in no district (`src/core2/modules/elections.ts:289`). Give each office its own calendar and primary method (`src/core2/elections-calendar.ts:130`) and its own term limit (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1112`).
5. Organize the U.S. House as the federal House (`src/core2/modules/leadership.ts:113`), and give legislatures committees by subject so votes differ by bill (`src/core2/modules/legislature.ts:1768`).

## Method

One Haiku agent per place wrote its file, and one Sonnet agent added a cause to all 192 items. Of the 192, 152 were confirmed at a code line, 28 were judged correct behavior or a quirk of the viewer, 8 are probable, and 4 were not found. The Virginia death was confirmed by a second run on the same seed.
