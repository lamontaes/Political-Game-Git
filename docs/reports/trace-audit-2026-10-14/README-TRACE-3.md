# In two territories a player cannot run for anything, and in the states officeholders keep moving away

One owner question: should the town's log record deaths outside the player's circle? Today it doesn't, so no one can say who died this year.

Twelve places ran for one game year each, ten states and two territories. In every state, towns, legislatures and Congress met, passed laws and adopted budgets. American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands have no local government and no legislature seats, so a player there has no office to file for. In the states, officeholders move away as often as anyone else. Of 27 local races, 17 had one candidate and 4 had none. A legislature does its whole year in one day. Congress is run as if it were a town council.

## Defects, ranked by how much they break play

Each defect names a place, its seed, a record and the code that causes it. "Measured" means the record is in the year's file or a re-run of the same seed. "Inferred" means it comes from reading the code.

1. **A territory has no government to run for.** In American Samoa (seed `2dfdf07a2bc5`), `office:US-AS:legislature-upper` exists with 0 seats, and there is no village or district government. The Northern Mariana Islands (seed `e239259b8609`) has the same gap. The territory budget is "carried forward by the budget rule" and logged as adopted. Measured. Cause: legislature seats come only from Census district records, and `src/districts/identities.generated.json` holds none for American Samoa, the Northern Marianas, Guam or the Virgin Islands (`src/core2/offices-state.ts:546-575`). Local bodies need a source GEOID that the territory villages lack (`src/core2/local-governments.ts:103-143`). Inferred from code; no stopgap covers it.
2. **Officeholders leave office by moving, at the resident rate.** In Southbridge, Massachusetts (seed `9ff7c242b94b`), all five council seats were vacated "moved away" between February 1 and April 1, 2025 (`seat:gus2025:224814:council:3` first). In Minnesota (seed `a9b64e500aad`), 11 state and federal seats emptied the same way, from `seat:US-MN:upper:053:0` on June 1, 2025 to `seat:US-MN:us-house:03` on January 1, 2026. Measured. Cause: an officeholder is a mover like anyone else, and a move out of the office's area vacates the seat (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:133-146`). Nothing in the move decision weighs holding office. Inferred.
3. **Replacements leave no record, and some are newspaper and TV owners.** Every emptied local seat was refilled, but only as a new term row. No record says who was appointed or why, so no one in the town can learn of it (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:849-905`). The pick of a replacement does not exclude newspaper and TV owners. A newspaper head holds Southbridge council seat 3 and sponsors its bills, and TV heads hold Fayette, Mississippi board seat 2 and its assessor seat (`src/core2/modules/offices.ts:256-289`). Measured on re-runs of both seeds; cause inferred from code.
4. **Most local races have one candidate or none.** The year's files print 27 local races: 17 had one candidate, 4 had none and 6 were contested. On November 4, 2025, Southbridge council seats 0, 2 and 4 had no filer; `contest:seat:gus2025:224814:council:0:2025-11-04` shows no winner and no votes, and the old members hold over. The mayor ran alone and got 3,894 votes. That count is right: it includes the modeled wider population, not only the 2,002 full people. Asbury, New Jersey (seed `4f6dec4a3e47`) elected its sheriff, clerk, treasurer and assessor unopposed. Measured. Cause: only state and federal seats get a stand-in challenger; a local seat waits for a resident to choose to run (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:937-1050`, stopgap SG-GOV-who-runs-stand-in). Cause inferred from code. A player who files for council will often face no one.
5. **A legislature does its whole year in one day, and Congress does its year on December 15, 2025 in every world.** Minnesota filed, heard and voted on 54 of its 55 bills for the year on May 19, 2025. Massachusetts, Michigan, New Hampshire and New Jersey each did theirs on June 30, 2025. Congress handled 21 to 25 of its 22 to 26 bills on December 15, 2025 in all 12 places; the rest was the budget on October 1, 2025. Town boards meet on the same dates in every world (February 4, February 19 and March 6, 2025, and so on), 15 days apart. Measured. Cause: `src/core2/modules/world-legislating.ts:184-190` sets one floor day per session, and `:811-835` schedules local meetings at a fixed interval from the opening. The dates are TUNABLE parameters, not registered stopgaps. Cause inferred from code.
6. **Congress is filed under each state's delegation and organized as a town council.** In every place, U.S. House bills sit in the state's own office (`office:US-MO:us-house` in Benton, Missouri, seed `70cdb7d9991e`), with sponsors and chairs from other states. On December 15, 2025 that office records a `chamber-organized` with a "council-president", `official:term:seat:US-MO:us-house:06:2025-01-20`. Measured. Cause: `houseOffice` takes the state's delegation office but every member nationwide (`src/core2/modules/world-legislating.ts:258-270`). Leadership doesn't recognize that id as the House, so it falls through to the council template (`src/core2/modules/leadership.ts:113-137`). Cause measured on a re-run and read in code. No stopgap.
7. **State seats outside the town's district never get a special election.** The seat sits empty for 90 days, then a stand-in takes it with no race. Michigan's state House voted its October 1, 2025 budget with 109 of 110 members, because seat 060 had been empty since September 1, 2025. Measured on a re-run. Cause: `src/core2/modules/elections.ts:399-400` opens a special race only for a seat the town covers; `src/core2/modules/offices.ts:949-1052` seats the stand-in after the 90 days. Cause inferred from code. Stopgaps SG-GOV-special-election-appointee and SG-GOV-special-election-timing are open, but their text does not say they cover every seat outside the town.
8. **A candidate who dies stays on the ballot and keeps campaigning.** In Mullen, Nebraska (seed `f84aaa128d73`), `person_2d09f6e295cdb677` died January 6, 2026 (`life.death:person_2d09f6e295cdb677:2026-01-06`). Their coroner campaign sent a message three days later (`campaign-message:filing:contest:seat:gus2025:130179:coroner:0:2026-11-03:person_2d09f6e295cdb677:2026-01-09`). Measured. Cause: no election, campaign or voting module listens for a death (`src/core2/modules/campaigns.ts:2246-2250` runs a turn for any on-ballot filing). Cause inferred from code. No stopgap.
9. **Primaries count no votes.** Every New Jersey primary in the file, held June 3, 2025, shows `votes: {}`. One is the governor's primary for the November 4, 2025 election, `contest:seat:US-NJ:governor:2025-11-04`. Measured. Cause: a party with one filer skips the count but still writes a result (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts:1317`). Cause inferred from code.
10. **Recall petitions can name the wrong body.** On March 6, 2025, recall petitions opened against Mullen city council seats 2 and 3 (`recall:term:seat:gus2025:108424:council:2:2025-01-20:2025-03-06`). The law they cite is the county's, not the council's, so two council members face recall over a vote their body may not have taken. Cause: a recall finds its bill by number and question, not by body, and bill numbers restart in every body (`src/core2/modules/recalls.ts:178-190`). Inferred; the raw file does not print the council's own bill.
11. **Unregistered placeholders.** `restrict-abortion` and `age-verification` have mechanic "none" and no stopgap (`src/core2/data/laws/reach-group-a.json:762-796`). "Raise the minimum wage" jumps every such state to a flat $15 an hour, whatever its current minimum (`src/core2/law-reach/group-a-wages.ts:134-152`). In the made-up history before the game starts, every winner's share of the vote is one of two constants: 0.56 for statewide offices and 0.635 for state legislators (`src/core2/data/offices/tenure.json:52-60`). That history also lets anyone 21 or older have held any office, the presidency included. These are tagged estimates or nothing, not registered stopgaps. Inferred from code.

Deaths outside the player's circle are not logged. So Southbridge shows "deaths: 0" in a year when its full people went from 2,002 to 1,998 alive. That is the log's scope, and it is the owner question at the top.

## Five things to fix first

1. Seat the territory legislatures and village or municipal governments from their own data, so the golden path exists in American Samoa, the Northern Marianas, Guam and the Virgin Islands. Owner: OPUS-GOV.
2. Make holding office a reason to stay in the move decision, write an appointment record when a seat is filled, and keep press owners out of the pick. Owners: OPUS-PEOPLE for moves, OPUS-GOV for offices.
3. Give local seats the same stand-in challenger that state and federal seats get, so a council race has an opponent. Owner: OPUS-GOV.
4. File U.S. House business under the federal House id and organize it with the House template. Owner: OPUS-GOV.
5. Spread a session's work across its floor days, and set each place's meeting days from its own calendar instead of a fixed interval from the opening. Owner: OPUS-GOV.

## The viewer itself

About a third of the readers' questions came from the viewer, not the game. It asks for several tables under module ids that don't hold them ("Unregistered module table"). It prints only the oldest 200 local news items. It filters rows by their first 400 characters, and it hides every local term. The trace tool prints fact names such as "measureId" as reasons. These are listed as C17 and C18 in the batch A check and C1 to C6 in batch B. Owner: OPUS-PLAY.

## Method

On October 14, 2026, one viewer year per place ran from `packages/office-play`, three at a time. Twelve readers each wrote one place file. Two checkers traced every question to code, re-running the seven batch A seeds outside the repository. Of 176 batch A questions, 118 had a real cause, 30 were viewer artifacts and 28 were misreadings. Batch B found 40 causes across 124 questions. Place files, both checks and the raw viewer output are in this folder.
