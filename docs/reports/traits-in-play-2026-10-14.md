# Personality changes a few everyday choices and almost nothing in politics

In one played year, a person's personality changed which everyday choice they made about 1 time in 37 for daily activities and about 1 time in 8 for how they answer a strong drive. In politics it barely registered: of 832 decisions about whether to run for office, personality changed 8 outcomes. Twenty-one of the 64 personality decisions never ran, including every corruption and campaign-helper choice. The everyday chooser gives personality one fifth the weight of needs, so a need almost always wins. The owner needs to decide one thing: whether personality should weigh as much as needs and goals do (a weight of 1.0 instead of 0.2).

## What the player sees

- Neighbors with opposite personalities live nearly the same day. Personality changed the pick in 543 of 20,260 daily-activity choices (2.7%) and 188 of 21,133 decisions to show up for a work shift (0.9%). (Measured.)
- People with a strong drive are where personality shows. When a drive pushes someone toward a cause, a grudge or a calling, personality changed what they did in 1,276 of 9,892 choices (12.9%). (Measured.)
- Who runs for office is decided by engagement, cause, standing and a fixed pull to stay out. A bold, outspoken resident and a cautious, quiet one in the same circumstances usually make the same choice: 8 of 832 run-or-stay-out decisions would have gone the other way without personality. (Measured.)
- The central bank board is the one place personality decides the result. It changed 44 of 96 rate votes, because members' options score within 0.19 of each other. (Measured.)
- Corruption, recalls, ballot measures, campaign volunteering, donating and endorsing, appointments and constituent cases did not run once in the year, so the personality reads they carry never reached the player. (Measured.)

## Where traits can't change anything

**The chooser at 0.2.** `traitLean` is held between -1 and 1 (`choice.ts:100`), so one offer's trait term is at most 0.2 and the gap between two offers is at most 0.4. The largest gap seen in the year was 0.26, and the average was 0.03 to 0.14 depending on the site. The other terms are far larger: the need term reached 25.6 in daily choices, the drive term reached 1.0 and module reasons reached 26 in job reviews and 71 in planning a child. Personality can only decide a choice the other reasons leave within about 0.1 of a tie. That happens in drive choices and rate votes, rarely in daily life and almost never in jobs, school, children or money.

A 0.2 weight does flip choices in practice, but rarely: 2,764 of 120,596 chooser decisions with two or more options (2.3%). A weight of 1.0, the same as need, goal and drive, would change 15% of daily choices and 25% of drive choices while leaving jobs, credit, children and founding a business mostly to their money reasons. That is the weight at which an average personality gap (0.4 to 0.7 at weight 1.0) matches the average gap between the top two daily offers (0.50). This is inferred from the counterfactual above, not from a run at 1.0.

**Moving away.** Both chooser offers carry the same act kind (`commit`, `moves.ts:318`), so the chooser's trait term is always zero by design. The only personality read is the hold term, 0.3 times the lean (`moves.ts:311`). The mean lean in the year was 0.16, so the mean term was 0.05, against holds of 1.21 for owning a home, 0.48 for a job and 0.40 for children. At the largest lean seen (1.25) the term is 0.38. Personality adjusts how fast the pull builds and cannot outweigh a home or a job. (Measured lean; the effect on the move date is inferred.)

**Running for office.** The run offer adds 1.0 times the lean (largest 1.86, mean 0.48) to engagement and cause terms weighted 2, against a fixed pull of 4.0 to stay out (`candidacy.ts:261`, `:888`). The mean gap between run and stay out was 2.79, so only the most extreme personalities near a close call change their decision: 8 of 832. (Measured.)

**Founding a business.** The trait term is 1.0 times the lean (largest 1.45) against a market-room term weighted 6 and a baseline of -3 (`business.ts:1204` to `:1250`). The mean gap was 9.04, and 12 of 302 decisions changed. (Measured.)

**An incumbent standing again.** An incumbent stands when 0.9 plus the lean is above zero (`voting.ts:627`). Only a lean below -0.9 retires anyone. The largest lean in the year was 0.98 in size, so this can happen, but the 15 reads in the year were not counted for outcome. (Inferred.)

**A lean larger than its stated scale.** `traitLeanScale` says the strongest personalities reach about 1. In the played year the largest scaled lean was 3.33 (claiming retirement), 2.05 (counted workers looking around) and 1.90 (lobbyists). The scale does not hold the value near 1 when one option's act kinds sit at the bound. (Measured.)

**Reads repeated for the same person.** `press.follow-news` was read 278,240 times and `news.pass-on` 108,796 times for about 2,000 people. Each read recomputes the same answer for a person whose traits did not change. This is a speed cost, not a personality defect. (Measured count.)

## Duplicated decisions

1. **Who runs for office.** Three paths. The first, `candidacy.ts:decideToRun`, uses the chooser plus a temperament term. Personality counts twice there: once at 0.2 over the run act kinds, and once at 1.0 from the same table row. The second, `voting.ts:627`, decides whether an incumbent stands again. It uses its own row and files only incumbents the first path never weighed. `world-races.ts:609` fills races elsewhere from party candidate inputs with no personality. The played world uses all three: the first for town residents, the second for sitting officials who were never weighed, the third for races outside the town.
2. **Claiming retirement.** `opening-income.ts:626` decides the claim age while the world is built (40,728 reads) and `retirement-claims.ts:92` decides it again during play (12,167 reads). Both use the same row and formula, so they agree, but the same person's decision is computed in two places.
3. **Looking for work and leaving a job.** Full people decide through the chooser (`jobs.ts:699`, `:912`, `:970`, `:1081`, weight 0.2 on unscaled lean). Counted people decide in formulas at `jobs.ts:1004` and `:1066` (0.2 times the unscaled lean difference) and `jobs.ts:1250` and `:1455` (0.5 times the scaled lean). The same personality weighs about 0.2 in one path and up to 0.8 in the other.
4. **Business owners.** A full owner decides through the chooser plus a trait term (`business.ts:1204`, `:1279`), so personality counts twice. A counted owner decides by the sum of the module reasons with no personality at all (`business.ts:465`, registered stopgap).
5. **Backing the incumbent.** The same row is read at three places in `voting.ts` (`:404` for a full voter, `:827` and `:1279` for counted voters and the counted appeal). The formulas match; the code is repeated.
6. **The rate vote.** A board member's view is built from hand-picked board traits such as patience (`central-bank-board.ts:430`), and then the chooser adds the general trait term over the rate act kinds (`macro.ts:335`). Personality enters twice through two different trait lists.
7. **Joining a party.** Two separate reads of `parties.join` at `parties.ts:310` and `:484`, one for members already in a party's circle and one for everyone else. Same row, two code paths.

## What to change, in order

1. Ask the owner to rule on `traitWeight`; if he wants personality visible, set it to 1.0 in `src/core2/data/parameters.json` and rerun this count (OPUS-CORE).
2. Remove the double count in candidacy by dropping the temperament term or giving both offers the same act kinds, in `src/core2/modules/candidacy.ts` (OPUS-PEOPLE).
3. Remove the double count in business founding the same way, in `src/core2/modules/business.ts` (OPUS-WORLD).
4. Make counted job seekers and retirees use the chooser's weight and scale, in `src/core2/modules/jobs.ts` at lines 1004, 1066, 1250 and 1455 (OPUS-WORLD).
5. Clamp `traitLeanBetween` to -1..1, or rescale it, so no site sees a lean of 3.3, in `src/core2/trait-decisions.ts` (OPUS-CORE).
6. Cache each person's lean per decision row for the day in `src/core2/modules/press.ts` and `src/core2/modules/news.ts` (OPUS-PEOPLE).
7. Run a played year with an election that reaches corruption, campaigns, recalls, ballot measures and cases, so their personality reads are seen at least once, using `src/core2/trait-coverage.test.ts` as the list (OPUS-TRAITS).
8. Decide claim age once, during play only, in `src/core2/opening-income.ts` and `src/core2/modules/retirement-claims.ts` (OPUS-WORLD).
9. Build the rate vote's view from the trait table instead of hand-picked board traits, in `src/core2/central-bank-board.ts` (OPUS-WORLD).

Method: one played year, run twice for the counterfactual weights, on the branch the tree was on (October 14, 2026). The counting script lives only in the session scratchpad. The bench's player was not driven, so player-only decisions such as blaming an official were not exercised.
