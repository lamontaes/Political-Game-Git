# Every state and D.C. has wages, counties, rents and commutes; ten small counties miss a figure, and ten sources are not yet in

Lamontae: to finish this data, widen this cloud environment's network access to the government and research sites below, or have the files fetched elsewhere. The session cannot reach them now. The data already brought in covers all 50 states and Washington, D.C. Every county has a wage area and a commute record. Ten small counties lack 14 published figures between them, and those stay blank rather than zero. Pay schedules exist only as one national table. Household spending exists for the nation and for four regions, so it cannot tell two states in the same region apart. Ten requested sources are missing or incomplete. All of this rests on four data pull requests that are still waiting for review.

## What is covered

The check reads only the source files already stored in the project. It sets no number the game uses. Everything in this section was measured on September 26, 2026.

- **Wages by state.** All 51 have a statewide all-jobs wage. Across the 51, the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes a yearly mean wage for 34,524 state-and-job pairs. It does not release 607 more. For 92 others the wage is above its top code: the job pays it, but the figure is not given. Every state has at least one unreleased job wage.
- **Wage areas.** Every state has at least one metro or rural wage area. Texas has the most, 32, and California 28; D.C. and Rhode Island have one each.
- **Counties.** All 3,144 counties and county equivalents in the 50 states and D.C. belong to a wage area.
- **Businesses.** 3,142 of the 3,144 counties have County Business Patterns totals. Kalawao County, Hawaii, and King County, Texas, have no row in the Census file.
- **Public pay.** Every state has state and local government pay rows. D.C. has 59 rows filed as state government and none as local. The Bureau of Labor Statistics files the D.C. government as a state, so that is how the source is built, not a loss.
- **Home values and rents.** Every county has an American Community Survey record. The Census Bureau gives no median home value for 5 counties and no median rent for 7. Kalawao, Hawaii, and Crockett, Edwards, Kenedy and Loving, Texas, lack the home value. Alpine and Sierra, California, and Borden, Kenedy, King, Loving and Terrell, Texas, lack the rent. That is 14 missing figures in 10 counties, because Kalawao, King, Kenedy and Loving each miss two.
- **Commutes.** Every county has a published commute total.
- **Pay schedules.** One national table of private employers, from February 2023, with no state rows and no government employers.
- **Household spending.** National tables by income and by household size, plus four Census regions: Northeast, Midwest, South and West. Every state falls in one region.

## Why the gaps are what they are

- The blank home values and rents carry the Census "cannot be computed" mark, `-666666666`, in the stored files (measured; the mark's meaning is from the [Census summary-file handbook](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/handbooks/acs_table_based_summary_file_handbook.pdf)). The check counts them as missing (`scripts/source/regional-money/coverage.ts`), and no value was put in their place.
- The two counties without business totals have no row at all in the Census 2023 county file (measured, per the earlier source report, `docs/reports/spine1-regional-money-data.md`). Both are among the least populated counties in the country; that this is why is inferred.
- The unpublished wages carry the Bureau of Labor Statistics marks `*`, not released, and `#`, above the top code (measured in the stored files; the meanings are the publisher's). A job without a published wage will need a figure from a wider area or the nation. Which one is not decided; Team A decides when it wires wages.
- The pay-schedule table has no state or government rows because the publisher's own table covers only private employers nationwide (measured: its stored description says government is out of scope). A state or government schedule needs another source or a design decision.

## What is not in yet

Ten sources are missing or incomplete:

1. Consumer Price Index categories: only the series list is stored; the price files are not.
2. Survey of Consumer Finances (savings by age and income).
3. Federal Student Aid balances.
4. Freddie Mac historical mortgage rates.
5. JPMorgan Chase Institute cash-buffer research.
6. Fiscal Survey of the States reserve funds.
7. Census government finances, the added state and local calibration.
8. Energy Information Administration gasoline and electricity prices.
9. Census trade import shares.
10. Tax tables: IRS Publication 15-T and bracket updates, with each state's revenue department tables.

All ten are blocked for the same measured reason. This environment's network policy refuses the publishers' sites: the proxy answered 403 for the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Census, IRS, Energy Information Administration, Federal Reserve, Federal Student Aid, Freddie Mac, the National Association of State Budget Officers, the Tax Foundation and JPMorgan Chase. A copy typed from a browser would not be the publisher's original file, so none was made.
