# Playtest, Oct 24: can the owner play tonight

Headline: Not tonight: he can file for council and campaign, but the ballot won't reach his own race, no screen says he won, and six weeks later the game locks on December 14, 2026.

Furthest golden-path step reached as one person: 5 in the game's records, where Bo Snider won Anson City Council seat 0, 448 votes to 246. On screen it is 4: I filed and campaigned for 21 months, and no screen said I won.

Build: v0.22.10, commit 4c943f3, the newest Mac release, run from its own files on Linux (see "How this was run").

Place and seed: Anson, Texas, drawn at random from the 56. Seed 670bd1f7-66a4-4345-9ccd-98b9cdc7e213 (not shown on any player screen).

## What this means

The owner can play for about half an hour of real time before something stops him. The opening scene, the character creator, the home scene, the menus, filing at the city secretary and the campaign screen all work. Time moves, the town changes in the records, and a debate invitation and election night come on their own. Every conversation, though, is a greeting and a goodbye. The ballot won't page past five county races. The game never says he won. Then a job offer he can't see locks the game for good. The first fix that matters is the lock; the next two are the ballot and telling the player the result.

## What worked

- **It launches and builds a world fast.** The title screen came up in about 3 seconds; Begin to the opening scene took about 25 seconds.
- **The opening is good.** The President's speech in front of the White House names my own U.S. senator, U.S. representative, state legislators and two of my council members as the camera passes them.
- **The character creator is complete and looks good:** gender, name, birthday, any of the 56 places, any town by search, and a drawn full-body figure with build, skin, face, hair, outfit and colors.
- **Filing works from the town's own records.** Travel lists the city secretary as a filing office; the secretary is a named person; filing shows the age and residency rules with the Texas Election Code citation, and I went on the ballot.
- **The world runs.** Time moves about 3 seconds a week, and family members visit. A debate invitation came the week before the election with four real issue questions. Election night came with a party room, results by county, exit polls and turnout. The game saved, and CONTINUE on the title screen picked up where I quit.
- **Under the screens, the election worked:** I won my seat 448 to 246 over four opponents, decided by votes, not shown to me.
- **No crash and no blank screen** in 55 minutes of play; no page errors in the browser log.

## How this was run

- Downloaded `OurCivicDuty-0.22.10-mac-arm64.zip` from GitHub Releases (the dmg holds the same app). This cloud container is Linux x86, so the Mac app itself can't run here.
- The app is an Electron window around two unchanged parts: a static client and a bundled play server (`Resources/play-api/play-api.mjs`), which the app runs in a separate process with developer routes off. I ran the release's own play-server host under Node 22 (the mode its desktop tests use), served its client with its own file-serving rules, and opened it in Chromium at the app's window size, 1440 by 900. The build stamp inside the app reads version 0.22.10, production, not dirty, staged 14:18 UTC October 24. Only the Electron window was replaced; this is not the bench, and no dev mode was used.
- Play ran from 15:47 to 16:42 UTC (55 minutes). Most of the 21-month campaign was played as one Knock on doors and one Week press per week, stopping by hand at every scene that asked for me.
- Two numbers came from outside the player's view, to explain what the screen didn't: the election result (the game's results record) and the job-offer hold behind the lock (the game's stop record). Both are marked where they appear.
- Not tested: the installed app's window, menus, updater and save location on a Mac; the Watch the world and Custom world modes.
