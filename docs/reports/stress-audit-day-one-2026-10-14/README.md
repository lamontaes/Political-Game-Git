# Lubec opens with 10,000 people and officials with no past

The game opens January 20, 2025, in Lubec, Maine, a real town of 1,346 people, and builds 10,000. Every official is an invented person, as you ruled, and every party matches the real one. Each official's campaign past is one date and a fixed vote share. Lubec's select board starts on opening day with no term end and no race. Maine's minimum wage law moves from the real $14.65 to $15.00 the next day. Six questions below need your ruling, the first two about town size.

## Day one, as the records tell it

These are the records of the first world (seed 1) on the first morning. The play build drew Lubec from the seed: first a state from all 56, then any town in it, of any size.

Lubec opens with 10,000 people in 5,798 households (measured). Its biggest employers are three general stores, Washington County Mercantile, Washington County Market and Gill's Store, with exactly 287 workers each (measured). After them come a wholesaler with 217 workers, two factories with 210 and 209, and the Washington County Diner with 104. The real Lubec has 1,346 residents.

The town is run by a three-member select board: Thomas Roach, 54; Jonathan Banks, 58; and Erica Salinas, 28. All three took office that morning (measured). Thomas Roach also leads the Maine Republican Party and works as a production worker (measured).

Above them, the president is Robin Stafford, a Republican born in 1958. Maine's governor is Michael Weber, a Democrat. Its senators are Jason Weber, an Independent, and Mark McPherson, a Republican; the two Webers share a surname by chance, with no relation recorded. Lubec's member of Congress is Joseph Cortez, a Democrat. All are invented people, and each holds the seat its real party held in 2025 (measured).

The player is Amelia Owens, 17, who turns 18 on January 23. She lives with her father Jeffrey, 46, and her brother Parker, 2. Jeffrey has no job and no retirement income on record. He has $1,790.75 in savings and $94.25 a day in living costs, which lasts 19 days (measured). Amelia knows the names of 15 people, all family and classmates. She knows no official, and no high school appears in her past (measured).

## How the two worlds differ

The second world, built for the same town, shares real history with the first and none of its people. Seed 2's president is Anthony Pace and its player is Zachary Hall, 17. All 204 seats, from president to select board, are held by different people in the two worlds. Both worlds hold the same party in every seat, the same 107 laws and the same national economy for all 49 months from January 2021 through January 2025. Of 63 Lubec organizations in each world, 23 have names found in only one. Nothing differs by cause yet, because no day has passed. The national economy reads nothing from the seed, so worlds can drift apart later only through what happens in play.

## What came back clean

- A counted worker filled in at the biggest employer matched the counted record exactly in both worlds: the same job, title, employer, pay and hours.
- All 204 seats, from president to select board, are filled: 193 federal and Maine seats, and 11 seats in Lubec and Washington County held by local residents. Each partisan seat holds the party the source roster gives, and every officeholder was 28 or older when the term began.
- Federal terms start on the real dates: January 3 for Congress and January 20 for the president.
- Of the 1,577 officials, 1,384 serve outside Maine and exist as people: 98 other senators, 437 other representatives, 49 other governors and 800 sampled state legislators.
- Birth dates, partners, parents, siblings, grandparents, job tenure and household membership agree with each other, with no exceptions found.
- 55 of 57 Maine policy rows match the research on value and date. The federal minimum wage ($7.25), Maine's sales tax (5.5%) and payroll taxes are correct.
- All 42 stopgap IDs named in the opening code are registered.
- In the Census table, 145 of Lubec's 178 occupied homes are owned, and the game keeps that share.
- The national economy for 2021 through 2024 is the real history in both worlds, as intended.

## What could not be reached

- Lubec's real age shares and household count at the town level: the repository holds them only for the Census village.
- Why the first day builds 5,115 homes for 5,798 households.
- Rent and mortgage dollar amounts per household, and whether the $15.00 wage raised anyone's pay on the first day.
- 1,387 of the 1,577 officials were not read one by one.
- Whether the player has a mother living elsewhere: her household records show only her father and brother.
- The real dates Maine's legislature and governor were sworn in are not in the repository's data; the game uses January 1 under a registered estimate.

## OWNER DECISIONS NEEDED

These questions are not already ruled in the owner decisions file.

1. **Town size.** Should a town smaller than 10,000 open at its real size? The play build picks any town, of any size, and fills it to 10,000. The core's own random draw, which the play build does not use, picks only places of 10,000 or more.
2. **Which Lubec.** Where the Census has both a village (277 people) and a town (1,346), which one is the place? Today the size comes from the village.
3. **A campaign past.** What must a generated official's past race hold: an opponent, vote totals, a platform, money raised? Today it holds a date and a fixed share.
4. **Paid family leave.** Maine collected premiums from January 1, 2025, and pays benefits from May 1, 2026. Which date counts as "in force"?
5. **The opening past.** May past falling-outs, favors and who held a job be seeded draws of "who exists", or must they come from each person's record like everything after day one?
6. **Party leaders.** Should a state party's leader come from the whole state rather than the player's town?

## Method, usage and time

Both worlds were built with the play build's own builder for January 20, 2025, every engine installed, and saved on the first morning. Seed 1 is `clock-1791951884267314954` and seed 2 is `clock-1791951972621435850`; each took 67 to 74 seconds to build. Ten Haiku agents traced one path each. One Sonnet agent checked all 35 of their claims against the code. 23 are listed above as defects. 2 were rejected, including a claimed error in the share of owned homes. The other 10 were true but break no rule or are already covered by a registered stopgap. I rechecked three claims by hand, and my checks agreed with the Sonnet agent on all three. The dump script is saved in this folder. No game code was changed.
