# M2-campaigns: campaigning and polls (golden path step 4)

**Specs:** M2-05 and M2-06.
**Built by:** OPUS-GOV, with OPUS-PEOPLE (voters, volunteers, donors and their words) and OPUS-PLAY (the screens).
**Systems model:** _The Political Process_: platform, doors, volunteers, fundraising, rallies, polls and ads, which move name recognition, opinion, enthusiasm and turnout, with resources scaled to the office. Our version adds real people, scenes and a real place.

## What you'll do

Between filing and election day, your time is the thing you spend. Each day you choose what to do, or skip ahead until something needs you.

- **Knock on doors:** pick a neighborhood and how many hours. Most of it is a montage. Sometimes you're on a real person's porch, talking. The people you reached now know you and your platform, and each decides for themselves.
- **Recruit volunteers:** friends, family and supporters who knock doors for you.
- **Raise money:** ask people you know, and people who like your platform. Donations stay within real legal limits and are sized to the office. A council race runs on very little; a governor's race on a lot.
- **Hold events and give speeches** whenever you want. Listeners react according to who they are, and tell their families and friends. What you say counts as a promise.
- **Run ads:** choose how much to spend and where. The ads reach people through the news and media they actually follow. An attack ad uses the other candidate's public record.
- **Get endorsements:** from people and groups who agree with you. They aren't for sale by default.
- **Hire staff,** such as a campaign manager: people you know or applicants. What they get done depends on who they are.
- **Commission a poll:** it asks real people and shows what they told the pollster. People don't always say what they think. You never see true support, only polls.
- **Your rivals** do all of this too, with the same tools. Your party might help, or might not, depending on the people in it.
- **Debates** are scenes, held where the race really has them.

## What gets built

- A campaign day built on time. Each activity reaches specific people, and each person changes what they know and think for their own reasons. No flat rates.
- Volunteers, donors (with real contribution limits), staff and endorsers, all as real people.
- Events and speeches that people hear and retell. Statements are recorded as promises.
- Ads with an amount and an area, reaching people through their media habits.
- Polls that sample real people and report their answers.
- Rivals and parties campaigning with the same engine.

## How you'll know it works

In a random place, after filing:

1. You spend campaign days knocking doors (a montage, with the odd porch scene), recruiting a volunteer, raising money within the limit, giving a speech, running an ad and commissioning a poll.
2. Each of those changes what specific people know and think, and you can click someone to see why.
3. Your rival campaigns too.
4. Then election day comes (M2-elections).

## Your decisions it follows

- Support comes only from the people a campaign actually reached (Sept 26).
- Campaign hours are a limited resource, not a currency or a weekly plan (D-11).
- Canvassing is "a montage… every now and then you end up on someone's front porch" (Oct 8). Signatures and doors work alike (Oct 12).
- Party HQ "depends on the simulation" (Oct 6).
- Ads have a chosen amount and area (Sept 26).
- Staff work from skill, potential, personality and passion; you can hire people you know (Sept 26).
- Endorsements aren't transactional by default (Oct 5).
- Donors are sized to the office and calibrated on real campaigns (Sept 29).
- Speeches at any moment, and a speech counts as a promise (Oct 10, Sept 28).
- People say one thing and believe another, and polls show what they say (Oct 9).
- Attack ads come from the record, not scripts (Sept 28).
- No visible meters or political-capital currency (Sept 17).
- The campaign screen gets designed after you play it (Oct 8).
- Opposition research uses only the public record in early access. This is my call, not yours: it follows your Oct 10 decision to move investigations to after early access.
