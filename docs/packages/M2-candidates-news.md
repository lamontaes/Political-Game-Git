# M2-candidates-news: who else runs, and basic news

**Specs:** M2-04 and M2-09.
**Built by:** OPUS-PEOPLE, with OPUS-GOV (filing and elections) and OPUS-PLAY (the TV and newspaper screens).
**Systems model:**
- *The Political Process*'s dynamic world, where hundreds of politicians run, retire and lose primaries, and results come every cycle.
- In our version, those politicians are the people of the world, deciding from their own lives.

## What you'll do

**Rivals come from the town.**
- The people who run against you are people from your town: the neighbor who's been angry about the zoning board, the incumbent deciding whether to go again, the challenger who has watched the incumbent's record.
- You can click any of them and see their life and why they're running.

**Most places stay light.**
- Candidates in places you never look at are short entries.
- They become full people when you open them, meet them, or they win.

**The news.**
- Local papers and TV report election results, new laws and big events.
- People hear the news through their own habits (some read the paper, some watch TV, some hear it from family) and pass it on by word of mouth.
- News from far away arrives later.
- A story only changes someone's mind if they trust the source.
- The TV and newspapers in the game show this live.
- Reporters, editors, owners and investigations come after early access.

## What gets built

- **Choosing to run.** People decide whether to run through the same choices the player has, from their own drives, views, standing and circumstances.
  - Incumbents decide about another term the same way. Ambition never forces another term.
  - Primary challenges grow out of an incumbent's record.
- **Detail by focus.** Candidates outside the focus are light entries until opened, met or elected.
- **Basic news.**
  - Local outlets report results, laws and big events.
  - Each person's news habit comes from their age, interests and temperament.
  - Word of mouth travels through relationships.
  - News from other places is delayed.
  - News moves views only through trust.
  - Every article's words come from the English engine, and nothing is invented: no crises, quotes, motives or numbers that didn't happen.

## How you'll know it works

In a random place, every candidate on the ballot filed through their own decision, with recorded reasons. An incumbent with a weak record draws a challenger. The local paper reports the result, and specific people hear it at different times through their habits and their friends.
