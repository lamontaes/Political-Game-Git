# M3-world-of-governing: staff, interest groups, constituents, corruption, other governments

**Specs:** M3-06, M3-07, M3-08, M3-09 and M3-10.
**Built by:** OPUS-GOV (staff, groups, cases, other governments), with OPUS-PEOPLE (corruption and the people behind every group) and OPUS-PLAY (screens).

**Systems model:** _The Political Process_: a dynamic world of politicians and governments acting on their own, while you work your office. Our version fills that world with people.

## What you'll do

**Staff.**

- As a legislator you have two or three people you deal with directly, plus aides. As an executive you have a cabinet.
- You hire people you know, or applicants.
- What staff get done depends on their skill, potential, personality and passion. You never see a potential score.
- Staff draft bills, prepare amendment packages and brief you, and they have their own motives.
- Delegating carries no hidden penalty.

**Interest groups and lobbyists.**

- Groups form from the people who lose money under a bill, and from causes. Someone whose relative died of leukemia might start a lobbying group.
- Lobbyists are people. They want specific votes, take you to dinner, donate, and remember what you did.
- The groups that already exist when the game starts grew the same way, from the years before.

**Constituent cases (simple).**

- Residents bring you real problems from their own lives: a benefit denied, a pothole, a permit stuck.
- You decide how your office handles cases: yourself, or through your staff. Most resolve in the background.
- Helping, or not, changes how those people see you.

**Basic corruption.**

- People can offer or take bribes and favors because of who they are and what they need.
- Nothing rolls to catch you. A bribe is a recorded crime, and it surfaces only through records, a leak, or someone who turns on you, sometimes years later in the news.
- Personal, campaign and public money stay separate.
- Investigations and prosecutions come after early access.

**Other governments.**

- Other councils meet on their real schedules.
- State legislatures and Congress pass real laws on the same engine, and those laws reach your town.
- Ballot measures and recalls happen in the world where a place really allows them.
- The years of lawmaking before your start date feed the town's history.

## What gets built

- **Staff:** people with skill, potential, personality and passion; real hiring; drafts, packages and briefings; motives.
- **Interest groups:** formed from money losers (the bill's cost estimate) and from people's drives; lobbyists as people with memory; real resources; starting groups grown from the same rules run over the past.
- **Constituent cases:** cases drawn from residents' records; the office's handling policy; background resolution; effects on standing.
- **Basic corruption:** bribes and favors chosen from people's own traits and money; crime records that surface through records, leaks or turned witnesses, with no detection roll; separate money accounts.
- **Other governments:** councils, legislatures and Congress acting on their real calendars on the same engines; their laws reaching the town; ballot measures and recalls run in the world; lawmaking history before the start.

## How you'll know it works

In a random place, as a council member:

- your staffer drafts a bill;
- a group forms against it from the people it would cost, and its lobbyist meets you;
- a resident brings you a case, and your office handles it;
- someone offers a bribe, and if it's taken, it sits on the record until something surfaces it;
- the state legislature passes a law in the world that changes something in your town.
