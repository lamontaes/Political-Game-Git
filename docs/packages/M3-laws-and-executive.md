# M3-laws-and-executive: laws change the town, and the executive's desk (golden path step 9)

**Specs:** M3-04 and M3-05.
**Built by:** OPUS-GOV, with OPUS-PEOPLE (who feels it, and the moments) and OPUS-PLAY (the desk and the budget screen).

**Systems model:** *The Political Process*:
- laws move 100+ measures, and cutting a program ripples into others;
- the executive signs or vetoes;
- leaders propose a budget, set taxes and fund or cut programs.

Our version adds real people who feel each change, and a desk with scenes.

## What you'll do

**When a law passes, the town changes.**
- On its effective date the rule, program or tax changes.
- People feel it when it reaches them: the bus route comes back, the clinic hires, the new rent rule applies to a real family, the shop owner pays the new fee.
- Funded work gets paid for, and it can run late or fail.
- Effects go beyond money: rules with penalties and someone to enforce them, who qualifies, rights, prices, the cost of complying.
- One area moves another: a cut to food aid can ripple into school results, crime and tax revenue.
- Everyone the law touches has it on their record, even if it didn't change much for them and even if they never notice.
- The journal gets a chapter for everyone, plus personal moments for the people it hit hardest.
- Repealing a law ends its effects.

**At the mayor's, county executive's or governor's desk:**
- **Sign or veto.** Bills arrive and you decide, with your staff's advice.
- **The budget.** You set priorities with staff advice, line by line if you want. The council or legislature votes on it, and budget fights are scenes. This replaces the stand-in budget rules from M1.
- **Appointments.** You pick people you know, or applicants, at the depth you choose. Offices come with patronage jobs.
- **Executive orders, regulations, enforcement, and using your platform** to push the public.
- **Emergencies** are scenes.
- **Pardons** where the office really has that power.
- **Mayor forms differ** by place: strong mayor, weak mayor, or council-manager, whichever that place really has.

The president's desk runs in the world on the same engine. Playing it comes later.

## What gets built

- **Law effects.**
  - Records change first, on the effective date.
  - Funded work is paid, delivered, delayed or failed.
  - Effects reach named people.
  - Non-money effects: rules with an enforcer and penalty, eligibility, rights, prices, compliance costs.
  - The outcome web links one area to another.
  - Every affected person's record gets the law, even at zero.
  - Repeal ends the effects.
- **One executive engine for all three levels.**
  - Sign or veto, weighed on principles, party, votes, relationships and advice.
  - Budget priorities, adopted by the body's vote.
  - Appointments and patronage.
  - Orders, regulations, enforcement and the bully pulpit.
  - Emergencies.
  - Pardons where the power is real.
  - Each place's real government form.
- **Scenes and screens.** Budget fights, appointments, vetoes and emergencies are scenes. The budget numbers are a screen.

## How you'll know it works

In a random place:
- An ordinance you passed takes effect on its date and changes something specific people experience. You can click one of them and see it on their record.
- A linked area moves because of it.
- As mayor, you sign one bill and veto another, set budget priorities the council adopts, and appoint someone you know.
- A state law passed in the world reaches the town.
