# The owner's voice, from his own notes

Facts only, each quoted from a file. Sources: `docs/coordination/voice-notes/` (VN) and `data/english/grades/voice-notes-*.json` (grades). Where he reads a displayed option and says "I'm going to say ...", the line is the bank's wording that he picked, so it is wording he approves more than wording he coined. What he composes himself is marked "his own".

## How he opens and answers

- Opens a reply with "Okay," or "All right,": "All right, let's look at batch 22" (2026-10-23-words-lead.md §6); "All right, I'm looking at batch 17 now" (2026-10-15-words-orders-part-2.md §23); "All right. So now this is me going to answer." (part-1 §16).
- Nov 8, 9:44 a.m., on how he talks: "instead of saying, you know, okay, that's great, you could say, all right, I got you, man, and stuff like that. I say that in a lot of the batches, so there's already some variants built in there." (2026-11-08-morning.md).
- His praise when a scene is right: "Perfect. Perfect. This is exactly what I've been wanting." / "Beautiful, beautiful, beautiful. That's what I want." / "Ooh, 2 scenes widened, I love that." / "Yep, there you go." / "Awesome." (same file, 9:44 a.m. and 10:06 a.m.).
- His openers when he turns a page: "Okay, so", "Oh, I see.", "Look at these laws." and a trailing "huh?" (same file).
- Agreement words: "Yeah, sure." (part-1 §16), "Sure. That's good, too." (§9), "Yep, that's good. That's solid." (§9), "Fair" ("That's also fair." §9), "That's fine." (§9, §35).
- Hedges and fillers: "I mean," "you know," "like," "I guess," "kind of," "or whatever," "or something" (every note; "Americans say must, but not in that way. Let me think about what you said." part-2 §23).
- Contractions throughout: "that's," "doesn't," "I'm gonna," "it's," "didn't." He says "gonna" aloud ("I'm gonna say, I'm still reading it", part-1 §16).

- Short, plain question openers: "Sorry to bother you. I like that." / "Is now a bad time? ... I like that." / "Got a second. Yeah, that's good." / "Mind if I ask you a few things? I like that." (voice-notes-oct15-28.json, batch 13).
- Confident one-liners: "I'm voting for it and you can quote me. That's very, very, like confident." (part-1 §9).

- Threats that sound like politics: "You'll need friends after this vote. I won't be one." "The party has a long memory." "I won't forget this." (voice-notes-batches-11-19.json, batch 13).
- A plea that gives its stake, a bargain that names the trade: "I could lose my primary over this"; "this is a two-way street" (same file).

- Replies with a reason, longer than a few words: "Because you've been on the fence all week and everybody on this floor knows it. You're the one I can still move. Ooh, I like this." (part-2 §23). "Then move me. What's it worth to you? That's a great response."
- Lines that leave a door open: "Let me sleep on it. I'll call you Tuesday." / "Don't make me chase you. I like that one. That one was great." (§23). "I'll have something to say after the vote ... is most natural." (part-1 §16).

## What he rejects

- A quick, neat resolution: "You've got it. We'll bring more people Saturday. Yeah, see, that sounds like AI. It's like, okay, quick resolution. That's not how normal people talk." (part-1 §16).
- An answer that does not answer: "Doesn't sound natural." / "I'm not going to talk about that ... awkward, unnatural." (§16). "Fine, I'll do it, but this is the last time. Weird response." (part-2 §19).
- A reply that does not follow the line before: "Okay for you, I'll do it is weird." "She said go home. I'll call you at 8. That's a little weird." (§19). "Where do you drive in from? That's creepy."
- Words that are not said: "Talk to you soon" after a question; "See you later"; "must" for "have to"; "Congratulations" at an office; "settled," "gaps," "the doors" (2026-10-24-playtest.md; part-1 §5; part-2 §23, §35; sitting-bans.test.ts).
- A scene that is "super mundane" or has no stakes, with a narrator that "only ever says one line" while the person always says two (part-2 §31). He wants the narrator to vary and to talk between people ("It's like Baldur's Gate").
- Replies "very vanilla" and "rehashed first options"; he wants options that escalate ("If he's kind of escalating, you should have an option to escalate", part-2 §21) and replies that are "longer ones. Some can be substantive, some can't be" (§19).
- Anything with no context: "There needs to be like a what or a why" (§23); "Why can't I say why?" (§21).
- "Still rough" and "still to do" notes; "Give me a finished product" (part-1 §16, part-2 §35).

## How he wants lines built

- Real people as the model: "look at like 60 Minutes ... Firing Line ... transcripts" (part-1 §16); "try to find examples of ... how real people have responded."
- Pieces, not scripts: "we're not authoring scenarios. We're giving the simulation pieces to work with." (part-1 §15). An engine that can "put together a coherent sentence" for a gap nobody tested (part-2 §20).
- Nov 8, 9:44 a.m.: "that's fine, having conditionals for lines. So if there's only one line that's and it needs conditional, we'll add a bunch of other conditions or non-conditions if needed." and "they gotta have like a variance. So even though there's only 5 in corruption scenes right now ... you could get ... 30 different scenes within there." He asked for work "specifically on adding lines and different tones and subjects" (2026-11-08-morning.md).
- Vary the voice by who speaks: "assigning lines to types of people ... not everyone should automatically get access to that" (part-2 §24).
- No reference to anyone's text, no credit lines: "There's no lawyer check needed." (2026-10-23-words-lead.md §12).

## Profanity

He swears in his notes. Nothing of that goes into a line. His mild registers ("shit," "damn") appear only in his speech about the game, never in wording he approved.
