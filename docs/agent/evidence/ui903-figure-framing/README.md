# Figure framing and registration

`figure-framing.md` and `figure-framing.json` are generated by
`npm run measure:figures` and checked by `npm run test`. This file records what
the measurement found and what was changed because of it.

## The defect: a clamp that moved the feet, not the head

`planLifeScenePeople` reserved a person's box as
`topPercent = Math.max(0, contactFloorY - height)`. The clamp reads as "keep
the box on the plate". What it actually did was move the BOTTOM of the box,
because everything downstream — the fallback fit included — puts the figure's
feet at `top + height`. In any room where the reserved height exceeds the
distance from the top of the plate to the contact line, the whole person was
pushed down through the floor by exactly the clamped amount.

Measured, across the registered rooms:

| Room                                     | Anchor                       | Sunk below the declared floor |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| `residence-apartment-living-ordinary-02` | `living-room-floor-standing` | 5.5% of the plate             |
| `shared-workroom-office-production`      | `workroom-floor-standing`    | 4.3%                          |
| `shared-workroom-office-production`      | `near-table-standing`        | 41.6%                         |
| `office-council-staff-fixture`           | `doorway-standing`           | 9.6%                          |
| `office-council-staff-fixture`           | `near-desk-standing`         | 20.7%                         |

The first row is an ordinary residence living room — the room ordinary play
actually happens in. A figure standing shin-deep in the floorboards reads as
broken art, and the art was fine.

The contact line is authored truth; the height comes from an assumed
proportion. So the estimate gives way, never the floor. Unclamped, feet land on
the declared contact line in every room, and a figure too tall for the space
above it is cropped at the head by the viewport — which is what a camera in a
small room does. The overflow is reported rather than absorbed: the person
carries `figure-taller-than-space-above-contact-line` with the exact
percentage.

## Registration

Zero unfitted components missed the body anchor they declare, across 116
placements × 4 deterministic review people in both libraries. That is now
asserted rather than observed: a component declares an origin and the anchor it
attaches to, the projection makes them coincide, and any non-zero residual on
an unfitted layer means the component metadata and the body's anchors disagree
— a defect that is invisible by eye at this size and is exactly what "the hair
sits wrong" turns out to be.

A non-zero residual on a FITTED layer is the fit bank doing its job, and is
reported separately rather than counted as a fault.

## What is still missing

The report's "Exact remaining asset needs" section is taken from the
compositor's own refusals, so it says what the pipeline will actually reject.
In short: there is no body art for the seated poses (`seated-at-desk`,
`seated-guest-neutral`), so every required slot fails at those anchors; and
several seat anchors permit only `away` facings while the seated poses face
`front`.

Seven of ten paintable rooms declare no standard body width. That is a missing
measurement. Nothing in this report may be copied into a scene's floor
calibration to fill it — the estimates here are labelled `visual-estimate`
precisely so they cannot be mistaken for one, and the fix for a missing
measurement is to measure it.

## One instrument error, recorded

The first draft of the measurement rebuilt the reserved box without the scene's
perspective ramp and reported contact residuals of twenty-odd percent in half
the rooms. That was the tool, not the game. The ramp is in it now, and the
residuals it reports after the fix are zero.
