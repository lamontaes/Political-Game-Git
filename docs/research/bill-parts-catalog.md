# Real bills use 61 kinds of parts, and five kinds of law have no bill variant in the game

222 enacted measures (laws, ordinances or parts of one law) were broken into parts: 61 kinds in all. The eight most used kinds appear in 25 to 41 measures each. The game's bill bank has 43 variants on main and 46 on a draft branch. Five kinds of real law have no variant in either: bans and penalties, tax changes beyond one flat rate, state overrides of local government, price caps and wage floors on private parties, and narrowing who qualifies. They appear in 90 of the 222 measures. The game records a passed law only as enacted, yet 5 sampled laws were later halted or suspended. The Claude CTO's bill-parts lane owns these gaps; no owner decision is needed.

## What the sample is

- A measure is one enacted law or ordinance, or one separable provision of a large law. Pub. L. 119-21 is split into 3 measures and Pennsylvania's 2025 budget package into 2.
- 222 measures: 32 federal, 158 state, 2 District of Columbia, 9 territory, 16 city and 5 county.
- All 50 states are covered. 38 states have 3 records, 7 have 4, 2 have 5, and 3 have 2 (Pennsylvania, Rhode Island and Vermont).
- Every one of the 18 subject menus appears. Every operation appears at least 13 times: create 64, restrict 49, expand 43, lower 21, replace 19, raise 13, repeal 13.
- Each record lists its parts, the numbers in each part with units, who it touches, and one line on daily life.
- 181 of the 753 parts are marked "inferred": they came from memory or a secondary summary, not the official text.
- Each record states its sourcing. 43 were read in the official text. 106 had their bill page, act number or signing found on an official site. 53 rest on news or official press releases. 20 are marked "inferred-details"; their numbers need checking before use.

147 of the 158 state measures were enacted in 2025 regular sessions, 9 in 2025 special sessions, and 2 in 2026. 2025 is the latest session with a confirmed law for every state. Montana, Nevada, North Dakota and Texas held no regular session in 2026. Federal laws run from June 3, 2023 to September 18, 2026.

## 2. The 18 menus

Counts are measures filed under the menu first. "Also" counts measures that touch it second.

**01 Infrastructure** (8, also 4). Instruments: appropriation, program authorization, tax, fee. Operations: create 3, expand 4, raise 1. Recipes:

- A fuel tax or fee increase with a revenue split between state, counties and cities (Oregon HB 3991).
- Bonds put to voters with named projects (Denver).
- State borrowing handed to towns by formula (Massachusetts Chapter 17).
- New tolling authority that still needs federal approval (Indiana HEA 1461).

Gap: no transit fare, rail or port law at state or local level.

**02 Healthcare** (19, also 13). Instruments: eligibility, mandate, prohibition. Operations: restrict 12 of 19. Recipes:

- A cost cap on insured patients ($35 insulin in California and Nevada).
- A ban on a business practice by pharmacy benefit managers, enforced by a regulator (Arkansas, Maine, Alabama).
- Medicaid eligibility narrowed by work hours, premiums or removal of a group (federal, New Hampshire, Minnesota, Idaho).
- A sunset removed so a program becomes permanent (Montana).

Gap: no hospital budget or health workforce funding bill with numbers.

**03 Education** (16, all state). Instruments: prohibition, program creation, mandate. Recipes:

- School phone bans with local policy deadlines (Alabama, Tennessee, North Dakota).
- School choice money per student with a cap, an income priority and a named agency (Texas, Tennessee, Wyoming, Idaho).
- Bans on DEI offices at public colleges (Kentucky, Ohio, Mississippi).
- Formula changes (Alaska's $700 per student).

Gap: no federal education law and no student aid or teacher pay law.

**04 Taxes & Public Finance** (55, also 20). The largest menu. Instruments: tax 31, appropriation 21. Operations: create 20 (budgets), lower 18. Recipes:

- A flat rate cut with future cuts tied to a revenue trigger (Georgia, Kentucky, Oklahoma, Mississippi).
- A property tax credit paid from a state fund plus a cap on local levy growth (North Dakota, Indiana).
- A new excise tax with an earmark (Hawaii, Michigan, Illinois).
- A one-time rebate from surplus (Georgia).
- A budget act with line-item vetoes and a fiscal-year availability window (Ohio, South Carolina, Maryland).

Gap: no debt-limit or state borrowing cap measure outside the federal Fiscal Responsibility Act.

**05 Assistance & Social Insurance** (8, also 13). Instruments: eligibility and appropriation. Operations: restrict 4 of 8. Recipes:

- A state asks for a federal waiver to change what benefits buy (Idaho SNAP soda ban).
- Emergency cash to bridge a federal lapse (Virgin Islands Act 9047).
- Funding rules that shift cost to states by error rate (SNAP cost share).

Gap: no unemployment benefit amount change and no disability or elder care program.

**06 Housing & Land Use** (14, also 3). 7 of the 14 are city or county measures. Recipes:

- The state overrides local zoning near transit or on commercial land (California SB 79, Nevada AB 241, New Mexico SB 96).
- A city replaces its whole development code (Bozeman, Missoula).
- A rent cap with a notice period and a new-construction exemption (Washington).
- A tax break in exchange for affordable units (Montgomery County).

Gap: no homebuyer assistance program.

**07 Labor & Employment** (20, also 3). Instruments: mandate 10, benefit 6. Recipes:

- A wage floor phased in over years with inflation indexing (Michigan, Los Angeles, New York City).
- Leave in days or weeks: an unpaid leave mandate keyed to employer size (Illinois), a state insurance benefit (Colorado), and paid leave for state employees (Iowa, Mississippi).
- Unemployment pay for strikers with a week cap (Oregon 10 weeks, Washington 6 weeks).
- A voter-passed law narrowed before it starts (Nebraska LB 415).

Gap: no workers' compensation law.

**08 Business & Economic Development** (8, also 11). Instruments: program creation, fee, license. Recipes:

- A license with reserve rules and a size split between federal and state oversight (GENIUS Act).
- An insurance backstop and loan program (Hawaii).
- Special tax and zoning zones for data centers with a revenue split (West Virginia HB 2014).

Gap: no general business license or corporate tax change at state level.

**09 Energy & Environment** (12, also 6). Instruments: mandate, prohibition, agency. Recipes:

- Permit deadlines for power plants (South Carolina's 6-month rule).
- A new energy department that absorbs an old office (Maine).
- Bonds to take charges off utility bills (Connecticut).
- The federal government voids a state emission waiver (Pub. L. 119-15).

Gap: no water quality or climate emissions target law.

**10 Justice & Public Safety** (15, also 12). Instruments: penalty, prohibition, procedure. Recipes:

- A new crime with a definition and a felony class (Alabama conversion devices, Wisconsin sextortion).
- Mandatory minimum jail terms (Oklahoma DUI).
- Pretrial release rules tied to prior convictions (North Carolina).
- A firearm sales ban with a grandfather clause and a one-year delay (Rhode Island).
- A local sales tax for jails put to voters (Kansas City).

Gap: no juvenile justice or fire and EMS law.

**11 Government & Democracy** (9, also 11). Instruments: procedure and election rules. Operations: replace 4 of 9. Recipes:

- A ballot design change (New Jersey office-block ballots).
- Harder citizen petitions (Florida HB 1205).
- A legislature taking approval power over agency rules (Kentucky).
- A public records release with a deadline (Epstein Files Act).

Gap: no redistricting or ethics commission law.

**12 Rights, Family & Consumer Protection** (4, also 13). Recipes:

- An "unfair or abusive practice" power for the attorney general (New York FAIR Act).
- All-in price disclosure (Colorado junk fees).
- A protected class removed (Iowa SF 418).

Gap: no marriage, custody, adoption or child welfare law.

**13 Science, Technology & Communications** (8, also 6). Instruments: mandate and prohibition. Operations: create 5 of 8. Recipes:

- Publish a safety plan and report incidents within a set time (California SB 53, New York RAISE Act).
- Age checks with parental consent (Utah app stores, Nebraska social media).
- A start date pushed back with no other change (Colorado SB25B-004).

Gap: no state cybersecurity or government digital services law.

**14 Agriculture & Food** (8, also 4). Operations: restrict 4 of 8. Recipes:

- Bans on food dyes or additives with a phase-in, schools first (Virginia, West Virginia, Arizona).
- Per-acre aid to farmers after disasters (American Relief Act).

Gap: no crop insurance, forestry or fisheries law.

**15 Parks, Culture & Community Life** (4, also 2). Recipes:

- A ban on development inside state parks (Florida HB 209).
- Bonds for libraries, museums and a zoo put to voters (Denver).
- An arts agency merged into tourism (West Virginia).

Gap: no confirmed state library, historic preservation or arts funding law.

**16 Immigration & Citizenship** (6, also 3). Recipes:

- Mandatory detention on arrest (Laken Riley Act).
- Required state and county cooperation with federal agents, plus grants per county (Texas SB 8, Florida SB 2-C).
- A ban on local "sanctuary" policies (South Dakota).

Gap: no settlement or integration services law.

**17 Defense & Veterans** (5, also 4). Recipes:

- Annual defense authorization tables (NDAA).
- A veterans' property tax exemption at a lower disability rating (Washington) or as a local option (New York).
- A higher cap on VA home care spending (Dole Act).

Gap: no state measure on veterans' health care, education or employment.

**18 Foreign Affairs & Trade** (3, also 3). Recipes:

- Sanctions with tariff rates up to 500%, a presidential waiver and a 5-year sunset (Pub. L. 119-111).
- Emergency foreign aid (Pub. L. 118-50).

The one state measure is a foreign land-purchase ban (Texas SB 17).

Gap: no state measure on foreign aid, trade or sanctions.

## 3. How levels differ

**Federal (32 measures).** Federal measures carry a median of 4 parts; state measures carry 3. Pub. L. 119-21 is split into three measures here: tax, Medicaid and SNAP. Congress used instruments no state in the sample used: 3 resolutions voiding an agency rule, 4 stopgap or full-year continuing appropriations, 1 rescission law and 2 reconciliation laws. The NDAA sets spending ceilings that a separate appropriations law funds; that the split is the federal norm is inferred.

**State (158 measures).** 5 state laws were enacted over a governor's veto. 3 state budgets were signed with line-item vetoes. 9 state measures carry a trigger part; 4 of those are revenue tests for income tax cuts. 11 state measures limit local governments.

**D.C. and territories (11 measures).** D.C. put a tipped wage change inside its budget support act. Congress reviews D.C. laws before they take effect (inferred). Guam enacted its budget over the governor's veto. The Northern Mariana Islands repealed and re-enacted its budget three months into the fiscal year. The Virgin Islands paid half of each household's November SNAP benefit from its own rainy-day fund when federal funding lapsed.

**Cities and counties (21 measures).** Their main instruments: tax 7, mandate 6, appropriation 3, prohibition 2, and one each of program, license and fee. They set property tax rates, held bond and tax elections, set wage floors for some industries, licensed and zoned, and banned specific products. Limits seen in the sample:

- Colorado cities need voter approval to borrow; Denver's bond ordinances cite the state constitution.
- Austin had to stay under the state voter-approval tax rate once voters rejected a higher one.
- Utah and Florida removed local choice on fluoride.
- Missouri moved control of the St. Louis police to a state board.
- Texas required every county sheriff running a jail to work with federal immigration agents.
- A citizen petition suspended the Los Angeles hotel wage.

Local powers in general, such as creating felonies, were not surveyed. Any wider rule about them is inferred.

## 4. Timing

- 42 measures have both an exact signing date and an exact start date. The median gap is 77.5 days. 9 took effect within one day. The longest gaps are Virginia's school dye ban at 832 days and Alexandria's leaf blower ban at 549 days.
- 96 of the 138 state measures with a known signing month were signed from March through June.
- 62 state measures list a start date in their record. January 1 is the start in 17 of them and July 1 in 16. The effective-date part in section 1 (34 measures) is coded only where the start date is itself a notable rule.
- Measured phase-ins run from 18 months (Alexandria) to 14 years (Philadelphia's business tax, to 2039). The Los Angeles and Michigan wage floors step up once a year.
- 4 state income tax cuts continue only if revenue tests are met (Georgia, Kentucky, Mississippi, Oklahoma).
- Measured sunsets run from 4 years (the tips deduction, 2025 to 2028) to 20 years (Kansas City's sales tax). Removing a sunset is its own kind of bill (Montana HB 245).
- 5 laws in the sample were enacted but halted or suspended. The game records a passed law with a single outcome, "enacted", and an optional start date; it has no status for a law halted by a court or suspended by petition (measured: `src/simulation/types.ts:4233`). Courts halted Mississippi's DEI ban and Wyoming's scholarships. Referendum petitions suspended the Los Angeles hotel wage and part of Oregon's transportation taxes. Utah's public union law was suspended by a referendum petition and later repealed (repeal date inferred).
- 1 law exists only to move a date: Colorado SB25B-004 delayed the state AI law by five months.

## 5. The game's bank against real patterns

**Size, measured.** Main (commit 1a3cefc41) registers 20 families and 43 variants. The count comes from calling the registry's configuration list (`src/simulation/legislation-program-families.ts:69`). 42 of the 43 are declared in the family files. The 43rd, a transit variant, is appended to the appropriations family (`src/simulation/legislation-fiscal-families.ts:1437`).

Draft pull request #685's branch, codex/world-outcomes-proof-integration (commit 12948fae5), registers 20 families and 46 variants. Its 3 added variants are all appropriations: passenger rail, a local fix-it-first variant and a state transit variant. That branch registers them at `legislation-fiscal-families.ts:1440`.

Both banks use the same 8 instruments: program authorization, appropriation, eligibility amendment, regulatory requirement, oversight reporting, sunset or repeal, revenue measure, and position authorization (`src/simulation/legislation-content-contracts.ts:163`). A bill may carry 6 kinds of clause: a funding cap, an eligibility scope, timing, an oversight duty, a revenue charge, and a reference to the authority it amends (`legislation-content-contracts.ts:107`). None of the instruments or clause kinds is a ban or a penalty.

Taxes have a separate route outside the bank. A tax proposal attached to a bill carries one base, one flat rate, exempt bases and an allowance (measured: `src/simulation/tax-types.ts:22`). An enacted tax takes effect 90 days after enactment (`src/simulation/tax-policy.ts:248`). The player reaches it through a tax workspace (`src/player/PlayerGame.tsx:5033`).

**Game features that match real patterns (measured against the sample; lines on main):**

- `appropriations` family: single program, supplemental and local-match variants (`legislation-fiscal-families.ts:107`). These match budget acts, supplemental bills (Delaware HB 226) and cost-share rules (WRDA 2024).
- `program-sunset` family: terminate, extend and repeal (`legislation-fiscal-families.ts:671`). These match federal extenders, Montana's sunset removal and the Social Security Fairness Act.
- `service-charges` family: flat permit fee and dedicated surcharge (`legislation-fiscal-families.ts:1125`). These match Missoula's rental fee and 5% technology surcharge, and Chicago's bag fee.
- The tax route: one flat rate with exempt bases (`tax-types.ts:22`). This matches Hawaii's lodging tax rise, Chicago's cloud tax rise and Arkansas's grocery tax repeal (a zero rate).
- `assistance-eligibility` family: raise the limit and add a category (`src/simulation/legislation-administration-families.ts:143`). These match Arkansas's presumptive eligibility and Washington's veterans threshold.
- `public-workforce` family: rules for classifying and filling public jobs (`legislation-administration-families.ts:1394`). North Carolina SB 124, which cut degree requirements for state jobs, matches it.
- `broadband-access` family: buildout to unserved areas (`src/simulation/legislation-infrastructure-families.ts:776`). This matches the vetting and service standards in the Rural Broadband Protection Act.
- `disaster-recovery` family: grants and waivers (`src/simulation/legislation-resilience-families.ts:97`). These match the American Relief Act.

One mismatch runs the other way. The `agency-reporting` family writes bills that only require reports (`legislation-administration-families.ts:680`). In the sample, reporting appears in 10 measures, always inside a larger bill; no sampled measure is reporting-only.

**Five kinds of real law with no variant, confirmed in code and ranked by count.** Each measure is counted once, so together they appear in 90 of the 222 measures.

1. **Bans and penalties: 51 measures.** 40 ban something, 17 set a penalty, and 6 do both. Measured: no instrument or clause kind is a ban or a penalty (`legislation-content-contracts.ts:163`). The 9 regulatory variants impose plans, inventories, notices, service duties, a hiring standard or a waiver; none bans an act. Recipe: definition, prohibition, exemption, penalty, enforcement, grandfather clause.
2. **Tax changes beyond one flat rate: 18 of the 42 tax measures.** These use credits, deductions, exclusions, rebates, phase-ins or revenue triggers. Measured: the tax route has one rate, one base, exempt bases and an allowance, and no field for any of these (`tax-types.ts:22`). The other 24 tax measures were not checked field by field. Recipe: rate, credit or deduction, phase-in, trigger, effective date.
3. **State override of local government, or local options: 18 measures.** 14 override a lower government: 12 state, 1 federal and 1 city measure. 11 of the 12 state measures limit local governments; Arizona's tobacco law instead matches a federal age limit. 5 give local governments a choice; 1 measure does both. Measured: no instrument limits or empowers a local government (`legislation-content-contracts.ts:163`). A search of the family files for "local government" finds them only as grant recipients, local-match payers, reporters and employers; the reporting variant's local-government option is one example (`legislation-administration-families.ts:739`). Recipe: preemption, local option, levy cap, deadline for local ordinances.
4. **Price caps and wage floors on private parties: 7 measures.** 3 are price caps (California and Nevada insulin, Washington rent). 4 are wage floors (Michigan, Los Angeles, New York City delivery workers, D.C. tipped wage). Measured by reading all 9 regulatory variants: they reach water systems, utilities, critical-infrastructure operators and public bodies, never landlords, employers, insurers or retailers. The water service line variant is one example (`legislation-infrastructure-families.ts:1163`). Recipe: covered population with a size threshold, cap or floor, indexation, phase-in, notice, enforcement.
5. **Narrowing who qualifies: 6 measures.** They add work hours, add premiums or remove a group (federal Medicaid and SNAP work rules, Idaho, Indiana, Minnesota, New Hampshire). Measured: two of the three eligibility variants widen eligibility (`legislation-administration-families.ts:143`). The third ties the threshold to a published figure and can disqualify households when that figure moves (`legislation-administration-families.ts:554`). No variant removes a group or adds a work requirement or premium. Recipe: covered population, work requirement or premium, exemption, effective date, administering agency.

A sixth confirmed gap is smaller: leave mandates on private employers, 3 measures (Illinois NICU leave, Michigan earned sick time, Nebraska LB 415). None of the 9 regulatory variants reaches employers.

**Further gaps, inferred from the instrument list and not traced in code:**

- Election and procedure rules: 11 measures.
- Creating, merging or taking over agencies: 7 measures.
- Bond questions put to voters: 5 measures.
- License and permit systems: 5 measures.
- Continuing resolutions and rescissions: 4 continuing resolutions and 4 measures with a rescission.
- Laws that only change a date: 1 measure.

How bills pass was not checked. The game already records a veto that is sustained (`types.ts:4233`); veto overrides and referendum petitions were not traced.

## What happens next

The Claude CTO's bill-parts lane owns three follow-ups, as its next assignment after draft pull request #685 merges. That merge is the Claude CTO's step, not an owner decision.

- Scope bill families for the five gaps above, in that order.
- Add a law status for "halted" or "suspended" beside "enacted", so a law can pass and still be stopped by a court or a petition.
- Check the 20 measures marked "inferred-details" against official text before any of their numbers feeds a game value.

The catalog changes nothing in the game. Real data calibrates the game only; the game generates its own laws.

Method. Research ran September 26, 2026. Sources were official legislature, Congress and city sites, GovInfo for federal text, and news or press releases where official pages refused automated reading. LegiScan, Congress.gov, and the New York, Hawaii and Colorado legislature pages refused it. No accounts or API keys were used. American Samoa is missing: no 2025 or 2026 law could be confirmed. Records and sources are in `data/research/bill-samples/`, one file per jurisdiction.
