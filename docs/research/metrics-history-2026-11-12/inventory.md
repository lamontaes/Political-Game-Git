# What the Metrics tab can show: the inventory (Nov 12, 2026)

Research for the owner's Layout A with the years table (brief: `docs/coordination/briefs/2026-11-12-METRICS-HISTORY.md` on `cto/fleet-briefs-nov12b`). Each row is one measure a player would call "how my place is doing": its plain name, the levels it exists at, the record it comes from, its unit, and whether the world keeps it over the years. "Kept" was checked two ways: in the code (what each engine writes and whether it removes old rows) and in a played game of six years (Gracey, Kentucky; `README.md`), by reading at the end of 2030 which earlier months and years were still in the records.

**The short answer.** Most of the history is already in the world. The town's economy row (`world-metrics.months`), the outcome web (`core2-outcome-web-v1.months`), the country's months (`macro.months`), crime, police and fire, the town bank, the President's approval and every government's budget keep every month or year from day one, and nothing removes them. Today's Metrics screen (`src/bench/metrics.ts`) reads only "now" and "a year before" from them, which is why no history has shown. The measures with no history at all are the ones read live from a row that is overwritten: the town's population, its rental stock, its homeless count, open jobs and job seekers, the state's population and figures from the country tier, and the federal debt.

Levels: **town** is the played place; **county** means a county government's books or the places the economy records around the town; **state** and **country** are the state and the whole country.

## What one change would give every "now only" measure a history

Following `cadence-and-summaries` (a yearly summary, not a daily row): one row per place per year, written on December 31 by the module that already reads these (`world-metrics`), with `population`, `namedPeople`, `countedPeople`, `households`, `rentalUnits`, `vacantRental`, `homeless`, `doubledUp`, `openPositions`, `seekers`, `claimsOpen`, `claimsPaidMinor`, `births`, `deaths`, `movesIn`, `movesOut`. And one row per state per December from `macro.countryStates` (`population`, `jobs`, `unemploymentPct`, `hourlyPay`, `prices`, `sales`), plus the federal debt's total. The tab's years table then reads one row per year for every area.
