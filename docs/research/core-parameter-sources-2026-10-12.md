# Sources for the core's prototype parameters (October 12, 2026)

Research notes from OPUS-CORE's helper. Nine values were applied on `opus-core` (see `docs/archive/2026-10-23/coordination/core.md`); the rest name the real targets each tunable parameter should be calibrated against. Treat unverified rows (marked "not found" or from search snippets) as leads, not sources.

Format: key | current | proposed (code units) | tag | citation | how the source maps to the code.
"Verified" means I read the number from the primary source in this session. ssa.gov returns HTTP 403 to this sandbox. The SSA claiming table came from a DocumentCloud copy of the official 2022 Supplement PDF; other SSA tables were not reachable and are marked "not retrieved".
Game opening date context: January 2021 (other rows cite the 2021 COLA and May 2021 OEWS).

## Time-use offers

1. careHours | 0.5 h | **2.23 h** per care act (spread 1.97–2.23) | SOURCED | BLS ATUS 2025, Table 1 "Time spent in primary activities and percent of the civilian population engaging in each activity, averages per day", released 2026-06-25, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.t01.htm. Caring for and helping household members: 0.49 h/person-day, 22.0% engaged, 2.23 h per participant-day. Nonhousehold members: 0.15 h, 7.6%, 1.97 h (verified) | careHours is the duration of one completed care act, so the instrument-matched quantity is hours per participant-day, not the population average 0.49. Use 1.97 for a relative outside the household and 2.23 inside it. The participation share, 22.0%, is the outcome target.
2. civicHours | 1 h | **2.18 h** (spread 1.41–2.18) | SOURCED | Same ATUS 2025 Table 1: Volunteering (organizational and civic activities): 0.12 h/day, 5.3% engaged, 2.18 h per participant. Organizational, civic and religious total: 0.28 h, 13.6%, 2.08 h. Religious and spiritual: 0.13 h, 9.4%, 1.41 h (verified) | Join, organize and approach acts are civic/volunteer episodes, so use hours per participant-day for volunteering. 5.3% of person-days is the participation check.

## Affect and stress

3. stressBaseline | 0.25 | keep 0.25 for now. Non-US cross-check is about 0.31 | TUNABLE (outcome target) | U.S. norms: Cohen & Janicki-Deverts (2012), "Who's stressed? Distributions of psychological stress in the United States in probability samples from 1983, 2006 and 2009", J. Applied Social Psychology 42:1320–1334, doi:10.1111/j.1559-1816.2012.00900.x. **U.S. means were not retrieved** (paywalled). Cross-check: Klein et al. (2016), BMC Psychiatry 16:159, German representative sample N=2,463, PSS-10 M=12.57, SD=6.42 (Table 3), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4877813 (verified) | If the stress channel is read as PSS-10/40, the German norm gives 0.31 (SD 0.16). The PSS-10 mean by age and sex, from the 2012 U.S. paper, is the target for the simulated stress distribution. A PSS norm is an outcome, not the baseline parameter itself.
4. moodBaseline | 0 | **+0.32 to +0.42** if mood is read as (PANAS PA − NA)/40; otherwise keep 0 | ESTIMATED (spread 0.32–0.42 across recall frames) | Watson, Clark & Tellegen (1988), JPSP 54(6):1063–1070, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.54.6.1063. Already in `src/core2/tooling/calibration-targets.json`: moment PA 29.7 / NA 14.8; today PA 29.1 / NA 16.3; general PA 35.0 / NA 18.1 (U.S. undergraduate samples, n≈660) | Net affect is positive in normal life ("positivity offset"), so a neutral 0 baseline understates everyday mood. The mapping (PA−NA)/40 is an assumption, which is why this is ESTIMATED. The sample is students, not a national sample.
5. moodHalfLifeDays | 2 d | **about 1 d (spread 0.06–1.5 d)** | ESTIMATED | (a) Verduyn & Lavrijsen (2015), "Which emotions last longest and why", Motivation and Emotion 39:119–127, doi:10.1007/s11031-014-9445-y (full text verified). Across 27 emotions, the median episode lasted 2 h to first return to baseline and 5 h to permanent return. Sadness had the longest median, 120 h; hatred 60 h, joy 35 h. N=233 Belgian high-school students, recalled episodes. (b) Bolger, DeLongis, Kessler & Schilling (1989), "Effects of daily stress on negative mood", JPSP 57:808–818 (abstract only). Diaries from 166 married couples over 6 weeks: emotional habituation by the second day for almost all stressors, except interpersonal conflict | The code uses exponential decay toward baseline. If "episode over" means about 90% decayed (about 3.3 half-lives), a 5 h median gives a half-life of about 1.5 h (0.06 d), and sadness at 120 h gives about 36 h (1.5 d). Bolger's next-day recovery puts daily hassles below about 0.5 d. Major game events sit at the long end, so 1 d is the center. The current 2 d is above the upper end. Neither sample is a U.S. national sample, hence ESTIMATED.
6. stressHalfLifeDays | 7 d | keep 7 (TUNABLE). Plausible window is 1.5–30 d | TUNABLE (outcome target) | No direct source found for a decay constant of persistent stress. Targets: PSS-10 is a past-month instrument (Cohen & Janicki-Deverts 2012, above), and Bolger 1989 found conflict-driven distress persisting past day 2 | Calibrate so that the simulated 30-day mean stress matches the PSS-10 distribution. The half-life should stay above moodHalfLifeDays (stress outlasts transient affect, per Bolger's conflict result) and below the one-month PSS recall window.

## Life utility weights (pure model weights: no direct source exists for any of them)

Outcome targets for all of rows 7–14, from BLS ATUS 2025 (news release https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm, 2026-06-25, and Table 1, both verified):

- Work: 81% of employed people worked on an average weekday and 30% on an average weekend day, which averages to about 66% of days. Full-time workers averaged 8.1 h on days worked.
- Daily participation shares: socializing and communicating 30.4%, care of household members 22.0%, organizational/civic/religious activities 13.6%, volunteering 5.3%, household activities 81%, leisure and sports 95%.

7. needWeight | 1 | keep 1 as the normalizer | TUNABLE | none (model weight) | Fix it at 1 as the scale anchor and calibrate the other weights relative to it.
8. goalWeight | 1 | TUNABLE | none | Calibrate so employed adults choose work-shift on about 81% of weekdays and 30% of weekend days.
9. driveWeight | 1 | TUNABLE | none | Calibrate so civic acts (join, organize, approach) occur on about 5.3% (volunteering) to 13.6% (org/civic/religious) of adult person-days. If drives are rare event-born states, check per-drive-holder rates instead.
10. traitWeight | 0.2 | TUNABLE | none | Target: spread in participation across trait groups. No sourced bound exists. Check that trait-sorted subgroups stay within ATUS demographic-group spreads (ATUS Tables 2–3 by sex and age; not retrieved here).
11. emotionWeight | 0.2 | TUNABLE | none | Target: the stress and mood distribution (PSS-10 and PANAS above) must stay stable over a year without runaway feedback.
12. effortWeight | 0.2 | TUNABLE | none | Target: the time-budget totals from ATUS 2025 (sleep 9.03 h/person-day, socializing 0.58 h, care 0.49 h, civic 0.28 h).
13. restEffect | 0.25 (stress × e^−0.25 per rest act) | TUNABLE | none (not a clinical quantity) | Target: with one rest per day, the stress spike from an ordinary stressor should be mostly gone by the next day, per Bolger 1989 day-2 habituation. Note: e^−0.25 = 0.78 per act combines with the half-life decay. Check that both together do not shorten recovery far below the mood/stress half-lives above.
14. careEffect | 0.15 | TUNABLE | none | Target: care act participation 22.0% (household) and 7.6% (nonhousehold) of person-days, plus the recipient's health/stress trajectory. No source exists for the relief magnitude.
15. companyHorizonDays | 7 d | TUNABLE. The ATUS-implied mean gap is about **3.3 d** | TUNABLE (outcome target) | ATUS 2025: 30.4% of people socialize and communicate on an average day, down from 38% in 2015 (news release, verified) | The need equals days since last contact divided by the horizon. If socializing days were independent, the mean gap between them would be 1/0.304 ≈ 3.3 days. The real target is that the simulated daily share of adults making a contact act is about 30%. A 7 d horizon likely produces too little contact pressure.

## Money stocks

16. moneyBufferDays | 30 d | **19 d central (spread 13–26 d pre-2020; 22–43 d at the July 2021 peak)** | ESTIMATED | JPMorgan Chase Institute, Wheat & Eckerd, "Household cash buffer management from the Great Recession through COVID-19", July 2023, Figure 1, https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/household-financial-health/household-cash-buffer-management-from-the-great-recession-through-covid-19 (verified). Median cash buffer by income quartile, 2009–Jan 2020 plateau: Q1 ~13, Q2 ~16, Q3 ~19, Q4 ~26 days of outflows. July 2021 peak: 22 / 26 / 32 / 43. Outcome check: Federal Reserve SHED 2020 ("Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2020", May 2021): 64% of adults would cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or its equivalent (63% in 2019), https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2021-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2020-executive-summary.htm | JPMCI "cash buffer days" are liquid balances divided by typical daily outflows, which is the same unit as the code (daily living cost × days). The fallback is used for households without pay, which skew to lower income, so use about 13–16 days for them. The January 2021 opening sits on the pandemic upswing, so the opening value may run above the plateau. The other use of this key, as the money-need horizon, is a model weight; keep it in days.
17. organizationReserveMonths | 6 months of payroll | Private: **27 days of total outflows** (about 0.89 months; it is already in the repo). Public: at least **2 months** of general-fund operating expenditures. Nonprofit: not found | ESTIMATED | Private: JPMorgan Chase Institute (2016), "Cash Is King: Flows, Balances, and Buffer Days", Finding 3, Figs 5–6. 597,000 small businesses, Feb–Oct 2015: median 27 buffer days, from 16 (restaurants) to 47 (real estate). Already recorded in `data/research/money/opening-employer-cash-buffers.json`. Public: GFOA Best Practice "Fund Balance Guidelines for the General Fund", https://www.gfoa.org/materials/fund-balance-guidelines-for-the-general-fund: unrestricted general fund balance of at least two months of regular operating revenues or expenditures. That is a recommended floor, not an observed level | The code multiplies payroll months. Six months of payroll is several times the observed small-business median, which is under one month of all outflows. Payroll is only part of outflows, so if the key must stay payroll-based, 27 days of outflows exceeds 0.89 months of payroll; convert with the IRS industry pay/receipts ratios the repo already uses. Nonprofit operating reserves: no verified U.S. figure found.

## Household formation and wage fallback

18. marriageReferenceAge | 24 y | **Cohort-specific women's median age at first marriage** from MS-2. Flat fallback **25 y** (spread 20.8–28.6) | ESTIMATED (sourced table, cohort mapping estimated) | U.S. Census Bureau, Table MS-2 "Estimated Median Age at First Marriage, by Sex: 1890 to Present", CPS ASEC 1947–2025, https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/demo/tables/families/time-series/marital/ms2.xls (downloaded and verified). Men / women: 1970 23.2/20.8; 1975 23.5/21.1; 1980 24.7/22.0; 1985 25.5/23.3; 1990 26.1/23.9; 1995 26.9/24.5; 2000 26.8/25.1; 2005 27.1/25.3; 2010 28.2/26.1; 2015 29.2/27.1; 2020 30.5/28.1; 2021 30.4/28.6 (revised 30.3/28.6); 2024 30.2/28.6; 2025 30.8/28.4 | The code sets the marriage start at (younger adult's age − reference age). 34. appraisalDistressTraitWeight | 0.5 | TUNABLE | none | Model weight. Target: trait-group spread in PANAS NA (SD about 5.4–6.4 points on 10–50) should not be exceeded by trait effects alone.
