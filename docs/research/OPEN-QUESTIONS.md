**The question.** For each state other than Washington, what is the governor's term length, what instrument establishes it, when does the term commence, and in which years do regular gubernatorial elections fall?

**Why it matters.** Measured today at main `1c4992e8`: `stateExecutiveTermRule` in `src/simulation/nationwide-world/state-executive-term-rules.ts` holds a `VERIFIED` map with exactly one entry, Washington. **A usable answer.** A row per state: term length in years, the constitutional or statutory citation establishing it, the commencement rule in the form the game already uses (which weekday, which ordinal week, which month, any offset in days), the election cycle length and one reference year in which a regular gubernatorial election was held. ### Which part of a person each formative situation actually bears on

**P0** · asked by hardcoded content audit (hardcoded content audit lane) · filed 2026-09-22 · `which-tendency-a-formative-situation-bears-on`

**The question.** For each of the twenty formative situations the game can put to a player, which personality tendency does repeating it bear on — and which of them bear on none, so that repeating them should propose no change at all?

**Why it matters.** Measured at main `1c4992e8`: when two distinct occasions of the same formative situation occur between the player and the same person, `character-history.ts` proposes a character development. The tendency it proposes is `mindCatalog.tendencyOrder[0]` — the catalogue's first entry, every time, whatever those occasions were about. In the synthetic catalogue that is `riskApproach`, so **every character development the game has ever proposed, in every life, has been about risk approach**, including after two lunch-table choices or two conflicts with a friend. The evidence decides whether a development is proposed and has no influence whatever on which one. This is the sharpest instance yet of the pattern the owner named after his ten-year run — a producer that runs, reads its evidence, and then ignores it. The repeated-count half is fixed; this half cannot be fixed without an authored link, and inventing twenty mappings is content this lane will not make up.

The twenty situations: activity-choice, belief-challenge, broken-object, care-conflict, caring-for-someone, civic-volunteering, friend-conflict, future-preparation, household, household-transition, illness-in-the-house, lunch-table, money-shortfall, school-entry, school-rule-input, small-money, student-organizing, teacher-mentor, teen-work-opportunity, workplace-rule.

The six tendencies available: riskApproach, responseTempo, conflictApproach, curiosity, loyalty, ambition.

**A usable answer.** A row per situation naming the tendency it bears on, or naming none explicitly. "None" is a real answer and the more useful one where it is true: a situation that bears on nothing should propose nothing rather than propose something arbitrary. Where a situation plausibly bears on a tendency the catalogue does not carry, say which tendency is missing rather than forcing it onto one of the six.

**Candidate answers.** The recommended one is marked.

- **Silence where nothing is borne on** — _recommended_. Map only the situations that genuinely bear on one of the six tendencies, and have a situation with no entry propose nothing at all. A proposal is never made on a default, a nearest match or the catalogue's first entry.
  - For: A development proposal is shown to the player and asks them to accept or refuse it. Asking someone to decide about a change to their risk-taking on the evidence of two lunch-table choices is not a wrong mapping, it is the game asking a question it has no business asking. Silence is the honest output when the link is unknown, and it is the same rule the missing-fact classification already sets for an optional observation: omit it or state its absence, never invent a value. It also degrades safely as the catalogue grows, because a newly added situation proposes nothing until somebody says what it bears on.
  - Against: Fewer proposals reach the player than today, and on the twenty situations as they stand it may be a lot fewer. Today's proposals are all the same one, so nothing true is lost, but the surface does get quieter until the table is filled in.
- **Map all twenty, nearest fit**. Assign every one of the twenty situations to whichever of the six tendencies it comes closest to, so a proposal always follows repeated history.
  - For: Keeps the frequency of proposals roughly as it is, and no situation is left inert.
  - Against: Several of the twenty do not plausibly bear on any of the six — a household transition or starting school is not obviously about risk, tempo, conflict, curiosity, loyalty or ambition. Forcing a nearest fit reproduces the present defect in a politer form: the player is still asked about a change the evidence does not support, and now with a mapping table that makes it look considered.

**Already checked.**

- src/simulation/character-history.ts, read and its behaviour traced at main 1c4992e8 — Shows that the tendency is taken from position zero of the catalogue order and carries no link from a situation to a tendency anywhere.
- src/simulation/mind-catalog.ts — Defines six tendencies with names, descriptions and expressions, and nothing that associates any of them with a life situation.
- The twenty formative situation definitions — Each carries its own prose and options; none names a tendency it bears on.

> SMALLEST HONEST CHANGE: a declared table from situation key to tendency, read where `tendencyOrder[0]` is read today, and no proposal at all where the table has no entry. That is C11's fourth case — an optional observation is omitted rather than invented. Nothing else needs building. What a player would see: a proposed change that is about what actually kept happening to them, and no proposal after situations that bear on nothing.

> PLAYER-FACING INPUT: yes, and it is the whole point. A development proposal is shown to the player and requires their choice (`requiresPlayerChoice: true`). So the player is currently being asked to accept or refuse a change about risk-taking on the evidence of two lunch-table choices.

> Related to the thresholds review's disposition on personality evidence: two copies of one event are one observation. That half is now fixed — occasions are counted rather than rows — and is a separate matter from which tendency the occasions bear on.

> WHAT THE SCHEMA HOLDS TODAY, because a disposition that names a record is only actionable if the record exists: the situation-to-tendency table does not exist anywhere. There is no field for it on a tendency definition, none on a situation definition, and no separate record. So this is a schema-and-content job, not a reclassification. What a save loses if the recommended answer is taken and the table starts empty: nothing a player would miss, because today's proposals are all the same one — but it is worth saying rather than discovering, since the surface does go quiet until the table is filled.

### What establishes when a published economic figure became available in the world

**P1** · asked by nationwide coverage (every-town-in-america thread (technical team lead)) · filed 2026-09-22 · `economic-figure-availability-date`

**The question.** The game will not show a player an economic figure it cannot prove had been published by the current in-world date, which is right. All three were fetched on 2026-09-03 and an ordinary life opens on 2026-01-05, so every figure in a 39 MB corpus is withheld from every player in every town for the first eight months of every save, Lexington included. And where a product's calendar cannot be established, should the fallback stay conservative and hide the figure, or should the game show it and say plainly that it cannot date it?

**Why it matters.** It decides whether the economic surface exists at all for an ordinary player. A 39 MB corpus of real BEA, HUD and BLS figures ships with the game and covers the whole country, and today not one number from it reaches any player in any town during the first eight months of a save, because the only date the game can prove is the day a lane ran the fetch script. Answering it turns a shipped, paid-for dataset into something a person in Bowling Green or Boise actually reads, and it settles the general rule for every future dated real-world product, not just these three.

**A usable answer.** For each of the three products — BEA regional (CAINC1), HUD Fair Market Rents, BLS LAUS — the publisher's actual release rule, stated as something derivable from an observation's own period: for example 'CAINC1 annual county estimates for year Y are released in November of Y+1'. Plus one decision covering the general case: where no release rule can be established for a product, whether the game withholds the figure or shows it while saying plainly that it cannot date it. A usable answer does not need exact historical release dates per record; it needs a rule per product and a stated fallback.

**Candidate answers.** The recommended one is marked.

- **Read each publisher's real release calendar** — _recommended_. For each of the three products, establish the publisher's actual release rule and derive the availability date from the observation's own period, rather than from when the game fetched the file.
  - For: It is the only answer that is true. BEA publishes CAINC1 on a known annual schedule, HUD publishes Fair Market Rents effective on a stated date each fiscal year, and BLS publishes LAUS monthly on a publicly documented calendar. Reading those three schedules once turns every figure in the corpus into something the game can prove a person could have seen, and it keeps working as the corpus is refreshed.
  - Against: It is a per-product research job with a maintenance tail: three publishers, each with its own calendar, and a vintage rule that has to be re-read whenever a corpus is refreshed. It also cannot be settled from inside the repository, because none of the three locked products carries a release date at all.
- **Use the observation's own period end**. Treat a figure as available from its `asOf` date — the end of the period it describes.
  - For: It needs no research and no external source, and it is derivable today from a field every record already carries.
  - Against: It is a guess dressed as a rule, and it would be wrong in the permissive direction: a 2019 annual estimate was not available on 2019-12-31. It would show a player figures nobody could have seen, which is the same failure as the current one with the sign reversed.
- **Keep the retrieval-date fallback**. Nothing changes. A figure becomes visible on the day the game fetched the file it came from.
  - For: It is conservative and it never shows a player something they could not have seen. It is also the current behaviour, so it costs nothing.
  - Against: It leaves the surface empty for the first eight months of every life, and it makes a fetch date — an accident of when a lane happened to run a script — into a fact about the world the player lives in. Refreshing the corpus would push the date later and take figures away from saves that already showed them.
