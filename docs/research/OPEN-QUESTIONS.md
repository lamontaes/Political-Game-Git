**Why it matters.** Alaska's legislator qualifications are refused on an ordinary 5 January 2026 start because the constitution was read on 6 September 2026 and nothing records when its words actually took effect. Alaska is one of two states with authored candidacy rules at all, so the refusal removes a large part of what can be played.

**A usable answer.** One row: the date the current language of each section took effect, whether that is a ratification date, a statehood date or a later stated effective date, and the material that establishes it. If the sections have never been amended since the constitution took effect, say that explicitly with the source, because that is the answer rather than an absence. If no material establishes it, say so with what you checked; the present refusal is the safe outcome and is much better than a guessed date.

- https://ltgov.alaska.gov/information/alaskas-constitution/, as already acquired — It presents the constitution as it currently stands. As acquired, it was not established whether the page carries a per-section amendment or ratification note of the kind Nebraska's publisher prints.

> The machinery is now ready for the answer. If it does, say so and quote it, and this becomes a repository fix rather than a research errand.

> A year without a day is a usable answer if that is what the source proves. Nebraska's were recorded as the January 1 after ratification, a bound the page establishes rather than a precise effective day it does not state. Do the same rather than inventing a day.

**Why it matters.** A press matter about a legislator picks its procedure from a registry that currently holds one entry, Kentucky.
