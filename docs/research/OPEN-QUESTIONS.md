### When the present words of ten Minnesota and Nebraska constitutional provisions took effect

**P1** · asked by hardcoded-content audit (hardcoded-content audit thread) · filed 2026-09-22 · `mn-ne-constitutional-provision-commencement`

**The question.** For each of ten named provisions of the Minnesota and Nebraska constitutions, on what date did the provision's current words take effect, and what primary or publisher material establishes that date?

**Why it matters.** Nobody can stand for office in Nebraska, and the Minnesota House refuses the same character the Senate accepts. The cause is not a rule and not a bug: the game will not apply a constitutional requirement to a date earlier than the day it observed the text, and the ordinary start date is 5 January 2026 while these texts were read on 9 September 2026. That discipline is right and has already caught real provenance leaks, so the repair is not to relax it. The repair is the commencement date, which is the fact the EXACT_INTERVAL rows already carry and these ten do not. With it, two states become playable without anything being assumed.

**A usable answer.** A row per provision: the citation, the date the current language took effect, whether that is a ratification date or a later stated effective date, and the material that establishes it — a ratification note, an election canvass, a session-law or amendment record, or the constitution's own provision on when an amendment takes effect. Where a provision has been amended since and the current words are older or newer than the article's headline date, say which. If a provision's commencement genuinely cannot be established from available material, say that with what you checked; an honest unknown keeps the present refusal, which is the safe outcome, and is far better than a guessed date.

Their own recorded reason states the gap: the acquired publisher snapshot proves the current words on the observation date, but no claim-specific primary material in this corpus proves how far back those same words applied. The other fifty rows, across Massachusetts, Missouri, New Jersey, Nevada and Ohio, carry EXACT_INTERVAL with a real validFrom.
- The publisher pages already acquired: revisor.mn.gov/constitution and the Nebraska Legislature's constitution pages — They present the constitution as it currently stands. Neither page, as acquired, states per-section when the present language was ratified or took effect.

> Ten distinct provisions: Minn. Const. art. IV sections 4 and 6, art. V section 1, art. VII section 6; Neb. Const. art. III sections 1, 7, 8 and 12, art. IV sections 1 and 2.

### Which body hears a legislative ethics complaint, state by state

**P2** · asked by hardcoded-content audit (hardcoded-content audit thread) · filed 2026-09-22 · `state-legislative-ethics-procedure`

**The question.** In each state and the District of Columbia, which body receives and hears an ethics complaint against a sitting legislator, what is that procedure called in the state's own words, and which constitutional provision, statute or chamber rule establishes it?

**Why it matters.** A press matter about a legislator picks its procedure from a registry that currently holds one entry, Kentucky. Everywhere else the game falls through to a generic inquiry, so the same story reads identically in fifty states and the one place-specific detail a player would recognise is missing. The registry is built and keyed by jurisdiction; the rows are what is absent.

**A usable answer.** A row per state: the body's own name, the term the state uses for the proceeding, whether the two chambers run separate panels or share one, and the citation that establishes it. Where a state routes legislator complaints to a general ethics commission rather than a legislative panel, say so rather than leaving the row blank.

**Already checked.**

- src/simulation/press/state-ethics.ts and the compiled jurisdiction corpus — Carries jurisdiction identity and names only. It records no ethics procedure for any state other than the one Kentucky row authored by hand.
- The legislative rule packs under src/simulation/legislature — Describe chamber composition and how a measure moves. They say nothing about discipline or ethics proceedings, and exist for nine states in any case.

> Only the identity of the body and the name of the proceeding are needed to make the rendered line place-specific. The full procedural sequence is a larger question and is not being asked here.
