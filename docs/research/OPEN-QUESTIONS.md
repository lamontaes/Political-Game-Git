### What vote shares real American elections produce

**P0** · asked by running for office across America (lamontae, 2026-09-22 at 6:11 p.m. Eastern ('93% is super unrealistic'), filed by the research intake thread) · filed 2026-09-22 · `realistic-vote-shares-and-how-far-a-campaign-moves-them`

**The question.** lamontae watched a playtest candidate win on election day with 93% of the vote and called it super unrealistic. Answer with empirical distributions, nationwide, not a single state. (1) For each race type (nonpartisan municipal council and mayor; partisan county office; state house and state senate; statewide executive; U.S. House; U.S. Senate), what is the distribution of the winner's share and of the margin, split by: contested versus uncontested; two candidates versus three or more; incumbent running versus open seat; and district partisanship (safe, lean, competitive)? Give the share of contested races won with more than 70%, 80% and 90%, and say when a 90%+ result is real (token opposition, a write-in, a one-party district). (2) How far a campaign can move support per week, and where it levels off, is asked separately in how-fast-a-campaign-can-move-support; answer it there. (3) How do opponents respond: how often do they campaign back, and how does a rival's own activity bound what one campaign can gain? (4) How do turnout and undervotes change the counted share in low-turnout local races?

**Why it matters.** The game counts the vote as each candidate's campaign support share plus a seeded swing of at most 3.5 points (src/simulation/campaigns.ts evaluateCampaignAwareOutcome, at main 2bf9ba97). Support rises with every outreach session as a transfer from the field, 1.5 basis points per volunteer-minute times a 0.6 to 1.41 draw, with no diminishing returns and no ceiling other than a 1% floor for everyone else (requestedGainBasisPoints and campaign-support.ts SUPPORT_FLOOR_BASIS_POINTS). The long-playthrough lane measured it: a statewide governor's race went from 48% to 96% in sixteen weeks of door-knocking (how-fast-a-campaign-can-move-support). The earlier answer on election nights (how-an-election-night-should-come-out, A09) says to reuse a candidate, organization and finite-audience model and supplies no empirical win-share or persuasion figures, so the fix has nothing real to calibrate against.

**A usable answer.** A table of winner-share and margin distributions (median, 10th and 90th percentile, and the rate of 70%+, 80%+ and 90%+ wins) for each race type and split in (1), with its data source and years; how often opponents campaign back and how that bounds a gain; the effect of turnout and undervotes in low-turnout races; and a stated fallback for race types with no data. Mark each figure as measured, synthesized or assumed.

## For whoever is answering these

A question belongs here if no explicit research has been done on it. That is
the owner's instruction and it is wider than the test this queue used to
apply, which asked whether a question was bulky enough to be worth handing
over. So expect this document to grow, and expect more of it to be judgment
than citation: design questions belong here too, not only factual surveys.

here is what it depends on" is a real answer to a design question. So is "I
checked and could not establish this", with what was checked — which beats a
plausible guess, because a guess silently disables the refusal that would
otherwise have told somebody the fact was missing.
