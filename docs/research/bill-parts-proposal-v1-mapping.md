# 41 kinds of bill part recur in real law; Proposal V1 has a place for 38 of them, and the game can write 8

The bill research keeps 220 enacted measures; 222 were collected, and two donor records were excluded because neither became law. Those measures use 61 kinds of part, and 41 appear in 5 or more measures. Proposal V1 gives 38 of the 41 a place: a drill-down, a shared section or an operation. The game can fully write 8 today. Its largest gaps are bans with penalties and enforcement, benefit amounts, tax credits, phase-ins and state control of local governments. The Register already settled the subject wheel as navigation only. Line 19 of the research catalog mistakenly counts it as part of Proposal V1, and Team C will correct that wording. Nothing is asked of Lamontae.

Proposal V1 is a content catalog, not a numbered drafting procedure. Its structure, quoted:

1. **Subject menus.** "15 core domestic subject menus and three additional menus covering national-policy subjects. These are content headings, not a requirement to display 18 wheel segments." The first nine: 01 Infrastructure; 02 Healthcare; 03 Education; 04 Taxes & Public Finance; 05 Assistance & Social Insurance; 06 Housing & Land Use; 07 Labor & Employment; 08 Business & Economic Development; 09 Energy & Environment. The other nine: 10 Justice & Public Safety; 11 Government & Democracy; 12 Rights, Family & Consumer Protection; 13 Science, Technology & Communications; 14 Agriculture & Food; 15 Parks, Culture & Community Life; 16 Immigration & Citizenship; 17 Defense & Veterans; 18 Foreign Affairs & Trade.
2. **Drill-downs** (four worked examples). For instance "Taxes & Public Finance > Taxes": "Individual Income Tax: rates and brackets; taxable income; deductions; exemptions; credits; withholding; indexing." "Assistance & Social Insurance > A Selected Program": "Eligibility: income; assets; household size; age or other qualifying category; residence; linked-program qualification. Benefit: amount or formula; maximum; duration; phase-out; inflation adjustment. ... Funding and capacity: appropriation; funding source; administering body; delivery capacity ... Appeals and oversight: review of refusals; complaints; reporting; audit. Timing: start; phase-in; transition for existing recipients; expiration."
3. **Shared content after a specific topic is selected** (eight sections, shown only where applicable). Codes used below:
   - **S1** "Current rules and programs — choose an existing target or begin a new supported proposal."
   - **S2** "Coverage and eligibility — who, what activity, institution or place is covered, with exceptions."
   - **S3** "Benefit, rate or standard — the actual payment, price, tax structure, service requirement, permission or restriction."
   - **S4** "Funding and revenue — appropriations, funding sources, grants, charges and matching requirements."
   - **S5** "Administration and delivery — responsible institution, staffing, award or allocation process and implementation terms."
   - **S6** "Enforcement and appeals — compliance checks, corrective action, penalties and review where supported."
   - **S7** "Reporting and review — disclosures, audits, performance reports and review dates."
   - **S8** "Timing and replacement — effective dates, transition, phase-in, sunset, amendment and repeal."
4. **Operations.** "'Raise,' 'lower,' 'expand,' 'restrict,' 'replace,' and 'repeal' belong inside the selected subject as supported operations, not separate top-level topics."
5. **Placement rules.** One identity and one primary catalog location per policy, with cross-links; outcomes (approval, GDP, mortality) stay out of drafting controls.

The approved drafting workflow (Register, September 25), paraphrased into steps with the codes used below:

- **W1** A subject index or wheel helps the player enter a policy area. It is navigation only; the owner rejected the recent colored wheel.
- **W2** Once a subject or current policy is selected, the bill on paper becomes the workspace.
- **W3** The player selects an editable provision on the paper, and a compact panel offers only supported operations.
- **W4** A parameter-specific micro-editor makes the change.
- **W5** The wording previews on the paper, as a clean copy or compared with current wording.
- **W6** Staff can prepare an optional revision.
- **W7** "Add another section" reopens subject selection and adds another compatible component to the same bill.

## Contradictions and mismatches found

Each item is marked **measured** (read in the named file or document) or **inferred** (reasoned from what was read, not traced).

1. **Wheel wording in the research (measured).** Line 19 of `docs/research/bill-parts-catalog.md` on `921cb554d` says "The owner accepted Proposal V1's drafting flow and policy wheel." In Drive, Proposal V1 is a content catalog whose menus are "not a requirement to display 18 wheel segments". The Register accepts it "as the catalog for bills" and keeps the wheel "navigation only". The drafting flow is a separate approval, the September 25 bill-on-paper workflow, in which the owner "specifically rejects the recent colored wheel treatment". The Idea Inbox copy of Proposal V1 still reads "Status: FOR OWNER REVIEW", and the Register's acceptance supersedes it. No decision is open. Line 19 needs correcting.
2. **Operations (measured).** The research codes 7 operations. The extra one is `create`: 62 of 220 measures have it as the primary operation. Proposal V1 lists 6 operations and covers new measures in S1 ("begin a new supported proposal"). The game's operation type has only insert, replace and repeal (`legislation-bundle.ts:137`). Raise, lower, expand and restrict exist only as changed parameter values, not as operations a player picks.
3. **The tax route is narrower than the research implies (measured).** The catalog counts 18 of 41 tax measures as "beyond one flat rate" and leaves the other 23 unchecked. Every tax proposal needs a tax-power record whose instrument can only be selective-excise, sales or property (`tax-types.ts:13`). 8 sampled measures name an income tax in their title: Georgia twice, Idaho, Kentucky, Mississippi, Ohio, Oklahoma and Utah. They and the 2 federal tax measures therefore cannot be written even as one flat rate. The count comes from a title search over the records.
4. **Vetoes (measured).** The catalog says veto overrides "were not traced". It codes veto overrides as a bill part and files line-item vetoes under waivers. The game already records a separate `"line-item-vetoed"` executive action (`types.ts:4220`). The 13 waiver measures therefore mix two things the game keeps apart.
5. **Phase-in in the game's contract (measured).** The timing clause says it covers "how it phases" (`legislation-content-contracts.ts:113`). A search of the `legislation-*-families.ts` files finds no stepped phase-in in any variant, yet 17 sampled measures carry one.
6. **Levels (measured type; classing inferred).** The research uses six levels. The law-effects reader has three: federal, state and local (`enacted-law-effects.ts:53`). How it would class the 11 D.C. and territory measures was not checked. They may fall under "local".
7. **Consistent, not contradictory (measured).** The research catalog says a passed law has no "halted" or "suspended" status. That still holds at `06e555415` (`types.ts:4233`). The family lines that the research catalog cites still point at the same code on current main.
