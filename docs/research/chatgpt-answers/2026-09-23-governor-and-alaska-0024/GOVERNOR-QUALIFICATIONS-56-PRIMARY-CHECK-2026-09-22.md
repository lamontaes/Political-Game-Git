# Every governor race has a checked rule; some filing details remain open

All 50 states, D.C. and the five inhabited U.S. territories appear in this single handoff. D.C. has a mayor rather than a governor. Every row now has at least one qualification checked against an official constitution, statute, court, or election authority. **UNKNOWN** means this research did not establish that field; it does not mean a person is eligible or ineligible. The 2023 Council of State Governments survey remains a secondary lead where noted and must not override the cited current authority.

## Differences that change a game rule

- [Kansas law](https://www.kslegislature.gov/media/statute/025_000_0000_chapter/025_001_0000_article/025_001_0001a_section/025_001_0001a_k.pdf) sets an age-25 and qualified-elector condition for governor candidates; the 2023 secondary survey reported no formal provision. The exact grammar of the elector filing deadline needs legal confirmation before a hard gate is compiled.
- [Kentucky's constitution](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/Law/Constitution/Constitution/ViewConstitution?rsn=80) states six years of Kentucky citizenship and residence before election. The survey's two-year figures conflict with that primary text.
- [Michigan's constitution](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Publications/MIConstitution.pdf) sets four years as a registered elector before election. The survey's separate four-year citizenship and residence cells cannot be treated as independent express rules.
- [Maryland's constitution](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=c2&enactments=false&section=5) expressly requires five years of Maryland residence and registered-voter status before election.
- [Louisiana's constitution](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/LawPrint.aspx?d=206436) measures age, elector status, and state and U.S. citizenship by candidate qualification. A five-year standalone residence period is not established by that clause.
- [West Virginia's constitution](https://www.wvlegislature.gov/wvcode/wv_con.cfm) tests age at the beginning of the term and state citizenship before election. A rule that tests everything on filing day would be wrong.
- [California's Secretary of State](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/prior-elections/statewide-election-results/statewide-direct-primary-june-5-2018/qualifications-running-office/gov-ltgov) says the printed five-year constitutional residence clause is unenforceable. The current enforcement status must be checked before compiling a game block.
- [South Carolina's legislative manual](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/man25/03_Con-StatProv.pdf) says its printed religious test is judicially invalid. It must not be turned into a character requirement.

## What this gives the team

The owner chose a brief eligibility rule on each race listing, with detail available on request. A rejection should explain the governing requirement and its date in ordinary election-office language. The game's automatic registration at age 18 is a play convenience; it is not a factual claim about real-world voter registration. A candidate's race path should use a verified jurisdiction rule and its own test date. Unchecked collateral bars, source conflicts, and enforceability remain research questions. They cannot be silently converted into permission or a rejection.
