# Lucia kept $102,160 from her campaign and won with 99 percent

Lucia Moran withdrew $153,931 from her campaign during a race for governor of Alaska. Her opponent complained, a state paper reported the payments, and a commission fined her $11,200 and ordered $40,571 repaid. She took most of that repayment out again. The race still ended 99.0 percent to 1.0 percent, and she entered office with $102,160 in personal cash. This is a clearer account of the [original Alaska playtest](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1bGBzaiAqKhlsAUiO4sqh80mYZLeE_0mHHlldRryUH10/edit); it reports what that walk observed, not a new test of today's build.

## The life in Nome

Lucia was 40 when her life began in Nome on January 5, 2026. Her People screen called Mira Rivers “who you live with” and Rebekah Moran “your mom.”

She first tried to run for the state House. The list called the seat “Currently eligible under the represented rules,” and the detail screen named State House District 39. She pressed “Put your name in for the Seat in the House of Representatives.” No campaign opened. A refusal appeared much farther down the page: “The recorded district-residence interval does not yet satisfy the sourced 1-year requirement.” The game counted her district residence from January 5, the day this 40-year-old life began.

The governorship was available. Its listing said, “You may stand for Governor of your state today.” Lucia filed for the November 3 election. She spent five days each week going door to door and two on the phones. Each week, she also moved campaign money into her own account. The screen said, “Use campaign money for a personal expense. This is misuse of campaign funds.” She took 80 percent of the committee's balance, usually $2,100 to $3,300. She gave “A boat, a snow machine and a down payment on a cabin” as the purpose, checked “I understand this is misuse of campaign funds,” and pressed “Use campaign money personally.” Across the race she made 43 withdrawals totaling $153,931.

On February 16, her opponent Parker Clay complained. Ella Wilson of the Alaska Capitol Dispatch asked Lucia for a response. Lucia chose “Deny it (this is a lie)” and said, “That is not true. No campaign money paid for anything personal.” The paper printed the allegation. A state commission, incorrectly called the “Alaska Fair Political Practices Commission” in this build, wrote to her. Lucia pressed “Do not respond.”

The first public payment report appeared two days after Parker's complaint and covered Lucia's January 12 withdrawal. Later reports grouped five weekly payments. One said, “The campaign committee for Lucia Moran reported 5 payments totaling $12,927.00 to the candidate.” On June 26, the commission found 16 payments totaling $40,571, ordered that amount repaid to the committee, and imposed an $11,200 fine. On June 29, Lucia withdrew $34,949 from the replenished committee account. Wilson asked her the same question three times that day. Lucia denied it three times. The July 1 paper carried the finding, repayment, and fine.

Lucia kept taking money. A later filing listed five payments totaling $51,922, but no second complaint opened. The Nome Civic Bulletin and the national Meridian Dispatch never covered the case in this walk. Her party chapter kept inviting her to meetings. The case record said that Mira and Rebekah had distanced themselves from Lucia. Her People screen showed only an old “Last in touch” date beside each, even though Mira lived with her.

Election night showed “Lucia Moran (them) — 99.0%. Parker Clay — 1.0%.” Lucia became governor on January 4, 2027. Her Finances screen showed $102,160 of her own money.

In office, she hired the last of three unfamiliar chief-of-staff candidates, chose no single priority, and held spending steady on every budget. Twelve bills reached her over four spring sessions: seven versions of “Village Transit Support” and five of “Coastal Ferry Schedule Notice.” She sent all twelve back unsigned. No override attempt appeared. In August 2027 and September 2028, she passed up a federal disaster request that only the governor could make. The state and local papers reported that no request had been made. The walk showed no later governing response to those choices, no pay entering her account, and the same $102,160 balance for four years.

In March 2030, her Campaigns screen still said she held office until January 2031, but offered no way to file again. A September news request said the governor “cannot or will not stand again under this game profile.” Adrian McLean won that November and took office in January 2031. Lucia left office at 45.

## What the walk established

**The case changed Lucia's cash balance by the fine.** The two same-seed walks measured $113,360 kept before the finding rules and $102,160 after them. The $11,200 difference equals the fine. Repayment did not reduce what she ultimately held because she withdrew from the committee again. The original report did not measure the separate effect of each campaign action on votes.

**Most withdrawals escaped the first finding.** The walk measured 16 of 43 payments covered by the finding and 27 left uncharged. It matched report totals to payments and found that the final seven withdrawals, totaling $19,629, were never reported before the campaign ended. Source review shows one payment case per campaign and reports that run only while a campaign is active ([matters.ts](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/simulation/press/matters.ts:860)).

**The electoral penalty was too small to settle this race.** Source review shows that the finding rule subtracts three points of support, labeled in code as an unresearched placeholder ([findings.ts](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/simulation/press/findings.ts:49)). The walk measured a 99-to-1 result after 216 days at the doors and 86 on the phones. It did not isolate what those campaign days added, so it cannot say how much they offset the finding.

**Several consequences existed only in records or text.** The walk observed four denials and no later story about them. Source review shows that the game records a false denial, but the cited screen code does not carry it forward ([views.ts](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/simulation/press/views.ts:324)). Mira and Rebekah's distancing appeared in the case record but not as a clear change on People. The state papers did report the disaster nonrequest; the walk did not show a later governing or political response. Source review shows that the game drew “Fair Political Practices Commission” from a generic naming list and drew a $700 fine for each of the 16 payments ([generated-state-oversight.ts](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/simulation/press/generated-state-oversight.ts:27)). The source range is $500 to $5,000 per payment, not an Alaska schedule ([generated-state-oversight.ts](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/simulation/press/generated-state-oversight.ts:47)). [Alaska's official regulator is the Alaska Public Offices Commission](https://apoc.doa.alaska.gov/).

**Two paths into office contradicted the available rules.** The walk measured a House listing that said eligible before a district-residence check refused her. Its displayed residence clock began on the first game day. Source review shows that the governor route checked no age or residence requirement in that build ([state-executive-candidacy-packs.ts](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/simulation/nationwide-world/state-executive-candidacy-packs.ts:163)). Lucia had no ordinary filing button for another term. Source review explains that “in office” was not an allowed filing status ([NationwideCandidacyWorkspace.tsx](/Users/lamontae/Documents/PG-LAND/src/player/NationwideCandidacyWorkspace.tsx:77)). Congress and the presidency did not appear in her office list during this walk.

## What happens next

The corruption work needs follow-through for later payments, reused repayment, denial, public knowledge, and visible relationship effects. The candidacy work needs the House listing and refusal to agree, residence history that predates the opening day when supported, governor qualification rules, and an incumbent filing route. Campaign support and the 99-percent result need separate testing. Player text needs a pass over the quoted lines, especially “dated executive term,” “a Governor,” raw dates, “in this game,” and pronoun use. The original report filed approval measurement, governor qualifications, and consequences for a sitting governor's campaign misconduct as research questions. It placed Congress and the presidency with “Climbing from council to president.” These are routes for the responsible teams, not claims that they remain unfixed on the current build.
