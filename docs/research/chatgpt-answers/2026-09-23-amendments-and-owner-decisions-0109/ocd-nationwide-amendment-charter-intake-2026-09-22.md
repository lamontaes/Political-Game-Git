# Nationwide amendment and city-charter procedures — sourced research intake

**September 22, 2026.** This is one combined set for 50 states, D.C. and the five inhabited territories. It covers constitutional or charter change routes and state city-charter frameworks, plus 245 Census-based city samples in 49 states. Hawaii has no qualifying incorporated-city row in the Census file. Every state and jurisdiction appears below; many route fields and most city-specific charter fields remain **UNKNOWN**. These are research findings for Claude to reconcile, not approved game rules. An unknown rule must remain unavailable for that place until its own legal source is verified.

## Reading and use

- **VERIFIED PRIMARY** means the linked current constitution, statute or official municipal text directly supports that field; **SECONDARY** identifies a nonofficial legal-text reproduction, an official explanation or older text; **UNKNOWN** means this pass did not establish it. A source link does not verify every field in a row.
- A general state charter framework never supplies an individual city's form or amendment route by itself. A city's actual charter or applicable charter act must establish the player-facing procedure.
- Official [2025 Census incorporated-place estimates](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/cities/totals/sub-est2025.csv) support city populations and sample selection. Samples aim at population ranks 1, 2, 5, 10 and 25 among city-designated records where available; ranks are not five guaranteed distinct sizes in every state.
- State entries below retain explicit unknowns. The D.C./territory entries distinguish federal organic-act changes from local constitutional routes. Mississippi's printed initiative text requires the court-status check described in its entry before anyone treats that route as operative.

## Fifty state procedures and city-charter frameworks

### Alabama

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Alabama Legislature, Ala. Const. art. XVIII §§284–285 (official legislature constitution PDF, current text accessed September 22, 2026: https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/constitution-of-alabama-1901). Either house may propose; 3/5 of members elected to each house must agree, with journal yeas/nays; proposal goes to next general election, or special election if 3/5 of each house so votes. Majority of votes cast on the amendment ratifies; the Governor proclaims the result. No statewide voter initiative amendment route appears in the constitution. No general entrenchment restriction was verified in this pass. The official searchable page is the 2022 Constitution of Alabama; the amendment procedure is Article XVIII §284. The official searchable page does not yield stable section URLs in this pass.
- **Convention/revision (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislature may call a convention under art. XVIII §286 by an act or resolution approved by a majority of all members elected to each house. The convention/no-convention question must then be approved by a majority of electors voting. A proposed replacement constitution requires majority ratification under §286.01 and takes effect January 1 after ratification unless it sets another date; convention proposals are voted on at the next general election at least 90 days after adjournment. Amendment 951 added §286.02, a recompilation provision; it is not an entrenchment rule. Official current 2022 text and explanatory material: https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/proposed-2022-constitution and https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/lsa/ALI/election_handbook/Elect_Hndbk_20th_edition/chapters/law-institute-final-chapter-22.pdf.
- **Local-application constitutional amendments (VERIFIED PRIMARY; secondary explanation):** The current Constitution of Alabama 2022, art. XVIII §284.01, applies to amendments affecting only one county or a political subdivision in one or more counties. The current text requires at least 3/5 of elected members in each house to pass the amendment and then provides for separate resolutions naming the affected area. If both houses approve the local-application resolution without dissent, the ballot is local and a majority of affected voters voting must approve. A dissenting vote on that resolution sends the measure to statewide referendum; adoption then requires a majority statewide and a majority in each named affected area. This wording was verified directly in the official current §284.01 text. The Alabama Election Handbook, 21st edition, ch. 22 §§22.3–22.3.2, is an older official explanatory source, not the basis for the current threshold: https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/lsa/ALI/election_handbook/Alabama_Election_Handbook_21st_Edition.pdf. Current constitution source: [Alabama Constitution of 2022, statewide volume](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/lsa/proposed-constitution/Statewide_Volume_2022.docx); [official Constitution page](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/proposed-2022-constitution).
- **City charter/general home rule (UNKNOWN):** No general-law Alabama municipal charter-amendment petition threshold or statewide charter ballot rule verified in primary source during this pass. Alabama’s municipal framework is substantially statutory/local-act based; do not infer city home rule or a universal petition threshold. Census 2022 Individual State Descriptions, Alabama entry, is organizational baseline only (secondary, April 2024): https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2024/econ/2022ISD.pdf.

### Alaska

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislature proposes by 2/3 vote of each house; Lt. Governor prepares proposition and places it on next general election ballot; majority of votes cast on the proposition adopts; effective 30 days after Lt. Governor certifies returns unless amendment specifies otherwise. No citizen petition route found. Source: Alaska Const. art. XIII §1, official legislature PDF https://akleg.gov/docs/pdf/AK_Const_layout.pdf (accessed September 22, 2026).
- **Revision/convention (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislature may call a convention at any time. If no convention in a ten-year period, question goes to next general election; majority affirmative triggers delegate election at next regular statewide election unless legislature sets special election. Source: art. XIII §§2–4, same official PDF. Convention proposal ratification rules are supplied by law/call; verify text for a specific convention before simulation.
- **City charter/home rule (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Alaska Const. art. X §§9–10 gives qualified voters of first-class city/borough the authority to adopt, amend or repeal charter as provided by law and requires voter approval by majority voting on that question. For an existing municipality, a charter commission election may be initiated by governing-body resolution or petition signed by voters equal to 15% of votes cast at the last regular municipal election. A commission of seven elected members prepares the proposal. It goes to voters and is effective on certification if a majority voting favor it. Official statutory title PDF (Title 29): https://www.akleg.gov/statutesPDF/Title-29.pdf; official constitutional overview by Lt. Governor: https://ltgov.alaska.gov/information/alaskas-constitution/ (official explanatory source). Applicable municipality class and local charter can alter details; the 15% rule is for the commission election, not itself a direct amendment petition.

### Arizona

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Either house may propose; majority of members elected to each house; statewide ballot at next general election unless legislature calls special election. Voter initiative is available at 15% of votes cast for Governor at last preceding general election. Majority of qualified electors voting on the proposition approves. Art. XXI §1 does not state a separate effective date; art. IV pt. 1 §1(D) provides proclamation mechanism for measures. Therefore effective-date rule for amendments: UNKNOWN in located text. Each proposal must be separately votable if multiple amendments are submitted. Source: official Arizona Legislature constitution https://www.azleg.gov/const/Arizona_Constitution.pdf.
- **Revision/convention (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislature cannot call convention unless the convention-enabling law is first approved by voters at referendum; convention’s changes/new constitution must then be submitted to voters and approved by majority voting. Art. XXI §2, same source. Entrenchment/single-subject general rule for amendments: no comprehensive express one-subject clause located; separate vote rule is verified and should not be restated as single-subject requirement.
- **City charter (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Ariz. Const. art. XIII §§2–3 recognizes city charter adoption and voter approval; charter cities have home rule on municipal affairs, subject to constitution and state law. Ariz. Rev. Stat. §19-143(B) permits initiative petition for a charter or amendment; §19-143(C) allows council proposal with or without initiative petition, filed at least 60 days before election, and requires majority of votes cast on the question. Official statutes: https://www.azleg.gov/ars/19/00143.htm. Signature threshold for charter initiative uses local election voting basis unless charter/ordinance supplies another basis; the percent varies by city charter/ordinance, so no universal general-law threshold verified. Charter amendments under charter city adoption statute may require Governor approval; see A.R.S. §9-283(C), https://www.azleg.gov/ars/9/00283.htm.

### Arkansas

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislative proposal at regular session by either house; majority of all elected members of each house; journal yeas/nays; published in each county with a newspaper for six months before next general election for Senators and Representatives; voters decide, majority voting adopts. Maximum three amendments submitted at once; separately votable. Ark. Const. art. 19 §22 (official constitution portal: https://www.arkleg.state.ar.us/; section text also indexed at https://law.justia.com/constitution/arkansas/article-19/section-22/). Amendment effective date after adoption: UNKNOWN in provision inspected.
- **Initiative/revision (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Amendment 7 reserves citizen initiative of constitutional amendments; signature threshold 10% of legal voters based on preceding gubernatorial vote, with geographic distribution requirements; majority voting adopts. Legislature may submit convention question; convention route/rules under Ark. Const. amend. 7 §2 and art. 19 §22 require direct confirmation from current official codification before parameterization (partly UNKNOWN). Legislature may refer constitutional amendments, subject to three-amendment cap.
- **City charter/general law (UNKNOWN):** Arkansas Constitution art. 12 provides municipal home rule subject to state law, but a universal general-law city charter amendment proposer/threshold/vote rule was not confirmed in an official current code during this pass. Do not infer from Amendment 7, which concerns statewide ballot measures.

### California

- **Constitutional amendment/revision (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislature may propose amendment by 2/3 vote of each house; submitted to electors; majority of votes cast adopts. An initiative amendment petition requires signatures equal to 8% of votes for all candidates for Governor at the last gubernatorial election. Revision is distinct from amendment. The legislature may submit a revision question by 2/3 of each house. The convention call is initiated by the legislature and decided by voters; the convention proposal goes to voters. Cal. Const. art. XVIII §§1–4; official Legislative Counsel constitution: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CONS&division=&title=&part=&chapter=&article=XVIII. Effective date: adopted amendment effective the day after election unless it provides otherwise (art. XVIII §4). Entrenchment: voter initiative amendments cannot revise the Constitution; revision requires convention or legislative proposal, art. XVIII §§3–4. No general single-subject rule for constitutional amendments established by the cited text.
- **City charter amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Cal. Const. art. XI §3(a) permits charter amendment proposed by governing body or petition signed by 15% of registered electors (charter may set lower percentage but not below 5%); must be submitted to city voters, majority voting approves. Effective rule generally follows canvass/certification and any charter terms; confirm the local charter and Elections Code timing for a particular city. Official constitution: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=CONS&division=&title=&part=&chapter=&article=XI. State limitations: charter controls municipal affairs; statewide concern/state law controls where applicable, art. XI §5.

### Colorado

- **Constitutional amendment/revision (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** The legislature proposes by 2/3 of all members elected to each house, and the proposal is submitted at the next general election for legislators. An initiative amendment petition requires signatures of 5% of votes cast for Secretary of State at the last election, with required distribution across all 35 state senate districts. Approval generally requires 55% of votes cast. An amendment limited to repealing constitutional provisions needs a majority. Convention question can be referred by 2/3 each house and is approved by majority voting. Colo. Const. art. V §1; art. XIX §§1–2. Official legislature constitutional text source: https://olls.info/crs/crs2024-title-00.htm (2024 constitution compilation); Amendment 71 implementation/explanation: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB17-152. Effective date: text/official ballot rule not resolved in this pass (UNKNOWN; verify the measure’s own provision and applicable law). Restrictions include 55%/distribution rules and legislative limit on number of articles amendable per session (art. XIX §2); amendment vs revision boundary requires source-specific analysis.
- **City charter amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Colorado Const. art. XX §§5–6 gives home-rule cities authority over local/municipal matters. General statutory procedure, C.R.S. §31-2-210 et seq.: charter amendment may be initiated by governing body or petition; petition threshold for charter amendment is 5% of registered electors; charter amendment submitted to municipal voters, majority voting determines adoption. Repeal of charter/new charter commission petition threshold is separately 15%. Official 2024 Title 31: https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-31.pdf. State limitation: charter may not conflict with constitution/state law on matters of statewide concern.

### Connecticut

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY / SECONDARY):** Legislature can propose by either (a) 3/4 of membership of each house in one session, or (b) majority of each house in two successive sessions; then statewide ballot, majority voting approves. Source primary is Conn. Const. art. VI, §1, official General Assembly constitution page: https://www.cga.ct.gov/asp/content/constitutions/CTConstitution.htm. Official OLR explanation confirms mechanics and 30-day effective date absent contrary proposal: https://www.cga.ct.gov/2015/rpt/2015-R-0240.htm. Effective 30 days after referendum unless proposal provides otherwise. No voter initiative amendment route located. Convention may be called by legislature and constitutional convention question is submitted to voters under art. XIII; threshold/election details not verified here (UNKNOWN). No single-subject/entrenchment restriction established in inspected text.
- **City charter/home rule (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Conn. Gen. Stat. §§7-188 to 7-191: proposal may be initiated by municipal appointing authority or petition; petition for charter commission generally  electors equal to 10% of votes cast at last regular municipal election (check §7-188 current exact formula and any local provision). Appointing authority decides submission; charter/amendment goes to municipal electors; at regular election majority of those voting on question approves, or special election requires majority amounting to at least 15% of electors on active registry list. Effective 30 days after approval unless specified otherwise. Current official CGA statutes, 2025 revision: https://www.cga.ct.gov/2025/pub/chap_099.htm. May be several ballot questions. State constitution/general statutes constrain charter provisions.

### Delaware

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Proposal may originate in either house. It must pass by 2/3 of all elected members of each house in one General Assembly. The text must be disseminated 90–120 days before the next general election. A second leg then requires concurrence by 2/3 of all elected members of each house in the next General Assembly after that election. The amendment becomes part of the constitution upon second-leg passage, without gubernatorial approval or statewide ratification vote. Del. Const. art. XVI §1. Current official Delaware General Assembly amendment text (2024 enactment compilation): https://www.legis.delaware.gov/SessionLaws/Chapter?id=41901; official explanation: https://legis.delaware.gov/docs/default-source/publications/memoranda/constitutionalamendmentsexplained.pdf. Convention: legislature by 2/3 each house may submit question; majority voting yes initiates convention. No statewide citizen initiative for constitutional amendment found. Entrenchment/single subject rules require checking current art. II §16 and amendment proposal rules; official 2026 bill synopsis states proposed constitutional amendments subject to single subject and title provision, but status must be checked against enrolled/current constitution before use (UNKNOWN whether adopted as of research cutoff).
- **City charter (UNKNOWN):** Delaware has numerous municipal charters, many special acts. General law framework/petition threshold and state-wide default charter-amendment vote rule not verified in current primary source in this pass. Do not project a single local threshold. A 1960s-era session law search result shows local referendum administration but does not establish current general rule.

### Florida

- **Constitutional amendment/revision (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Art. XI allows proposal by legislature (3/5 vote of each house for joint resolution), revision commission, constitutional convention, and citizen initiative. Initiative signatures: 8% of votes cast in last presidential election in each of at least half of congressional districts and statewide. Convention call by people requires petition 15% of presidential-election vote in each of half the congressional districts and statewide; voters first decide call; majority voting yes triggers convention. Each proposal must be submitted at general election more than 90 days after filing; special election possible if 3/4 of each house and single proposal. Approval: at least 60% of electors voting on measure. Effective first Tuesday after first Monday in January following election unless amendment specifies another date. Fla. Const. art. XI §§1–5. Official Florida Legislature compilation: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?Mode=Constitution&Submenu=3&Tab=statutes (current online text accessed September 22, 2026). Subject restriction: initiative proposal must embrace one subject and matter directly connected therewith, art. XI §3; revision commission/convention route differs. No entrenchment identified in inspected text.
- **City charter/home rule (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Fla. Const. art. VIII §2(b) authorizes municipalities to adopt charters and exercise municipal self-government, subject to general law; amendment method is prescribed by law. Fla. Stat. §166.031: charter amendment may be proposed by ordinance of governing body or petition signed by 10% of registered electors; election is held and majority of electors voting on question approves. Official state statute: https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0166/Sections/0166.031.html. State limitations: charter/ordinances cannot conflict with general law and constitutional provisions.

### Georgia

- **Constitutional amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** General Assembly proposes by 2/3 of total membership of each house; notice published in each congressional district newspaper for two months before election; submitted at next general election; majority of votes cast ratifies. Ga. Const. art. X §1. Effective when proclaimed by Governor (art. X §1, current text); no citizen initiative amendment route found. Convention: legislature may submit convention question by 2/3 of each house; question decided by majority voting. Convention’s revision/amendments submitted to voters under law; exact procedural statutory details not verified here. No general single-subject/entrenchment clause established in this pass. Official Secretary of State constitution PDF: https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/2022-01/Georgia_Constitution.pdf.
- **City charter (VERIFIED PRIMARY, framework; UNKNOWN thresholds):** Georgia municipalities are created/chartered by local acts or general law; state constitution art. IX §2(c) authorizes General Assembly to provide home rule by general law, and Ga. Code Ann. §36-35-3 permits municipal home rule to amend charter subject to enumerated restrictions and limits. Local governing authority may initiate charter amendment; changes commonly require local referendum under applicable local act/general law. A uniform general-law citizen petition threshold and universal voter approval/effective-date rule not verified from primary source in this pass; record as UNKNOWN. Official constitution above; official Georgia Code search: https://legis.ga.gov/ (Title 36, ch. 35).

### Hawaii

- **Constitutional revision/amendment (VERIFIED PRIMARY):** Legislature may propose amendment by 2/3 vote of each house on final reading at any session after 10 days’ written notice to Governor, or majority of each house on final reading in each of two successive sessions. Proposed amendment published in each senatorial district within two months before next general election. Electors approve by majority of votes tallied on question at general election, and that affirmative vote must constitute at least 50% of total votes cast at that election; special-election rule requires majority of votes tallied and at least 30% of registered voters. Effective upon ratification unless proposal specifies otherwise (confirm exact text in §3). Convention: legislature may submit convention call at any general/special election; if not submitted within ten years, Lt. Governor must certify it for first general election thereafter; majority of ballots on call in favor elects delegates; convention’s proposal requires voter ratification per art. XVII §2. No voter amendment initiative currently located in current constitution. Official current constitution: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol01_Ch0001-0042F/05-CONST/CONST_.htm (accessed September 22, 2026). 2026 SB2315 proposed to clarify the ballot standard but committee materials show proposal, not enactment; do not treat pending bill as law: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2026/Bills/SB2315_.PDF.
- **City charter/home rule (VERIFIED PRIMARY / UNKNOWN petition details):** Hawaii Const. art. VIII §§1–3 gives legislature broad authority to create/organize counties and permits county charters; city/county charter status and amendments are governed by charter and state law. Hawaii has one consolidated city-county (Honolulu), so no general-law “city” class analogous to most states. A statewide general-law charter amendment petition threshold/proposer/voter/effective default was not located in this pass (UNKNOWN); Honolulu Charter and Hawaii Revised Statutes must be checked for that specific local government. Constitution: https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol01_Ch0001-0042F/05-CONST/CONST_.htm.

### Illinois

- State amendment proposer / legislative vote and process — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** either General Assembly house may initiate; three-fifths elected in each house, after three readings on separate days and full reproduction before final passage. Submit at the general election at least six months later; possible withdrawal by majority elected in each house. [Illinois Constitution, art. XIV §§1–2](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/lrb/con14.htm).
- State initiative signatures / subjects — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** petition initiative only for Article IV, limited to structural and procedural subjects in Article IV; signatures ≥8% of votes cast for Governor at prior gubernatorial election; signed within 24 months and filed ≥6 months before general election. [Art. XIV §3](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/lrb/con14.htm).
- Convention — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** legislature may refer convention question by 3/5 of members elected in each house; otherwise question must be put in the twentieth year after last submission. Called by 3/5 voting on question or a majority of all election voters. [Art. XIV §1](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/lrb/con14.htm).
- Ballot threshold / effective date — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** legislative and citizen amendments pass with either 3/5 of those voting on question or a majority of those voting in election; convention proposals pass by majority voting on the question. Effective as the amendment/convention provides. [Art. XIV §§1–3](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/lrb/con14.htm).
- Single-subject / entrenchment — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** no more than three articles may be amended at one election; no legislative amendment route while convention is called and until convention measures voted. Initiative only reaches Art. IV. No general single-subject clause stated in this cited article. [Art. XIV](https://www.ilga.gov/commission/lrb/con14.htm).

### Iowa

- State amendment proposer / legislative vote and sessions — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** either house may propose; majority of all elected members in each house in two successive General Assemblies, with publication for three months before election choosing the second legislature. [Iowa Constitution, art. X §1](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/law/statutory/constitution); [official codified text, PDF](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/ICP/769933.pdf).
- State voter approval / effectiveness — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** majority of electors qualified to vote for legislators, voting on the proposal; becomes part of the constitution on approval. Separate vote for each when multiple amendments submitted. Same sources above.
- Convention — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** question at each tenth year from 1970 and when legislature provides by law; majority of electors voting for/against question calls it; next legislature provides for delegate election. [Art. X §3](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/ICP/769933.pdf).
- Initiative signatures — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** no constitutional citizen-initiative mechanism appears in Article X, which assigns proposal to either house; absence here is not a universal claim about every possible legal theory.
- Single-subject / entrenchment — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** only separately voted amendments are expressly required when multiple amendments appear. No single-subject or entrenchment term established by cited text.
- City charter amendment — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** council may refer by resolution; council may amend by ordinance, subject to a qualifying petition within 30 days; voters may petition for a proposed amendment. All require majority of those voting if referred. [Iowa Code §372.11 (2025 official)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/372.11.pdf). Petition threshold is **VERIFIED PRIMARY** by Iowa Code §362.4, summarized by official legislative research: 10% of votes at last regular city election. [Legislative Services Agency report](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/publications/LU/7056.pdf). Local home rule authority is subject to Iowa Constitution and general law; specific charter substantive limits require issue-specific review.

### Louisiana

- State amendment proposal — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** joint resolution; may be proposed at regular session (prefiled at least 10 days before session unless alternate statutory provision) or extraordinary session only within call and introduced in first five calendar days. Two-thirds elected members of each house. [Louisiana Constitution art. XIII §1](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=206363).
- Initiative signatures — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** no voter initiative route stated in Art. XIII §1; proposal route specified as joint resolution. No claim about other legal vehicles.
- Ballot / effective date — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** legislature submits to electors; official constitutional amendment history records amendment-specific effective dates. Exact general approval threshold and effective-date rule not visible in retrieved §1 excerpt: **UNKNOWN pending full subsection review**.
- Convention — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** legislature may call convention by law enacted by 2/3 elected members of each house; convention’s revision/new constitution and alternatives go to people; majority of electors voting thereon approves; governor proclaims new constitution. [Art. XIII §2](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/LawPrint.aspx?d=206364).
- Single-subject / entrenchment — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** legislature may propose as one amendment a revision of an entire article containing multiple objects or changes; other boundaries not established in retrieved passage. [Art. XIII §1](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=206363).
- City charter amendment — **VERIFIED PRIMARY:** any municipality/parish may amend a home-rule charter; charter commission may be appointed or elected.
