# A law changes the record before people feel its effects

The owner confirmed two rules for playable legislation. A passed bill must change a concrete law or budget record. Its effects on people, businesses and the economy occur when that rule reaches them, such as the next tax collection or an actual violation. When a city proposes a measure whose authority is disputed, the city attorney should warn the player and a lawful challenge may proceed. A clear, settled prohibition blocks enactment. The jurisdiction's actual law determines the outcome.

## Decision 1: legal authority and a possible challenge

The question put to the owner on September 22, 2026, was whether a city council proposal that state law may forbid should be introduced and contested. The recommended option described a warning from the city attorney, a plausible legal challenge, and a block on enactment where the prohibition is clear and settled. The owner selected **“Warn, then allow a lawful challenge.”**

This is a product decision about the player's route. It does not identify which states preempt which city subjects. Those rules remain in the nationwide `what-each-level-of-government-may-legislate` research request and must come from that place's law. An unknown rule must not become automatic permission or automatic refusal.

## Decision 2: every enacted bill has a traceable change

The owner selected **“Yes, law first; effects when encountered”** when asked whether passage should first change a legal or budget record and later affect people when the rule reaches them. The examples in the question were a tax change showing up at the next collection date and a ban mattering when someone breaks it.

An implementation inference from this answer is to connect the enacted measure to its specific legal, tax or budget record and the relevant later collection, spending, enforcement or behavior event. Neither answer sets a fixed immediate opinion or economic gain for every bill.

## What is still research

These two answers decide the player's authority and consequence flow. They do not establish which city subjects a particular state preempts, which tax rate applies, or what any jurisdiction's law already says. Those facts need jurisdiction-specific sources; an unverified rule remains unknown.
