# Office, election, meeting, and player-knowledge questions: what is already answered

The current request registry still has open office and election research questions, but several adjacent questions are already answered by accepted decisions or have left the open queue. D-078 establishes a bounded playable candidacy and election path while explicitly leaving nationwide law and several election mechanics unknown. The audit below separates those accepted answers from genuinely open work; it does not treat a draft or a missing request as authoritative resolution.

| Candidate question | Existing answer and source | Truly open remainder / disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Can a character run for office and experience an election? | **Answered at the substrate level.** D-078 (accepted, September 3) records canonical candidacy, committee-owned campaign funds, time-costing campaign actions, ordinary-time election resolution, campaign support truth, a separate imperfect field memo, and loss/win continuity. | **Not a universal office route.** D-078 says available offices derive only from accepted legislative rule packs and expressly leaves offices outside the pack registry unimplemented. The Board describes the current route as partial. Keep the open governor-qualification request and broader office/legal coverage. |
| What makes a person eligible, when do they file, and which district/term applies? | D-078 explicitly marks standing eligibility, filing deadline, term length, and district unknown where packs establish only the chamber and elected-member count. It uses the existing adult threshold as a game rule, not as a claim about law. | **Open nationwide law research.** `governor-qualifications-in-every-state` remains P0. D-078 also lists ballot access, finance, districts, primaries, term value/start, and electorate as unimplemented. Do not present the game's conservative threshold as state law. |
| What vote shares do real elections produce, and how much can campaigning move support? | D-078 answers only the game architecture: campaign action effects depend on workers, hours and spend plus seeded variation; support is hidden from the player and distinct from the memo. Its ten-seed note is implementation evidence, not a real-world rate. | **Open and high priority.** `realistic-vote-shares-and-how-far-a-campaign-moves-them` is P0; `how-fast-a-campaign-can-move-support` is P1. No accepted nationwide empirical vote-share or campaign-movement calibration was found in the checked decision record. Do not turn the ten-seed result into an annual or election probability. |
| When are town elections held under local law? | No accepted nationwide calendar answer identified. D-078 preserves an authored first-election calendar limitation and only admits a later, exact-key continuation for sourced Kentucky House/Senate term rules; that is not municipal law. | **Open.** `town-election-calendar-from-state-municipal-law` remains P1. State and town variation must be researched directly; unknown dates remain unknown. |
| How are results called, recounted, or ties broken? | D-078 says the accepted resolver determines the first election on ordinary time advance, but it does not establish real-world calls, recount triggers, or tie procedures. The Drive item “Playable Campaign Content Bank” is a proposal/draft and is not an accepted decision or primary-law source. | **Open.** `election-results-calling-recounts-and-ties` remains P1. Verify by jurisdiction from authoritative election sources before specifying game behavior. |
| Can someone hold one office while running for another? | No accepted cross-office rule identified. D-078 only says a win records the governing state and describes the supported first-election route. | **Open.** `holding-one-office-while-running-for-another` remains P1. Determine the relevant law/office combinations; do not infer permission or incompatibility from the existing route. |
| What happens between election and taking the seat / at a term boundary? | D-078’s September 14 continuation admits sourced exact Kentucky House/Senate term-rule data; the named contract preserves authored first-election calendar limitations and states that existing clock transitions enter/end work at expiry. | **Partially answered, bounded to named Kentucky keys.** It does not settle other states, special remainder terms, oath production, national allocation/counting, or a broader legislative consumer. See `docs/systems/legislative-term-continuity.md` and the D-078 continuation. |
| Who actually votes in a chamber? | No current open request from the live request CLI list; no broad accepted answer established in this audit. Do not mark this answered merely because it is absent from the queue. | **Status unresolved/UNKNOWN.** Check the current scoped legislative decision/source before use; request registry absence is not evidence. |
| How does a player know a fact, claim, or campaign number? | **Answered as a governing information rule.** D-016 separates canonical event truth, memory, person-specific knowledge, claims, and relationship history with provenance. D-018 keeps sparse political knowledge/beliefs separate; D-020 states exposure does not itself create belief. D-078 applies this to campaign memos: a written but unread memo is not player knowledge, and truth stays hidden. | **Question is not wholly open.** Any request for missing factual content still needs an actual source/knowledge path. Current Board cautions that contact/presence does not imply knowing a name. No unverified fact should be exposed as known. |
| When does a person appear on the player's People card? | **Owner decision in this chat, distinct from code or law:** add a person after an actual introduction or meaningful exchange; sharing a space alone is insufficient. | **Design choice answered.** Do not treat proximity, attendance, or co-presence alone as player knowledge or as a reason to put a person on the card. |
| What public meetings/contact with officials does an ordinary person encounter? | The accepted decision record does not establish general civic-contact or meeting frequencies. D-016 supplies the knowledge model, not event rates. An accepted entry says the community meeting hall is banked in its empty state; adulthood or employment alone does not mean someone has spoken at a public meeting. | **No substantiated national meeting rate found.** Treat as UNKNOWN pending scoped primary-source research or authored event design. Do not use social “people meet” research as a proxy for attendance at public meetings. |
| Does a player have to attend every meeting? What if they do not? | **Owner decision in this chat, distinct from code or law:** players may attend all their meetings; simulation/absence alone carries no penalty; attendance may bring positive or negative consequences; the player retains agency. | **Answered as a design choice.** Do not re-ask whether absence itself should penalize the player. Whether a particular meeting exists, its consequences, and jurisdiction-specific attendance rules still need their own evidence and implementation. |
| What election-night and post-election flow should the game use? | **Owner decision in this chat, distinct from accepted implementation and primary law:** use projection plus certification, a few skippable election-night updates, lawful recount/concede handling, and a decisive tie event; include staff/priorities transition. | **Design direction answered.** It does not supply jurisdiction-specific legal rules or prove the full flow is implemented. Keep result timing, recount triggers, tie procedure, certification, and transition authority grounded in law and separately verified before real-jurisdiction behavior is claimed. |
