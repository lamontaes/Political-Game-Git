# Every jurisdiction has a sourced industry baseline, not a list of open jobs

This file gives the game a county-equivalent industry reference across all 50 states, D.C., and five inhabited territories. It contains 55,093 rows from the Census Bureau's 2023 County Business Patterns release: each county or statewide-unassigned area has its published total and available major-industry rows. This is the latest CBP reference year as of September 22, 2026. It is a starting reference for a fictional economy; the game's organizations and jobs must drift and evolve from there.

## The delivered file

[Download the 56-jurisdiction county-sector CSV](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1NG3w-Ukf-mIjYmGsOzbJVf2kD7O2B7in/view). Every row preserves the two-digit state code, three-digit county code, five-digit county GEOID where applicable, Census geography name, source NAICS code, establishment count, March 12-week employment, employment noise flag, reference year, and exact Census source file URL. Its 55,093 rows cover 3,246 named county-equivalent GEOIDs and 54 statewide-unassigned areas. The latter have county code 999 and a blank county GEOID; they must not be attached to a particular town. All 56 jurisdiction codes appear.

The file selects only each published all-industry total and major sector, not every four- to six-digit industry row from the source. Missing sector rows are **UNKNOWN**, not zero. Twenty-six published rows have an explicit numeric employment value of zero; retain that distinction. Employment values carry Census noise flags: G means less than 2% adjustment, H means 2% to less than 5%, and J means at least 5%. The flags and original numbers stay together.

## Source and checks

The Census [2023 CBP download page](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2023/econ/cbp/2023-cbp.html) publishes separate [50-state and D.C. county data](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23co.zip) and [Puerto Rico/Island Areas county-equivalent data](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/datasets/2023/cbp23pr_ia_co.zip). Census also publishes matching [state/county names](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/reference/state-county-geography-reference/georef22.txt) and [territory/municipio names](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/reference/puerto-rico-geography-reference/georef_pr_ia.txt). Extraction checked unique jurisdiction-county-sector keys, preserved leading zeroes, and matched all county names. The source ZIP SHA-256 values are `e9539e96ceb91608ad44ab1cfc651d7c1ff9b88bddfe7a8ff64134ccde6e9603` and `d6aef1604af728969ea185db0b5aaa197bed95d40cf83354a7dc315d24f37d31`; the delivered CSV SHA-256 is `826e3dc9127a6e7e0112467f882f343665e5e988bd9f16511bfe28a6db7e33c7`.

Census [CBP methodology](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/methodology.html) excludes self-employed people, crop and animal production, most government employment, railroads, and private households. Establishments are work locations, not necessarily distinct companies. County numbers may include confidentiality adjustments; an industry count does not name a private employer or prove a current vacancy. A place may span or differ from a county. Do not apportion county jobs to a town without an actual place-level source or explicit game calibration.

**Use with the [Jobs source intake](https://drive.google.com/file/d/10ObjEhzmUI_4_gTPuSw3XasoiVzLRp9c/view):** CBP can shape the kinds of employers and jobs around a place. It does not settle whether the game uses generated private employers, real public bodies, or a mix; what an individual job pays; or whether a character is hired. Those remain separate design and simulation decisions.
