# Jobs can reflect a place without pretending public data names its openings

Official U.S. sources can tell the game what kinds of employers and occupations are plausible around a place and how wages vary by occupation and area. They do not publish a current named private-employer roster or a vacancy at each business. The Jobs screen can use these sources as a starting reference while the simulated world creates and changes its own organizations and openings. The owner has already asked for a varied, mostly ordinary job world with some full-time work and optional detail. The mix of real and generated employers remains unsettled.

## What the sources actually provide

| Source | Coverage as checked September 22, 2026 | Safe use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Census County Business Patterns](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/data.html) and its [2023 files](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2023/econ/cbp/2023-cbp.html) | Latest reference year is 2023; Census has no planned 2024 release date yet. State and county-equivalent industry, establishment, employment, and payroll counts cover the 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, American Samoa, Guam, Northern Mariana Islands, and U.S. Virgin Islands. | A nationwide, place-sensitive industry baseline. It is not a town business registry or a job-posting feed. [Methodology](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/methodology.html) excludes self-employment, most government work, agricultural production, railroads, and private households. Confidentiality rules prevent publishing an individual employer's operations. County totals cannot be assigned to one town without further evidence. |
| [Census LODES workplace jobs](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/lehd-code-samples/sections/lodes.html) and [current structure guide](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/doc/help/onthemap/LODESTechDoc.pdf) | Workplace block counts can be joined to place geography. The current Version 8.4 guide lists 2022–2023 coverage in 49 state/D.C. areas total: 48 states plus D.C., with Alaska and Michigan missing. Puerto Rico is not included; the guide's full universe is the 50 states plus D.C. | Useful where present for jobs by workplace rather than residents' jobs. It does not reveal the employer's name, vacancies, individual wages, or a specific person's job. Do not backfill missing states or territories with zero jobs. |
| [BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/oes/tables.htm) and [May 2025 technical note](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ocwage_05152026.pdf) | May 2025 occupation estimates cover the 50 states, D.C., Guam, Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands, and about 530 metropolitan or nonmetropolitan areas. American Samoa and Northern Mariana Islands are not listed in this release. | A wage and occupation reference, often at an area wider than a town. It is not the pay offered by a specific employer. The nonfarm wage-and-salary survey excludes self-employed people, most agricultural production, private households, and unpaid family workers. Its annual figure often represents 2,080 hours; it does not prove a job actually supplies those hours. |
| [BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages](https://www.bls.gov/cew/overview.htm) | Publishes covered employment and wage totals by county and industry, with more than 95% of U.S. jobs covered. | Cross-check county employment and wages. Suppression protects employer identities, so do not use it as a named employer list or vacancy feed. |
| [Census 2025 Government Units Listing](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2025/econ/gus/public-use-files.html) and [NCES school-district directory](https://nces.ed.gov/ccd/pubagency.asp) | Names actual state/local government units and public education agencies in their respective coverage. | Candidates for real public-organization identities after an exact jurisdiction and current-status match. A listed government or district does not establish an open job or a particular wage. Coverage of each territory's public bodies still needs a separate check. |

## What this settles for the requested Jobs design

- The [all-jurisdiction county industry extract](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1NG3w-Ukf-mIjYmGsOzbJVf2kD7O2B7in/view) now carries every published 2023 CBP county-equivalent total and major-sector row for the 50 states, D.C., and five inhabited territories. Its [readme](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1be1272-B_swuL0eXmsP4A05cPqiMAVwM/view) explains the 3,246 named county-equivalent GEOIDs, suppressed or absent cells, and unassigned `999` records. An independent agent compared all 55,093 rows with the official Census source ZIPs. This is a research artifact, not an imported game dataset.
- Industry mix can differ by place using national public data. CBP supplies a consistent fallback for every state, D.C., and five inhabited territories at county-equivalent scale. LODES can sharpen workplace geography where present. It cannot be the sole nationwide source because its recent state coverage has gaps and it lacks territorial data.
- Occupation and wage tables can support plausible ranges where the occupation, area, and covered worker type match. A published median is context, not the employer's offer. A listing should state its actual in-world pay and hours once those are established; it need not show a national median to the player.
- Named public bodies can be sourced. Named private employers and current vacancies are **UNKNOWN** from these statistical products. Generated private organizations are a design option, not a sourced claim that a particular real business exists.
- A county industry count, block job count, and area wage estimate have different denominators and vintages. Do not multiply them into an exact number of open positions. The game's opening supply, application outcome, start date, and abandoned-offer consequences remain simulation and owner decisions.

## Settled owner direction and remaining choices

In the supplied chat exports, the owner said job search should be a screen where a player can inspect location and pay without making ordinary work a substantial chore. He asked for more jobs than the same three everywhere, including roles open to younger or less educated people, jobs tied to credentials when needed, and real full-time possibilities. The repeated Neighborhood Market offer was rejected. Ordinary jobs should lead the list; political and campaign roles may arise naturally through a person's connections and community activity. Work and school are also settings for meaningful relationships. Work hours, pay, colleagues, family, schooling, and political activity should have real consequences. These are owner decisions, not deductions from the statistical sources.

The filed Jobs request still asks whether to use generated private employers, real named employers, sourced public bodies, or a mix; what exact listing fields and pay granularity to use; and how offers, applications, and starts work. The current decision log establishes canonical work and organization histories but does not settle those details. Before asking, compare candidate choices with the running decision ledger and past chats. Keep public, campaign, office, and union work connected to their existing world records rather than treating every job as a generic shift.

**Research limit:** The CBP source rows were extracted into a separate research file, but no game dataset was imported, no town-specific counts were computed, and no game changes or playtest were made. This is a national source map, with explicit jurisdiction gaps.

## Later owner decisions

The owner subsequently answered the remaining core Jobs choices. Use place-based generated private employers with verified public-body names; show the actual offer and place labor-market medians behind optional detail; quote pay in the terms of the job; allow both applications and relationship opportunities; and let a missed start prompt context-dependent follow-up or withdrawal. The [eight-point Jobs decision handoff](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1gOyHuRUGCYV-9U2OSwLkQ36T4pY3xdIl/view) carries the full answers and two explicitly fictional listing examples. Its decisions supersede the “remaining choices” paragraph above; source coverage and legal unknowns remain as stated.
