# American Samoa and CNMI need territory-specific job evidence

**Lead.** BLS OEWS and LODES do not supply the same occupation, wage, and workplace geography coverage for American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands as they do for states. Primary alternatives exist, but they answer different questions. Use Census Island Areas and employer-business products for their stated vintages and geographies; use CNMI's prevailing-wage study only for its survey scope and visa-wage purpose. No source here supports inventing town-level jobs or assigning a territory-wide rate to each town.

## BLS boundary and use

BLS describes OEWS as covering states, D.C., and selected territories. Its program page names participating workforce agencies in the states, D.C., Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Virgin Islands; it does not name American Samoa or CNMI. [OEWS overview](https://www.bls.gov/oes/oes_emp.htm). Do not fill either gap by copying Guam, Hawaii, or a national occupation wage as though it were locally measured.

For employer-industry structure, the Economic Census is the strongest shared source found: its 2022 Island Areas release explicitly covers both territories and reports establishments, employment, payroll, and receipts at published NAICS/geography levels. Do not convert total payroll divided by employment into an occupational wage. For American Samoa, use Census occupation/income tables as resident evidence and keep any local occupation-specific wage marked UNKNOWN unless a directly applicable local table is found. For CNMI, use PWS occupations and rates only with their reference period, aggregation, and prevailing-wage purpose attached.

The Census Bureau's Island Areas Economic Census states that local estimates are released only for covered NAICS/geographies meeting publication standards. It reports American Samoa districts and county/place equivalents, and CNMI municipalities, rather than arbitrary game towns. [Coverage and geography statement](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/economic-census-island-areas.html). The Census Bureau separately says American Samoa's 2020 social/economic census tables exclude group-quarters residents. [American Samoa cross-tabulation release](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/2020-island-areas-cross-tabulation-american-samoa.html).

**Still unknown:** current American Samoa occupation-specific wage distributions; whether yearbook labor tables provide a newer usable breakdown; whether 2026 PWS wage rates generalize beyond their prevailing-wage purpose; and any reliable mapping from these territory, island, municipality, district, or county figures to finer fictional towns.
