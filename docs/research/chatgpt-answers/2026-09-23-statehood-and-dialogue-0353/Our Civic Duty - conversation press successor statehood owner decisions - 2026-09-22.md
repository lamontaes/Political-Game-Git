# Four game decisions now have an owner answer

The owner answered four questions across conversations, press, succession, and statehood. Each answer keeps consequences tied to people and institutions already in the world. These are product directions for engineering and playtest design, not claims that current gameplay already delivers them. They should be added to the running decision record without reopening earlier answers about character knowledge, editor resistance, successor identity, or residents' views on statehood.

## Conversation: a listener can pass on private news

**Who and what.** When the player tells an existing person about a recorded event or plan, that person learns what was actually conveyed. The listener's reply and any later decision to tell someone else should follow their own knowledge, temperament, goals, relationship with the speaker, and situation. A listener may ask, help, disagree, stay quiet, or act later when the recorded context supports it. The owner selected **yes, with character-based consequences** for passing on a private story.

**When, where, and why.** The listener need not respond with a stock line immediately or pass every story along. A disclosure can remain between two people, circulate through a believable social path, or change a relationship when the recipient has reason to react. The player learns about later sharing only through an encounter, message, report, or other route their character could access. Mark a confidence or request for privacy as part of the encounter if it occurred; do not silently assume one. This answer settles permission for character-driven onward sharing, not those numerical limits.

## Press: fewer local reporters can mean fewer local stories

**Who and what.** A media owner may share a story among its outlets and may reduce a newsroom's staff. A shared piece should credit its originating newsroom. If local reporting capacity falls, the outlet may cover fewer local meetings, investigations, and everyday events; the lost coverage is a world consequence, not just a staffing number. The owner selected **yes, local coverage can shrink**. Editors and staff may resist directives with consequences, as the owner decided earlier.

**When, where, and why.** The effect belongs to each affected outlet and the places it covers. Its extent should follow the recorded staffing, beats, owner action, and editors' response; a cut does not automatically erase all local reporting. Local relevance still determines which shared stories appear to that audience. The filed [media-owner question](</Users/lamontae/.codex/attachments/62ad4279-a618-490b-85b0-4c74ffe6cc65/Pasted%20text.txt:535>) leaves share frequencies, newsroom merger rules, owner triggers, and purchase prices open for source work. Do not turn this answer into fixed percentages.

## Succession: playable younger people are eligible

**Who and what.** At a handoff, the player can use the connected-person shortlist or search for another existing eligible person in the world, as already decided. The owner now selected **allow playable younger people**. A child or teenager can be chosen if that person's age has a genuine playable route at that point in the game.

**When, where, and why.** Selection continues from that person's own history, home, relationships, schooling, work if any, and knowledge. Do not invent a spouse, job, or inherited friendships to fill a card. A younger person should experience age-appropriate agency and constraints through the applicable world rules. The new decision does not make an unsupported age route playable by label alone; verify the ordinary journey, Save/Continue, and return flow for each age band offered.

## Statehood: local opposition changes the fight

**Who and what.** Residents of D.C. and the inhabited territories hold individual, changeable views about statehood, as the owner already decided. The owner now selected **yes, opposition shapes the fight**. A divided or opposed local public does not automatically end a political effort; it changes organizing, local votes when applicable, public arguments, and congressional support.

**When, where, and why.** Each person's view can change through information, events, relationships, and politics. A statehood drive unfolds through the actual institutions and procedures represented for that place, with key decisions playable and routine steps summarized, as the owner previously chose. This answer is about political possibility and consequences. It does not assert that every territory uses the same referendum, that a local vote is legally unnecessary, or that Congress has already admitted a place. The [D.C. and territories source intake](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1iknvxQ7k4hxYppYeKUNLviZ7Ph316dlt/view) gives the current institutional baseline and marks place-specific legal steps for further research.
