# Census establishment-size data cover all 56 jurisdictions at state and county-equivalent levels

The best current nationwide source found for employer establishment size is Census County Business Patterns (CBP), reference year 2023. It offers size-by-industry counts for the 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and all four Island Areas, including county-equivalent files for the territories. The latest CBP page says 2023 remains the most current year and gives no planned 2024 release date while Census evaluates a replacement for its prior disclosure method. These are counts of establishments by size category, not named firms, vacancies, or a town-level employer list.

## Primary source: 2023 County Business Patterns

The [2023 CBP release page](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2023/econ/cbp/2023-cbp.html) publishes CSV files on June 26, 2025, with establishment counts, employment in the pay period including March 12, first-quarter and annual payroll. Separate state and county files cover the 50 states and D.C.; a state-equivalent Puerto Rico and Island Areas file and a county-equivalent file cover Puerto Rico, American Samoa, Guam, CNMI, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. The [record-layout page](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/record-layouts.html) confirms separate state-equivalent and county-equivalent layouts for these files. The [CBP methodology page](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/methodology.html) says state files report employment and payroll by employment size, while county and county-equivalent files report the number of establishments by size; county employment/payroll totals are available by industry, not split into size bands.

CBP's unit is the employer establishment, generally one physical business location. Its size category is based on the establishment's employment, not the parent firm's total employees. For a fictional employer profile, CBP can support a distribution of establishment counts by industry and employee-size band at a jurisdiction level. The county-equivalent counts should only be used as a reference where that geography matches the question. They cannot be divided among towns, converted into a specific town's vacancy count, or turned into named organizations.

CBP covers establishments with paid employees in most industries. It excludes self-employed people, private households, railroad employees, agricultural production, and most government employees. It also excludes some industries such as Public Administration (NAICS 92). It may classify employers without a fixed known county under a statewide category. These gaps mean CBP is a private-employer baseline rather than a complete inventory of every kind of work or organization. [CBP scope and geography](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/technical-documentation/methodology.html).

The current [CBP Data page](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/data.html) calls 2023 the latest data and says there is no planned date for the 2024 release while the Bureau evaluates alternatives after Department of Commerce Administrative Order DAO 216-26. The current methodology page warns that its detailed disclosure-method explanation is no longer current. A released 2023 table also carries a Disclosure Review Board approval number. Keep any source noise/publication indicators exactly as published, mark suppressed or absent cells unknown, and do not use residual subtraction to fill them. The current CBP glossary dates the order to June 4, 2026. [2023 data-product review statement](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2023/econ/cbp/2023-cbp.html); [current glossary](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/about/glossary.html).

The [2022 Economic Census of Island Areas](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/island-areas/2022.html) covers all five inhabited territories and reports size of establishments. Its geography includes American Samoa districts, CNMI and Guam municipalities, Puerto Rico municipios, and U.S. Virgin Islands islands; selected place-equivalent areas include American Samoa counties and U.S. Virgin Islands towns. Geographic and industry detail varies by product, and its published boundaries generally reflect January 1, 2022. This is a territory-specific cross-check, not a reason to downgrade the broader 2023 CBP coverage.

## Safe use in employer generation

- Use 2023 CBP to calibrate broad counts of establishment sizes by industry and county-equivalent geography. Treat categories as ranges, not individual employer headcounts.
- A game town is not automatically a county or municipality. Without a direct town measure, the town-level count, mix, and business names remain **UNKNOWN**; any generated town roster is a game choice that should use bounded inputs and retain its fictional status.
- CBP does not reveal any establishment's name, payroll, wage offer, open position, hiring probability, or the player's employer. Use a separate sourced government roster for names of public bodies, and separate in-world records for actual vacancies and offers.
- Keep the distinction between establishment employee-size and SUSB enterprise-size explicit. Do not combine their counts as if both described one-location employers.
- Keep agricultural producers, most public administration, self-employment, and other excluded categories outside a CBP-only roster. Add them only through a separate suitable source or an explicit bounded simulation profile.
- Preserve source flags and distinguish a suppressed/missing cell from a true zero. CBP size groups support distributions, not a permission to silently fill a missing town category.
