# Dialogue tree: what the full audit shows today

**Recommendation.** Treat the September 4 [full dialogue audit](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1hOZ-bE-_gB6fhqKvEFJ4biN47Fd3-LDeXATpBlDoRcs/edit) as a historical map, not a current player-route verdict. The game uses conditional conversation turns and later scenes rather than one fixed tree. The useful question for engineering is whether an ordinary player can start a conversation with the intended person, see context-appropriate choices and replies, and carry consequential facts into later play. The current source supports several connections, but no played-save walkthrough was run for this review.

Topics can include a greeting, a current scene or activity, a life event the character can share, a remembered exchange, news, a date where age and relationship permit it, or a specific proposal. Spending time follows an accepted proposal and costs 30 in-game minutes; ordinary reading and talk do not advance time. These are conditional paths, so there is no defensible single maximum branch depth from the static contracts. (`src/presentation/life-conversation.ts`, `src/player/SceneConversation.tsx`.)

## Owner direction that controls the next review

The [current decision register](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1bZWrzjUgDql2CIo_k1ElcQ2GrBZOzC_xJs8D-JswKpE/edit) and the owner's later answers supersede several easy fixes suggested by the old audit:

- A greeting may preserve familiarity and, in an exceptional admired or meaningful encounter, sustain or slightly increase trust. It is not a universal trust point.
- A later scene should return only when recorded history creates a meaningful relationship change, opportunity, or new decision. The owner rejected the blue-crayon and repeated-tag examples and asked to see revised broken-toy scenes individually before approval. Do not convert the old audit's “add one childhood callback” recommendation into a content quota.
- People have their own intentions, knowledge, schedules, and boundaries. The owner wants contacts to initiate relevant calls, invitations, or requests and to pass on private information when their character gives them a reason. A conversation choice cannot guarantee the other person's compliance.
- Player-selected intent and consequential wording must be clear before commitment. A deliberate lie has a visible Lie marker and must bind an actual claim and the speaker's knowledge; ordinary statements must not secretly become lies. Information reaches only plausible listeners and later recipients.
- In-game time belongs to the committed activity. Browsing and dialogue presentation add no separate time charge. The player may read another menu and return to a pending exchange without losing or submitting it. Condensing a conversation for convenience is not an in-world refusal or a hidden penalty.
- Natural American English and facts the character could know govern the displayed lines. A record-rich conversation is not sufficient if every reply sounds interchangeable or tells the player a consequence that has not occurred.

## Focused player acceptance still needed

Run one fresh ordinary life in a newly chosen non-Kentucky place and a second contrasting life elsewhere, without developer routes or manually planted scene bindings. In each, try a conversation with a known person and one with a person met through an actual introduction. Check the selected addressee, available topics, what the other person knows, normal/quiet/private listeners where the setting permits, exact response, time advanced, any promise or relationship change, a later return if one is justified, and save/Continue. Include a normal greeting, a meaningful admired greeting, a private story that may later spread, and a proposal the other person can decline. Record unavailable paths as unavailable rather than manufacturing a scene to satisfy the test.
