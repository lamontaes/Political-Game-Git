# U.S. gubernatorial election calendar: national research snapshot

**Retrieved:** 2026-09-22. **Scope:** 50 states, District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico. This is a legal research snapshot, not a game rule approval. Election cycles below mean regular election years (not special elections); a listed reference year plus cycle length defines the series. “November federal general” means the Tuesday after the first Monday in November. Evidence status distinguishes directly located current official text from an official portal/locator that still needs the operative provision checked before compiling a verified runtime row. A term-start date prefixed UNVERIFIED is a research lead, not a legal rule or approved runtime value.

## Exceptions, current-law cautions, and game assumptions

- **Only Washington is a verified runtime row today.** The repository's `src/simulation/nationwide-world/state-executive-term-rules.ts` contains one fully sourced state row (WA). Other states currently receive the disclosed game profile: four years, election cycle anchored in 2026, November federal general, and first Monday of January. That profile is expressly a simulation assumption, not a claim of state law.
- **Odd-year gubernatorial elections:** Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Jersey, and Virginia. New Hampshire and Vermont have two-year terms and therefore elections every two years. Regular election cycles for other states are generally every four years.
- **Louisiana has a distinct election calendar.** Its official election statute sets a fall primary on the third-to-last Saturday in October of a gubernatorial election year and a fall general six Saturdays later if required, subject to prohibited-day shifts. Check the Secretary of State calendar for actual dates. The 2026 Act 414 amendment is about governor term limits, not calendar timing; its ratification has no bearing on this row.
- **D.C. has no governor.** Its mayor is the comparable local executive included because the question expressly invited D.C.; do not call the mayor a governor.
- **Puerto Rico has a governor** and belongs in the same comparative table, while its institutional status differs from a state. The English constitution portal is a convenience; confirm against the Spanish controlling text and current election statute for runtime evidence.
- **Election date nuance:** the conventional November federal-general rule has statutory/constitutional grounding in most states; Louisiana’s election is conducted under its distinct state primary/election system. Except for Louisiana’s necessary primary/general distinction, primary procedures, recounts, vacancy elections, and transition-only terms are outside the regular-cycle rows.
- **Vintage and evidence limitation:** retrieval date is 2026-09-22, but many state portals provide continuously updated text without a dated consolidated edition. The older effective/vintage date is supplied where the repository's recovered legal ledger already records one (NM, NY, NC, ND, OH, OK, OR, PA, RI, SC). For the remaining official portals, the table gives retrieval vintage, not the effective date of each clause. Rows marked “locator” still need provision-level transcription, amendment-history review, and independent election-calendar corroboration before promotion to `verified`. This is a complete 52-jurisdiction research inventory, but not a claim that all 52 rows have passed that verification gate.

## Broad secondary cross-check (not controlling law)

NCSL is used only as a cross-check here; each row links to an official source locator. This pass has not verified every row's precise commencement formula from its operative clause, so the evidence-status column must travel with this table.
