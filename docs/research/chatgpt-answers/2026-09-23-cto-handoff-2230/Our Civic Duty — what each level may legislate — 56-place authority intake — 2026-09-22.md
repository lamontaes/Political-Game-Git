# What each level of government may legislate: 56-place authority intake

**September 22, 2026.** This single nationwide research intake covers Congress and all 50 states, D.C. and five inhabited territories. It identifies cited authority for each legislature and the state or territorial basis for local lawmaking where found. **UNKNOWN** marks a provision, preemption, act form or remedy that this pass did not verify; it is not permission or a prohibition. Local powers vary by charter and statutory form. This does not yet supply every subject-specific preemption or the current answer to every game policy question. Those remain separate research tasks.

## Owner decisions and use in play

- A governing body can propose and decide bills under its actual legal authority. Governors and legislators pursue regular agendas driven by circumstances without requiring the player to initiate every proposal. [Owner agenda decision and long-run diagnostic](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rDVcArflTULVqIYv2n9NuOJdEIIiJ1RU/view).
- Where a local proposal may exceed state authority, an official can warn the player; a plausible question can lead to a lawful challenge. A clear settled prohibition prevents enactment. Passage first changes a concrete law or budget record; people and the economy respond when they encounter the rule. [Owner legislation decisions](https://drive.google.com/file/d/121zo2UJNzKqOFYY7_Ak1-YQAT5tZABrQ/view).
- A source describing a category of local government does not settle the authority of every city, town or county in that state. Resolve the actual jurisdiction, charter, law effective on the game date, and federal/state hierarchy before an action is treated as legally effective.
- The common act/effect record below is a recommended game schema. It does not substitute for each jurisdiction's legal effective date, procedure, tax rate or budget cycle.

## Federal lawmaking and act effects

### Federal row — legal baseline

**Authority.** Article I, Section 8 empowers Congress to tax and spend for debts, defense, and general welfare; borrow on U.S. credit; and regulate foreign, interstate, and tribal commerce. It can establish uniform naturalization and bankruptcy rules, coin money, set weights/measures, punish counterfeiting, and establish post offices and roads. Congress can secure copyrights and patents, create lower federal courts, and define or punish maritime and international offenses. It can declare war, raise and support armies and a navy, govern the armed forces and militia, govern the federal district and federal property, and enact laws necessary and proper to execute these and other constitutionally vested federal powers. The Constitution also grants enforcement powers in later amendments. The annotated Constitution describes Congress as lacking plenary legislative power. [Article I, Section 8](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-1/); [Necessary and Proper Clause doctrine](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S8-C18-3-3/ALDE_00001240/); [Article I overview](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-1/ALDE_00000242/).

**Act forms and procedure.** Congress makes general federal law through bills that pass both chambers and are presented to the President; signature, a veto override by two-thirds of each chamber, or the applicable ten-day presentment rule can complete enactment. Revenue-raising bills originate in the House, though the Senate may amend them. Appropriations and tax provisions are statutes, not distinct constitutional enactment tracks. Joint resolutions can become law through the same process; simple and concurrent resolutions do not become federal statutes. An Article V amendment proposal is a separate constitutional process, not an ordinary statute. “Federal ordinance” is not an ordinary act form for Congress. [Article I, Section 7](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-1/); [Origination Clause](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S7-C1-1/ALDE_00013355/); [Article V](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/).

**Material limits.** Congress must act within an enumerated or otherwise constitutionally vested power, using appropriate means under the Necessary and Proper Clause. It is bound by express constitutional prohibitions and individual rights, including Article I, Section 9 limits (for example, no bill of attainder or ex post facto law; restrictions on direct taxes and export taxes; no Treasury withdrawals without appropriations) and applicable amendments. The Tenth Amendment reserves powers not delegated to the United States or prohibited to the states. The Supreme Court determines constitutional validity in cases; an unconstitutional enactment is not reliably modeled as self-erasing at passage. [Article I, Sections 8–9](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-1/); [Tenth Amendment](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/amendment-10/).

**Federal/state hierarchy.** The Supremacy Clause makes the Constitution, valid federal laws made pursuant to it, and treaties supreme over contrary state law. Preemption can be express or implied; implied preemption includes field and conflict forms (impossibility or obstacle). A federal law beyond Congress’s constitutional authority is not a valid supreme law merely because enacted. [Supremacy Clause and modern preemption doctrine](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artVI-C2-3-4/ALDE_00013402/).

**State constraints relevant to hierarchy.** Article I, Section 10 bars states from treaties, coining money, bills of credit, bills of attainder, ex post facto laws, and impairment of contract obligations. It limits import/export duties, tonnage duties, peacetime troops, interstate/foreign compacts, and war absent specified conditions or congressional consent. This is a federal baseline only. It does not determine each state’s local-government doctrine or subject-specific preemption. [Article I, Section 10](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-1/).

**D.C. and territories.** Congress has exclusive legislative authority over the federal district under Article I, Section 8, Clause 17, subject to the constitutional structure and the Home Rule Act’s delegation of local self-government. The current D.C. Code bars Council action outside the Act’s limits, including certain matters concerning federal functions/property and matters not confined to D.C. Most Council acts transmitted to Congress take effect after the defined 30-day review period (calendar days with listed exclusions), unless Congress enacts a joint resolution of disapproval; acts codified in Titles 22, 23, or 24 use a 60-day period. The operative mechanism requires passage by both Houses and presentment to the President, not a one-house veto. Emergency acts and statutory exceptions exist; check the act-specific rule. Congress also retains ultimate authority to legislate for D.C. [Article I, Section 8, Clause 17](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-1/); [D.C. Code § 1-206.02](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-206.02); [D.C. Code § 1-206.01](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/1-206.01).

Congress has constitutional authority to dispose of and make rules for U.S. territory under Article IV, Section 3, Clause 2. The exact governing instruments, delegated powers, constitutional application, and congressional review/override mechanics differ by territory. **UNKNOWN here:** territory-by-territory authority and constraints for Puerto Rico, Guam, U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and Northern Mariana Islands; do not infer them from D.C. or one another. [Article IV, Section 3](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-4/).

### Cross-level act/effect record — recommended game schema

Keep enacted authority separate from a later implementation, transaction, or experienced outcome. Each instrument record should identify its jurisdiction, level, kind, local name, subject/policy IDs, legal authority, status, votes and assent or override, enactment date, effective date or condition, duration or repeal, source citation, and provision version. Resolve authority and preemption before treating a proposal as valid law. Where authority is genuinely disputed, warn the player and allow a plausible legal challenge under the owner's decision. A clear settled prohibition blocks enactment. Return unknown when source evidence is missing.

| Kind | What enactment changes in the canonical record | Later record of experienced effect |
|---|---|---|
| Statute / ordinance | Enacted legal rule, scope, affected policy, effective date, administering body, eligibility/duties/prohibitions and penalties; ordinance is local/state-law dependent, not a federal Congress instrument. | Each authorized implementation, compliance event, permit/benefit/service, enforcement/citation/adjudication, and downstream measured outcome, linked to the controlling provision. A rule’s enactment alone does not assert compliance or outcome. |
| Appropriation | Legal budget authority: amount/ceiling, account/recipient or purpose, fiscal period, availability, conditions, and effective date. Federal appropriations authorize obligations/payments; they are not cash already spent. | Budget obligation, award/contract/payment/outlay, recipient and date; service delivery and outcomes are further events. [GAO appropriations-law guide](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-464sp.pdf). |
| Tax levy / tax statute | Tax rule: liable class, base, rate/formula, exemptions/credits, collecting authority, tax period, due date, and effective date. Federal revenue bills originate in House. | Accrued liability for a person/entity and taxable period; assessment, withholding, invoice, payment, refund, or delinquency are separate records. Revenue received updates receipts only when collected. |
| Borrowing authorization | Legal debt authority and limits: issuer, principal ceiling, instrument, permitted purpose, maturity/term, rate or pricing rule, repayment source, and effective window. | Actual issuance/closing creates debt principal and terms; later draw, interest accrual/payment, and principal repayment are separate cash/debt events. |
| Constitutional amendment | Proposal and ratification status. A congressional proposal alone does not change constitutional rules; under Article V it becomes part of the Constitution upon ratification by three-fourths of states through the mode Congress specifies, subject to the equal-suffrage protection. | At constitutional effectiveness, update the controlling constitutional provision/version; downstream statutory changes, official action, and lived effects remain separately recorded. |

For federal appropriations, Article I, Section 9, Clause 7 supplies the Treasury-draw restriction; GAO explains that appropriations generally authorize obligations and payments within purpose, time, and amount limits, rather than representing cash already disbursed. For all jurisdictions, specific effective-date and finance mechanics remain **UNKNOWN until sourced per jurisdiction and instrument**. The game may use this schema as a shared record shape, but should apply jurisdiction-specific legal rules and actual effects rather than treating passage as the effect itself.

It must not enact a local or special act where a general act can apply and must not grant irrevocable special/exclusive privileges (art. II, § 24) ([Revised Constitution, American Samoa Bar Association legal library](https://asbar.org/revised-constitution-of-american-samoa/)). Congress retains territorial authority under [U.S. Const. art. IV, § 3, cl. 2](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-4/). Village/county councils are consulted in appointments, but authority for these councils to enact laws: UNKNOWN. Specific preemptions and ultra vires procedure: UNKNOWN.

### Northern Mariana Islands

Commonwealth legislation is bounded by the Covenant, applicable U.S. Constitution and federal law, and CNMI Constitution. The CNMI Legislature's legislative power is vested in its two houses (CNMI Const. art. II, § 1; [CNMI legal resources](https://cnmilaw.org/)). The Constitution provides for legislative delegations for local legislative functions; their subjects depend on grants defined by the Commonwealth Legislature (art. II, § 6; 1 CMC § 1402). Official CNMI legal publication describes the Local Law Act's definition of exclusively local matters and the requirement for a separate enabling grant if the matter is outside the defined set ([CNMI official legal publication discussing 1 CMC § 1402](https://www.cnmilaw.org/pdf/cnmiregister/2004_Volume_26/2004_Number_03.pdf)). Actual local legislative delegations are district-specific; a universal municipal/county ordinance power cannot be asserted. Federal Covenant limits apply, including applicable federal supremacy; details per subject: UNKNOWN. Specific ultra vires procedure: UNKNOWN.
