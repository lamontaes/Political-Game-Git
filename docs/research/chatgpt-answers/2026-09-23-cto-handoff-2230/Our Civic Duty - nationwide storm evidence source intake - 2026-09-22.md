# Storm evidence sources: weather, experience, damage, and declarations are separate

**Lead.** Official data can establish observed conditions, NWS alerts and reported storm events, estimated impacts, and federal disaster declarations. Those records do not prove the same thing. A weather observation is not proof a character noticed a storm; a storm report is not automatically local damage; damage is not a federal declaration. No nationwide dataset measures whether each town resident personally experienced a storm.

## Source map

| Evidence tier | Primary source and useful fields | What it supports | Coverage and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background conditions | NOAA/NWS ASOS observations and NCEI climate records: temperature, wind speed/direction/gusts, precipitation type/amount, visibility, pressure, observation time and station. [ASOS overview](https://www.weather.gov/asos); [ASOS reports](https://www.weather.gov/asos/InformationReporting.html). | Observed weather at a named station and time; repeated daily observations can establish local weather context. | About 950 ASOS stations, primarily at airports. Stations are not every neighborhood or town. The data do not establish who noticed, felt, or discussed the conditions. A missing observation is not proof of calm weather. |
| Forecasts and warnings | NWS API and CAP alerts: alert event, headline, affected area/zone, onset, expiration, urgency, severity, certainty, instructions and description. [NWS API](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api); [NWS alerts API details](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-alerts). | A warning/advisory or forecast was issued for a defined area and period. Could support evidence that a character might receive a warning if the game establishes access. | Alerts communicate forecast risk and public advice; they do not prove the weather occurred, that a person received the alert, or that damage followed. CAP alert archive availability has limits; API `/alerts` only carries the past seven days. |
| Reported storm event / possible experience | NOAA/NCEI Storm Events Database (SED): event and episode IDs/narratives, event type, source, county/forecast zone/marine area, beginning/end time, location/coordinates, magnitude, injuries/deaths, property/crop damage estimates. [SED user guide](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/storm-events-database/assets/pdf/Storm_Events_Database_User_Guide.pdf); [bulk CSV field guide](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/swdi/stormevents/csvfiles/Storm-Data-Bulk-csv-Format.pdf); [database](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/stormevents/). | NWS-reported significant weather phenomena. The event narrative/source/time/location may establish a local report and help identify candidate events worth investigating. | Period begins in 1950, but event types and reporting practices vary over time; event types are limited before 1993 and entries can take about three months to appear. Small-area events may be county-coded; larger events use forecast zones. Multiple rows can belong to one episode or affect multiple areas. Crucially, NWS guidance says reports with no impact or no damage may still be included. A record is not proof the whole town or a given person felt a storm. |
| Damage and response | SED has estimated property/crop damage and direct/indirect casualties, with source and narrative. For formal local/state impact, use documented local emergency and damage-assessment records where available; no single nationwide local-response registry was verified here. | A nonblank damage estimate or narrative can support reported impact at its stated geography. FEMA preliminary damage assessment / request documents can support assessed impact for a declaration request. | SED estimates have accuracy disclaimers; blank means not reported/available, not zero. A county total cannot be assigned to one town or household. SED is not a complete inventory of all property damage or local government actions. Local response datasets and event-to-address matching remain UNKNOWN. |
| Declared disaster | FEMA OpenFEMA Disaster Declarations Summaries: disaster number, declaration type, incident type/begin date, declaration date, designated county/area, and program flags for Individual, Individuals and Households, and Public Assistance. [Dataset and field definitions](https://www.fema.gov/about/openfema/disaster-declarations-summaries); [FEMA declaration process](https://www.fema.gov/disaster/how-declared). | A specific federal emergency, major disaster, or fire-management assistance declaration and its designated areas/programs. | A declaration is a legal/administrative decision after a request or other applicable process; FEMA considers severity, state/local capacity, damage assessments, and assistance factors. Many damaging or locally declared incidents receive no federal declaration. No federal declaration does not mean no storm, felt experience, damage, or local response. Program flags and area designations matter; the declaration does not automatically cover every resident or type of loss. |

## Distinctions and UNKNOWNs

- **Observed weather:** measured at a station, not necessarily across a whole town.
- **Felt storm:** no official national measure identifies which residents perceived or discussed each weather event. Character-level experience is **UNKNOWN** unless an event, location, exposure, or testimony supplies it.
- **Damage:** SED has reported estimates and narratives, but no verified national mapping from each report to a town parcel, household, or government response. Treat blank estimate as **UNKNOWN**, not zero.
- **Disaster declaration:** FEMA records the federal declaration and designated scope, not a universal severity threshold for all storms. Local/state emergency declarations and local response are separate evidence and require jurisdiction-specific sources.
- **Timing:** Storm Events has event and episode dates/times; event occurrence is not necessarily a single townwide day. A fixed first-of-month date is not supported by these sources.

## Research limits

Primary sources checked September 22, 2026. NOAA and NWS provide the condition, alert, and storm-report sources. FEMA provides federal-declaration records and process materials. No empirical threshold was found that converts a county/zone report into a locally felt storm or converts a specific damage amount into a local or federal disaster. Numerical tier cutoffs, affected-resident counts, town-level damage allocation, local-response coverage, and character awareness therefore remain **UNKNOWN**.
