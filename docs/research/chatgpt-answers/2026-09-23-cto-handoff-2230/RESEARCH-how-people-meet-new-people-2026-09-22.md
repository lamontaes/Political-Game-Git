# People meet through shared settings; the evidence does not set an annual rate

**Lead.** The evidence supports letting new contacts emerge from settings a person actually shares, with optional player action and autonomous contact. It does not support a fixed number of new acquaintances per year. Record a first meeting as an acquaintance; let conduct and temperament shape whether the relationship deepens. This follows the owner's decision that proximity creates candidates, not bonds.

## Recommended rule boundary

- **Owner choice:** Shared settings create possible contacts. The people’s actions and personalities determine their relationship. A greeting may sustain or improve trust when the context supports it; a greeting alone does not create automatic friendship. People may initiate contact.
- **Research-grounded behavior:** Use eligibility and actual shared events as exposure triggers. Do not create a contact solely because two people live in the same town, attend the same large institution, or belong to the same broad category.
- **Calibration:** Keep annual encounter counts, chance per interaction, personality modifiers, effects of moving/place size, and conversion from acquaintance to trust **UNKNOWN** until directly measured or explicitly labeled as a game calibration. The cited surveys report accumulated ties or participation, not per-year encounter rates.
- **First meeting:** Recommended record: two people became acquainted, with date, setting, and introducer if applicable. Do not store friendship, trust, or romantic interest without subsequent evidence. This follows the owner's stated rule and is a design boundary, not an empirical estimate.

## Evidence limits

The 2021 Survey Center survey was a probability-based U.S. adult panel (2,019 adults, interviewed May 14–23, 2021); it was conducted during the pandemic and measured close-friend venues cumulatively. [Methodology](https://www.americansurveycenter.org/research/the-state-of-american-friendship-change-challenges-and-loss/). Pew's teen reports are surveys from 2014–15 and 2018, so their online findings are not current platform benchmarks. The BLS American Time Use Survey measures activity time and participation, not the formation of relationships. The research supplies valid setting candidates and broad differences by age or participation, but no defensible annual NPC-meeting count.

The design boundary follows DECISION-LOG D-092: proximity produces candidates, not bonds. The queue entry `how-people-meet-new-people` also says first contact should be an acquaintance and that the pace must not be invented as fact.
