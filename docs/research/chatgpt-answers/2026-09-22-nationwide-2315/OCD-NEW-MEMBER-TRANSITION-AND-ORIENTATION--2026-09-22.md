# New member transition and orientation research

Research date: 2026-09-22. Scope follows `new-member-transition-and-orientation` in `OPEN-QUESTIONS-2026-09-22T2115Z-9540769a.md`.

**Owner decisions received 2026-09-22:** Every certified winner gets a short transition appropriate to the office and local evidence. Where a formal program is documented, it may appear by name; elsewhere, offer a modest staff briefing without presenting it as a real academy. Playable choices emphasize staff and political priorities, while routine access, forms and orientation are summarized. The transition does not grant office powers before their lawful start.

## Findings at a glance

There is no uniform transition/orientation program across U.S. elected office. Congress has recognizable recurring programs. State legislative onboarding varies by state and cycle. Governor-elect transition teams are commonly assembled by the winner, but the examples reviewed do not establish a universal state-administered program. Local onboarding ranges from a city-specific executive/council curriculum to state association or university training, sometimes legally required for a particular office.

For an ordinary general-election cycle, Congress's transition spans the interval from November election through January 3 seating. House orientation has a documented 119th Congress session on November 20, 2024. Senate orientation is typically several days in November and coordinated with party leadership elections; that is a standing historical description, not proof every class had an identical agenda. Members-elect may do party and committee/caucus activity in the interval. They do not yet exercise legislative power: the Senate requires credentials and oath before senators can exercise responsibilities.

The source record supports administrative setup, ethics and rules briefings, hiring preparation, and office assignment/selection as plausible transition activities. It does not support giving every winner a generic cash hiring bonus. House's Members' Representational Allowance (MRA) and Senate's SOPOEA are official annual operating accounts with their own rules, and regular-election House member pay/allowances begin with the new Congress. A game should avoid implying unrestricted pre-seating personal funds.

## Federal Congress

### House

- **Program and timing:** House Committee on Ethics' activity record says it trained 136 Members-elect and incoming staff at the New Member Orientation Program on **November 20, 2024**, for the 119th Congress. This is direct cycle-specific evidence. The 119th Congress began January 3, 2025. [House Ethics activity record](https://cha.house.gov/?File_id=E5463A1C-B631-4A66-93C5-DEA05A8226D7&a=Files.Serve); [House historical Congress dates](https://history.house.gov/Institution/Saturday-Sunday/110-Current/).
- **Coverage:** The ethics committee's 2024 report says it presented to Members-elect during orientation; the 2024 session involved incoming staff too. Current Ethics training guidance requires new Members, officers and employees to receive training within 60 days of their start date. Thus an orientation presentation is an early briefing, while the formal deadline is after service begins. [2024 activity report](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Committee-Report.pdf); [current training guidance](https://ethics.house.gov/training/).
- **Administrative setup:** CAO's report for July–December 2024 says House Web Services and the Committee on House Administration redesigned the New Member Orientation website for the 119th transition; 65 new Members selected website themes at orientation and websites were ready on day one. It also says Members-elect received SalarySense benchmark information during orientation for staffing decisions. [CAO semiannual report, covering July–December 2024](https://cao.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/cao.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/semiannual_july-december-2024_2025.2.25_digital.pdf); [House Administration handbooks](https://cha.house.gov/handbooks-and-rules).
- **Office selection:** The House History, Art & Archives account describes the office lottery as a numbered drawing that determines selection order among available rooms, with seniority affecting which rooms remain available. This is a longstanding practice description, not a 119th-specific schedule or guarantee that every incoming Member receives the same kind of room. [House office lottery history](https://history.house.gov/Blog/2019/January/1-10-officelottery/).
- **Hiring / resources:** Current House materials establish that staffing is governed by the MRA and Members' Handbook. The MRA is an official representational operating allowance, not personal income. The publicly accessible handbook is the current Committee on House Administration download; its public page is not itself a transition itinerary. The CAO report documents orientation access to staff salary benchmarks, not a separate start-up grant. [Members' Handbook index](https://cha.house.gov/handbooks-and-rules); [House Ethics manual on employment and MRA](https://ethics.house.gov/manual/general-employment-and-compensation-provisions/).
- **Ethics:** House Rule XI requires new Members to complete ethics training within 60 days after commencement of service; the current online program states the same for new employees. The gift rule applies to Members-elect when pay and allowances begin; for a regular-election winner, that is at start of new Congress, per House Ethics guidance. [Training](https://ethics.house.gov/training/); [gift-rule guidance](https://ethics.house.gov/manual/who-is-subject-to-the-gift-rule/).
- **Limit:** A detailed, public 119th orientation agenda/handbook was not located in the sources reviewed. Do not claim a complete official curriculum from these materials. The House public handbook page carries current operating rules, not necessarily the closed Members-elect orientation portal.

### Senate

- **Program and timing:** Senate Historical Office says post-election orientation programs familiarize newly elected Senators with procedure and traditions. It says they **typically** last several days in November and coincide with party leadership elections; presenters may include party floor leaders, senators from the prior freshman class, Senate officers, parliamentarians, security experts, curators, and historians. Typical topics include procedure, setting up a new office, and Senate life. Each party also holds its own briefings/retreats. This is a general institutional account, not an exact 119th class agenda. [Senate New Senator Orientation](https://www.senate.gov/about/traditions-symbols/orientation-programs.htm).
- **Ethics:** Senate Ethics Manual (S. Pub. 108-1, an older publication) says administrative questions for Senators-elect are usually addressed in a comprehensive orientation shortly after the general election and that part of the orientation discusses ethics law, Senate ethics rules, and committee functions. Because this is an older manual, use it as evidence of the established practice, not as a current 2026 syllabus. Current Senate Ethics Committee resources should govern any specific rules claim. [Senate Ethics Manual](https://www.ethics.senate.gov/public/index.cfm/senate-ethics-manual).
- **Office assignment:** Senate office placement should not be modeled as the House-style freshman office lottery without more evidence. Senate.gov says the Senate is a continuous body and office assignment uses an order-of-service system; party conferences organize before the new Congress. That system is different from the House's random number drawing. [Senate FAQ: New Congress](https://www.senate.gov/general/common/generic/NewCongress_faq.htm).
- **Hiring:** Senate Employment Office currently states that every two years, beginning in mid-November of even-numbered election years, it helps new Senate offices fill staff vacancies and provides senators-elect resume collection information after the general election. Each office retains its hiring process and structure. This supports early staff recruitment, not an identical centrally assigned staff complement. [Senate Employment FAQ](https://employment.senate.gov/senate-employment/); [Senate Office Opportunities](https://employment.senate.gov/senate-office-opportunities/).
- **Operating funds:** Senate staffing and office expenses are funded through SOPOEA. The official FY2024 Senate Appropriations report describes it as funding salaries/benefits and office expenses, with administrative/clerical allowance varying by state population. It is a dated appropriation reference, so do not reuse its dollar values as 2026 values. No source reviewed establishes a personal hiring allowance paid to a Senator-elect. [Senate Appropriations FY2024 Legislative Branch report](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fy24_legislative_branch_report.pdf).
- **Authority begins at oath:** Senate.gov says senators-elect must present their certificate and take the oath before exercising legislative responsibilities. [Senate rules and procedure](https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/senate-stories/rules-and-procedure.htm).

## State legislature examples

These illustrate patterns rather than a national standard.

| State / source vintage | Observed pattern | What is grounded |
|---|---|---|
| Maine, 132nd Legislature / 2024–25 | Multi-stage state-run sequence: New Member Orientation November 19–21, 2024; Pre-Legislative Conference December 2–5; further policy forums and a January economic-development bus tour. | Official member resources list orientation materials and separate conference/training. State schedule says eligible newly elected lawmakers may be reimbursed for travel/lodging for the orientation and conference after taking office on December 4. That is expense reimbursement, not a general stipend. [Member resources](https://legislature.maine.gov/9686); [orientation schedule](https://www.legislature.maine.gov/doc/11258). |
| Montana, 2025 session | One-day official orientation on November 12, 2024, plus caucuses, leadership election, mentor-group introduction, IT essentials, official-business tools (e-signatures, badges, forms/keys), and other scheduled sessions. | The published 2025 agenda gives concrete components. It documents a mentor and practical onboarding model, but not a longer program for all years. [2025 orientation agenda](https://archive.legmt.gov/content/For-Legislators/orientation/2025/FINALAgenda_25Orientation.pdf); [official training page](https://www.legmt.gov/support/training/). |
| Kansas | Statutory model: orientation meetings for members-elect are provided on day(s) after a preorganizational meeting as set by Legislative Coordinating Council; party leaders may convene conferences. Law provides compensation, expenses and allowances for attendance under the referenced law. | This is a legally structured program. The linked statute is the 2023 codification, so confirm current codification before implementing dollar/rate claims. [Kansas Statute 46-146a (2023)](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li_2024s/b2023_24/statute/046_000_0000_chapter/046_001_0000_article/046_001_0046a_section/046_001_0046a_k/). |
| North Dakota, 2025 legislative committee review | A legislative committee report characterizes the existing orientation as brief, intermittent within organizational session, provided by Legislative Council and caucuses; it recommends a phased multi-day curriculum and cites Nevada (three phases/eight days across Nov., Dec., Jan.) and Ohio (three days beginning in November) as examples. | This is a report and recommendation, not itself proof of those other legislatures' detailed current itineraries. Nevada/Ohio examples need direct source confirmation before exact implementation. It is useful evidence that legislators themselves see major variation. [ND Legislative Procedure and Arrangements Committee final report, 2025](https://ndlegis.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/committees/69-2025/27.5136.02000presentation145finalreport.pdf). |

**Common pattern visible in this sample:** some body or staff welcomes incoming legislators before session; useful content includes ethics/open-records, legislative process, IT/access, office services, committee/leadership organization, and peer mentorship. Timing ranges from post-election November through December/January, while some training is folded into organizational session. This small purposive sample cannot establish how common any component is nationwide.

## Governor-elect examples

- **Washington, 2024:** Governor-elect Bob Ferguson announced a bipartisan transition team on November 12, one week after Election Day. The team's stated work included developing first-100-day budget/policy plans, service-delivery review, and identifying/vetting cabinet and administration personnel. This is a campaign/elect transition organization preparing an incoming administration, not a state legislature orientation. [Governor-elect announcement](https://governor.wa.gov/news/2024/governor-elect-bob-ferguson-announces-transition-team).
- **West Virginia, 2024:** Contemporary reporting described Governor-elect Patrick Morrisey naming transition co-leads, meeting lawmakers, and working on a common agenda before a January 13, 2025 swearing-in. This is secondary reporting, so it should be treated as an example only; an official transition source was not located in this pass. [AP report, Nov. 12, 2024](https://apnews.com/article/1ecdbec0f5bda56f7876661c8147e377).

These examples support that a governor-elect may create an ad hoc transition team and prepare appointee/priority plans. Sources reviewed do not establish a uniform state-run governor orientation, standard transition allowance, guaranteed office/staff allotment, or identical incumbent-to-incoming briefing process. Code “transition team” as conditional, not universal.

## Local patterns and examples

1. **City-specific integrated onboarding — Portland, Oregon (2024–25).** The mayor-elect and councilors-elect took part in sessions on government fundamentals, public meetings law, budget process, team building, and civic leadership before the new council's January 2, 2025 first meeting. The plan continued with work sessions, briefings, and tours of city-owned assets. This was designed around Portland's new form of government and is a strong example of bespoke transition. [City of Portland onboarding announcement, Dec. 9, 2024](https://www.portland.gov/transition/news/2024/12/9/portlands-incoming-elected-officials-begin-onboarding).
2. **State technical-assistance service / recurring cohort — Tennessee county officials (2026).** University of Tennessee CTAS runs a County Officials Orientation Program for newly elected and re-elected officials, with office authority, oaths/bonds, deadlines, ethics/conflicts, open meetings/records, personnel, budgets and purchasing. County commissioners separately have statutory orientation within 120 days after election/appointment, plus eight hours annual continuing education. This is unusually formal and should not be generalized to every county or official. [2026 COOP](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/training/coop2026); [commissioner training rule](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/training/commissioner-training).
3. **Association/university professional-development pattern — Michigan county commissioners.** Michigan State University Extension and the Michigan Association of Counties offer a New Commissioner School in November/December after county elections, including an in-person day, with a self-paced option for midterm entrants. [MSU Extension New Commissioner School](https://www.canr.msu.edu/new_county_commissioner_training).

The reviewed local sources are strong for cities/counties but do not establish one national municipal orientation program. Many jurisdictions may provide only clerk/manager briefings, a handbook, an oath and access setup; where no official/local program is documented, code “uncoded local onboarding” and use a small generic briefing sequence rather than asserting a formal academy.

## Evidence boundary and design recommendations

### Facts supported

- Transition is an interval after election and before authority begins; it can include party organization and logistics.
- The House has a November member-elect orientation, and Senate orientation typically spans several November days.
- Ethics, legislative procedure, administrative setup, hiring preparation, office assignment, technology/access, and peer mentorship are all documented components in at least some programs.
- House and Senate office allocation differ; House has a lottery tradition, Senate uses order of service for assignments.
- State and local practices vary substantially. Some state/local programs are recurring or statutory; some are ad hoc or city-specific.
- Election winners' authority begins at the legal oath/term date. House ethics guidance clarifies when pay/allowances start for regular-election Members; this should not be translated into unrestricted pre-service personal cash.

### Game options (recommendation, not fact)

**Recommended baseline:** after a certified win, open a short transition phase with three to five optional actions: attend official orientation; set up access/office; hire or shortlist staff; attend ethics/administrative briefing; meet caucus/mentor/constituents. Show a countdown to the jurisdiction's legal seating date. Keep legislating and exercising official powers locked until that date.

- **Congress-specific depth:** House member-elect orientation in November with a House office selection event and office setup; Senate multiday orientation and party caucus meetings, office assignment by service order. Represent operating allowances as restricted office budgets that activate according to current chamber rules; avoid a one-time personal “hiring bonus.”
- **State legislature default:** use a jurisdiction profile with one of three sourced patterns: formal multi-day orientation, one-day/staff-led orientation, or brief organizational-session onboarding. If uncoded, offer a generic half-day services/ethics/IT briefing and clearly mark its content as game-authored, not a real program.
- **Governor default:** optionally establish a winner-led transition team, build a cabinet shortlist and first-100-day agenda, and hold handoff meetings. Make team formation and cooperation by outgoing administration conditional. Do not assume state-provided transition resources without jurisdiction evidence.
- **Local default:** select a city/county onboarding pattern by government profile: bespoke mayor/council curriculum; state university/association cohort; or basic clerk/manager orientation. Put ethics/open meetings, budget, authority boundaries and access first. Treat external association courses as opportunities, not mandatory, unless jurisdiction law establishes that requirement.
- **Story design:** transition should be consequential but brief. It can shape staff quality, office readiness, knowledge and early relationships. The election win should not be followed by a hidden accept/reject employment gate.

### Questions for the owner

1. **DECIDED:** Every certified winner gets a transition phase, based on local evidence; where no formal program is documented, use a modest generic staff briefing without claiming it is a real institution.
2. Should orientation be optional for the player, with a small knowledge/readiness cost for skipping, or mandatory but summarized in one time step?
3. Do you want the campaign's staff to automatically form the transition team, or should the player appoint a transition lead and several advisers?
4. For governor-elect gameplay, should transition priorities be cabinet hiring, a first-100-days agenda, agency handoff/briefings, or all three?
5. Should a legislative winner be allowed to hire staff before the legal seating date? My recommendation: allow recruitment and conditional offers, but official payroll/office budgets start only when the jurisdiction's rules permit.
6. Should House and Senate transition differences be a detailed playable sequence, or should Congress share a compact transition flow with chamber-specific office assignment and orientation content?
7. Do you want local onboarding differences by jurisdiction, or one generic pattern with notable exceptions such as Tennessee commissioner training and Portland's bespoke city onboarding?
8. **DECIDED IN PART:** Playable emphasis is staff and political priorities; routine administrative setup and orientation are summarized. Relative weight of relationships, policy learning and narrative events remains open.
