# Newsworthiness by government level — research handoff

**Research date:** 2026-09-22. **Scope:** local, state, and national political coverage. **Status:** source-grounded pattern and design proposal; no owner-approved scoring rule. **Owner decision:** reach helps a newsroom discover a story; broader relevance and editorial judgment determine whether it becomes national coverage. Attention alone is not an automatic national-news trigger.

## What the sources support

- The Associated Press describes accuracy, verification, independence, and conflicts of interest as newsroom standards. These are constraints on reporting, not an algorithm that assigns every event one fixed news score. [AP News Values and Principles](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/).
- AP's statehouse reporting network covers elections, budgets, policy, new administrations, and accountability, shares work with local newsrooms, and works with national beat teams. This is direct evidence that state events can be covered through local, state, and national channels rather than belonging to only one tier. The cited program announcement is from 2020, so its headcount is not a present-day staffing claim. [AP statehouse reporting announcement](https://www.ap.org/the-definitive-source/announcements/ap-names-17-new-statehouse-reporters-in-report-for-america-collaboration/).
- AP describes using local reporting to examine national political divisions through lived community effects. This supports a route in both directions: national decisions can generate local stories, and local evidence can illuminate a wider pattern. [AP, When national becomes local](https://www.ap.org/insights/when-national-becomes-local-covering-americas-divides-from-main-street/).
- Pew's original content study found that local communities received federal-government coverage from a mix of sources, including local outlets and larger organizations. It is evidence of cross-level coverage, not a fixed probability for any particular fictional newsroom. [Pew Research Center, 2015](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2015/12/03/who-provides-coverage-of-the-federal-government-to-local-communities/).
- ProPublica's Local Reporting Network explicitly seeks accountability investigations with importance to local communities; its program shows that a locally grounded public-interest story need not start at a statehouse or in Washington. [ProPublica Local Reporting Network](https://www.propublica.org/how-to-apply/local-reporting-network).

## Recommended game rule for owner review

1. Record a single underlying event with its actual place, affected people, office, decision, money/stakes, available evidence, and participants. A press story reads that event; it does not create a new event or change its facts.
2. Each outlet decides independently whether it can learn and verify the event, whether it serves its audience/beat, and how much editorial space it has. Use a shared vocabulary such as consequence, timeliness, public accountability, novelty, conflict, and human stakes, with outlet-specific audience and reach. Avoid a universal score that guarantees identical coverage everywhere.
3. A town paper can report a city decision because residents are directly affected. A state outlet may pick it up when it affects state policy, many communities, a state officeholder, or a recognizable pattern. A national outlet may pick it up when it bears on federal action, broad consequence, national figures, an unusual precedent, or a documented cross-state pattern. A local outlet can also localize a federal decision that affects its readers.
4. Syndication, quotation, and follow-up stories can transmit a verified report outward; they should keep provenance and outlet voice. High reach increases the chance an editor encounters the story. It need not automatically make the underlying event nationally consequential.
5. Sparse press areas should still be allowed to have a local outlet or an outside beat reporter when the world's media records support it. A missing compiled municipal government should not itself erase a local newspaper. This matches the owner's previously accepted code correction.

## Player-facing example

A mayor redirects funds from a neighborhood flood project. The town paper covers residents' immediate risk. A state outlet covers it if the money came from a state program or similar diversions appear elsewhere. A national investigation follows if the same documented pattern crosses states or involves a federal funding rule. The event's facts stay the same; each outlet's angle, evidence and audience differ.

## Open owner choice

**DECIDED:** Reach affects discovery; broader relevance and newsroom judgment determine national coverage. The numeric threshold, if any, remains undecided.

## Unknowns and limits

- These sources do not provide universal numeric thresholds or outlet-specific coverage probabilities. Neither the owner nor these sources has approved a scoring formula.
- Real newsroom capacity, ownership, syndication agreements and beat assignments vary by outlet and time. Do not infer a single rule from the AP staffing example.
- Coverage is distinct from event truth, audience beliefs, public opinion change and electoral effects. Each needs its own in-world causal path.
