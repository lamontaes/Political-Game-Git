# Owner intent for inhabited territories and statehood play

**Date:** 2026-09-22. **Status:** owner intent and research handoff to existing engineering owners; no code assignment, rule-pack approval, playtest pass, or release claim.

## Who

Residents, candidates, territorial officials, D.C. officials, territorial House delegates, Puerto Rico's Resident Commissioner, state/federal legislators, parties, press, and other voters continue to act as people in the same persistent world. The scope is the 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the U.S. Virgin Islands: 56 inhabited jurisdictions. D.C. is a federal district, not a territory. Uninhabited Minor Outlying Islands are outside this playable-life scope. American Samoan nationality and local voting must not be collapsed into U.S. citizenship, and the territorial delegates/Resident Commissioner must not be treated as ordinary voting House members.

## What the player can do

A character who meets an office's actual requirements can run through the normal candidacy, campaign, election, seating, and governing path. Statehood is an issue that candidates and other actors can advocate or oppose. A player may be an unknown local candidate who becomes widely known, or enter with a large media audience; reach affects who hears an argument, while each person's existing interests, beliefs and experiences affect the response. Fame by itself is not a vote. The player has no special scripted statehood-leader office or button. Territory residents hold individual, changeable opinions about their own status, including uncertainty; do not seed an entire electorate with one answer. The player may stay at a concise level or inspect the deeper institution and opinion records.

## When and where

At age 18, voter registration is handled in the background once the person otherwise qualifies under the applicable jurisdiction's rules. It is not a player chore and is a game simplification, not a statement that real jurisdictions use automatic registration. Campaign statements and media exposure change knowledge/reach only when they occur in world time; subsequent reactions and elections use recorded audience and opinion. The political route depends on the territory or district's real offices, electorate and ballot process. D.C. has presidential electors already; territory residents do not vote in the Electoral College general election while domiciled there. A change to statehood would alter office and federal representation rules prospectively, preserving prior history and seat identity. The owner wants key transition decisions playable, with routine steps automatic; each admission measure still controls its own legal transition.

## Why this matters

The ordinary player should be able to live and seek office in all inhabited U.S. jurisdictions, rather than seeing a place label with missing institutions. A statehood campaign should emerge from real disagreement, organizing, audience reach, candidates, legislatures and Congress. Historical votes in one place or decade do not establish what today's residents think.

## How engineering should receive it

Use one canonical jurisdiction/place/people/office/election/history route with distinct profiles for each territory and D.C.; do not reuse a generic state law where legal facts are unknown. Keep official legal facts, sourced demographic or status-vote evidence, generated game profiles, and owner-approved mechanics labeled separately. Use the existing seat IDs through candidacy, election, term and saved continuation where an office already exists. For new offices and eventual admission, define prospective jurisdiction transitions and representation in a separate approved design. Validate an ordinary candidate's path and saved follow-through in each jurisdiction; include cases that expose American Samoa's national/citizen distinction and D.C.'s different federal status. Never infer a person's opinion from geography alone.
