## Why the sources disagree in ways that matter

Warshaw's state economic measure is the average of changes in wages and employment from one year to the next; his governor-party vote result is about 1.4 points per point of that composite. Ebeid and Rodden use real per-capita income and unemployment variants and report that the relationship depends on whether a state economy is dominated by primary products. Neither offers a directly matched conversion between the game's **unemployment change over a whole term** and vote share. [Warshaw, methods and Table 2](https://polisci.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/u245/christopher_warshaw_paper_05-08-17.pdf); [Ebeid and Rodden, abstract and measures](https://web.stanford.edu/~jrodden/bjps_ebeid_rodden_2006.pdf).

The game's current `UNRESEARCHED_RECORD_IN_OFFICE` value—60 initial-support weight for each point unemployment fell or rose during a governor term, capped at 150 on a draw of 850–1150—has **no direct research validation** from these sources. Those weights are not percentage points of vote share. The present evidence is insufficient to replace them with a factual numeric unemployment coefficient. A design choice, if made, must be labeled separately from the empirical estimates and checked against a simulated electorate's people/participation accounting.
