# Media owners coordinating outlets: U.S. evidence and game boundary

**Research date:** 2026-09-22. **Scope:** owner types, measured share, coordinated actions, independence, and data sources. This is factual research for fictional game owners; it does not assign motives to any real company or prescribe a political view.

|---|---|---|

## Coordinated actions documented in primary material

1. **Buying, selling, merging and closing papers.** Medill's 2025 census recorded 246 paper ownership changes and 148 newspaper disappearances through closures or mergers. It says only about one tenth of that year's vanished papers were controlled by the top 10 firms; smaller owners also acted. Group ownership can coordinate transactions, but a “large chain always closes papers” rule is unsupported. [Medill 2025](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2025/report/).
2. **Shared content and production.** Medill's 2024 audit of home pages from 500 sites held by the five largest chains found, on average, more than one third of displayed stories originated outside the local newsroom. This measures home-page mix, not a rule for every story or an owner's motive. [Medill 2024](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2024/report/). TEGNA's 2024 securities filing describes shared resources across stations as part of its own production strategy. [TEGNA SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/39899/000095017025029076/tgna-20241231.htm).
3. **Staffing, print and distribution changes.** USA TODAY Co.'s 2025 annual filing attributes part of lower payroll expense to reduced headcount and documents mail conversion in multiple markets and distribution changes. These are company-reported operating actions, not evidence of one universal private-equity behavior. [SEC 2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579684/000157968426000026/annualreport_2025forfiling.pdf).
4. **Centrally supplied editorial content.** An original 2019 study of local television transcripts reports that stations acquired by Sinclair in 2017 shifted the balance between local and national political coverage and discusses centrally produced “must-run” segments. This is evidence that editorial coordination can occur, not that every broadcast owner uses the practice or that all local editorial decisions are centrally dictated. [Martin and McCrain, American Political Science Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/local-news-and-national-politics/C8EEA488A777C37C7987964F8F85AEB5).
5. **Network-wide publication choices.** Corporate filings and Medill's audit document network-wide content and distribution strategies. A current, directly sourced cross-chain table of centrally set political endorsements was not established in this pass. Treat that action as **UNKNOWN by owner**, not a default for all chains.

## Editorial independence is outlet-specific

Ownership grants potential authority over budgets, leadership, shared services and sometimes content; the amount of editorial control actually exercised varies by organization, contracts and newsroom policy. The Medill home-page audit demonstrates substantial content sharing within major chains. INN's member standards require donor noninterference for participating nonprofits. AP's public standards describe its own editorial independence, but they cannot be generalized to all media. [Medill 2024](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2024/report/); [INN standards](https://inn.org/inn-network/membership-standards/); [AP values](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/).

## Data to seed fictional owners

- **Newspapers and local digital outlets:** Medill State of Local News inventory and [methodology](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2025/methodology/). Snapshot by year, not a live perfect registry; its print/digital categories and independent-owner definition must be retained.
- **Broadcast license and parent ownership:** FCC Form 323/323-E and station public files. Filings have their own cycle and later amendments; arrangements to operate/sell programming can differ from license ownership. [FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-23-35A1.pdf).
- **Public-company group actions:** securities filings show acquisitions, payroll, production, print and debt arrangements, but are company self-reports. Private firms often disclose less, so absence of a filing is not proof that no action occurred. [USA TODAY Co. 2025 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579684/000157968426000010/tday-20251231.htm); [TEGNA filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/39899/000119312526084392/tgna-20251231.htm).
- **Nonprofit policies:** INN directory/standards are useful for editorial-independence rules of members, not all nonprofits. [INN standards](https://inn.org/inn-network/membership-standards/).

## Game design boundary for owner review

Represent the owner and each outlet as separate actors. The owner may choose a group-level budget, acquisition, shared-service or editorial directive; each outlet has staff, capacity, audience, local knowledge and an independence policy that determine its response. Record what was ordered, what the outlet did, what readers saw and when. **Owner decision received 2026-09-22:** editors and staff can resist an owner directive, with consequences. The exact forms of resistance and employment consequences still need bounded design and source support. Keep group action distinct from the truth of a story and from audience belief or voting.

**Unknowns:** current nationwide private-equity share across all news media; a universal ownership-type-to-editorial-freedom coefficient; group-level endorsement prevalence; numerical rates for layoffs, closures or resistance. The sourced proportions above are print or standalone digital snapshots, not generic probability settings.
