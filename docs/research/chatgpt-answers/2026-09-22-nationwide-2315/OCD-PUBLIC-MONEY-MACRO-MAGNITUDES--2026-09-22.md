## Finding

No single study located estimates all requested outcomes for marginal **own-source state or local dollars actually paid/collected**, by purchases, household transfers, and taxes, with both state output growth and unemployment responses and a monthly decay profile. The closest US evidence is heterogeneous by financing, program, time period, outcome, and identification strategy. The question's existing `+/-0.10pp`, `+/-0.02pp`, `$50m`, and `0.6 monthly retention` settings are unverified placeholders, as the packet itself says; the literature reviewed here does not validate them.

“Scale by GDP” is a useful way to express a shock's size (`dollars / jurisdiction GDP`) and to compare economies. It does not establish a linear Wyoming-versus-California response function. State GDP is a production measure; it includes production sold elsewhere and does not measure household residence or local purchasing leakage. BEA also does not publish quarterly GDP for cities/counties in the same way it does for states. Census local finance classifications distinguish a locally imposed tax from a state-collected tax share and intergovernmental revenue, so those channels should not be combined.

## What the official measures mean

- **Output denominator/outcome.** BEA defines GDP by state as the market value of goods and services produced by labor and property located in the state. State quarterly GDP growth is a quarter-to-quarter real percent change conventionally annualized; chained-dollar levels are nonadditive. State GDP is not the same thing as resident personal income and may include output sold outside the state. BEA has annual GDP by county, but no equivalent routine quarterly city/county GDP series. [BEA, GDP by State additional information](https://www.bea.gov/news/stgdppipce-release-additional-information); [BEA, GDP by State](https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gdp-state); [BEA, GDP learning center](https://www.bea.gov/resources/learning-center/what-to-know-gdp).
- **Purchases versus transfers.** BEA government consumption expenditures and gross investment measure government-produced services and fixed-asset investment/purchases that enter GDP. Cash transfers and interest are excluded from GDP's government-purchase component: they transfer income and can affect recipient consumption, but are not themselves purchases of current output. Census finance expenditures, by contrast, cover broader government outlays and classify intergovernmental aid separately; a transfer is not automatically an end-recipient purchase. [BEA government spending FAQ](https://www.bea.gov/help/faq/552); [BEA NIPA Handbook, chapter 9 (Dec. 2024)](https://www.bea.gov/resources/methodologies/nipa-handbook/pdf/chapter-09.pdf); [Census finance glossary](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/state/about/glossary.html).
- **Tax receipts.** Census defines taxes as compulsory contributions for public purposes and classifies a local government's share of taxes imposed/collected by a state as intergovernmental revenue, not local tax revenue. BEA's “current tax receipts” includes personal current taxes, taxes on production and imports, and corporate income taxes. Neither accounting definition gives a causal output effect of an extra dollar collected. [Census finance glossary](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/state/about/glossary.html); [BEA GDP-by-state methodology](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/methodologies/0417_GDP_by_State_Methodology.pdf).
- **Unemployment.** BLS unemployment rate is unemployed persons divided by the labor force, with unemployed defined through CPS job-search/availability concepts. A jobs or job-years estimate cannot be converted into percentage points without labor force denominator, duration/timing, and labor-force participation response. LAUS is residence-based/local-area estimation; payroll employment is establishment-based and counts jobs. [BLS CPS definitions](https://www.bls.gov/cps/definitions.htm); [BLS household/establishment guide](https://www.bls.gov/bls/empsitquickguide.htm).
- **Finance data.** Census Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances / Census of Governments covers revenue (taxes, charges, etc.), expenditure, debt, and assets, using government classifications. These are accounting inputs, not outcome multipliers. [Census 2024 state/local government finance datasets](https://www.census.gov/data/datasets/2024/econ/local/public-use-datasets.html); [Census user notes](https://www2.census.gov/govs/local/state-local_govt_fin_user_notes.pdf).

**Important interpretation:** a local/cross-sectional multiplier often captures output displaced or stimulated across regions under common national monetary policy; it is not automatically an aggregate GDP multiplier. An own-funded state purchase can induce expectations of later taxes/spending cuts, so “windfall” money (federal aid) is not comparable to local tax-financed spending. State GDP is production located in the state, and some induced demand leaks to other states. A multiplier is a cumulative dollars-of-output-per-dollar-of-fiscal-impulse measure, not an immediate percentage-point GDP-growth coefficient.

## City and county evidence

- **County:** Erickson, Owusu-Nantwi & Owensby (2015) find state/local spending multipliers below one, but its real-earnings growth outcome and individual/time fixed-effect design do not isolate a robust causal effect of an extra locally paid dollar. It is the closest US city/county-level estimate found, and is not sufficient to populate the requested unemployment or lag fields.
- **Municipalities abroad:** Brazilian municipal-transfer research (e.g. Nakamura/Steinsson is US; transfer RDD literature in Brazil) can identify local grant effects but differs in institutions and cannot calibrate US city/county own-source purchases. For US large-city/county own money, a matched causal output/unemployment response and monthly decay profile is **UNKNOWN** in this search.
- BEA's GDP by county is annual; city boundaries and county/city overlap create an additional denominator/authority mismatch. Using county GDP as a city economy denominator would be an approximation, not an official city GDP series. [BEA GDP learning center](https://www.bea.gov/resources/learning-center/what-to-know-gdp).

## Design implication for the open question

Keep the accounting distinction from C02: record completed purchases, transfers, and tax collections separately at the jurisdiction that incurs/receives them. For research-facing reporting, normalize the realized impulse by a matched jurisdiction scale (state real GDP is measurable quarterly; personal income is a distinct resident-income denominator). Then label response evidence by funding source, fiscal channel, recession/slack state, outcome, and horizon. Do not turn the estimates above into a universal game coefficient or infer a monthly exponential decay. If a numeric playable rule is needed before better calibration exists, it should be owner-approved as a design abstraction and visibly remain separate from empirical estimates.
