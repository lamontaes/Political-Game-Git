# Political culture evidence inventory: 56 U.S. jurisdictions

As of September 22, 2026, comparable evidence does not establish political culture for all 56 jurisdictions across the game’s 14 principles. This inventory preserves those gaps and records two territory status observations without treating them as evidence for a broader principle. Party returns and generic ideology categories cannot substitute for directly measured beliefs. No policy direction, strength rating, ranking, or recommendation is assigned.

## Coverage and interpretation

The table covers the 50 states, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and Northern Mariana Islands. Only Puerto Rico and Guam have dated, jurisdiction-specific political-status observations recorded above. These are factual status observations, not principles, mainland left/right proxies, or recommendations. The remaining 54 rows explicitly preserve the evidence gap. No within-jurisdiction city/rural or regional variation estimate was established, so none is implied.

The evidence gate for a non-unknown principle entry is a direct measure of that principle or a directly relevant, dated policy choice with enough context to interpret it. Election returns for candidates and party shares fail that gate. A ballot measure may document a discrete decision, but it cannot by itself establish a broad cultural disposition. A single statewide result also cannot establish uniformity among residents or subregions.

The game research question's examples about Southern fiscal restraint and liberalism in D.C. or Massachusetts are prompts, not evidence, and are not encoded. This inventory therefore does not resolve the requested regional groupings or the pace of change. Doing so requires comparable original state-level survey items or carefully documented repeated policy measures, together with within-state geography and a distinct, status-centered evidence plan for each territory. The reviewed broad state-ideology tracking and national political typologies are not substituted for the catalog's principle measures: their constructs and geographies do not establish those fields ([Gallup state ideology, 2012 vintage](https://news.gallup.com/poll/160196/alabama-north-dakota-wyoming-conservative-states.aspx); [Pew 2026 typology, national survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/06/10/beyond-red-vs-blue-the-political-typology/)).

## Source and coverage audit

- Catalog vocabulary: 14 principles confirmed in `src/simulation/policy-pack-us-policy-positions.ts`.
- Jurisdiction coverage: 56 named rows, with all 14 fields marked `UNKNOWN` in each row (784 unknown principle cells).
- Direct dated observations: Puerto Rico's certified 2024 status plebiscite; Guam's official decolonization commission description and study. Neither observation was converted into a principle rating.
- Unsupported for this inventory: comparable contemporary principle measures for states, D.C., and the other three territories; measured intra-jurisdiction variation; regional principle clusters; longitudinal principle movement.
- Scope: read-only research deliverable. No game data or policy position was changed.
