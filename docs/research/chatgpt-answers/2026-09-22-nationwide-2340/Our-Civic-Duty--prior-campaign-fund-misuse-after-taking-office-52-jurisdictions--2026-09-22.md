## Source key and limits

- **S baseline:** [National Conference of State Legislatures, “Recall of State Officials,” updated August 27, 2025](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/recall-of-state-officials). It lists 19 states plus D.C. with state-official recall elections and separately describes Virginia’s court-decided petition process. “No statewide recall election listed” means absent from that chart, not a primary-law finding that removal is impossible. Puerto Rico is outside its listed coverage. The detailed grounds, threshold, and timing remain UNKNOWN in the primary-law columns unless a V cell cites a direct official source.

- **AK-C:** [Alaska Constitution, art. II §20](https://akleg.gov/docs/pdf/AK_Const_layout.pdf) (official legislature PDF). It establishes the reverse-house process and removal-only impeachment judgment; it does not define offense grounds.
- **AK-R:** [Alaska Statutes, §§15.45.470–.720](https://www.akleg.gov/statutesPDF/Title-15.pdf) (official legislature PDF), especially §§.490, .500, .510, .560. Application and petition stages use different percentages.
- **CA-I:** [California Constitution, art. IV §18, in Assembly Handbook](https://www.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-02/2023-assembly-handbook.pdf) (official Assembly publication, p. 323). The cited section names the bodies and Senate conviction vote but gives no explicit pre-office standard.
- **CA-R:** [California Secretary of State recall procedures](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/recalls) and [2024 gubernatorial recall timetable](https://elections.cdn.sos.ca.gov/recalls/newsom-recall-2024.pdf), citing California Constitution art. II §§13–14. The petition contains a statement of grounds, but cited text does not impose a cause test.
- **CA-P:** [California Constitution, art. V §13](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?article=V&lawCode=CONS&sectionNum=SEC.+13.) (official legislative code). This establishes general Attorney General powers, not a specific campaign-finance charging route.
- **MI-I:** [Michigan Constitution, art. XI §§7–8](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Constitution-XI.pdf) (official legislature PDF). The word “or” between in-office corruption and crimes/misdemeanors does not alone resolve earlier conduct.
- **MI-R:** [Michigan Constitution, art. II §8](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Constitution-II.pdf) and [Michigan Election Law, ch. XXXVI](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-116-1954-XXXVI.pdf), especially §168.951 (official legislature PDFs).

## Actual cases with verified amount and route

No case is listed as a sitting-governor or other statewide-official precedent. For example, the [federal prosecution of Sam McCann](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdil/pr/former-illinois-state-senator-gubernatorial-candidate-sam-mccann-sentenced-42-months) verifies more than $600,000 converted, but he was a former state senator and an unsuccessful gubernatorial candidate. The [Martha Shoffner prosecution](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edar/pr/former-arkansas-state-treasurer-martha-shoffner-sentenced-30-months-prison-extortion) identifies concealed personal campaign spending by a former state treasurer, but the stated prison term was for extortion and bribery and the release does not give an aggregate campaign-use amount. An empty qualifying case list is not evidence that no such cases exist.
