Official rules support the named bodies and processes below; unverified details remain **UNKNOWN**. A committee's investigation or recommendation does not itself establish guilt or impose chamber discipline. Campaign-finance regulators are not substituted for legislative ethics bodies.

## Coverage and gaps

This is a complete 28-row inventory of the jurisdictions in the question. It is not a complete 28-jurisdiction procedural code. The strongest fully sourced sequences in this pass are Colorado House, Iowa (selected statutory stages), Maine, Utah, Virginia (filing and screening), and Wyoming (authority and sanctions). Many other official sources establish the body or complaint intake but do not establish every stage, deadline, finding, and sanction requested. Those cells remain UNKNOWN rather than borrowing another state's process.

Highest-priority source gaps for a follow-up are: Arkansas, Delaware, Idaho, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Mississippi, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, South Dakota, the current Tennessee Senate process, Puerto Rico House, and current Michigan/Indiana chamber complaint procedures. Montana, Vermont, and portions of Tennessee/Virginia need current-rule refreshes because the sources retrieved are dated, partial, or do not reproduce the full disposition provisions.

North Dakota illustrates why regulator identity alone is insufficient: the Ethics Commission is a real route for legislative officials, while chamber discipline remains separate. Maine illustrates the reverse distinction: a shared commission investigates and finds facts, but transmits its opinion to the respondent’s chamber to act. Arizona and California have separate chamber panels. No campaign finance regulator was used as a substitute for these ethics routes.
