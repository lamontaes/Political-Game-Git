# The dialogue tree is still choosing prewritten events

The new four-part dialogue export names each scene, choice, reach condition, and withheld item. A source trace confirms that the opening-life route selects an authored scene and records its premise as a new event. Other playable scenes also state specifics their printed conditions do not establish. Some Journal sentences turn a choice into a later outcome. The owner wants the simulation to produce what happens, then assemble dialogue from that record. Fixed chains should be test examples, not the model for ordinary life content. Routine activities should be optional.

## Product rule for modular dialogue

**Owner decision in this conversation:** Ordinary dialogue should grow from what the simulation decides happened, rather than a list of specific incidents that the game selects. The world establishes who initiated an exchange, what they want, what happened before, where and when this occurs, what each person knows, and what each can do. Reusable conversation patterns then offer grounded questions, requests, refusals, and replies. The person responding should have their own knowledge and circumstances. Personality and accumulated history should deepen those responses as those systems mature; a complete personality-driven language system is not a prerequisite for making the current design modular. This direction aligns with `docs/GAME-CONSTITUTION.md:11-19,29,41,55,61` and the bounded authored phrase-family decision in `docs/decisions/DECISION-LOG.md:599-605`.

**Existing owner authority:** The [current Assignment Board, paragraph 59](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1s0YTUaYcWpOi_MqbaXNW71-nhYrVTTd57AnNPa8pv0E/edit) already rejects ordinary production play depending on world-specific hardcoded instances. The [Decision Register, paragraph 61](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1bZWrzjUgDql2CIo_k1ElcQ2GrBZOzC_xJs8D-JswKpE/edit) calls for composition from actual people and circumstances. [ALIVE44, paragraphs 159 and 176](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1wDpqx3b9a_O0iqLgxm3YREJNN469pC4LPyJ2eJWD_jA/edit) describes reusable encounter components and says word variants cannot create more incidents or evidence. The owner's latest clarification reinforces these accepted rules rather than opening a new product question.

**Inferred design consequence:** Fixed scene chains can be useful as development fixtures or exceptional events with an actual recorded producer. Adding more chains alone would increase the inventory without making ordinary life more responsive. A callback should arise because a saved occurrence still matters to someone, not solely because a stage timer elapsed. This consequence follows the owner's stated direction and the fixed-stage gates measured in [Part 1](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1vq53nXW4CUXexQ4RiRIi82ZOoy3wFT-gVCjSFTtMrZ8/edit).

**Acceptance rule:** In ordinary play, the dialogue selector must not create a named incident by selecting its prewritten scene. It may select a response pattern only after the world has produced the incident and recorded what the participants know. Changing the names or choosing among alternate fixed details does not satisfy this rule. An exceptional authored event still needs a producer, a place in history, and character-specific knowledge before anyone talks about it.

**Interim recommendation:** Keep fixed examples in isolated tests or a development view while their ordinary-player routes are replaced. A quiet stretch is preferable to filling the life screen with an unsupported incident. This is a recommendation for implementation sequencing, not a claim that the existing branch has already made the change.

## Next engineering handoff

First, treat the authored opening-life and growing-up selectors, plus fixed adult request instances, as confirmed content problems. Trace the other tabled scenes and follow-up chains through their producers and saved records. Classify each as **confirmed producer**, **missing producer**, or **unverified**. A producer that merely stamps a fixed instance into history does not meet the owner's modularity goal. Use one invitation or request from an initiator's actual circumstances as a modular proof: named initiator, actual date, reason, and recipient knowledge, rendered through reusable choices and responses. Show it with different people and circumstances in two ordinary lives in newly chosen, different non-Kentucky places. Record the assembled scene, chosen option, other person's actual reply, elapsed time, Journal line, later return, and Save/Continue result. Route routine activities as optional actions.
