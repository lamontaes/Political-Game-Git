# The dialogue tree is still choosing prewritten events

The new four-part dialogue export names each scene, choice, reach condition, and withheld item. It raises a potential grounding problem: some scenes labeled playable supply specific events or other people's responses that their printed conditions do not establish. Some saved Journal sentences turn a choice into a later outcome. The owner wants the simulation to produce the event and its consequences, then assemble dialogue from that record. The current fixed chains should be treated as test examples, not the model for adding ordinary life content. Routine reading, resting, drawing, and sketching should be optional actions.

## Product rule for modular dialogue

**Owner decision in this conversation:** Ordinary dialogue should grow from what the simulation decides happened, rather than a list of specific incidents that the game selects. The world establishes who initiated an exchange, what they want, what happened before, where and when this occurs, what each person knows, and what each can do. Reusable conversation patterns then offer grounded questions, requests, refusals, and replies. The person responding should have their own knowledge and circumstances. Personality and accumulated history should deepen those responses as those systems mature; a complete personality-driven language system is not a prerequisite for making the current design modular. This direction aligns with `docs/GAME-CONSTITUTION.md:11-19,29,41,55,61` and the bounded authored phrase-family decision in `docs/decisions/DECISION-LOG.md:599-605`.

**Inferred design consequence:** Fixed scene chains can be useful as development fixtures or exceptional events with an actual recorded producer. Adding more chains alone would increase the inventory without making ordinary life more responsive. A callback should arise because a saved occurrence still matters to someone, not solely because a stage timer elapsed. This consequence follows the owner's stated direction and the fixed-stage gates measured in [Part 1](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1vq53nXW4CUXexQ4RiRIi82ZOoy3wFT-gVCjSFTtMrZ8/edit).

**Acceptance rule:** In ordinary play, the dialogue selector must not create a named incident by selecting its prewritten scene. It may select a response pattern only after the world has produced the incident and recorded what the participants know. Changing the names or choosing among alternate fixed details does not satisfy this rule. An exceptional authored event still needs a producer, a place in history, and character-specific knowledge before anyone talks about it.
