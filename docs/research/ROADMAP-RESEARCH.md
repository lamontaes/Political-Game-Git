# Roadmap research list

Owner-requested Oct 11, 2026: one research session works through the real-world research the whole roadmap needs (M1 to M7), so findings are ready before each work order starts. Built from three inventories: the research sections of the M1 work orders, every M2 to M7 spec, the roughly 160 placeholder numbers already registered in the new core and the lifted rules, and the 209 unanswered requests in `docs/research/requests/`. This page is the list and its rules. The session keeps its own log (below); the CTO keeps this page.

## Rules

1. **Research only.** No game code, no changes to `src/`. Findings are data files and short notes.
2. **Stay out of running sessions' folders.** Don't write in `data/research/laws/` (CODEX-LAWS), `data/research/macro/` (CODEX-MACRO), `data/research/views/` (CODEX-VIEWS), or `data/research/renewal/` outside its `moves/` subfolder (CODEX-RENEWAL). Their topics aren't on this list.
3. **Where findings go:** the folder each topic names below. **Create the folder if it doesn't exist.** In a folder that already exists, add new files and don't change existing ones. Every row carries its source, URL, retrieval date, the source's terms, which of the 56 places it covers, and whether it's an estimate (with its spread and basis). Research sessions skip the startup bench check in `AGENTS.md`; there's no game code to check.
4. **Depth:** federal and state facts are exact. Local facts come from a representative sample of real places of each kind in every state (largest city, a mid-size city, a small town, a county, an unincorporated area), then an estimate with a spread for the rest. The 56 places are the 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands.
5. **Sources:** the agency or government that publishes the fact, not a mirror. Check and record every source's terms first. U.S. government data is free to use. **Don't use:** the Council of State Governments' Book of the States, Zillow research data, Eviction Lab, S&P Case-Shiller, Freddie Mac's survey, ANES or GSS, or Ballotpedia, unless the CTO confirms permission. Pew is allowed with its notices, attribution and disclaimer. If terms are unclear, record the question in the log and move on.
6. **Store derived tables, not raw dumps.** Save the summary the game will read (for example, hazard events and damage by county, kind and year), not lists of individual records. Keep each committed file under 5 MB; reference raw files by URL and checksum.
7. **Answer existing requests in place.** Before each topic, search `docs/research/requests/` for matching requests without an `answer`, and fill in `answer` and `sourcesChecked` there.
8. **Search properly, then move on.** Before recording a gap, try every source the topic names and at least two other searches for that item. For topics that need published studies (behavior, effect sizes, how people feel or decide), summarize at least three studies with their numbers, samples and citations; prefer U.S. studies, and label foreign ones as foreign. Only then record what's still missing, with the best sourced estimate, and move on. Never invent a number. Numbers reported in published studies are facts and may be used with citation; don't copy tables or text wholesale.
9. **Real people:** collect aggregates only. Never record real officeholders' or candidates' names, even when a request's question names one as an example.
10. **Named examples aren't rules.** Many requests use a real state, city or event as an example ("coal hurts West Virginia"). Answer with data for every place, or with the general mechanism, never a rule for the named place.
11. **Open requests by topic** are listed in `docs/research/roadmap-research-requests.md` (209 of them). Answer each in place when its topic is done.

## Where to log

Branch `pool/research-roadmap` from `main` (Priorities 0 and 1) or `pool/research-roadmap-2` (Priorities 2 to 4), one draft PR each. After each topic, add one line at the top of `docs/research/roadmap-research-log.md` (topic id, files written, places covered, gaps, terms questions) and commit. Put the same line at the top of the PR body. The CTO merges finished topics as they land.

## The list, in priority order

### Priority 0: numbers the new core already uses

About 160 registered numbers in the new core and its branches (`src/core2/data/stopgaps.json` and `parameters.json` on `pool/P8-part-1`, the drives, people and director branches) are estimates or tunable guesses waiting for real figures. The ones that decide money come first, because the M0 money year depends on them. Write findings to `data/research/core-figures/<topic>/`; the owning session (SOL-1258, OPUS-PEOPLE, OPUS-DIRECTOR or the drives owner) adopts them. Never edit `src/`.

- **P0-1 What households and governments buy from local businesses:** household spending by category, region and household size (BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey); what counties, schools and cities spend on local goods and services (Census of Governments, county budgets); what visitors spend in a town (BEA and Census travel and trade data). Replaces the opening customer and public procurement estimates (177 rows).
- **P0-2 How businesses behave:** cost and margin ratios by industry and size (IRS SOI, Census Annual Business Survey); cash on hand in days by industry (published firm liquidity studies; check the JPMorgan Chase Institute's terms before relying on it); how local sales respond to local income and how prices respond to competition (published studies of local multipliers and pricing). Replaces `business-books-priors`, `business-books-dynamics` and the cash buffers, which decide which firms fail first.
- **P0-3 Pay:** pay frequency by industry and employer size (BLS), each state's wage payment law (how often and how soon after a period ends), how pay rises with tenure by occupation, and shift start times and schedules (BLS, American Time Use Survey). Replaces the $45,760 fallback wage, the tenure percentiles and the pay calendar.
- **P0-4 Time use:** hours for sleep, work, care, errands, civic activity and free time by age and sex (American Time Use Survey), volunteering (CPS), work absence rates (CPS), and how much cash households keep on hand (Survey of Consumer Finances).
- **P0-5 Mood, stress and choice:** how mood and stress rise and fade (published daily-diary studies), used to replace the 2-day and 7-day half-lives and the choice weights.
- **P0-6 Ties:** how often people contact family, friends, coworkers and old classmates by tie type and distance; how ties grow and fade; how much of a person's circle turns over in a few years; how often friendships end and how often people help each other, from probability-sample surveys (replacing the commercial YouGov figure).
- **P0-7 Families:** age at first marriage and first birth, time between births, partners' age gaps, how often grown children move away (NSFG, NCHS, ACS), and published personality norms and how traits correlate (check terms; many norm tables are licensed).
- **P0-8 Grief:** published bereavement trajectories (resilient, recovering, chronic) and what bereaved people do in the months after.
- **P0-9 Retirement:** when people claim Social Security, career lengths and benefit distributions (SSA), and pension coverage.
- **P0-10 What life events weigh:** modern published measures of how significant life events feel (the director's current weights come from the 1967 Holmes-Rahe scale), and how long people keep recalling them.
- **P0-11 Elections numbers already in the rules:** how much incumbency helps in primaries and in general elections by level, and how often people decide to run unprompted (replaces `elections.primary-pull`, `elections.incumbency-share` and `elections.self-starter-share-gate`).
- **P0-12 Health, death and disasters:** how long serious illnesses last by age, causes of death by age (CDC), how often officeholders die in office compared with the life table; how often hazards strike each county and the damage they do to homes (FEMA declarations, NOAA storm events), and seasonal illness severity (CDC), so the old seeded damage draws can be replaced by causes.
- **P0-13 Names:** given-name and surname patterns by place and birth year, including Puerto Rico and the territories, and how real schools are named by state and era (Social Security name data, Census surnames, NCES school directories).

The start date moves to 2026 (M1-history), so the newest data vintages are the right ones; don't chase January 2021 tables.

Not research, for later: 152 rule constants carried over unchanged from the old campaigns and governing engines are registered without their values. Their port finishers will list what each one holds; add any real-world figure they find here then. The old core's own marker ledgers (`data/research/assumption-markers.json`, `data/research/powers-catalog/placeholder-ledger.json`) matter only for code that gets ported; research them when that port's work order names them.

### Priority 1: M1 work orders that haven't started

Each work order's "Research first" section in `docs/packages/` is the exact brief.

- **R1 State and metro economies** (M1-regional): state and metro employment by industry (BLS CES state and area, QCEW), state output by industry (BEA), LAUS unemployment history to 2000, how each industry follows the national cycle and how long a state's gap lasts (requests `local-jobs-react-to-industry-shocks`, `unemployment-return-to-normal`), territory labor statistics. → `data/research/regional/`
- **R2 Credit and banks** (M1-credit): household debt by type and delinquency (New York Fed; check terms), consumer credit (Federal Reserve G.19), bank balance sheets (FDIC), who holds what (Survey of Consumer Finances), small business line decisions (request `town-bank-line-underwriting`), household underwriting from published federal guidance. → `data/research/credit/`
- **R3 Jobs** (M1-jobs): JOLTS openings, hires, quits and layoffs by industry and region (request `job-market-calibration`), the OEWS industry-by-occupation staffing matrix, unemployment duration (CPS), time to fill. → `data/research/jobs/`
- **R4 Housing** (M1-housing): every place's eviction steps from statute (notice and its length, cure period, filing, time to hearing, judgment, time to removal, right to counsel), with the section cited, for all 56; FHFA house price index by state and metro; vacancy and rental stock (Census); how fast supply responds; how often renters move. → `data/research/housing/` (new files)
- **R5 Treasuries, taxes and budgets** (M1-budgets-taxes): county and city fiscal year starts and adoption deadlines (sample per state), territory revenue by source, the share of public spending bought from local businesses (request `realized-public-money-macro-magnitudes`). → `data/research/budgets/`
- **R6 Programs reaching people** (M1-budgets-programs): unemployment insurance by state (Department of Labor's comparison of state laws), Medicaid and CHIP eligibility by state (CMS), SNAP by household size and the territories' nutrition programs (USDA), Social Security and Medicare rules (SSA, CMS), Title I and special education allocations (Department of Education), a sample of state school aid formulas, federal highway apportionments and pass-through (FHWA). → `data/research/programs/`
- **R7 Offices and seats** (M1-officials): every office's structure in all 56 (chamber sizes, districts, terms, term limits, staggering, statewide executive offices, how each vacancy is filled) from constitutions, statutes and official sites (requests `state-legislature-chamber-sizes-and-quorum`, `legislature-packs-for-every-unbuilt-jurisdiction`, `island-territory-legislatures-and-local-offices`); county and city offices by sample; officials' age, tenure and prior jobs in aggregate (request `state-legislator-age-tenure-and-district-lean`). → `data/research/offices/` These also replace Book of the States values still in code, which the owner ruled must go (Oct 11): legislature seat counts and party balances (`src/simulation/legislature-game-profile.ts`).
- **R8 Businesses** (M1-business): business births, deaths and location changes by age, size, industry and state (Census BDS, BLS BED), how owners fund a start (Census Annual Business Survey; the Small Business Credit Survey, check terms), owner pay and draws (IRS SOI), the share of establishments in multi-unit firms (CBP, BDS). → `data/research/business/`
- **R9 Moves** (M1-renewal Part 2): state and county flows (IRS migration data, ACS), components of population change, immigration from abroad including the territories, and why people move (CPS; request `migration-rates-and-reasons`). → `data/research/renewal/moves/`
- **R10 Drives from economic loss** (M1-drives-economy): how often job loss, eviction or a failed business turns into lasting anger at someone, a new ambition or joining a cause, and in whom; political participation after plant closings. → `data/research/drives/` (new files)
- **R11 Other places** (M1-other-places): Census population estimates by county and place, with Puerto Rico's municipios and the territories, and their recent rates of change. → `data/research/places/`
- **R12 Prior budgets** (M1-history Part 2): each level's adopted budgets and actual revenue and spending for the fiscal years 2021 to 2025, from Census finance data and state budget documents. → `data/research/history/`

### Priority 2: M2, running for office

- **R13 Election calendars and filing rules** (M2-02, M2-03, M2-10), all 56 for state and federal, local by sample:
  - election dates: state primaries and runoffs for the next two cycles, uniform municipal election dates where state law sets them, special election rules;
  - filing windows and deadlines by office and level; **which office takes filings** (clerk, registrar, board of elections, secretary of state) and its name pattern;
  - qualifications by office: age, residency length, citizenship, registration, felony and dual-office rules;
  - petition signature requirements (count or share of votes cast) and how many signatures are typically ruled invalid; filing fees and fee-instead-of-petition options;
  - primary type by party (open, closed, semi-open, top-two, top-four), runoff thresholds, where ranked-choice voting is used, write-in rules, independent and minor-party ballot access;
  - automatic recount margins, certification deadlines, and when winners take office;
  - what a candidate is handed at filing: the published candidate guides of secretaries of state and county clerks, cataloged with their terms (they teach how campaigns work, which M2-03's conversation needs). → `data/research/elections/` (new files)
- **R14 Campaign money** (M2-05): contribution limits by office, donor type and state (FEC; state ethics and election agencies); disclosure thresholds and deadlines; what campaigns raise and spend by level (FEC and state filings in aggregate); unit costs a campaign pays: printed mail and postage (USPS rates), yard signs, filing fees, staff pay (BLS). → `data/research/campaigns/`
- **R15 Who runs** (M2-04): how often seats are contested and how often they're open, by level; how often incumbents run again, retire or seek higher office; who candidates are compared with the population (age, sex, occupation, prior office), all in aggregate from academic and government sources (Ballotpedia excluded). → `data/research/elections/` (new files)
- **R16 Campaign effects** (M2-05): turnout and persuasion effects of door-to-door canvassing, phone calls, mail, digital and TV ads and events, from published field experiments and meta-analyses; how fast effects fade; contacts per volunteer hour; endorsement effects (party, unions, newspapers, local leaders); incumbency advantage by level. → `data/research/campaigns/`
- **R17 Polls** (M2-06): survey response rates by mode, how public polls weight, typical sample sizes and frequency by race level, and polling error by level of race. → `data/research/campaigns/`
- **R18 Voting and election administration** (M2-07): turnout by election type (primary, general, municipal, off-cycle) and by state; registration rates and deadlines (same-day registration); mail and early voting rules and usage; ballot rejection and provisional ballot rates; counting time (Election Assistance Commission's Election Administration and Voting Survey, U.S. government). → `data/research/elections/` (new files)
- **R19 Parties** (M2-08): registration by party where states report it; how party committees are organized (state, county, precinct) and how leaders are chosen; how often people switch registration; minor-party ballot access; presidential nomination rules (delegate allocation) and Electoral College allocation by state. Parties are data; record whatever parties the sources show. → `data/research/parties/`
- **R20 Basic news and word of mouth** (M2-09): local outlets per county and their reach (check terms on any news-desert dataset), news habits by age and education (Pew), how fast and how far news spreads by word of mouth (published diffusion studies), and what share of people can name their local officials. → `data/research/news/`

### Priority 3: M3, governing

- **R21 Legislative procedure** (M3-03, M3-10) for every state, Congress and a sample of councils: session calendars and lengths, special sessions, committees and how bills are referred, quorum and required votes, veto and override windows (requests `legislative-step-pacing-and-veto-override`, `how-congress-moves-bills`, `local-council-legislative-volume`, `council-ordinance-passage-vote`, `legislative-committees-and-assignment-by-chamber`, `generated-legislature-session-frequency-and-carryover`). → `data/research/legislatures/` These also replace Book of the States veto windows and executive-order rules still in code (`src/simulation/legislature-game-profile.ts`, `src/simulation/executive-authority-game-profile.ts`), owner ruling Oct 11.
- **R22 How legislatures behave** (M3-02, M3-03, M3-10): bills introduced and enacted per session by chamber, committee kill rates, how often floor votes are unanimous or close, veto rates and override success, laws passed per year by subject, and what political science finds about how members weigh district, party, conviction, colleagues and lobbying when they vote (request `how-state-legislators-vote-without-a-stated-position`). → `data/research/legislatures/`
- **R23 Bills and their amounts** (M3-01, M3-04): state and local bill drafting manuals (public), how fiscal notes are produced and by whom, and **unit costs of what governments buy** by region: a lane-mile of repaving, a sidewalk, a streetlight, a police officer or firefighter fully loaded, a teacher, a bus service hour, a park acre maintained, a water main replaced (state transportation departments, city budgets, BLS). Bills carry real amounts, so these are needed to price them. → `data/research/public-costs/`
- **R24 How laws get delivered** (M3-04): time from enactment to effect for programs and projects; how often public works run late or over budget and by how much (government audits, GAO); procurement timelines from bid to contract; program take-up rates; how long before residents notice a change. → `data/research/implementation/`
- **R25 Executive powers** (M3-05, M5-02): governors (line-item veto, appointments, emergency orders, budget role, how many appointments they make), mayor and manager forms by sample, county executive forms, how often executives veto and issue orders, cabinet sizes. → `data/research/executive/`
- **R26 Staff** (M3-06): legislative, executive and council staff sizes by state and level, their pay (BLS public-sector wages), turnover and hiring times. → `data/research/executive/`
- **R27 Interest groups and lobbying** (M3-07): registered lobbyists and spending by state and issue, who lobbies on what kinds of bills, and how groups form when a bill costs them money. → `data/research/interest-groups/`
- **R28 Constituent cases** (M3-08): what residents bring to officials and how often (city 311 open data where its terms allow; congressional casework studies), how often offices resolve them and how fast. → `data/research/constituent-cases/`
- **R29 Corruption** (M3-09): public corruption convictions by state and kind (Department of Justice Public Integrity Section reports), amounts in prosecuted bribery and kickback cases, ethics complaints and outcomes, how long cases take to surface. → `data/research/corruption/`
- **R30 Ballot measures and recalls** (M3-10): where each is allowed for state and local offices and laws, signature thresholds and windows, how often they happen and pass. → `data/research/direct-democracy/`

- **R39 Constitutions and home rule** (M3-10, laws): how often and why state constitutions are amended, by subject; home rule and how states limit local governments; how the federal Constitution has been amended. → `data/research/constitutions/`
- **R40 Schooling and college** (people's lives): who goes to college, admission and cost (NCES, College Scorecard), how students pay, credits and calendars, and how schools open and close over time. → `data/research/education/`
