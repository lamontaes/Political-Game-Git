## Failing lines by rule

### no-literary-phrasing (4)

- `outline-talk.verdict.talk-address-point-is-steering-the-wrong-way-if-you-ask-me`: "{president} is steering the wrong way, if you ask me." — The steering metaphor is vague about what is wrong.
- `outline-talk.why-first.talk-address-point-i-kept-picturing-while-went-through`: "I kept picturing {place} while {president} went through {said}." — 'Kept picturing' the town you are sitting in reads composed, not like talk at home.
- `conversation.own-view-answer-disagree.we-will-never-see-the-same-way`: "We will never see {about} the same way." — 'We will never see' is stiff and too dramatic for a talk at home.
- `conversation.own-view-answer-why.where-does-that-come-from-on`: "Where does that come from, on {about}?" — The tacked-on ', on {about}' is awkward.

### vary-the-shape (3)

- `outline-talk.why-first.talk-address-point-everything-costs-more-in-lately-so-got-to-me`: "Everything costs more in {place} lately, so {said} got to me." — Same 'X in {place}, so {said} got...' shape as 014 and 016, and 'got to me' is unclear.
- `outline-talk.why-first.talk-address-point-all-i-could-think-about-was-while-got-to`: "All I could think about was {place} while {president} got to {said}." — A near twin of 018 ('{place} while {president}...') with the same odd thought about your own town.
- `outline-talk.verdict-after.talk-address-point-which-is-why-will-have-to-try-harder-with-me-on`: "Which is why {president} will have to try harder with me on {about}." — Same 'Which is why' shape as 021, the full name again, and 'try harder with me' is clumsy.

### one-idea-to-a-sentence (3)

- `outline-talk.why-first.talk-address-point-out-of-work-since-i-couldn-t-tune-out`: "Out of work since {employer}, I couldn't tune out {said}." — 'Out of work since {employer}' puts an employer where a time belongs and reads composed.
- `outline-talk.ask.talk-address-point-does-bother-you-at-all`: "Does {said} bother you at all?" — 'Does {said}' breaks with a plural subject ('Does drug prices bother you').
- `conversation.own-view-answer-private.i-d-rather-keep-to-myself`: "I'd rather keep {about} to myself." — Keeping the measure itself 'to myself' says the wrong thing; it's the view that's kept.

### stance-is-plainly-for-or-against (2)

- `outline-talk.lead.talk-address-point-hear-that-is-going-after`: "Hear that? {president} is going after {about}." — 'Going after' reads as attacking the measure when the President is for it.
- `outline-talk.verdict-cold.talk-address-point-fine-by-me-that-is-holding-out-on`: "Fine by me that {president} is holding out on {about}." — 'Holding out on' a measure can mean resisting it or stalling, so the stand is unclear.

### no-non-replies (2)

- `outline-talk.verdict.talk-address-point-ask-me-again-once-weighs-in-on`: "Ask me again once {decider} weighs in on {about}." — It puts off the view instead of giving one.
- `outline-talk.verdict-cold.talk-address-point-give-me-a-week-with-and-i-ll-tell-you`: "Give me a week with {said} and I'll tell you." — Promises to report back in a week instead of saying anything, and 'a week with drug prices' is odd.

### "It. What is it?" (2)

- `outline-talk.why.talk-address-point-from-where-i-sit-in-it-looks-right`: "From where I sit in {place}, it looks right." — 'It looks right' never names what 'it' is.
- `outline-talk.why.talk-address-point-from-where-i-sit-in-it-looks-wrong`: "From where I sit in {place}, it looks wrong." — 'It looks wrong' never names what 'it' is.

### look-up-how-real-people-say-it (1)

- `outline-talk.verdict.talk-address-point-count-me-in-with`: "Count me in with {president-object}." — People say 'I'm with the President', not 'count me in with'.

### promise-only-what-the-speaker-can-do (1)

- `outline-talk.verdict.talk-address-point-i-d-vote-the-other-way-on-and-i-d-tell-so`: "I'd vote the other way on {about}, and I'd tell {president-object} so." — A housemate gets no vote on a federal question and will not tell the President.

### every-line-gives-its-context (1)

- `outline-talk.heard.talk-address-point-did-register-with-you-at-all`: "Did {said} register with you at all?" — 'Did drug prices register with you' is awkward and never says it was in the speech.

### no-reply-asks-what-the-listener-cannot-know (1)

- `outline-talk.why-first.talk-address-point-i-spend-my-days-at-so-got-my-attention`: "I spend my days at {employer}, so {said} got my attention." — Tells a housemate where the speaker works, which they already know, in the same shape as 014.

### tense-of-the-record (1)

- `outline-talk.ask.talk-address-point-would-you-have-gone-along-with-on`: "Would you have gone along with {president-object} on {about}?" — 'Would you have gone along' talks about a proposal still ahead as if it were already decided.
