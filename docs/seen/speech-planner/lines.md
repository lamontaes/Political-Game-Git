## St. David, Illinois, seed 7febec3d252cef05

**Lori Lopez, Wednesday, January 22, 2025**

> Lawmakers, and my fellow citizens. Tonight I want to talk with the people of Illinois about workers and their unions. This past year, collective bargaining for public workers took effect, and people across the state are already living under it.
>
> Why would we change course, when workers who get the contract chip in for what it costs? Because I hold that the right to organize built the middle class in this country, I am against right-to-work rules, and if the legislature passes them, I will veto them.
>
> I have always believed a worker standing alone has a lot less say than workers standing together. Teachers and other public workers have a seat at the table. I will keep collective bargaining for public workers, and any bill in this legislature to undo it will meet my veto.
>
> Let us say no to the right-to-work rules, and let Illinois remember that we did. Thank you.

**Sara Mullen, Tuesday, February 11, 2025**

> Members of the board, and neighbors. I am here to report on the work of our local government and to put specific proposals before the board. For 2024, we are taking in more than we spend here, and I mean to keep it so.
>
> I have always believed an officer should be judged by people who have done the job. At this moment, the department reviews complaints against its own officers. I will oppose any bill to give civilians oversight of the police.
>
> I am for a bigger police budget, and I ask the board to give the police department more to work with this year. That is because I believe people who break the law should answer for it. Today, calls for help wait longer than they should for an officer.
>
> As we stand here, the jail runs short of guards on most shifts. Because I believe the first job of government is to keep people safe, I will ask the board to staff the jail fully, and I will not let it rest.
>
> On how we keep people safe and treat them fairly, St. David has waited long enough. Let us say no to civilian oversight of the police. Thank you.

## Olympia Heights, Florida, seed bbe1df4166577b65

**Sally O'Connor, Wednesday, January 22, 2025**

> Members of the legislature, and citizens of this state. I have come to speak to Florida about who makes the rules where you live, because it reaches every family here.
>
> I am against letting cities set their own minimum wage, and if the legislature passes it, I will veto it. That is because I believe a business or a family should know the rules from one place to the next. Today, one minimum wage covers the whole state, wherever a shop opens.
>
> I believe some rules ought to be the same no matter where you live. Every town makes its own rules, and they change at the town line. I will sign a repeal of the law giving towns more power when the legislature sends it.
>
> If we say no to letting cities set their own minimum wage, Florida will be the better for it. Thank you, and good night.

**Courtney Murray, Tuesday, February 11, 2025**

> Members of the board, and my fellow citizens. I have come to speak to Miami-Dade County about how we help people through hard times, because it reaches every family here. For 2024, we are taking in more than we spend here, and I mean to keep it so.
>
> I believe the best way out of hard times is a job. As things stand, the assistance budget grows whether or not anyone gets back to work. I am against more assistance spending, and I will ask the board to vote it down.
>
> If we tie more of our aid to getting back to work, Miami-Dade County will be the better for it. Thank you, and good night.
