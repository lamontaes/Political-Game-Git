## After

The relative names one point from that world's address once (a policy the President took a side on, or what the address said about the country's year), takes their own stand and gives a reason. Each reply carries its own insert: the relative ("Mom"), the President, the town, and only one names the point. The answer to the town reply says what the world keeps: the state's own law on the question, else the town's figure. Retaken after Fable B's review of #8736.

- Anderson: "I heard the President on legal cannabis sales. Trying to legalize cannabis sales would cost more than it's worth. Whatever Russell Franklin says, I know where I stand." Replies: "What would that mean for folks in Anderson?" / "What should happen with legal cannabis sales, then?" / "Same as you, Mom." / "I don't buy what Russell Franklin said there." Answer to the first: "Missouri did that already. Anderson wouldn't notice much."
- Mount Pleasant Mills: "So the President talked about legal cannabis sales. It makes sense to me not to legalize cannabis sales. I've had my say on it before, Melissa Yang or not." Answer to "You're right, Dad.": "We'll see what Mount Pleasant Mills makes of it."
- Russell Springs: "There was the bit about pay getting ahead of prices. That's how it's been in this house, Lori Miller or not." (a point on the year, borne out by the house). Answer to "What would that mean for Russell Springs?": "This is a town with rents most folks can still manage. It matters here."

Two of the three addresses' policy point is still legal cannabis sales: the laws engine holds a federal law row for only two questions the President could raise. The address's year now gives each world more points, and a relative who follows politics less than people like them talks about the year as their own life shows it.
