## Before

The same fixed sentences in every town, about nothing the President said:

- Anderson: "Good to hear somebody talk about people like us." Replies: "I'm with you on that." / "I don't see it that way." / "We'll see what the President actually does." / "I'll wait to see if any of it reaches us."
- Mount Pleasant Mills: "Good to hear somebody talk about people like us." with nearly the same four replies.
- Russell Springs: "I've heard those promises too many times." Answer to "I'm with you on that.": "Good. I thought it was just me."

## After

The relative names the point the President made in that world's address, takes their own stand on it, and gives a reason. The line is composed from parts, and each reply names the point or the town:

- Anderson: "I heard the President on legal cannabis sales. Trying to legalize cannabis sales would cost more than it's worth. Whatever Russell Franklin says, I know where I stand." Replies: "How would it land in Anderson?" / "What would you change about legal cannabis sales?" / "I see legal cannabis sales the same way." / "I think you've got legal cannabis sales wrong." Answer: "For Anderson, I couldn't tell you yet."
- Mount Pleasant Mills: "So the President talked about legal cannabis sales. It makes sense to me not to legalize cannabis sales. I've had my say on it before, Melissa Yang or not." Answer to "You're right about legal cannabis sales.": "That makes two of us on legal cannabis sales."
- Russell Springs: "Out of that whole speech, I heard legal cannabis sales. Saying no to a plan to legalize cannabis sales is a mistake. I'd say the same if Lori Miller had never brought it up." Answer to "Who in Russell Springs would that help?": "For Russell Springs? Hard to say until it's written down."

All three addresses spoke on legal cannabis sales. In these worlds the laws engine holds a federal law row for only two questions the President could raise (an income tax and legal cannabis sales), so the address has few points to give.
