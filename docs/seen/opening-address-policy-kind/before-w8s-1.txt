Friends and fellow citizens, I greet Speaker Janice Pierce and the Congress, whose help I will need on every task I am about to set out. Across the generations, the gains on a growing paycheck add one more line to a long American record.

I am here to set out the work ahead, starting with the grocery bill, and to say plainly what each step will take. Vice President Gloria Schultz and I swore to faithfully execute the office, and that means plans, budgets and results, not slogans.

On a growing paycheck, I will protect the gains without raising taxes. The rules on the grocery bill will apply alike to my voters and my opponents'.

Across the country, pay rose considerably, and prices did not keep pace with it. Even so, more Americans are out of work than a year ago, from Georgia to the coasts. I will put finding work first, ahead of anything else on my desk.

From the first stop to the last, the families of Georgia asked for little more than a fair hearing.

To this Congress, first, Congress should ban members of Congress from trading stocks, and I will sign that bill.

Next, Congress has the power to pay for raising the debt limit, and I urge its members to use it this session.

And third, Congress has every reason to raise tariffs, and I hope it will.

Where I will not bend, if a bill on forgiving student loans comes to me, I will send it back unsigned.

I will ask a great deal of Vice President Gloria Schultz, and of myself.

To the governors and legislators of every state, I will work with you on a ban on stock trading by members of Congress, because no federal measure works well without the states beside it.

Finally, thank you for listening; I ask for your help on the grocery bill.
