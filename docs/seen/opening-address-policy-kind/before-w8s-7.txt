West Vero Corridor, Florida, seed w8s-7, Dennis Guzman

Distinguished guests, my fellow Americans, I take this office with grocery inflation at the top of the list, and I intend to say plainly what we will do about it. I am here to set out the work ahead, starting with grocery inflation, and to say plainly what each step will take. Vice President Heidi Long and I swore to faithfully execute the office, and that means plans, budgets and results, not slogans.

From the first stop to the last, I heard about rising pay from people who had seen it in their own towns and in their own work.

Over the past year, the progress on rising pay tells us which approaches are worth keeping. Across the country, earnings grew considerably, a gain that holds even after prices are counted. Even so, the jobless rate rose marginally over the past year. I will keep finding employment at the top of my list until it eases.

Every plan I send should pass a test I learned in Alaska: does it leave families freer and the budget smaller? Help will go by need, not by vote, in Alaska and everywhere.

First, I request that Congress ban members of Congress from trading stocks.

Next, I ask Congress to pay for raising the debt limit before this session ends.

And third, I will press Congress to raise tariffs until it does.

Where I will not bend, on forgiving student loans, my answer is no, and I am prepared to veto it to keep it that way.

I will ask Vice President Heidi Long to tell me when I am wrong.

To every state capitol, I will ask your offices to report to mine on a ban on stock trading by members of Congress.

Finally, thank you, and may we meet again with better news on grocery inflation.
