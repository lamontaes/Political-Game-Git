Friends and fellow citizens, I thank Vice President Gloria Schultz, and I will keep this address to the work in front of us and how we mean to do it. Across the generations, the gains on a growing paycheck add one more line to a long American record.

The first task before us is to deal with the grocery bill. This oath holds me to a growing paycheck and the work ahead.

Every plan on the grocery bill should cost less and leave families freer. Help will go by need, not by vote, in Georgia and everywhere.

Across the country, pay rose considerably, and prices did not keep pace with it. Even so, joblessness climbed a little over these months. I will put finding work first, ahead of anything else on my desk.

From the first stop to the last, the families of Georgia asked for little more than a fair hearing.

To this Congress, first, Congress has every reason to ban members of Congress from trading stocks, and I hope it will.

Next, Congress can pay for raising the debt limit this session, and I urge it to do so.

And third, the American people have waited long enough for Congress to raise tariffs.

Where I will not bend, I will oppose forgiving student loans with every tool this office provides.

I will ask a great deal of Vice President Gloria Schultz, and of myself.

To the governors and legislators of every state, I will work with you on a ban on stock trading by members of Congress, because no federal measure works well without the states beside it.

Finally, thank you for listening; I ask for your help on the grocery bill.
