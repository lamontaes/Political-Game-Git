# Ten weeks in Baltimore, running for governor

Walked 2026-09-22 09:35Z on `main` at `0f1db2d`, in Chromium 141. One life,
forty-four years old, made through the ordinary creator in Baltimore,
Maryland. Filed for governor on day one, then let ten weeks run through the
shell's week control, January 5 to March 17, 2026.

This is the first walk in this lane to let real time pass. Everything before
it looked at a first day.

## The finding

**The world moves and the press grows. The campaign does not, and nothing in
the world reacts to a candidacy being filed.**

## Filing works, and the screen it opens is good

Pressing the governor's **Put your name in** produces a real campaign:

> CALDERON FOR GOVERNOR OF MARYLAND · 232 DAYS TO GO
> Running against Martin Wooten.
> The committee has USD 0.00.
> Nobody has counted anything yet.

Under it a weekly planner: a field week or a relationships-and-fundraising
week, spent across field shifts, fundraising call sessions and advertising
buys, with "4 sessions planned; a week holds at most 7." It states its own
limits without being asked:

> How many people an advertising buy reaches is not modeled. Channel limits
> and minimum buys are game defaults.

> This proposal uses only the committee's records and schedule. It is
> attributed advice, not hidden electorate truth or a guaranteed result.

> Committing puts the sessions on your calendar. No money is spent until a
> buy is signed off.

And the legislature seats above it change their reason from "Currently
eligible under the represented rules" to "This character is already running
for something", which is the right sentence in the right place.

## The world moves, on a visible rhythm

The world-event line — the living-room television, established in the morning
walk — changed five times in ten weeks, and the changes tell a story rather
than shuffling:

| Weeks | The line                                                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–2   | Shipping delays were reported along a busy international trade route.           |
| 3     | The shipping delays along the international trade route continued.              |
| 4–5   | The national unemployment rate for January 2026 was reported at 5.1 percent.    |
| 6–7   | Shipping along the international trade route returned closer to its usual pace. |
| 8–10  | The national unemployment rate for February 2026 was reported at 5.1 percent.   |

**This qualifies the morning walk's finding.** That walk said the same
sentence sits above every screen, which is true, and read it on a first day
as never moving. It moves — every one to three weeks, when the world produces
a new lead. The complaint that survives is spatial, not temporal: one line is
visible everywhere at once. The complaint that it never changes does not
survive contact with a second week.

## The press grows, and staggers its coverage

Day one had one outlet on the front page. By March 16 the dateline reads five:

> BALTIMORE NEIGHBORHOOD NEWSLETTER · MARYLAND STATEHOUSE REVIEW · CIVIC
> LEDGER · THE EVENING COMPASS · CIVIC ARC BROADCASTING

With named reporters — "Reported by Chloe Parsons for The Evening Compass",
"Reported by Rachel Weber for Civic Arc Broadcasting" — and the same story
carried on **different dates** by different outlets: the shipping recovery
runs in Civic Ledger on February 12 and in The Evening Compass and Civic Arc
on February 17.

That is worth saying because of what is on file elsewhere: three outlets
printing the same sentence on the same day. Over ten weeks they do not. They
print the same _story_ at different times under different bylines, which is
what newspapers do.

## What did not happen in ten weeks

This is the gap, and it is one gap with several faces.

- The committee still holds **USD 0.00**. No money arrived and none was
  asked for, which is consistent — the player never committed a week — but
  the campaign also never nudged, never lapsed, never noticed.
- **"Nobody has counted anything yet"** after ten weeks of a governor's race.
- **"The campaign has no field memo yet"**, unchanged.
- **"Planning without campaign staff"**, unchanged, with no route offered to
  get any.
- The proposed next step was identical in week 1 and week 10: "Spend a
  session on the phones."
- The named opponent — Martin Wooten — did nothing visible. No opponent
  activity, no polling, no endorsement, no press about the race at all. **In
  ten weeks of front pages across five outlets, not one story mentioned the
  governor's race a player had entered.**

The last one is the finding under the finding. The press system works, the
world-event system works, and a filed candidacy is not an event either of
them knows about.
