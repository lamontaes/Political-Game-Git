# A legislative seat resolves too, on a much shorter clock

Walked 2026-09-22 12:00Z on `main` at `865a2092`, in Chromium 141. Ordinary
creator, Springfield, Illinois, age 44. Every result this lane had reached was
a governor's race, and the governorship is the one office that works
nationally while everything below it splits three ways, so "does a legislative
seat behave the same" was worth its own walk.

## It does, and it is faster

Selecting **Seat in the House of Representatives** in the Illinois General
Assembly enables filing, and filing opens a real race:

> **Yang for the House of Representatives · 28 days to go**
>
> Running against Malcolm Leonard.

Twenty-five week presses later it has resolved, on **2 February 2026**:

> Gia Yang lost. That is a thing that happened to them, not the end of them —
> tomorrow is still there.

Same shape as the governor's races, same closing sentence, a named opponent,
and a clock of **28 days rather than 302**. So a legislature race is not a
special case and is not missing; it is the same machinery on a shorter fuse.

## Two details worth keeping

**Filing establishes an election date that browsing says is not established.**
Before filing, both Illinois seats read "Upcoming election timing is not
established in this save". After filing, the band reads 28 days to go. The
office browser is describing what it knows before a filing exists rather than
reporting an absence in the world, which reads as a gap when it is not one.

**The legislative control is disabled until a seat is selected, and the screen
says so.** "Choose one of the offices above to see whether you can file for
it." An earlier walk in this lane filed for governor by accident because it
clicked the first enabled button rather than reading that line, and reported
the legislative route as unavailable in a state where it works. That is this
lane's own instrument error, corrected here, and it is the same shape as every
other one tonight: the screen said what was needed and the walk did not read
it.
