This settles a question the drafting-table walk left open and corrects

## The finding

**You can stand for governor everywhere. Below that, the country splits three
ways, and one of the three breaks a rule the owner has already set.**

In all six, the same offer, word for word:

> You may stand for Governor of your state today. Filing opens a campaign
> with nothing in it.

So "there is nothing to run for" is wrong as a general statement, and a life
started anywhere measured here can enter a real statewide race on day one.

## Three answers below the governorship

### Seats are offered — Illinois, Maryland

Both open a **Standing for something** panel listing state offices:

> Seat in the House of Representatives, Illinois General Assembly. Currently
> eligible under the represented rules. Filing rechecks them.
> Upcoming election timing is not established in this save.

Maryland the same, with the House of Delegates. The eligibility sentence and
the timing sentence are separate, which is right: one is about the player and
one is about the save.

### A residence clock has not run yet — Alaska

Sitka refuses, and names the exact reason:

> The recorded district-residence interval does not yet satisfy the sourced
> 1-year requirement.

**This answers "when would Alaska open", which has been on the not-measured
list all night.** It opens after a year of recorded residence in the
district, not never, and the blocker is a sourced rule rather than a gap.
Sitka is standable in principle; a life one day old is not.

### The state has not been read — Maine, Georgia, Arizona

All three give the same sentence:

> The game has not read this state's elected offices yet, so there is nothing
> to stand for here. It will not borrow another state's rules to fill the
> gap.

**This is the one that breaks a rule already settled.** The owner's standing
instruction is that an unresearched jurisdiction gets a realistic range drawn
nationally — not a refusal, not an average, not a median — varying state to
state, stable per state across saves, with real law always overriding. Three
of six states measured here return the refusal instead. The second half of
the sentence, about not borrowing another state's rules, is exactly right and
should survive whatever replaces the first half: a range is not another
state's rules.

So the count that matters for a report is not "how many states have sourced
law" but **how many states a player can stand for a legislature in**, and in
this sample it is two of six, with a third gated on a clock.

## A correction to this lane's own drafting-table walk

That walk, an hour ago, found a Nebraska life told:

> You can't run for office in Nebraska this early. The game knows Nebraska's
