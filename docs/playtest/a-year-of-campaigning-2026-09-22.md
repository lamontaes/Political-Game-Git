# A governor's race ends, and campaigning makes time crawl

Two walks, 2026-09-22 10:45Z on `main` with the guard in (`96221481`), in
Chromium 141. Ordinary creator, Phoenix, Arizona, age 44, no fixtures and no
injected records. **The two lives are separately generated**, so their
candidate names and opponents differ and this is not a strict A/B on one seed;
what is compared is the shape of the year, not an outcome against an outcome.

## The good finding: the race resolves

Walk one let time pass and never campaigned.

Filing opens **"Rios for Governor of Arizona · 302 days to go"** against a
named opponent, Shannon Austin. The countdown tracks the clock across
forty-eight week presses — 295, 288, 260, 218, 176, 134, 92, 50, 8 — and on
**3 November 2026** the band becomes "Decided November 3, 2026" and the screen
says:

> Jasmine Rios lost. That is a thing that happened to them, not the end of
> them — tomorrow is still there.

A whole year of play, filing to result, through the ordinary creator. The
campaign loop closes. That had never been walked before in this lane, and the
sentence it ends on is the kind of writing this game is good at.

The treasury read **"The committee has USD 0.00"** at every check across all
forty-eight weeks, because this life never campaigned. Losing may be entirely
correct.

## The finding that matters more: campaigning costs about six presses a week

Walk two committed every week the planner offered one, and cleared each stuck
session through the new give-up control.

**Sixty presses got it to 11 April 2026 — 206 days still to go.** Walk one
reached election day in forty-eight. The same controls, pressed the same
number of times, take a campaigning life about a fifth as far.

|                        | Walk one, no campaigning | Walk two, campaigning |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Presses                | 48                       | 60                    |
| Days elapsed           | 302                      | 97                    |
| Reached election day   | **yes**                  | no, 206 days short    |
| Weeks committed        | 0                        | 12                    |
| Stuck sessions cleared | 0                        | 48                    |

The mechanism is visible in the dates. A press on an uncommitted week moves
seven days. A press on a committed week moves to the next booked session —
10:00 AM, 2:00 PM, 6:00 PM, then the following morning — so the week presses
advance four hours at a time until the week's four sessions are cleared.
Forty-eight cleared sessions across twelve committed weeks is exactly the four
per week measured in `giving-up-a-stuck-commitment-2026-09-22.md`.

**So the guard's cost is not four presses once. It is four presses every week
the player campaigns, and time crawls in four-hour hops while they do it.** A
player campaigning all the way to election day would press somewhere near
three hundred times instead of forty-eight.

This is the concrete weight behind the first of the three options in
`committing-a-campaign-week-stops-time-2026-09-22.md` — let a campaign session
happen where the player is. That option removes the cost. The guard only
bounds it.

## And twelve committed weeks changed nothing visible

After twelve committed weeks of field shifts the treasury still read **USD
0.00**, and no other band on the screen had moved. Committing a week, clearing
its sessions and pressing on produced no money, no counted contacts and no
memo.

That is consistent with the ten-week walk in
`ten-weeks-in-baltimore-2026-09-22.md`, which watched a filed campaign sit
still, and it sharpens it: the stillness is not because the player did
nothing. They committed twelve weeks and the committee is still empty.

## What these walks did not establish

- **Whether campaigning changes the result.** Walk two never reached election
  day, so no outcome was compared. The honest statement is that campaigning
  changed nothing _observable during_ the race, not that it changes nothing.
- **Whether a player can ever win.** One result was reached and it was a loss,
  by a candidate who did not campaign. A win has not been seen.
- **Whether the four-hour hop is specific to campaign sessions.** Only
  campaign weeks were committed here.
