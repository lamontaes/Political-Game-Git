# Concrete life requests and activity-owned time

PLAYTEST34 SECTION LIFE supersedes the adult/formative/episode presentation's
implicit pacing after choices. It preserves the accepted shared clock and #244
study/routine semantics. The authored stakes-based 12/26/41-day and episode
34/71/128-day choice jumps are removed. Explicit quiet-time controls remain and
use the existing ordinary canonical-minute path, including due dispatch, routine
work, wages, study funding and protected activity/travel boundaries.

The prospective request writer stores versioned terms on its canonical event:
requester participant, concrete task, spoken opening, optional offered limit,
and actual performance minutes when applicable. Projections bind to that exact
saved event and canonical person relation. Missing old terms stay missing; old
history is not rewritten using current content. The versioned event-tag payload
uses the existing World codec; it adds no parallel save or fact store.

A proofreading answer appends a response tied to its exact request. Agreement
creates an expectation, not a completed favor or strengthened relationship.
Performance explicitly schedules and completes the saved twenty-minute activity,
sends wording feedback and records the one performance interaction. An overlap
with another scheduled activity or travel blocks performance without charging
time. Withdrawal terminates the agreement at zero minutes. Terminal requests
return the same World on another completion/withdrawal attempt, including reload.
The existing aftermath writer/evaluator may raise the same request later or let
it rest. Returned prose binds the original person/task/condition and reads actual
performance or withdrawal; it cannot assume either from agreement alone.

An individual dialogue, reading a dialogue line, changing topic, leaving the
conversation or browsing history costs zero minutes. A sustained activity (read,
rest, draw, spend half an hour together, travel, work or study) retains its explicit
interval. Opening choices declare actual elapsed minutes by action rather than
charging the whole surrounding scene for any click. Stop-reading does not charge
again; read-more retains its disclosed five minutes. Repeated half-hour together
is unavailable in the same scene after completion. Speech itself grants no skill
or personality grind. Activity and due handlers remain the canonical owners of
work, wages and tuition outcomes.

Dialogue reads the speaker's age and canonical kinship/authority relation.
An adult parent talking to a child is not voiced as a child; scene-topic replies
use saved premises. Generic optional personal questions remain available as
explicit topic choices. Surname, art filename or player age does not establish
the counterpart's family role or voice.

Regression ownership: `playtest34-life.test.ts` covers ordinary request binding,
reload, terminal repeat guards, callback identity, whole-interval conflicts,
ten free lines and one illustrative sixty-minute meeting, plus family speech.
Existing opening/routine/study suites retain real reading, travel, pay and study
intervals. The ordinary replay command is:

```sh
node --import tsx scripts/life-proofs/playtest34-life.ts /private/tmp/life-proof.json
```

Fixture intervals are illustrative authored test values, not universal life or
meeting durations. Automated browser proof is technical evidence; owner visual
and narrative acceptance remains a separate gate.

Ordinary NPC activity offers are persisted as versioned terms on the actual conversation event, including actor, supported activity, duration and condition. Responses reference that exact offer. A still-valid offer is not a new willingness evaluation; a changed active privacy need yields a specific withdrawal reason. Agreement and cancellation are free; the disclosed existing30-minute company action is performed once per scene. Optional OrdinaryLifeDayAdvance and transitionHandlers caller seams carry the composed S clock through story waits and actual opening activities. They default to the existing clock and never introduce another scheduler.
