# Traits

A trait is a fictional tendency in how a person acts. It is never read off a
real person, never inferred from a name or a place, and never shown to a
player as a number. This page is the route a trait takes to a decision today.

## The route

A trait reaches a decision through three tables and one chooser. No decision's code names a trait.

1. **The packs say which traits exist.** Three packs declare them, each one a
   `TraitPack` loaded by `loadTraitPacks` (`src/core2/trait-packs.ts`):
   - `people-trait-pack.ts`: the five ordinary-life traits (deliberation,
     sociability, conflict, reliability and risk), under `people-mind-v1`;
   - `legislature-trait-pack.ts`: the legislator's traits, under
     `legislature-v1`;
   - `personality-catalogue.ts`: the named qualities a person can be known
     for, under `personality-v1`. Its scales are
     `src/core2/data/personality-scales.json`, derived from the personality research and locked to it by a test.
2. **The act pulls say what a trait leans toward.**
   `data/content/trait-act-pulls.json` has one row per trait (98 rows). Each
   row names, for the trait's high side and its low side, the act kinds it
   pulls toward and away from, with the research behind both poles.
3. **The decision table says what each option expresses.**
   `data/content/trait-decisions.json` lists every decision a person makes,
   its options and the act kinds each option expresses
   (`data/content/act-kinds.json` is the list of kinds). A decision is either
   `chooser` (scored by `src/core2/choice.ts`) or `formula` (the module adds
   `traitLean`). `src/core2/trait-decisions.ts` reads the table for modules.
4. **The chooser reads both.** `kindLean` works out a person's pull on every
   act kind once, weighted by how far each trait sits from the middle.
   `traitLean` turns that into one term between -1 and 1 for any two sets of
   act kinds. Every labeled decision, chooser or formula, gets its trait term
   from these two functions.

So a trait the pulls file names reaches every decision whose options express
an act kind it pulls on. Calm leans toward keeping things the same, and the
live job-review, staying-together and stay-put options all express that.

## Adding a trait

Two data rows and no code:

1. Declare it in a pack (a row in the pack's trait list, or a scale row for the
   personality catalog).
2. Add its row to `data/content/trait-act-pulls.json`.

`src/core2/trait-coverage.test.ts` holds the code to the decision table, and `data/content/trait-coverage.json` (generated by `scripts/traits/trait-coverage.ts`) lists each trait's decisions.

## Adding a decision

Add it to `data/content/trait-decisions.json` with its options and the act
kinds each option expresses. The coverage test finds every decision in code that weighs traits and fails one that
is not in the table.

## What a trait never does

- It never decides an outcome by a roll. Traits weigh options; the world
  (money, ties, the law in force) weighs them too, and the choice is the one
  with the most reasons (`no-dice`).
- It never shows as a number on a screen. Screens use the pole label and the
  words from the English engine.
- The played character's own traits never decide anything for the player.
  They reach other people only through how the character is portrayed to them.

## Debugging

`trait-probe.ts` is a switch that costs one null check when it is off. With it
on, `scripts/traits/trait-wiring-audit.ts` counts which decisions read traits
and which option each lean moved.
