# Time

Simulated time and limited attention organize play.

## Rules

- The world owns a monotonic simulated date independent of wall-clock time.
- State changes occur through explicit simulation actions with deterministic ordering.
- Events record when they occurred and when they were recorded; scheduled effects retain enough identity to execute reproducibly.
- Detailed periods may use weekly play, while routine or distant activity may be summarized at coarser resolution.
- Time and attention are meaningful resources. Greater responsibility should produce more competing consequential demands, not proportional clerical clicking.
- Delegation and summary are normal handling for routine work.
- Inactive people and geography may advance at lower resolution without ceasing to exist.

The first build implements UTC-safe date-only arithmetic, exact whole-day differences, a deterministic positive-day advancement action, a system audit record, and one synthetic demo occurrence. Stage 4 historical reasoning uses both an as-of date and an exclusive append-sequence cutoff: date alone cannot prevent a later-appended backdated record from leaking into an earlier decision. Stage 6 metric, observation, causal, effect, baseline, estimate, and realization queries use the same two-dimensional cutoff.

Temporary internal states use effective-dated half-open intervals, `[startsAt, endsAt)`. A state may contribute to a tagged decision while active, remains inspectable after expiry, and does not require a continuous mood tick. Advancing time does not by itself run population opinion formation or autonomous character development.

Stage 5 implements a bounded time-demand and recovery seam. Active work roles, care responsibilities, and exceptional commitments carry an expected weekly range plus attention, concurrency, rigidity, interruptibility, and optional location constraint. Education enrollment and organization participation do not duplicate those scheduling fields; content that imposes meaningful recurring demand records an ordinary life commitment. Qualitative load derives both expected and exclusive-equivalent demand so concurrent low-attention care is not treated like equally long exclusive work. A completed seven-day period can be explicitly resolved with normal, push, or recover effort and a recovery level; overload can create later fatigue, and substantial recovery can restore capacity. Fatigue reuses the existing half-open temporary-state system and is not a parallel clock or player-facing meter.

Run B formative progression uses date intervals and sparse anchor situations. It may use the existing positive-day advancement action to move a simulated world through a selected interval, but does not introduce a second scheduler, weekly childhood loop, 168-hour accounting model, or automatic decision tick.

Run C compensation/resource cadence is an effective arrangement term, not a second clock or automatic transaction scheduler. Time advancement alone never fabricates a paycheck, support payment, missed obligation, debt payment, or housing state change; content explicitly resolves an actual outcome. Meaningful relationship contact may reuse an ordinary life commitment when it consumes consequential time, while routine messages and periods of inactivity produce no automatic history or decay tick.

Stage 6 Run A adds the one generic persistent future due-item mechanism and integrates it into `advanceWorld`; it does not add a second time API. A due identity contains its due date, open transition key, stable domain references, jurisdiction/provenance, and append-oriented state—not an opaque payload, closure, formula, recurrence rule, or copied domain record. Scheduled work due today is valid pending end-of-day work; only a latest scheduled item strictly before the current date is overdue/invalid. An advance selects due-today as well as later crossed items, resolves them by due date and then creation/global sequence through injected deterministic nonserialized handlers, and cannot commit time past a pending due-today item. The framework exposes the due date as valid handler chronology, requires exactly one resolved/blocked/cancelled terminal state, and optionally links an ordinary outcome event. Handlers can use normal validating canonical writers, including scheduling a later follow-on; terminal items never rerun.

Before a proposed advance commits, every currently due/crossed transition key must have a handler. Missing or failing handlers, input mutation, rewriting existing scheduler history, and invalid outcome links reject the entire immutable result; the caller's world is unchanged. A handler may append a new future scheduled item through the canonical scheduler, but cannot rewrite an existing due lifecycle. Worlds without pending due items retain prior behavior. Synthetic handlers prove the contract, while elections, policy phases/sunsets, terms, appointments, incidents, mortality, finance recurrence, and other domain transitions remain with their owning later systems.

Stage 6 Run B aggregate evaluation is explicit and writes no automatic daily/monthly/quarterly tick. Stage 6 Run C may schedule exactly one typed delayed realization for one current estimate through the existing due-item mechanism. Its due date is the earliest operation start. If later history supersedes that scheduled estimate or implements its alternative, the due item terminally cancels at the frontier with a typed reason; generic time advancement therefore crosses the frontier without substituting or repeating policy implementation. Otherwise it resolves once through normal policy writers and the ordinary terminal due lifecycle. Integrity rejects duplicate or stale newly scheduled policy due work; it adds no recurrence, parallel clock, or opaque recurrence expression.

The current load thresholds are deterministic gameplay calibration rather than a health, disability, labor, or family-quality model. Calendars, hourly schedules, recurrence/billing/payroll, player weekly mode, automatic scheduled decisions, health consequences, and multi-resolution population processing remain deferred. See [Core Life](life.md) and [World State, Observations, and Future Transitions](world-state-and-observations.md).
