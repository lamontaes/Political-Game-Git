# Nationwide chief executives

Every jurisdiction that has a chief executive of its own: the fifty states, and
the District of Columbia separately. This is the NATIONWIDE1 scope for the
executive layer; local general-purpose governments are in
[jurisdiction-portability](./jurisdiction-portability.md) and
`nationwide-world/local-governments.ts`.

## Three kinds of rule, never merged

A term date comes from exactly one of these, and the game says which:

| Basis        | What it is                                                             | Where it lives                                        |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Admitted     | a value RULES admits through rules-capability/v1                       | `rule-capability-resolver.ts`                         |
| Verified     | an instrument this repository read and hash-locked, with its own words | the `VERIFIED` map in `state-executive-term-rules.ts` |
| Game profile | the game's own disclosed rule, versioned and labelled                  | `STATE_EXECUTIVE_GAME_PROFILE`                        |

An admitted value beats a verified one; a verified one beats the profile. The
profile is never a claim about law, and it is never copied from a neighbouring
state.

## Calibration is evidence, not promotion

`chief-executive-baseline.ts` carries one research row per jurisdiction: which
office is its chief executive, how many years its ordinary full term runs, the
source that was read, and the class of that evidence. It comes from the
NATIONWIDE1 checkpoint-3 input, regenerated by
`npm run export:chief-executive-baseline` from
`data/research/nationwide1/chief-executive-baseline-51.json`.

That research sets the term LENGTH the game profile uses — so New Hampshire and
Vermont run two years — and nothing else. It does not make the rule verified.
The basis stays `game-profile`, `sources` stays empty, and the research travels
beside the rule as `calibration`, with a sentence saying what was read and what
was not. For the governors that evidence is an institutional summary rather
than fifty constitutions; for the District it is the official Code section,
cited but not retrieved word for word here.

The election cycle follows the term, because an office cannot be elected less
often than its term ends.

## The District of Columbia is one government

`district-of-columbia.ts` is the whole of it. Two things are true at once and
both are load-bearing:

- The District is **not** a state. Its chief executive is a Mayor, under the
  Home Rule Act as the official Code carries it, and nothing derives it from
  the governor template. `US_STATE_USPS` stays at fifty, so congressional
  seats, electors and statewide contests keep counting states;
  `CHIEF_EXECUTIVE_JURISDICTIONS` is the fifty-one.
- The District is **one** government. The Census listing records a single
  general-purpose government for it — the city — and no county government for
  county area 11001. So the district-wide scope and that city unit resolve to
  the same jurisdiction (`governingJurisdictionIdFor`), and the District gets
  one office, one jurisdiction and one public account rather than a
  district-wide shadow of a government that is already there.

What none of this claims: how the Mayor is elected, when a term begins, what
the Council may pass, or what Congress may do with any of it. Those are rules,
and they arrive through the capability port or not at all.
