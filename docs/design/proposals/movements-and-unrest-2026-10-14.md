# Movements and protests come from people who share a loss

Superseded by docs/coordination/OWNER-DECISIONS.md (the owner ruled on movements on Oct 14: in early access, with police, the Guard, boycotts and war at 1.0)

The recommendation: build movements from what happens to people, and bring marches, petitions, primary challenges, boycotts and the player's part into early access. Your October 10, 2026 roadmap put movements after early access, so that needs your yes. Your decision register already settles most of the rest, and the proposal quotes it rather than asking again. Five questions remain at the end. A movement here is people who share a loss, people they trust who speak for it, and news and speeches that spread it. About half the pieces exist; the crowd, strikes, police and war do not.

## What other games do

- **Democracy 4.** Each voter belongs to several voter groups at once, and a policy moves how many belong and how angry they are. Anger grows into protests and "situations" the player must handle. It gets the chain from policy to anger to protest right. It is wrong for us because the player runs a whole country through meters, with no single life underneath. Sources: gamepretty.com/democracy-4-basic-gameplay-guide, democracygame.fandom.com.
- **Victoria 3.** Interest groups and ordinary people back political movements, and a movement's radicalism drifts toward a target set by living standards. Better living standards make loyalists and take radicals away; enough radicalism brings civil war or secession. This is the closest match to "a loss in daily life becomes a movement." It runs on large blocs, and some of its triggers are random events, which we cannot use. Sources: Paradox developer diary 130; thegamer.com on radicals and revolution.
- **Crusader Kings 3.** Unhappy lords join a faction; when the faction is strong enough it issues an ultimatum, and the ruler gives in or fights a civil war. Play continues as the heir. The ladder of support, demand, then a deal or a fight fits us, and so does continuing as the heir. Its factions are lords with armies, not citizens. Source: Paradox developer diary 19, factions and civil wars.
- **Suzerain.** A president in a country leaving dictatorship, in a branching story with one save and no reloading. It gets permanence and tension right: you live with a choice without knowing how it lands. Its crises are written storylines, which we have ruled out. Sources: Wikipedia; blog.dante.cool review. Our research did not confirm how its protests work in detail.
- **Tropico.** Unmet needs lead to protest, crime or rebellion; factions make demands; each citizen has their own loyalty. Per-person loyalty is close to what we want. Rebels as an action minigame and the dictator frame are not. Source: Wikipedia, Tropico 4.
- **Frostpunk.** Two city-wide meters, hope and discontent, move with your laws; hold them too far too long and the people overthrow you. It makes a hard choice cost trust, which is right. A city-wide meter with a hard fail line breaks our rules twice. Sources: Wikipedia; macmyths.com on its laws and hope.
- **Rebel Inc.** Each district's support grows with services and shrinks with fear, and spending causes inflation. Spending wins support but raises prices, and that trade is worth keeping. It is a top-down counterinsurgency game with no individual life. Sources: rebelinc.wiki.gg.
- **The Political Process,** the baseline, has scripted events and a "dictator mode" you have never ruled on. Our version has no scripted events; dictator mode stays unplanned until you say.

What we take: Victoria 3's link from daily life to grievance, Crusader Kings 3's ladder and heir play, and Suzerain's tension. What we leave: meters, fail lines and random triggers. One idea from your own research note of September 28, 2026 replaces the meter: each person acts once enough of the people they watch have acted, and how many is "enough" comes from that person's record. That one rule covers joining a march, a strike, a boycott and a bandwagon.

## What a movement is in this game

A movement is three things read together:

1. **A shared loss.** Many people hold the same cause: the same topic and the same change wanted, from things that happened to them. A plant closing, a rent increase, a new law, a scandal, a police killing.
2. **People with standing who speak for it.** Someone the others have heard of and trust: an official, a pastor, a union steward, a candidate, or the player.
3. **Reach.** Ways the cause spreads past the people who lived it: news stories, speeches, retellings and word of mouth.

None of the three is a stored number. A movement's "size" is the people who hold the cause and have acted on it, counted when someone asks. Its "strength" is never a field; each engine reads only what it needs, such as how many people marched or how many customers stopped buying.

**At both tiers.**

- **Named people** hold the cause as their own drive, join the movement's group, speak, march and vote. Their reasons are recorded.
- **The counted population** holds it as counts: in this town, this many workers aged 30 to 44 in the jobs the closure hit hold the cause. The counts come from the same causes that hit the named people. When the player meets a marcher, that person is filled in as one of the counted marchers, with the cause and the reason already on record.

**What already exists.**

Each item below is measured from the code on October 14, 2026.

- **Causes a person holds.** A person can hold a cause or a grudge with a topic, the change they want, and a strength that fades without new fuel (`src/core2/modules/drives.ts:172`). Losing a job, a closure, an eviction, a health event and hearing a speech can each start or grow one (`src/core2/data/drives.json`).
- **Cause groups.** A person with a strong cause can already found a cause group, and others can join it, share the cause, donate and volunteer (`src/core2/modules/drives.ts:1276`).
- **Speeches.** A speech already moves its listeners' positions on an issue, and listeners retell it to their households (`src/core2/modules/speech.ts:99`).
- **News.** Any public event already becomes a news story that reaches people by their news habits and passes along through their contacts (`src/core2/modules/news.ts:57`).
- **Name recognition.** Campaigns already record, for each voter and candidate, whether the voter has heard of the candidate and how warmly they feel (`src/core2/modules/campaigns.ts:70`).
- **Collective action, in one place.** Recalls already have one form of it: a law takes effect, the most aggrieved resident organizes, people sign, and a recall election follows (`src/core2/modules/recalls.ts:112`).

**What is missing, plainly.** Each item is measured from the code on October 14, 2026.

- No movement, protest, strike, boycott or unrest exists in the new core. The old core had a protest file and a movement-succession file. The old pressure ladder fired unrest at a fixed anger line, and the porting plan has already ruled it out (`docs/design/core2-porting-plan.md:333`).
- **A cause group affects nothing else.** It has no leader, no demands and no activity, and no other engine reads it; it is an organization plus a lookup private to the drives engine (`src/core2/modules/drives.ts:1295`).
- **Three causes have no source.** No event starts a cause when a law's effect reaches a person, when a person is a crime victim, or when they witness an injustice (the "gaps" list in `src/core2/data/drives.json:647`).
- **Counted workers feel nothing.** When counted workers lose their jobs, the layoff names nobody, so none of them forms a cause (`src/core2/modules/jobs.ts:614`).
- **Opinion never reaches the vote.** A person's left-right lean is set at the start and never moves. Positions on issues move, but voting does not read them (`src/core2/modules/views.ts:11`; the only reader of positions is `src/core2/modules/speech.ts:89`).
- **Nothing gathers a crowd.** Every speech and meeting has a chosen audience, and nothing counts a turnout.
- No strikes, unions or walkouts exist in the jobs engine.
- No police, National Guard or military exist as people or bodies that make decisions; police are only a budget line and crime rates in the town's outcome measures (`src/core2/modules/outcome-web.ts:598`).
- No war engine exists, and no foreign government; the porting plan says military service has no engine anywhere (`docs/design/core2-porting-plan.md:88`).
- No new party can be founded, and no endorsement comes from a group; parties are fixed at the opening (`src/core2/modules/parties.ts`).
- **The story director has no public moments.** It has 31 kinds of scene, all private human moments. None is a rally, a march or a crowd (`src/director/data/situation-types.json`).

## How one starts and grows

A movement starts the day a cause lands on many people at once, and it grows only as fast as people act. There is no monthly chance anywhere.

**The causes it reads:**

- A law takes effect and reaches people: a fee they now pay, a service they lose, a benefit they gain.
- A price moves: rent, groceries or gas, read against what the household earns.
- A layoff wave or a closure in the town.
- A scandal surfaces about an official.
- A speech by someone with standing names a cause the listener already half holds.
- A broad event: a killing of a prominent person, a disaster, a recession.

**How a person joins.** Each person looks at the people they know: family, coworkers, neighbors, their congregation. They act once enough of those people have acted. How many is enough depends on their own record:

- how strongly they hold the cause;
- what acting would cost them (a job they could lose, children at home, savings);
- their traits (risk, conformity, temper);
- whether someone they trust asked them.

A bold person with a strong cause goes first. A cautious parent with a mortgage goes only when half their street has gone. A speech by a trusted figure lowers the bar for the listeners who trust that figure.

The counted population joins the same way. Each counted group holds a spread of these bars, set from the traits and circumstances of the people it counts and checked against real turnout at protests. The spread is fixed for that town; it is not drawn fresh each day.

**Why it grows or stalls.** Because each person waits on the people they know, a movement can sit quiet for months and then spread in a week once the first bold few act. Inferred, not yet sourced: the 1955 Montgomery bus boycott and the 1937 Flint sit-down strike look like this pattern, and the research on rights movements will check it. It can also stall in a town where the bold few have nobody watching them. Nothing flips at a line; each person's bar is different.

**Speed.** The engine acts only on the day something changed: a cause landed, someone acted, a speech was heard. It never scans the whole town every day.

## What it does in the world

Each action is a record that other engines read when they decide or pay. That is what makes it real.

| Action            | What it is                                                         | Who reads it, and what changes                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| March or rally    | A gathering on a day, at a place, with a named and counted turnout | The press covers it, sized by turnout and by who spoke. Views move for the people who hear of it. Officials who decide on the cause read it as a recorded reason. Candidates read the turnout when they choose to run.                             |
| Petition drive    | Signatures toward a recall, where the place allows one             | Recalls already read signatures. A movement supplies the organizer and signers.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Primary challenge | A member with standing files against an incumbent                  | Candidacy reads the movement as the challenger's cause and as part of their name recognition. The primary is the one that already exists.                                                                                                          |
| Boycott           | Members stop buying from a business                                | The business engine reads lost sales. The owner then decides to give in, wait or cut costs, from the owner's own books.                                                                                                                            |
| Strike            | Workers stop working                                               | The jobs engine reads missing work. Unpaid wages hit households; the business loses revenue. Owner and workers each decide from their own money. **Missing:** no strikes or unions exist.                                                          |
| New party         | A movement founds a party                                          | Parties and the ballot read it. **Missing:** no party can be founded today.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Unrest (rare)     | A gathering that turns to damage                                   | Businesses read damaged premises and closed days; budgets pay for the damage and the response; insurance and prices follow. Officials decide on a response. Depicted without graphic detail. **Missing:** no police or Guard as people who decide. |

The story director reads all of it. Each action is a cause for moments in the lives of the people it named or reached, the same way a closure is today.

## How the player meets it

- **Asked to speak.** A movement's people want someone with standing. If the player has it, through office, a campaign or a name, they come and ask, for their own reasons. Saying yes ties the player's name to the cause in every listener's record. Saying no is remembered too.
- **Asked to join.** A friend, a coworker or a pastor who already joined asks the player. The ask comes from that person's own reasons, the same chooser every person already uses.
- **Targeted.** As an official, the player casts votes, and a movement's demands read those votes. A march may come to the player's office. A primary challenger may file. A recall may start. Every one of these names the vote it read.
- **Founding one.** The player can found a cause group today. With this proposal it gains a leader, demands and reach, and grows only as fast as people act.
- **Inheriting one.** When the player's character dies or steps away, the members choose who leads next from their own reasons, as the written spec for life after office and passing on a movement already lays out (bank spec b20). If the player continues as that person, they inherit the members and their goodwill, not the parent's friendships.
- **Riding the wave as an heir.** This is the Indianapolis test. A grieving crowd gathers after a killing. The heir has standing because people knew the parent, and the crowd's own records decide what the heir's words do. Their grief, their trust in the heir, whether local pastors and organizers are present, how the police posture reads to them: the same speech can calm one city and do nothing in another.

**The scenes.** The scenes come from the director's existing types where they fit: a request for a favor, advice, a warning, news arriving. The march, the rally and the crowd need new public kinds of scene, which package 6 builds; your register ruling lets designs that carry out the emergent-not-authored rule be built without coming back to you.

## How it ends

- **Absorbed by a party.** A party adopts the demand into its platform, and members' reasons for staying in the movement weaken as their party carries the cause.
- **A law passes.** Members' causes are met. Each member's cause fades as the law's effect reaches them, not on the day of the vote.
- **Repression.** An official orders arrests or a crackdown, within the law in force. Its effect depends on who sees it. Members who are afraid leave. People who hear of it and already lean toward the cause may join, Inferred, not yet sourced: televised repression in Birmingham in 1963 is the example. Whether repression shrinks or grows a movement depends on who saw it; the filed research on rights movements sets how much each side weighs.
- **Fading.** Nothing new feeds the cause, and each member's cause fades on its own half-life.
- **The leader dies or is jailed.** The members choose a successor, or split, or drift.

## How the rare extremes become possible without scripting

- **A demagogue.** A demagogue is a candidate whose platform reads a movement's grievance and whose standing comes from that movement. The game needs no special type. It needs candidacy to read movements, which this proposal adds. Whether that candidate is dangerous is in their traits and principles, which the game already records.
- **A realignment on the 1933-to-1945 scale.** This needs four things:
  - a national shock, which the macro economy engine can produce;
  - movements in many places at once;
  - a party taking up their cause;
  - people's left-right lean moving over years.

  The first three come from this proposal. The fourth is already your ruling (quoted below): a person's views come from their life, and their party follows their views. The engine breaks that ruling today, because lean never moves. Fixing it is a package in the build order, not a question.

- **An overthrow.** An overthrow is a movement plus a security force that stops obeying, each officer reading their own records: pay, orders, the law, who they trust, what they saw. No security force exists as people who decide, so overthrow is a 1.0 question. Until then the game cannot produce one, and dev mode will say so.
- **Drift toward war.** No war engine and no foreign governments exist. Your October 8, 2026 ruling says war waits on its own design with you. A movement can hold a cause about a foreign dispute, and that is all until that design exists.

## What the real cases say

The capability study written on October 14, 2026 (`docs/research/capability-cases-2026-10-14.md`) checked 40 real episodes against the engines. None of the 40 runs start to finish today. Two of its ten missing pieces are this proposal's subject: organized blocs and movements, needed by 14 cases, and crowds and violence from grievance, needed by 9. The study assigns both to OPUS-PEOPLE, as this proposal's build order does. The cases below test the design; none is a script.

**Cases that support the design.**

- **Huey Long's Share Our Wealth, 1928 to 1935 (case 24).** A governor turned mass anger at the Depression into a national movement of clubs. After his killing, the opposing president took in much of the program. This is the demagogue as a candidate whose standing comes from a movement, and the ending "absorbed by a party." The study also needs patronage (its gap U9) and family name (U17) for this case.
- **Robert F. Kennedy in Indianapolis, 1968 (case 25).** The study's reading: a candidate who had lost a brother told a crowd of a leader's killing in plain words, the city stayed calm that night, and a coalition carried him to primary wins. After he was shot, the delegates went to party organizers. That last step supports the ending "the leader dies": without its leader, a movement loses ground to the party's own people.
- **The Moral Majority and the 1980 evangelical vote (case 28).** Pastors registered their members around school, tax and moral questions. The study reports that the shift varied with how strongly local organizing reached people. That is the design's join rule: people act through the people and leaders they know, not from a national number.
- **The Ohio collective-bargaining repeal, 2011 (case 13).** Citizens gathered about 1.3 million signatures and knocked on a million doors, and the referendum repealed the law. It supports the petition drive as an action, beyond recalls.

**Cases that change the design.**

- **The Scott Walker recall, 2012 (case 12).** Huge protests and over 900,000 signatures did not remove the governor; the voters who turned out matched the ones who elected him. A march must therefore be a cause that voters and officials read, never a score that decides the election. The proposal already says this. The case makes it a test: in a played year, a large march must be able to fail at the ballot box.
- **Britain's Winter of Discontent, 1979 (case 30).** Public-sector strikes against a pay limit helped bring down a government. Strikes need the study's labor-disputes gap (U11) and a kept measure of how voters rate each official (U4). This raises the weight of question 2 below on strikes.
- **The Poll Tax revolt, 1989 to 1990 (case 31).** Non-payment spread, a demonstration ended in riots, and the prime minister's own party removed her. Two pieces the proposal did not name are needed: a party or body removing its own leader (the study's U5), and the voters' rating of each official (U4). Mass refusal to pay a tax is a form of boycott aimed at a government, not a business; the proposal adds it to the boycott package as a 1.0 case.
- **Movements need the vote to listen.** The study's gap U3 (voters weigh what they heard about an official, not an on-off scandal flag) and U4 (a rating for each official) are owned by OPUS-GOV and OPUS-CORE. Without them a movement can march, but no election reads what voters thought of it. Package 5 below depends on them.

## Build order

9. **1.0, each its own package once you rule:** strikes and unions (OPUS-WORLD), new parties (OPUS-GOV), realignment tests in dev mode (OPUS-PEOPLE with OPUS-GOV), inheriting a movement (OPUS-PLAY with OPUS-PEOPLE), and the security forces with unrest (a new engine; owner unassigned until you rule). ## Method

- public pages on the seven games;
- a read of the new core's drives, views, interest-group, speech, press, news, campaign, party, candidacy, recall, ballot-measure and electorate engines, and of the director's scene types.
