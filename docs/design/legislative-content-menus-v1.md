## Proposed subject menus

The proposed catalog has 15 core domestic subject menus and three additional menus covering national-policy subjects. These are content headings, not a requirement to display 18 wheel segments. Assistance receives its own heading instead of being buried inside healthcare. National subjects are retained rather than treating the state/local vocabulary as the full game's ceiling. Government profile and office authority determine available actions; a heading never grants power. Veterans services and other applicable subjects can also be reached from state/local contexts.

### 01. Infrastructure

Transportation; water and wastewater; flood control and drainage; waste collection and disposal; public buildings and facilities; digital infrastructure (linked to Technology).

Transportation expands to Roads & Bridges; Rail; Public Transit; Aviation; Ports & Waterways; Walking & Cycling. Rail then separates passenger and freight service. Physical water infrastructure is distinct from water-quality regulation.

### 02. Healthcare

Coverage and insurance; hospitals and clinics; mental health and addiction services; public health; medicines and medical products; reproductive health; long-term care; healthcare workforce.

### 03. Education

Early childhood and preschool; K–12 schools; school funding; school facilities; teachers and support staff; special education; colleges and universities; student aid; vocational and adult education.

### 04. Taxes & Public Finance

Taxes; fees and charges; budgets and appropriations; grants and transfers; debt and borrowing; reserves and fiscal rules; fiscal and monetary institutions.

### 05. Assistance & Social Insurance

Food assistance; cash assistance; child-care assistance; disability support; unemployment benefits; retirement and survivor benefits; elder and caregiver support; utility-bill assistance.

### 06. Housing & Land Use

Planning and zoning; building permits and standards; affordable and public housing; rental rules; homeownership assistance; homelessness services; neighborhood redevelopment.

### 07. Labor & Employment

Wages and working hours; paid and unpaid leave; workplace health and safety; hiring and employment protections; unions and collective bargaining; workers’ compensation; employment training; pensions.

### 08. Business & Economic Development

Business formation and licensing; small-business finance; development incentives; competition and market rules; banking and credit; insurance markets; manufacturing and industry; commercial development.

### 09. Energy & Environment

Electricity and the grid; fuels and extraction; energy efficiency and renewables; utility regulation; air and emissions; water quality; land, wildlife and conservation; pollution cleanup; climate and resilience.

### 10. Justice & Public Safety

Policing; courts and legal aid; criminal law and sentencing; prisons, probation and reentry; juvenile justice; firearms and weapons rules; fire and emergency medical services; emergency management and disaster recovery.

### 11. Government & Democracy

Elections and representation; campaign finance; ethics and conflicts; public records and meetings; agencies and public employment; procurement; local-government powers; intergovernmental relations; constitutional and institutional reform.

Constitutional changes, chamber rules and other distinct instruments retain their own supported processes rather than being disguised as ordinary bills.

### 12. Rights, Family & Consumer Protection

### 13. Science, Technology & Communications

### 14. Agriculture & Food

### 15. Parks, Culture & Community Life

### 16. Immigration & Citizenship

These menus describe legislative subject matter. Negotiating an agreement, commanding forces or setting an interest rate is not automatically an ordinary bill-editor action.

## Next-level content: four expanded branches

These examples establish menu depth without requiring a separate interface for every policy.

### Infrastructure > Transportation

Roads & Bridges: new construction; repair and replacement; inspections; maintenance priorities; local road aid; tolls and user charges; crossings and road safety.

Rail: passenger service; freight service; tracks and stations; crossings; operating support; fares and discounts; service requirements.

Public Transit: bus and local rail services; routes and frequency; fares and passes; accessibility; fleet and depot facilities.

Aviation: airports and terminals; runway maintenance; airport grants; airport access; noise and land-use requirements.

Ports & Waterways: ports and terminals; navigation and dredging; locks and canals; ferry facilities; freight access.

Walking & Cycling: sidewalks; crossings; cycling routes; accessibility; maintenance.

### Taxes & Public Finance > Taxes

Individual Income Tax: rates and brackets; taxable income; deductions; exemptions; credits; withholding; indexing.

Corporate & Business Taxes: rates; taxable profits or receipts; deductions; credits; minimum taxes.

Sales & Consumption Taxes: rates; covered goods and services; exemptions; collection responsibilities.

Property Taxes: valuation and assessment; rates; exemptions and relief; levy limits; taxing authority.

Payroll & Social-Insurance Contributions: covered earnings; employer and employee contributions; caps; exemptions.

Excise & Activity Taxes: fuel; alcohol and tobacco; extraction; tourism and lodging; other supported activities.

Fees and charges remain a separate sibling branch. A fee amount is not estimated total revenue. Tax liabilities, collections, appropriations and actual payments remain separate.

### Healthcare > Coverage and Insurance

Public coverage programs: eligibility; covered services; cost sharing; provider participation; reimbursement.

Private insurance rules: required coverage; exclusions; enrollment; pricing rules; appeals.

Premium and out-of-pocket assistance: eligible households; assistance formula; caps; renewal.

Coverage continuity: changes in work, age or household; renewal; transition rules.

The selected program or rule supplies the actual current terms; no universal invented coverage baseline is implied.

### Assistance & Social Insurance > A Selected Program

Eligibility: income; assets; household size; age or other qualifying category; residence; linked-program qualification.

Benefit: amount or formula; maximum; duration; phase-out; inflation adjustment.

Applications and renewals: evidence required; application routes; renewal frequency; automatic enrollment where authorized.

Funding and capacity: appropriation; funding source; administering body; delivery capacity; waiting-list or allocation rules where applicable.

Appeals and oversight: review of refusals; complaints; reporting; audit.

Timing: start; phase-in; transition for existing recipients; expiration.

The same sections can serve food, child-care, disability, utility or other assistance without treating eligibility, enrollment and receipt as the same event.

## Shared content after a specific topic is selected

Current rules and programs — choose an existing target or begin a new supported proposal.

Coverage and eligibility — who, what activity, institution or place is covered, with exceptions.

Benefit, rate or standard — the actual payment, price, tax structure, service requirement, permission or restriction.

Funding and revenue — appropriations, funding sources, grants, charges and matching requirements.

Administration and delivery — responsible institution, staffing, award or allocation process and implementation terms.

Enforcement and appeals — compliance checks, corrective action, penalties and review where supported.

Reporting and review — disclosures, audits, performance reports and review dates.

Timing and replacement — effective dates, transition, phase-in, sunset, amendment and repeal.

Show only applicable sections; do not force every bill to contain every heading. These groups describe editable content, not mandatory numbered statutory sections.

## Content placement rules

Each policy has one identity and one primary catalog location, with cross-links elsewhere. School meals can appear under Education and Food Assistance; broadband under Infrastructure and Technology; veterans’ care under Healthcare and Defense & Veterans; unemployment benefits under Assistance and Labor. Cross-links do not create duplicate laws or effects.

“Raise,” “lower,” “expand,” “restrict,” “replace,” and “repeal” belong inside the selected subject as supported operations, not separate top-level topics. The menu must support different directions, not only program expansion.

Keep approval, political capital, GDP, mortality and other outcomes out of direct drafting controls. A proposal changes a rule, resource or institution; outcomes are separately produced and observed.

Unsupported content belongs in the development inventory, not an ordinary docket of laws with no operative consumer. Supported direct rule or fiscal effects can be useful before deep downstream modeling; an intentionally delayed, unfunded or unsuccessfully implemented law is different from missing implementation code.

This proposal does not impose a complete-every-topic-before-release gate. The existing 20-family bank should be mapped into this catalog, with reusable funding, eligibility, reporting and repeal mechanisms exposed within appropriate subjects rather than treated as rival subject headings.
