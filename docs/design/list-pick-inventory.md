# List-pick inventory

Main picks from a collection with a seeded stream or a hash in 99 places (104 picks counting repeats). 22 of them decide what someone does or how something turns out: who signs a petition, who sits on a jury, a state's tax rate, an outlet's owner, which seats start vacant. The other 82 choose names, looks, wordings or a person's past, and are allowed. The 22 decisions are grandfathered so main stays green, and each names the real reason that should replace it. The list only shrinks, and a new pick of any class fails the guard.

## The four classes

- **IDENTITY**: names, faces, appearance, art variants. Allowed.
- **PRESENTATION**: choosing among equivalent wordings or images with no effect on the world. Allowed.
- **GENERATION**: who exists and what their past was (world creation, deep past, husks, backward generation on the click). Allowed only when the seed is keyed by the id of the thing it describes (a person's fact keyed by that person) and no real reason exists to decide it instead. A school a child attended follows from where the family lived and the child's age, so a school pick is not generation. A per-person fact keyed only by place is a DECISION.
- **DECISION**: what an actor does, or how something turns out. Never allowed. The ones here are grandfathered so main stays green, and each names the real reason that should replace it.

Four of these came out of review as wrongly keyed or wrongly sorted:

- The sibling's age gap in `production-world.ts` is a per-person fact, but its stream is keyed only by the world seed and a constant label, so every world draws it the same way regardless of the family. It is a DECISION until it follows the family's recorded birth order and is keyed to the sibling's own id.
- The judge's home jurisdiction is a per-person fact keyed by the seat, not by the judge.
- The quiet-stretch length in `life-story.ts` is described as pacing, but `time-command.ts` advances the world clock by it.
- A state's executive-authority rule value, where no pack was read, is picked from the six read states by a hash of the jurisdiction. The estimate rule asks for the middle of the read values, not a pick.

The guard reads picks from a collection. A seeded draw that sizes a value (an age, a number of terms, a date inside a span) is not a pick, and the guard does not flag it. The remaining integer draws on main generate a person's or a world's past, and none is keyed by place alone. They cover working age and household size, prior terms for House delegates and opening chambers, birth dates for invented people and the player's peers, and the central-bank chair's term length. One to watch: the central-bank members' trait values are drawn from minus two to two by seed, and traits are supposed to follow a person's life.

Rolls (a draw compared with a chance) are a separate kind in the same allowlist and are unchanged by this work.

## How the guard reads a pick

The guard (`npm run zero-dice`) flags a pick call (`rng.pick`, `pickDistinct`, `weightedPick`, a shuffle). It flags an index taken from a draw, such as `list[rng.integer(0, list.length)]` or `Math.floor(rng.next() * n)`, and a splice or an at() of a draw. It follows a draw kept in a variable to its later use as an index or as a number counted down through weights. It flags a weighted pick that multiplies a draw by a total, and an index taken from a hash.

The hash rule is a heuristic: a modulo by a list length counts when a hash, digest, checksum or character fold appears in the eight lines above it. A pick written some other way may slip past it. That is why each entry in `scripts/zero-dice-allowlist.json` is a line a person classified, and why a reworded line must be reclassified by hand.

The guard also reads the player screens and the future new-core folder for picks, but only for picks. When the new core lands, its picks go through the same check. The deep-past school pick in the new core's first branch is a known DECISION: a child's school follows from where the family lived and the child's age.
