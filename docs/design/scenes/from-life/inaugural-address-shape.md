## What real inaugurals do

Read for shape and length only; nothing of their words ships. The calibration set is the twentieth- and twenty-first-century inaugurals most often studied for form (1933, 1961, 1981, 1993, 2001, 2009, 2017, 2021). They run about 1,400 to 2,500 words and, across very different Presidents, keep one order of movements:

1. Greeting to the people and the officials present.
2. The occasion: the peaceful handing over of power and the oath, often with thanks to the predecessor.
3. What the President believes, in their own tradition, and a word to those who voted the other way.
4. The country's place in its long story: the generations before, the unfinished work.
5. The moment: the hard or hopeful conditions of the day, in human terms (work, prices, homes), almost never with figures.
6. The people: who the country is, the work they do.
7. Turning to the institutions: Congress, sometimes the states, sometimes named partners.
8. What the President asks of the people themselves: service, responsibility, each other.
9. Hope, and the close: a blessing or a call to begin.

Statistics are rare in them; the conditions are told as lives.
