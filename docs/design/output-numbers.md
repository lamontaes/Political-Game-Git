# One number for every figure on Metrics

Today a figure has no single home. Of the 106 measures the Metrics tab can show, 43 have one writer that every view reads. Another 42 are kept in two or more places that can disagree, 11 are never kept, 2 are worked out by the screen itself, and 8 are wrong. The four odd figures from the six-year game all trace to causes in the code, and none of them is a display fault. The recommendation is one table of output numbers, written by the engine that already knows each number, monthly or yearly, and read by every screen, story and trace. It adds about 120 rows a year and no daily work, so a year runs no slower.

## The answer to your question

Today it is several places, not one. The research's list of 106 measures is the Metrics tab's list (74 town, 3 county, 13 state, 16 nation). Read against the code:

- **43 have exactly one writer that every view reads** (verdict ONE). Examples are the nation's months, the budgets' rows, the town bank and the President's approval.
- **42 are computed in two or more places that can disagree** (DUPLICATED). Most of these pair the outcome web with an engine. Each month the web either stores its own copy of an engine's figure or keeps its own modeled rate for a fact an engine already counts. Crime, police, schools, homelessness, moves, births and turnout are all like this. The state's jobs, pay and prices come from two engines that give different numbers for the same state and month.
- **2 are computed by the screen itself** (VIEW-COMPUTED): evictions served and open unemployment claims.
- **11 are never kept, so they have no history** (NOT KEPT): the town's people, rentals, empty rentals, people without a home, open jobs, deaths, and the state's and nation's people and sales.
- **8 are wrong** (WRONG): the four odd figures and the budget lines in the same chain.

Of the 19 rows today's Metrics screen shows, 5 have one writer, 5 are duplicated, 3 are never kept, 2 are worked out by the screen, and 4 are wrong. The rent and home value it shows are the opening's figures, never updated, while the town's monthly row holds the moving ones.

**Recommendation.** Make one table of output numbers the only place a figure lives. The game already has most of it: the town's monthly economy row, and the outcome web's list of which engine owns which measure. Extend those two into one registry. Each figure gets one id, one owning engine, a cadence, a unit, a level and a source tag. A December fold writes one row per place, per state and for the nation each year. The outcome web reads the registry instead of storing copies. Its modeled rate steps aside wherever an engine counts the real thing. Every screen, story, speech, Journal line and dev-mode trace reads only the registry. The figures are written by the engine that already computes them, on the day the fact changes or at month's end, so nothing new scans the world.

## Rulings this plan follows

- **One engine per mechanic, and every screen is a view of the same records (owner, October 27 and 28, in the repository guide):** "A view that computes or stores its own version of a fact is a duplicate." This decides that the web's stored copies, the screen's own counts and the second state figure are deleted, not kept beside the registry.
- **Graphs are readable views over canonical data, not separate truth (decision register, OCD-UI-008).** It also says to "use correct geography, date and denominator." This decides that each figure carries its denominator and its level.
- **The Metrics tab is never blank (owner, November 5):** last year on day one is the opening's source figure, marked as an estimate, and from month 13 both columns are simulated. The registry's first year row is the opening's figure tagged ESTIMATED.
- **Numbers stay the main view, and a measure opens a timeline (owner, November 12, about 3:00 p.m.):** "go with A plus the years table." The years table and the timeline read the registry's yearly rows.
- **Nothing runs or is kept every day by default (owner, November 10; the daily register).** Every new series here is monthly or yearly.
- **One model, two tiers (owner, October 14):** a counted group is read by the same rule as a full person, times its count. Eleven figures break this today; the table names them.
- **Dev mode's Trace is the ultimate trace (owner, October 14):** from any record, click every decision behind it. Dev mode's Trace already opens any kept row by its id. It answers "no cause kept" for the town's monthly row, which an open pool item already names.
- **Bookkeeping is a debug switch (owner, October 11).** The counted deaths' month row is written only with bookkeeping on, which is why the research found it empty. A figure the Metrics tab reads is play, not bookkeeping, so its yearly row is kept in play.

## Every figure, where it comes from

Each row gives what the player sees, with its unit, and the engine and record it comes from today. It names every other place the same fact is computed and whether it is kept year by year. It says whether counted and full people feed it by the same rule, whether dev mode's Trace reaches it, and the verdict. "Row, no cause kept" means the trace opens the whole kept row by its id but names nothing behind the figure. "Web row" means the outcome web's monthly row for the place. "Live row" means a row that is overwritten, so the trace shows only today. File locations are under the game's core folder unless they name the bench.

## The four odd figures, traced by reading

Each cause below was found by reading code, not by a run. Each is marked inferred until its one measurement is made.

### Town unemployment at 15% while the state stood at 4.6%

- **The formula.** The town's rate is people looking over people at work plus people looking (`townMetrics`, src/core2/modules/world-metrics.ts:186 to 191). People looking are the jobs engine's seekers, each row counted once (line 137). People at work are residents holding a job record (lines 96 and 115). In Gracey in 2029 that was 13 over 73 plus 13, which is 15.1%.
- **The state's figure** is a different engine: the regional model's months (src/core2/regional-model.ts:293). It never reads the town. The open pool item that makes the town count inside its state says so in code (macro.ts reads no town household when a state is stepped).
- **Most likely cause, inferred.** It is not one bad formula. Three things stack. First, a town of 189 people has a labor force of about 86, so one seeker moves the rate by more than a point. Second, the town shrank: its businesses fell from 31 to 22 and its residents at work from 108 to 69, with no business opened in any December. The open item on local businesses selling 35 to 65% below their opening forecast names that shrink. Third, the counted tier is read by a different rule here than in the jobs engine. A counted seeker or worker counts once, not by household weight, and workers placed out of town are left out of the labor force (world-metrics.ts:102 and 115, against world-panels.ts:59 and 75).
- **One measurement would confirm it.** In the Gracey game, read December 2029: the 13 seekers by tier, months looking, age and whether their last employer closed. Read the same month's labor force both ways (by weight with out-of-town workers, and as the row does now). Then count businesses founded against closed, 2025 to 2029.
- **Covered by:** the open item on the Guam town at 25.76% unemployment, which still reads "Needs cause"; this trace is its cause. The open item on local sales below forecast covers the shrink. The open pull request for the town counting inside its state covers the gap between the two engines.

### $344,636 per student, for one student

- **The formula.** `recordSchoolSpending` (src/core2/school-outcomes.ts:876) takes the county's schools line per county resident (line 898), multiplies by the town's residents and divides by the town's students (line 910). Gracey had 185 residents and 1 student aged 5 to 17 in 2029, and 2 in 2027 and 2028. That gives $1,863 per resident times 185, which is $344,636.
- **Most likely cause, inferred:** a count of one. Children are about one in six residents in a real town, so 185 residents should hold about 30 students. Either the town's people were drawn with almost no children, or the student count misses counted children (`populationByPlace`, school-outcomes.ts:832). The outcome web meanwhile shows its own figure, $15,455, unchanged in six years. Two numbers for one fact is the duplicate.
- **One measurement:** on September 1, 2029, count Gracey's residents by age band, named and counted separately. Compare the share aged 5 to 17 with the opening's census share for the place.
- **Covered by:** nothing open. A closed item fixed the opposite case, a small town reading $987.

### A state budget falling $12 billion in a year

- **The formulas.** Budgeted revenue is the published per-resident base times the government's residents at the opening (`publishedBudgetFor`, src/core2/modules/budgets.ts:771 to 774). The fiscal year is never used. So budgeted revenue is the same dollar figure every year at the county, state and nation: a budget that is never re-budgeted. Spending is the same fixed base scaled by what the government collected last fiscal year over that plan (`receiptsFactor` and `legislatedBudget`, budgets.ts:1381 to 1430, the scaling at line 1425).
- **Most likely cause of the drop, inferred.** Kentucky's spending went from $49.8 billion to $37.3 billion, three quarters of its base. That means it collected about three quarters of its fixed plan in fiscal 2029. A state's collections include federal aid. The federal debt stopped moving in 2029 (below). In December 2030 the U.S. Treasury paid about one dollar in six of its Social Security line and recorded the rest as short. Federal payments prorated that hard would cut the state's aid, then its collections, then the next year's spending. The county fell by an eighth the same way, through state aid.
- **One measurement:** Kentucky's monthly treasury rows for fiscal 2028 and 2029, with collections and federal aid received side by side, and the U.S. rows for the same months with borrowing and the share of each line paid.
- **Covered by:** no open item covers budgeted revenue that never moves. The open pull request that has the country tier read the federal budget is related but does not re-budget revenue. Other states' budgets already move with their own economies through the country tier's ledgers. The played state and county still use the fixed base, so two engines budget a state today.

### A federal debt that did not move

- **The formula.** The debt is written. The U.S. Treasury borrows whatever a month's payments need beyond what it holds, from the debt's holders, in their shares, and adds each amount to the holder's row (`borrowFederal`, src/core2/budgets-treasury.ts:975 to 1033). The debt was $36.54 trillion at the end of 2029 and the same to the cent at the end of 2030, with nothing borrowed in December 2030.
- **Most likely cause, inferred.** Borrowing stopped for one of two reasons. Congress may have passed the question "pay for a higher debt limit", which holds borrowing to the room under the limit (line 989). It opens as "no" from the real law, so a bill would have to pass in play. Or the outside account lent less than asked (the amount lent is what the transfer paid, line 1024). Either way the month's payments were then scaled down to what the Treasury held (line 1394). That proration is the start of the state budget chain above.
- **One measurement:** the law row for the debt-limit question in 2029 and 2030, any law-applied rows saying borrowing was held to the limit, and the U.S. monthly treasury rows' borrowing through 2029.
- **Covered by:** the open pull request keeping the debt holders' balances exact past very large numbers is a different defect. Nothing open covers borrowing that stops.

## The design: one registry of output numbers

**Where it lives.** It extends two things that already exist, instead of a new system:

1. **The list of figures** grows out of the outcome web's `engineMeasures` table in src/core2/data/outcome-web/outcome-web.json. It already maps a measure id to its owning engine, its record, its field and its unit scale. It moves to its own data file, src/core2/data/output-numbers.json (new), and the outcome web reads it from there. Each row holds:
   - `id`: the web's measure id where one exists (`labor.unemployment-pct`), otherwise a new one in the same style;
   - `owner`: the module that writes it;
   - `unit`, `level` (place, county, state or nation) and the denominator in words;
   - `cadence`: on change, monthly or yearly, never daily;
   - `source`: SOURCED, ESTIMATED or TUNABLE, with the citation;
   - `fold`: how a month folds into a year (the December value, the year's sum, or the year's mean);
   - `traceInputs`: the named inputs kept beside the value (for unemployment: seekers named, seekers counted by weight, at work named, at work counted by weight).
2. **The kept values** live in the `world-metrics` module, which already writes the town's row monthly and is read by the outcome web and the screens. It gains one table, `years`, with one row per place, county, state and the nation per year, and an index by place. The owning engine writes its figure through one function, `putOutputNumber(api, id, placeId, value, inputs)`, in src/core2/output-numbers.ts (new). That function is a thin writer into the `world-metrics` tables, not a scheduler.

**Who writes what, and when.**

- **Town economy figures** (jobs, pay, prices, rent, poverty, loans) are already computed monthly by `townMetrics`. They stay there, under one rule for both tiers. That means counted people by weight and out-of-town workers in the labor force, the rule the jobs engine and `townJobsPanel` already use. `townJobsPanel`'s own count is deleted, and it reads the row.
- **People and homes** (named people, counted people by weight, households, rentals, empty rentals, people without a home) are written at month's end by the residents and housing engines. They already hold these counts in the residents store and in the housing stock row. The homeless count writes on change as it does now and the registry keeps its December value.
- **Open jobs, seekers and open claims** are written monthly by the jobs engine from its positions index and seekers tables. Open claims are counted when a claim opens and closes, not by a scan.
- **Births and deaths** are counted on the day they happen. The renewal engine adds a birth to the place's year row; the health engine and the residents engine add a death the same way. The counted deaths' month row stops being bookkeeping-only, folded to one row per place per year.
- **Budgets and revenue** are the budgets engine's yearly rows, read as they are. Budgeted revenue becomes re-budgeted each year: build order 8.
- **State figures** have one owner, the country tier. It already holds each state's people, jobs, pay, prices, sales and ledger, and the state legislatures already read it. It writes a December row per state. The regional months stay as the town's region inside its state and stop being read as "the state".
- **Nation figures** stay the macro engine's months and the national opinion rows. The federal debt and the year's borrowing get a December row from the budgets engine.

**How it folds.** A December step on the `world-metrics` calendar, which already runs on the 2nd of each month, closes the year row for each place. The monthly town rows older than 24 months fold away for places other than the played town and its county. Their year rows keep what play reads. The played town keeps its months so the timeline can open any month.

**Everything reads it.** The Metrics screen (src/bench/metrics.ts) becomes a lookup of registry rows by section: no counting, no scans. The years table and the timeline read the year rows. The outcome web reads engine measures from the registry, and its kept outcome steps aside wherever an engine counts the real thing. The director, the newspaper and speeches read a figure by its id. Dev mode's Trace opens `figure:<id>:<place>:<period>` (new). It shows the value, the formula in words from the registry, the inputs kept beside it with each tier's share, and the record ids it was read from.

**What is deleted when a figure moves in.** Counted from the table above:

## Speed

Every new series is written by an engine that already holds the number, at month's end or on the day it changes. Nothing new reads the whole world or the whole history. The world-metrics module runs on calendar work on the 2nd of each month, not as a daily step.

| New kept series                                                          | Rows a year                                                   | Cadence                                                              | Written by, without a scan                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Town and county year rows (economy, people, homes, jobs, births, deaths) | 64, one per place the economy records in the register's world | December fold of the monthly row; births and deaths added on the day | world-metrics' own monthly step; renewal, health and residents on the event |
| State year rows                                                          | 56                                                            | December                                                             | the country tier, which already steps every state monthly                   |
| Nation year row (debt, borrowing, deficit, people)                       | 1                                                             | December                                                             | the budgets engine's December treasury close; the country tier              |
| Monthly town rows folded after 24 months                                 | about 750 fewer a year from year 3, in a 64-place world       | December                                                             | the same step                                                               |

That is about 121 new rows a year against the daily register's 3,063 rows after four years for the town's monthly table alone. Once the fold runs, the total kept falls. The register's growing tables run to 150,000 rows by year four.

**Why a year does not get slower.** Every figure is computed today already, by the engine that owns it, on its current cadence. The registry stores it once and stops two other places from computing it. Two scans that run today go away:

- the screen's eviction and claims counts, which walk every person and every case on each open (src/bench/metrics.ts:125);
- the outcome web's monthly read of the whole history of the town's rows to find this month's places and each place's latest row (`placesOf` and the latest-row sort in src/core2/modules/outcome-web.ts:159 and 375). It becomes a lookup by id.

The check is the speed rule's own: `npm run speed:year` before and after each build order on one machine, one process at a time. A year may not run more than 20% slower, and should not run slower at all.

**Figures that cannot be kept without a scan, and what to do instead.**

- **Named people in a place.** No index of living people by place was found. Count it on the events that change it (a birth, a death, a move in or out, a fill-in) rather than walking every person each month.
- **Open unemployment claims.** Count on the day a claim opens and closes, not by reading the claims table, which grows.
- **Evictions served in the last twelve months.** Count on the notice event into the year row.
