# The Political Process: how its systems look and work

Studied Oct 26, 2026 from the installed game's screen layout, styles and on-screen strings (read-only, on the owner's Mac). Complements `docs/roadmap/political-process-baseline.md`, which maps its module names to our specs; this page describes how each system works for the player. It is the systems model (AGENTS.md); its content is not copied.

## Screens

- A top bar of the player's standing (name recognition, influence, approval, messages, schedule, search), each opening its own popup; seven main tabs (Office, Campaign, Politicians, Elections, Metrics, Profile, News); a week display and a Next Turn button.
- Everything is styled as manila folders with tabs and paper sheets; menus open as centered modal folders, never as spreadsheet rows.

## Legislation

- Topic tabs; a bill is a titled list of provisions, each a real change with a from and to amount ("raises the first bracket rate from X to Y"); special bill types (tax reform, constitutional amendments at two-thirds, reconciliation with its own limits); drafting costs the player hours.
- The cost estimate comes at the committee hearing, where named officials testify ("compared to current policy...") and the committee vote shows authorized spending and revenue by category. We go further: the estimate sits beside each term as the player drafts (owner, Oct 14 and Oct 26).
- A whip count: each member's position is unknown until the player spends time asking; it then shows reasons in plain words and who is pushing them (leaders, the White House, caucus, the player); favors can be spent.
- Every stage is reported: committee assignment, the chair granting or denying a hearing (members can lobby the chair), committee vote, amendments, cloture, each chamber, conference, signature, veto and override. Floor votes play as a live roll call with speed controls and "skip to my vote". Laws have a page with status, history and sponsor, and an archive; the news reports filings.
- No earmarks or vote trading; bargaining is favors, leadership influence and conditional endorsements. We add pork and trading (owner, Oct 26).

## Time

- Weekly turns with an hours budget; "skip time" to the next significant week; leftover hours are used automatically. Each turn the background runs chair decisions, votes, budgets, leadership elections, deaths, polls, news and appointments; other politicians file bills all the time.

## Campaign

- Activities arrive as offers with a host, a place, an audience and an hours cost (door knocking, fundraising, interviews, speeches, rallies). Better staff bring more offers. Debates are chosen strategies per topic with a results screen. Endorsements are negotiated with conditions (a post, money, changing caucus). Election night is optional coverage with counted percentage, projections, a map to drill into, and speed controls.

## Travel

- A map per state with counties; choosing a place shows its cost and travel hours; events happen only where the player is; each state has its own activities.

## Offices

- The player can start in any office from none to President, set starting money and standing, add history and traits, continue as a grown child, become party chair, or take a legal path to judgeships.

## What not to copy

Elections balanced by adjusting turnout and scripted waves (breaks emergent outcomes); flat rates and global point meters (breaks no-dice and realism); canned text for debates, letters and legislators' reasons (our words come from the English engine and scenes).
