## Lessons he asked to carry to every line, not only these

- **No dangling fragments** ("All right. Yes.", "You've made your case." on its own).
- **No lines without a subject** ("I'll be watching what they do with it." with no "it" set up).
- **Every line needs context:** the listener has to know why it matters to the speaker, and a moment's setup has to make sense from the player's side.
- **People don't quote exact figures they wouldn't know** (the cost of a race, savings in months). They ask plain questions: "Can we even afford this?"
- **Look up how real people say each thing** before writing it: "look at real-life examples of that and see how real life people talk about it."
- **Spelling:** he prefers "Alright" to "All right" in dialogue.
- **Don't offer what the player can't do:** no voting when you can't vote.

## What he wants next

- **More speeches, mixed in with everything else.** Mix the kinds instead of three of one kind in a row: a piece of legislation, a speech, a reaction, a constituent.
- **Reuse the moments he graded.** Put graded and ungraded moments back in, mixed differently.
