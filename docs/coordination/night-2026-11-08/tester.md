## Phases

1. **Every law** (89: yes-or-no policies, rates and amounts, spending lines). For each, in two places drawn at random from all 56 jurisdictions (a small town and a city, in different states): pass it through the body that really has the power, run past its research lag plus a month, and check every effect it should have: who it reached; money moved; what people may or must do; services and eligibility; place outcomes (direction, size against the research, timing against the lag); whether people heard of it (news, journal, what they remember) and whether views moved. Then repeal it and check the effects unwind. Check the level rules: a town can't pass what its state bars; federal law governs only where it really preempts; Congress can act where real law lets it. Grade each: works / works partly / broken / can't test yet (name the missing control). Flag what should NOT happen: an effect with no law behind it, an effect outside a law's reach, figures negative or exploding, people reacting to a law they never heard of, a dev control that wrote a result directly. Fix as you go.
2. **Beyond laws, the same way:** bills at every body (introduce, committee, amend, vote, veto, override, repeal); elections (filing, campaigns, turnout, results like real ones, fields of 2 to 15 candidates); bribes (who offers, who takes, who exposes, what follows); each of the director's 91 scene kinds forced with real people (does it play, make sense and change something); what people remember and forget; jobs, prices and rents; speed (each game year under 2 minutes). Then whatever a real political world would do that this one might not: decide from the owner's rulings and the code.
3. **Worlds drifting.** Once dev mode can run years, run several random worlds for 20 years with nobody steering. For each place, the same VITAL STATISTICS start and end (income, jobs, poverty, rent share of income, uninsured, high school and college graduation, crime, turnout, births and deaths, businesses, party in power); WHAT EMERGED, each with its cause chain from dev mode's trace; which laws spread where and how fast; how far places drifted apart and the national picture (party balance, trust); the strangest place and why; anything outside real history's ranges. **Twin worlds** for big laws (rent stabilization, the minimum wage, ending cash bail): several pairs in different places, because chance chains make any one pair differ in ways the law didn't cause; report what shows up across most pairs. The owner's capability questions: can a movement start, do people move between states as places drift, do states copy each other's laws, does a protest movement or a demagogue appear, a realignment like 1932 to 1936, in rare cases a government falling, a drift toward war? Report what happened, never script it; a missing cause goes to Laws and effects or Story through the coordinator.
