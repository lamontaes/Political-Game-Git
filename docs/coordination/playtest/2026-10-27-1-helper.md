## Q17 (priority). Day/Week stop at midnight, and no scene for three weeks

Owner, verbatim: "Again, I'm seeing simulations with no screens happening, and it's staying at midnight for whatever reason. There's no scenes or director interjecting. It's literally just sitting in a room simulator."

### (a) Why Day and Week always land at 12:00 AM
- The Day and Week buttons call `/api/advance?mode=day|week` (`src/bench/play.mjs:1231-1236`), which calls `BenchPlayer.advance` (`src/bench/handler.ts:1226`, `src/bench/player.ts:169-215`).
- `advance` builds the target from `at = this.packet?.at ?? { date: core.date + 1 day, minuteOfDay: 0 }` and then `through = { date: at.date + 1 or 7 days, minuteOfDay: at.minuteOfDay }` (`player.ts:182-199`). It keeps the minute of the last stop and adds days.
- At the opening there is no stop yet, so `at` is **tomorrow at minute 0** (Tue Jan 21 12:00 AM) and the first Day adds a day: **Wed Jan 22 12:00 AM**, skipping Tuesday. That is exactly what you saw (Mon 8:30 PM to Wed 12:00 AM).
- After that every packet's `at` is minute 0, so every Day and Week keeps minute 0. `run()` (`player.ts:223-275`) then steps one civil day at a time with `advancePlayerV1(...through: next)` and the core rests at midnight. Only a stop (a director scene, a filing story, an appointment, a canvass) puts the clock at another minute. With nothing to stop it, the clock never leaves midnight and nothing in the room changes.
- Known and fixed in an open PR, not on main: **PR #6059** "Day lands on the next morning; an outing moves the clock" (open, not draft, `mergeable_state: dirty`, last update Oct 26 19:27 UTC; pool items `play-stop-07-every-day-starts-at-midnight-a` and `play-day-lands-at-midnight`, both open). Its own Merger 3 recheck says Day twice reaches consecutive mornings (Jan 21 and 22 at 7:00 AM), Week reaches 7:00 AM seven days later, and a 3-hour canvass advances 3 hours. Remaining failure named in the PR: in a held filing story the buttons "Mark gap" and "Say nothing" are inert (`play.mjs:1188-1195` returns when `talk` is unset). It has been sent back twice and is now conflicted with main (dirty). Nobody has landed it, so v0.56.2 and main both still land at midnight.
- The room's light and office hours ignoring the clock is the sibling item `play-stop-07-every-day-starts-at-midnight-b` (open, no PR). See Q21.

### (b) Why the director gives Carlos no scene
- A scene is staged only from a **director moment** in his ledger, ranked against his other moments (`data/situation-types.json` `scheduling`: adult pace 4 scenes a year over the trailing 365 days, repeat 30 days). Moments come from records the core writes that he learns of. A settled household with no event writes none.
- Types that fire at home for a household with no event (own records only): `householdScenes` = job ended, a death, a serious illness; `householdRecordScenes` = eviction; `ownRecordScenes` = filing, a race result, a press story, an interview/debate invite, a volunteer offer, a rival's word or filing, a party ask. For Carlos (no job to lose, no race yet, no birth, death, illness or notice) none of these has a record, so none fires.
- The library has 78 situation types. 25 have an `awaiting` list: the type has **no cause at all** because the records it needs are not produced yet (for example `gathering-missing` awaits `record:birthday-gathering` and `record:holiday-calendar`; `interview` awaits `record:job-application`; `warning` awaits `record:arrears`; `letter` awaits `record:contact-from-someone-away`; `argument` awaits `record:dispute-over-money`; `favor-asked`, `confession`, `proposal`, `reconciliation`). Of 98 causes in total, 28 name an `awaitingProducer` (a cause whose producer the core does not have: `departure`, `reach-out`, `first-meeting` for a new contact, `date`, `breakup`, `celebration`, `arrival` for a birth, `advice`, `family-absence`, `eviction-notice`). Only 70 causes can fire from a real record. The ones that need a tie turning (`argument`, `becoming-friends`, `reunion`) need a thread turn of `soured` or `grew` on a contact; none happens in three quiet weeks at home.
- For a jobless 35-year-old specifically: `layoff` and `first-day` need a job record started or ended (he has neither); `interview` needs a job application record (nothing applies for him); `new-baby`/`arrival` need a birth. So the list of types that could fire for him is: none at the start, and every one that might later (`news-arrives`, `law-takes-effect`, `filing`, race types) needs something he or the world does first.
- Quiet-day talk at home is **on request**, not a director scene: a household member has a `quiet` subject read from records (`src/director/household-quiet.test.ts` describes it: weekday from the calendar, time together from the relationship record). The player must click that person; nothing interrupts.
- So "sitting in a room simulator" is by design of the build: the director interjects only from records. The records for an ordinary jobless week are not written.

## Q8. Pants patchy after recolor (blue blazer outfit, navy, heavy build)
Owner: clothes recolor patchy on the player's pants.
**Cause found and measured: the clothes mask leaves every dark pixel gray.**
- `regionOf` (`src/bench/figure-colors.ts:62-76`) calls a pixel `clothes` only if saturation < 0.12 **and** 60 < luminance < 235. Anything at or below luminance 60 (the painted shadows, creases, the hem, where cloth meets the outline) is `other` and `recolorFigure` skips it (`:~115-120`). Anything with saturation of 0.12 or more (a tinted gray) is also skipped.
- I measured the real art (`git` blob at `da6a035d6`): the standing heavy male blazer figures `art/people-engine/v2/figures/v2-32-m-heavy.png`, `v2-34-m-heavy.png`, `v2-36-m-heavy.png`, lower 45% of the figure (pants and shoes). Of the opaque pixels there, 3,513 of 25,858 (14%), 2,893 of 28,053 (10%) and 2,706 of 26,433 (10%) are at or below luminance 60, and 100, 68 and 84 are tinted (average RGB 108,65,40, brown shoes or a cuff). So 10 to 14% of the pants area keeps its painted gray when navy is applied.
- Why it shows: the recolor keeps the picture's shading, `shade = lum / mean` of the clothes region, times the swatch (`:~121-125`). Navy `#34507e` at a pixel just above luminance 60 becomes about 40 to 45% of navy, a very dark blue; the pixels just below 60 stay painted gray, which is **lighter** than their dark-blue neighbors. So the creases and shadow bands read as gray patches on the pants. The same hard cut-off at 60 and at 235 explains patches at highlights.
- Same mask recolors **any** unsaturated mid-gray: gray hair, shoe tops, shirt cuffs, and any gray in the face art that falls in 60 to 235, which is the likely reason clothes colors reach places that are not clothes (eyes, hair). It is registered as stopgap `SG-PLAY-figure-color-masks-from-picture` (comment at `figure-colors.ts:~58-60`): "found from the picture's own colors... not painted per figure".
- The fix the code points to: paint per-garment masks (a mask image per figure, as the stopgap says it should) and recolor by mask with the shading kept; or at least lower the dark floor and soften the edge with a ramp instead of a cut. Per-garment colors for the creator (his ask: shirt, pants, shoes) need masks anyway. Not started.


## Q9. The male player was given a male partner (Jacob Lara, 38)
Owner, verbatim: "Is my character gay or something? Why is my significant other a guy? Again, last talk, January 20th, 2025, with zero history."
**Cause: the household is drawn before the creator's gender is applied, so it fits whoever the world's resident was, not the character he picked.**
- With a chosen birthday, the world builder makes the player **the existing resident born nearest that day** (`entrantBornNear`, `src/bench/world.ts:~180-216`), sorted by days apart and then id only, passing over residents whose parents or children would be too close in age (`kinFit`). **Sex is not a criterion.** That resident's partner, children, parents and schooling were drawn earlier (`buildPopulation` then `buildDeepPast`, `world.ts:~310-340`).
- The creator then calls `chooseIdentityV1(id, { givenName, familyName, birthDate, looks: { gender, recipe } })` (`src/bench/creator.ts:~264-285`, `src/core2/state.ts:1579`). The comment says it outright: "only who they are called and how they look is set here." So if the nearest resident was a woman with a male partner, picking "male" leaves her partner a man. The partner stays; the player gets a new name and a new look. Cousins of this: the player's name, gender and look can disagree with their records (parents named for the original person).
- How the partner is drawn at all: the partnership model (`src/core2/opening-partnership.ts`, stopgap `SG-P15-partnership-model`, open) gives a household its partner by age gap from survey tables, for opposite-sex and same-sex couples alike (`opening-partnership.json` note: "Spouses' age difference is the national CPS distribution for every place and both opposite-sex and same-sex couples"). The sexes of the couple come from the generated household sample (I did not trace that file). Stated plainly: nothing lets the player choose a partner or a partner's sex; the creator has no field for it.
- "How they met / shared past": a partnered household gets `household:partners-met` and `household:partners-now` facts dated before the first shared child (`src/core2/past-family.ts`, header at `:1-24`), and the Journal tells where they met (`src/director/journal.ts:198-213`) **only if** the fact has a place that resolves (otherwise it is skipped). In v0.56.2 that is the thin household slice (Q1); the memoir that tells more is #6345, after the build.
- Fix paths for the owner to choose: (a) draw the player's resident by the chosen gender too (`entrantBornNear` gets a sex), so the household is of a person of that gender; (b) let the creator set the partner (type, none, sex); (c) redraw the household after the identity call. Option (a) is a one-parameter change; it does not decide whether the partner is a man or woman, which comes from the sample.
