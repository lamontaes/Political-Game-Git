# People research, Oct 27 (owner's mannequin idea)

Owner, verbatim: "Now I need you to work on, like I said, the people. Do research about my idea. I don't want you to just affirm me... ChatGPT said you couldn't generate a blob. You would need one for each body type and each hand position and stuff. I don't believe that... I think the important part is the depth of things. So like sitting on a chair, like actually sitting on it and backfilling."

Read-only on game code. Images under `docs/coordination/playtest/people-research/`. Code at v0.56.2 (`da6a035d6`); nothing after the build touches `whole-figures.ts`, `scene.ts`, `art/backdrops/staging.json` or `art/seat-slots` on main (`git log` empty).

## R1. How the bench draws seated people today

**One figure per person, a flat cut-out, placed by hand-measured spots, with a rectangular crop for furniture.**
- **Which figures.** Only the "people-engine v2" set (`art/people-engine/v2/index.json`, 132 body records with no prop at the build: 84 sit three-quarter, 36 sit back, 36 sit front, 72 stand three-quarter, 36 stand front, 36 lectern). `FIGURES` are the standing three-quarter views (`src/bench/whole-figures.ts:~107-110`); `SEATED` are all sit records (`:~111`). A look is a sex, build (slim, medium, heavy) and outfit; the figure is the same person turned or seated (`seatedFor`, `whole-figures.ts:127-141`: same look and build, angle back if the spot faces away, else three-quarter).
- **Spots.** `art/backdrops/staging.json` lists per place `horizonY`, `metersPercent`, `floors` and `spots` of `{x, y, pose, facing, depth, seatY?, clipBelowY?, clipBandEndY?, group?, floor?, role}`. `us-senate-floor` has 78 spots: 70 "sit" facing "away", 3 sit facing the viewer, 4 standing or leaning (`staging.json`, place `us-senate-floor`; measured from the senators' desks at 0.8 m, comments in the file).
- **Placement.** `stagePeople` (`src/bench/scene.ts:586-740`) gives each person a spot (the speaker takes the podium, others the nearest free spot that fits; spots are skipped when two outlines would cover each other, `hides`, `scene.ts:~742-760`), then `figureOn` (`whole-figures.ts:382-443`). A seated figure goes through `placeSeated` (`whole-figures.ts:489-531`): scale = the person's standing height in percent of the picture divided by the standing figure's foot line (`personPercent`, `:448-463`, from `heightMeters[band]`, the room's `metersPercent` and the spot's distance below the horizon); the hip line is the figure's recorded `seatY` or 60% of its height (`SEATED_HIP`); it is drawn with the hip at the spot's `seatY ?? y`. Depth is by draw order only: people are sorted far to near by `depth ?? y` (`scene.ts:~693`).
- **The crop that "cuts legs off".** For a spot with `clipBelowY` the server tells the client where to cut: `clipBelowPercent` (and an optional `clipBandEndPercent`). The client makes the figure's box only as tall as `clipBelow - top` and squeezes the image into it (`src/bench/play.mjs:227-243`: `bottom = f.clipBelowPercent ?? top + height`, `visible = bottom - top`, `img.style.height = (height / visible) * 100%`) with overflow hidden. So furniture (a desk, a chair back, a counter) hides the body with **one straight horizontal line** per spot; a figure behind a desk loses everything below the line, including parts that should show beside or above a chair arm or desk corner. A spot with `clipBelowY` also gets no shadow (`hidden`, `whole-figures.ts:417-418`). That is the cut-off legs.
- **Turning.** `roomFronts` (`src/bench/data/whole-figures.json`) names the x of a room's front (rostrum, dais); `turnOf` (`scene.ts:780-807`) turns a person toward it, shows the back view if the spot says `facing: "away"`, and mirrors the sprite with `flips`/`towardOf`.
- **What the figure set lacks** (relevant to the idea): the set paints seated people from only three angles (three-quarter, back, front), mirrored for left and right; arms and hands are part of the single body picture, so a person sitting with hands on a desk is the same picture as one with hands in the lap (the set's README says "some base sheets hold a cup or a tablet" as props, left out of the room tests). Nothing models the desk's depth against a figure's arm.

## What `art/seat-slots/*` is and whether anything reads it
- **Content:** Firefly-painted people for **every seat** in twelve chamber and meeting-room paintings: 12 folders (council chamber x3, county commission x2, public meeting room, school board room, state house, two state chambers, `us-house-floor`, `us-senate-floor`), each with sprite sheets per light (`<place>__seats__<light>.png`) and `seats.json`: per seat a head point and `variants` of people with `rect`, `at` (where the cut-out goes per light), `bottom` (draw order) and `activity` (`plain`, `reading`, one `talking`). Counts per the README: Senate 48 seats and 160 people, House 26 and 126, council chamber 26 and 117, state house 18 and 129, and so on. `art/seat-slots-source/<place>/sparse-v*.jpg` and masks are the generating sheets (a few people at a time, far apart, each cut out alone).
- The README states its own design: "The cut-outs include only what is visible from the camera, so a chair back or desk in front of a person is already left out of the cut-out and the room's own furniture shows through." That is the same idea as the mannequin plate, done with painted people: occlusion is baked into each cut-out.
- **Does anything read it? No.** `git grep seat-slots` over `src`, `scripts`, `tests` and `package.json` at `da6a035d6` and on main finds nothing that reads `seats.json` or the sheets (only the README and the art folders). It is unused art. It cannot be used as is for the game's people because each cut-out is one specific painted person (not the player's household) and has no look or color control; the people-engine figures were chosen instead.

## R2. Depth prototype (the key test)

**What I ran.** CPU torch 2.14 and transformers 5.21 in a venv; `depth-anything/Depth-Anything-V2-Small-hf` (license on the model page: **Apache 2.0**) on `art/backdrops/us-senate-floor__midday.jpg` and `council-chamber__midday.jpg` (1672x941). About 8 seconds per painting on this 4-CPU container, model load included. Scripts: `people-research/depth-run.py` (depth) and `people-research/composite-test.py` (the composite). Everything is in this folder.

**Depth maps** (brighter is nearer): `depth-us-senate-floor.png`, `depth-council-chamber.png`.
- **They are good on these paintings.** In the Senate map each desk, each chair back and the curved rows separate cleanly with sharp edges; the dais and rostrum are a distinct mid-gray; the aisle is a smooth ramp. In the council chamber every chair is its own blob, rows step back evenly, the lectern and the dais desk read correctly. Nothing needed cleaning for these two. I did not test the other 306 paintings; painted rooms with fog, windows or reflective floors may be worse.

**Composite test.** Stock people-engine figures (`art/people-engine/v2/figures`, seated back view and one three-quarter; slim, medium and heavy; both sexes; blazer, vest, polo, long sleeve) placed with the game's own scale and hip rule (`personPercent`, `placeSeated`: scale from the room's `metersPercent` and horizon, hip at the spot's `seatY`), then:
1. **Plain paste**, no occlusion: `senate-1-plain-paste.png`, `council-1-plain-paste.png`.
2. **Today's straight crop line** (a reimplementation of `clipBelowY`, not a screenshot of the game): `senate-2-today-crop-line.png`.
3. **Depth-tested**: a figure pixel is drawn only where the painting's depth at that pixel is **farther** than the figure's depth, taken as the painting's depth at the seat's hip point (median of a 24x24 window) pushed back by a margin (so the chair back in front of the seat covers the body): `senate-3-depth-tested-margin-0.02.png`, `senate-3b-...-0.00.png`, `senate-3c-...-0.05.png`, `council-2-depth-tested-margin-0.05.png`. The edge is soft by 1.2% of the depth range.

**Where it works**
- **Senate, back views on real seats** (front row left and right, second row, mid row): the bodies sit **behind the desk in front of them** and the cut follows the desk's top edge and its curve, and the chair back covers the person's lower back where it should, while shoulders and heads stand above. This is what today's straight line cannot do: the crop line is flat, so the body ends in a floating horizontal edge across a slanted desk (compare `senate-2` and `senate-3c`). Margin **0.05** was cleanest; 0.00 lets chair backs and desk edges show through the figure's pixels (chair-back pixels equal the figure's depth, so they are drawn over), 0.02 is close. No hand-drawn masks were needed.
- **Different builds** (slim female, heavy male, medium) all work with the same rule: the test depends on the seat and the painting, not on the body.
- **Council chamber**: chair backs in front of a person hide the lower body correctly for the far and middle rows.

**Where it fails (honest)**
- **Arms and hands on a desk.** The desk is nearer than the person's torso, so the whole forearm that rests on the desk top is erased below the desk edge. Seated people at a desk need the arm layer drawn after the desk (a per-spot exception) or a lower margin on that region. The v2 figures have arms at their sides, so I did not hit it hard here, but any "hands on the desk" pose will.
- **One depth per figure.** A real body has depth (shoulders nearer than knees). With one flat plane, a person seated *in* a chair is cut at the chair seat's edge instead of the chair arm or back in the right places. The council-chamber front-row figures show this: patchy removal around the chair legs and seat, where the figure's own legs should show through the gaps. A per-pixel figure depth (see below) fixes it.
- **Noisy depth on thin things.** Chair legs, the gaps between slats and thin desk rails give bands and halos (`council-2`: ragged areas at the knees of the front-row figures and on the right figure's lower legs). Smoothing the figure's visibility mask and eroding by a pixel would hide most of it; I did not.
- **Scale mismatches.** The game's staging gives the front-row council figure a size that does not fit the chair it sits in (the woman at front left is too large). That is a staging problem, not depth, but it is precisely what a mannequin plate (scale fixed by art) would solve.
- **Not tested:** stand poses behind a desk or counter, arms crossed, three-quarter views at an angle, night and rain variants, other places, an animated or moving camera. Depth maps would be computed once per painting (not per light: the geometry is the same in the midday, morning, night, rain variants).

## R3. Research (sources; plain)

How old and new 2D games put people behind and in front of painted things:
- **Sierra SCI/AGI: a second hidden image.** Each background carries a **priority image**; a sprite is hidden wherever the priority color there is lower than the sprite's own priority number. AGI sets a sprite's priority by its y-position; SCI draws white lines along an object's edge and fills the area so the hero walks behind it. A tutorial notes fixing a sprite that shows through is drawing the priority lines with no gaps ([SCI Wiki: The Priority Screen](https://sciwiki.sierrahelp.com/index.php/The_Priority_Screen), [AGI priority screens](https://agiwiki.sierrahelp.com/index.php/An_Explanation_of_AGI_Priority_Screens), [SCI Studio tutorial ch. 6](https://wiki.sierrahelp.com/index.php/SCI_Studio_Tutorial_Chapter_6_-_Editing_Pics)). This is a hand-drawn depth map with 16 levels.
- **LucasArts SCUMM: z-planes by walk box.** Each walk box carries a z-plane value 0 to 3 and actors are masked by objects on higher planes; a bug thread shows an actor ending up behind a tombstone because it was moved outside any box and fell to plane 0 ([ScummVM ticket 461/791 and commit logs](https://bugs.scummvm.org/ticket/791)). I did not find a source that explains the mask drawing itself, so that part is unverified.
- **Infinity Engine (Baldur's Gate): occlusion polygons and masks.** Per forum accounts the engine uses a prerendered background plus small bitmaps for walkable areas, height maps, shadow maps and walk-behind masks drawn as polygons around anything a character can walk behind ([GameBanshee: Baldur's Gate and the Infinity Engine](https://gamebanshee.com/k4fkd), [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/how-the-innovation-of-the-infinity-engine-brought-baldurs-gate-to-life), [Obsidian forum thread on depth](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/64376-question-about-depth-and-what-solution-obsidian-used)). These are fan and forum descriptions, not BioWare documentation.
- **Pillars of Eternity and Disco Elysium: 3D characters on pre-rendered or painted backgrounds with a depth image.** A Disco Elysium developer blog (via the search) says the backgrounds use rendered **height maps**; a forum comment describing Pillars' pipeline says the background's depth is rendered first, then 3D characters discard fragments behind it, and the same depth and normals drive light and shadow ([Reddit thread on per-pixel occlusion for pre-rendered backgrounds](https://redlib.hackliberty.org/r/gamedev/comments/1l9ntij/unity_per_pixel_occlusion_for_prerendered)). No official ZA/UM or Obsidian write-up was found; this is community-reported. Developers warn that compressing the depth image causes artifacts and that bit depth matters.
- **Pentiment: little to learn.** The art director says historical woodcuts were so flat that depth was unreadable, so the team moved away from that precedent for navigation ([Game Developer deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-the-art-of-pentiment), [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/how-obsidan-brought-the-past-to-life-in-pentiment-1221583/)). I found nothing on how sprites are layered.
- **Wildermyth: layered paper-doll, not depth.** Characters are stacks of PNG layers in five groups (body, head, weapons), each layer with a depth number inside its group, and the body layers are rig-specific ([Wildermyth wiki: Image layers](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Image_layers)); scenes are placed on torn-paper backgrounds, so there is no scene depth to test. Nothing found on how poses are picked.
- **Depth Anything V2 on illustrations.** The model is trained on 595K synthetic images and pseudo-labeled real images; the Hugging Face card says it is more detailed and more robust than V1 and diffusion-based Marigold/GeoWizard ([Depth Anything V2 Small, license Apache 2.0](https://huggingface.co/depth-anything/Depth-Anything-V2-Small-hf); [paper arXiv 2406.09414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414)). I did not find a source on its quality on painted or anime art; my own test (above) is the evidence: good on two painted rooms. Licenses differ by size: Small is Apache 2.0 (checked on its page); the search result for Base showed CC-BY-NC, so check before using a larger model.

## Evaluation of the mannequin-plate idea

Owner's idea: a second painting of each room with plain white mannequins at every seat and spot, at the right scale; the engine draws the person's figure at that spot; the empty painting fills the rest. His mockup is an edit of `us-senate-floor` (1672x941).

**Is ChatGPT right that you need a blob per body type and per hand position?** My view, after the test: **only if the blob is used as a stencil.** The blob can do three different jobs:
1. **Position, scale and facing of each spot.** One blob per spot is enough for any body and any person; the figure is scaled and flipped to fit it. No blob per body type. (Today's `staging.json` does this by numbers; a painted mannequin does it by eye and at the right scale for the painter's perspective: this is the council-chamber mismatch I saw.)
2. **What is in front of the person** (chair back, desk, the person in the row ahead). A **depth or priority map** gives this for the whole room, or an occluder mask baked per spot gives it with no runtime depth: no blob is needed for this job at all. The test above is that.
3. **The person's silhouette** (the outline that the figure must fill). Here ChatGPT is right: if the cut-out is the mannequin's own shape, a heavy person or a person with arms out does not fit one blob shape. Do not use the blob as the person's outline; use it only for jobs 1 and 2.

So: **one blob per spot works for any body** when the blob only gives position, scale, facing and the seat's occluder mask; a depth map (or masks baked from it) gives what is in front. The owner's instinct ("the important part is the depth of things, sitting on a chair and backfilling") is right and is what the test shows working. Where the plain mannequin idea has an edge over depth alone:
- **A mannequin painted into the room carries its own depth.** If the mannequins are painted in the actual chairs by an image model (as `art/seat-slots` was painted with people), the picture of the mannequin plus the empty room can be depth-estimated *together*, giving a per-pixel depth for the sitting body (shoulders nearer, knees farther, knees behind the desk), which fixes my "one depth per figure" failure. A cheaper way to get the same: run the depth model on the **figure** (or render the figure's depth from its seated pose) and compare per pixel. I did not test it.
- **Authored hands.** Arms resting on a desk cannot come from the scene's depth; they are an exception per spot (or per pose), whichever way it is built.
- **`art/seat-slots` already did a version of this with painted people** and nothing reads it (R1): painted people are the "blob per body" ChatGPT warned about: 160 distinct cut-outs for the Senate alone, fixed people, no control over who is sitting there. That is the evidence for ChatGPT's point and against using the plate as a person.

**Recommended path (for the owner to decide):** (a) compute a depth map per painting, once, offline (about 8 seconds each; 308 places is about 40 minutes of CPU); (b) for each spot, bake an **occluder mask** by the test above (a small PNG per spot, or a bit-packed mask) so runtime needs no depth at all, like a Sierra priority screen or an Infinity Engine walk-behind mask; (c) keep `staging.json` spots but let the owner's mannequin plate set their position, scale and facing by eye if he wants (job 1); (d) per-spot exceptions for hands on desks. The plain-white mannequins are then one way to author the spots, not the thing the engine draws. That answers both views: his depth instinct and ChatGPT's warning about silhouettes.

**What I did not do and what would change the answer:** the test used 5 to 7 stock figures on 2 rooms, a flat figure depth, a depth model I ran once, and a reimplementation of the game's crop, not a screenshot of the bench. A fair next test is the same with a per-pixel figure depth, hands on a desk, and 4 more rooms (a courtroom, a classroom, an office with a counter, a diner), plus the bench rendering a real scene both ways.
