## Left, each with its cause and its plan

### 6b. Pre-start as a history source

The reader (`src/core2/past-history.ts`) reaches the pre-start store in eight places with indexes a `HistorySource` doesn't have: rows by person (`preStartHistory`), by household or employer key (`pastRowsNaming`), by kind, by id (`pastIdsOf`), and the whole list (`pastRowsOf`, behind a cache keyed by its length, `ledToIndex`). The records proof does not judge the reader.

1. Build the capture first: `historyOf` for every full person, every household, a few employers and the place, with and without kinds, for `past` and `played`, hashed; take it before and after.
2. Read `pastRow` (`past-history.ts:246`) and the life source's `words`. If `words` needs only the five `WordedRecord` fields and `pastRow` leaves facts unchanged, the source hands rows back through `rows` and `row`; if not, the raw record must cross and this waits.
3. `PAST_MODULE` (`past-module.ts:233`) already carries a `history` for bills and laws: make that one source cover both. Do not add a module.

### 8. Fill-in out of state.ts: built (#10792), two things left

The functions moved whole to `people-fill-in.ts` and the plug carries them as `CoreModule.fillIn`; the 76 tests that fill people in all build people worlds, so a thin call in the state is safe. ### 9. One way to start a world (not built: three reasons)

`newGame` (saves.ts) takes a recipe and builds the opening, the core, the pre-start and the starting circle. The screens' `buildBenchWorld` (src/bench/world.ts, 965 lines) does more, through `benchWorldStages`, a generator that yields a stage at a time so the loading screen can show each: the place draw and `passOver` (the draw taken again, without the place, when no player can be picked), the opening's own steps, the pre-start, the warm-up (`warmUpDays`, `OPENING_WARMUP_DAYS` 31), the creator's choice of who the player is, and the opening story (`onWorld`, the director's). The replay calls `newGame` with no warm-up.

1. **It waits on Past and Day One's warm-up change** (their row 13: `OPENING_WARMUP_DAYS` changes in world.ts first); moving world.ts under it would collide with their edit.
2. **A design call, before the move:** the stage generator is the world assembler and imports the engines it assembles; in the core it becomes imports the `core-imports-no-engine` guard counts. The audit calls `life.ts` "the place that assembles the game" and normal; this is the same kind of file. The call is whether the assembler is a core file (and its engine imports are listed in that guard's baseline, shrinking only as engines hook in) or a file of its own that the core's `newGame` calls (`src/core2/generated-world.ts` and `past-sessions.ts`, kept in the game by item 2, are its two halves today).
3. **The loading screen and the opening story are the screens' and the director's:** the generator's stages and the `onWorld` callback are an interface the screens' play server drives; the core's one door takes the recipe and a `stage` callback, and the screens keep the route table (the CTO's 12:36 a.m. line).
   Plan once the three are settled: `newGame(recipe, { onStage })` runs the stages `benchWorldStages` runs now, the recipe grows `warmUpDays`, `playerBirthDate`, `playerGender`, `settings` and `placeKey`; `buildBenchWorld` becomes a call to it (as does `loadGame`); prove a loaded game matches a played one by the records proof of a world built both ways.

1. `coreAPI`'s builder (state.ts:881, about 1,400 lines) is split by area: the cash journal stays core as the one money ledger (`postJournal`, `completeJournalSource`, `registerCashAccount`, `releaseJournalSource`); finance contracts and credit move behind the budgets engine's door; work, jobs and business operations behind the jobs engine's door; the subject hooks (`onWorkPayroll`, `onSalesReceipt`, `onCountedWages`) leave `CoreModule` for the engines that own them. state.ts has 222 importers, so each move is a re-runnable script.
2. One folder per engine, by a re-runnable move script after every other session lands: `move-workshop.ts` (#10716) already holds the machinery (it rewrites relative imports, written paths, the families map, the guards' lists, `tsconfig.app.json` and `docs/current.json`, and keeps a tracked map of its moves); its `destination` becomes "the family's folder from `tests/guards/families.json`".
3. Judge each step by `npm run typecheck`, the guards, and the records proof byte for byte.
