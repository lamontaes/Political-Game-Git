### Is it one engine?

Mostly, yes.

The three are answerable-asks.ts ← scene-talk's types, spot-tags.ts ← situation-casting's lists, and **bill-sections.ts ← `properName` from director/words.ts**. That last one is a word helper living in the director. The director reads the world (575 imports of core2 against the English engine's 22).
- **Whole sentences go through the engine.** The repo's own line register counts 26 player lines written in code, across 9 files (13 in bench/progress.ts) and just 1 in the director.
- **The English engine is one pipeline:**

  - one admission gate, `admittedParts` with `directorSlots` (bank-parts.ts:85 and 101), shared by all seven composers;
  - the composers layered on top: talk, scenes, news, speeches, the Journal and screens.
- **Merging the two families into one would change no code.** What breaks the "one engine" is below: the small pieces of a sentence are built beside the engine.

### How grading works in the code, against his rulings

The pipeline, one chain:
1. A voice note becomes grades: scripts/dialogue-batch/voice-note-reader.ts (`readVoiceNote`) and spoken-grades.ts (`spokenToSpans`), into data/english/grades/*.json.
2. `foldGrades` (apply-grades.ts:227) counts each grade against the part keys of the line, into data/english/part-grades.json.
3. `heldByGrades` (english-grades.ts:78) holds a piece graded BAD or FIX and never GOOD (two shared bad lines if it only shared). `admittedParts` (bank-parts.ts:85) applies it in every composer.
4. `run-rules` (scripts/words/run-rules.ts) runs line-rules.json over every bank line and writes data/english/pulled.json, which the game reads.
5. review.ts plants his real verdicts into reviewer batches, and verdicts.ts folds reviewers' answers into data/english/judged/.
6. `npm run words:grade` (after-grading.ts) runs steps 2 and 4 in one command.
