## What the owner can play now

New on `core/money-officials-estates` (PR #4191, Oct 14): every official, up to the president, is paid for holding office, and when someone dies their money goes to their family or their state.

New on `core/wiring-office-and-husks` (PR #4167, Oct 14): when someone in town goes to the town office about a cause, a staff member answers within a week, and the player reads it in that person's journal ("I heard back from County of Jefferson."). Relatives who live out of town no longer act in town. Cause groups spend their donations on ads, and the people the ads reach change their minds and hear about it in the news. People the player meets later have health, and a capable 22-year-old can now be appointed to a town post.

In the evening build (PLAY's bench on the core): every game opens on January 20, 2025, and FEMA's real disasters reach the town on their dates through October 9, 2026 (1,610 of 3,282 counties had one begin in that span; White Plains, Kentucky saw 4 in 2025): news, federal aid to the town and its households, and repair work for its builders. Retirement claims follow Social Security law as Congress sets it. Children go to school on school days. A Woodmere, New York year from the 2025 opening runs in 76.6 seconds (Oct 14, one run). Businesses mostly stay open and their prices rise with the price index (where they had risen 21% a year); a raise, such as a new minimum wage, shows up in the employer's prices at its next monthly review. A sales tax collects on every purchase. The rest of the town is born, dies and moves. Counted voters start at their county's 2024 lean and their town's citizen share. Not visible until PLAY's opening uses the core's steps (Needs): the public school district and its teachers, private-school families, and the representative sample of the town. Saves in the base world load from a snapshot.
