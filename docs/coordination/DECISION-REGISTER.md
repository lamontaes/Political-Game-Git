OCD-LEG-NUM-001 — Bill numbers work the way they do in real life (September 27)

Lamontae, September 27: bills should be numbered as in real life, with the first bill of a session being HB 1 of 2026, "or whatever it's called in that jurisdiction." This binds:

- Numbers restart every session. A chamber's numbering returns to 1 when a new regular session opens: every year or every two years, as that state's session cycle is recorded. Congress restarts with each two-year Congress. Special sessions number as that jurisdiction does. Only the session the game opens in starts partway up (bills were already filed before the player arrived); every later session starts at 1.

- The session is part of the name. A bill shows its session, for example "HB 1 (2027 Regular Session)" or "H.R. 1, 120th Congress", so two sessions' HB 1 are never confused.

- Each place's own prefixes and chamber names. Every state uses its real bill prefixes and chamber names (for example AB and the Assembly in California, HF and SF in Minnesota and Iowa, A and S in New York, LB in Nebraska, the House of Delegates in Maryland, Virginia and West Virginia). They are taken from the recorded research (the verified enacted-bill samples for all 50 states), not typed from memory. A place with no recorded pattern keeps a clearly labeled game default. Cities and counties number ordinances and resolutions in their own recorded style where it is known.

- Deterministic and saved. The same seed and the same filed history give the same numbers; a filed bill keeps its designation forever.

Status: not built yet (today every game-profile state uses HB and SB, and numbering starts at a seeded number and never resets). Claude CTO builds it next, after the usage reset on September 27.

## The game's vision and scope, in Lamontae's answers (September 26)

These answers bind what goes into the game. Work outside them is cut or parked.

- **Who the player is.** A person living a life in which politics is essentially the only path. Jobs exist for income, because money has to have value (and later for corruption to matter materially). A few careers get light depth where they feed politics, such as law school, lawyer or law clerk. The player's character is not the world's main character.
- **The first "this is cool" moment.** Seeing the President and Vice President, clicking them and reading their records; then seeing a bill and everything legislation can do. The audience is people who want exactly that. Then inspecting the world: the news, how the world develops, that it is alive and that you can rise and make decisions. Later: personalities matter, you can build a movement, and even if you fail your son can ride the wave of your movement.
- **The long arc.** Open-ended, like Crusader Kings and RimWorld: one player reaches the presidency young, another makes a long Senate career, another starts over because they dislike how the world turned out. A save ends when the player wants; a save can always continue.
- **Cut from the game:** chores ("Waiting on you" errands such as groceries), favors, household evenings and shop shifts. Jobs remain as income, not as clicking work.
- **Kept, light:** a home, with some real benefits (commute time, access to different people) and a mortgage that creates money pressure; rent and living costs as part of the simulation. The rich part of the game is the political simulation, including its statistics.
- **Childhood is optional flavor** for players who care about the role-playing side: formative experiences, seeing the world develop before you decide, school friends or rivals who return later, small acts that matter later, or nothing consequential at all (over in a few clicks). It can create pressures, such as growing up poor and entering politics.
- **Schools and colleges.** If the real school exists in the game's data, use it by name; if not, generate one in real naming patterns (named for a local famous person, a county, a numbered public school). School districts stay in the simulation because they matter for taxes. School boards exist in the world; whether a player can serve on one is undecided. College applications are very light: three or four in-state options and a few out of state, applying to two or three with four or five questions. The main purpose is meeting people, including people from other places whose formative events differ; the qualification itself still matters.
- **Politics scope.** Every office listed is in scope: town council, mayor, county, state legislature, governor, U.S. House, Senate, President, appointed posts, party roles and judge. In office the player does all of it: bills, negotiation, administration, budgets, press, constituents and campaigns. The player can delegate to staff, whose results depend on a mix of their skill, potential, personality and passion (not a single rating), and staff can improve. Knowing people must pay off; the game keeps some notes for the player.
- **Budgets and foreign policy are in scope. As governor or president the player sets priorities and lives with the results, with advice from staff; going line by line, or anything in between, is optional depth.**
- **Real data and rules.** The game is modular like RimWorld and the Sims: shared blocks (for example one legislature module that several similar states plug into) rather than state-by-state custom work. At the start the world is similar to today with seeded drift; some small and a few medium laws may differ, recognizable rather than exact. State-level detail helps; small intricacies (for example a rule that the legislature fills a governor vacancy within 90 days and the governor after that) are too much detail, useful at most as fragments for generating laws. For municipalities, use a representative sample from a few places in each state, not exhaustive research.
- **Every place.** A player from the most rural place in America should find their place and feel they can start in their own community and make it better. All Census places stay. D.C. and the territories are included.
- **Pictures.** Backgrounds and interfaces mostly stay the same; people are what changes, and people have been the hardest part visually. The game does not ship without both the modular people and the backgrounds.
- **Reference games.** Suzerain for decisions that feel like they matter and real political tension (not knowing whether a media mogul will turn on you; losing after trying hard, then winning after working for it). Crusader Kings for lineage, the detail of nations and its interface. The Sims for modular people and how people are represented. The Political Process for its systems. BitLife for letting a player spend as much or as little time on a year as they like: depth is optional. He has never played Democracy.
- **What "playable" means.** A player can run for an executive or legislative office in any state and any city (municipalities from a representative sample), pass laws that affect the world, see people react, and do the same in an executive role.
- Movements. A movement can be all of these: a party someone founded, a cause organization, a body of followers and ideas that spread. A successor who is similar to the founder can inherit leadership of the movement, as Taft did from Theodore Roosevelt, Grant after Lincoln, or Lyndon Johnson carrying Kennedy's agenda while also acting on his own ambitions. Successors have their own motives; that is the depth the game is after.
- Pacing. The player can skip ahead until something needs them, over months or years, when they choose to; depth is optional.
- Bill catalog. Lamontae accepts "Legislative Content Menus — Proposal V1" (Idea Inbox) as the catalog for bills: 18 subject menus with drill-downs, the shared sections after a topic is chosen (current rules and programs, coverage and eligibility, benefit or rate, funding, administration, enforcement, reporting, timing and replacement), one identity per policy with cross-links, operations (raise, lower, expand, restrict, replace, repeal) inside each subject, and outcomes kept out of drafting controls. The subject wheel is navigation only (September 25). The drafting screen follows the approved bill-on-paper workflow. The real-bill research (222 enacted measures, September 26) scopes the catalog and the bill wording: it decides which drill-downs, sections and operations the menus offer (what recurs in real law, not every small item), and it supplies the wording patterns the English engine uses for legislative text, so a generated bill reads like a real one.
- Staff. Eventually a full staff: an executive has cabinet positions; a legislator has two or three people they deal with directly, plus generated general aides who are real people without being unique characters. The player can hire people they know (their son, someone they think has great potential) and can also take in applicants.

## How laws reach people, and the order to build it (September 26)

Lamontae approved these on September 26, after reviewing the audit and the Register with Claude CTO. Most of the direction was already decided (DEPTH1 items 2, 5 and 9; OCD-SIM-003; OCD-HIST-001; D-093; the September 22 tax and jobs entries); the engine did not follow it. These entries add what was new and settle the conflicts.

- **Depth is where the player is, and it connects everywhere.** Your area is simulated person by person each day. The wider population and other places run as modeled groups and on their own schedules, updated when something reaches them (a pay period, a news cycle, a meeting date). The causal links are the same everywhere.
- **Every bill has at least a money effect.** Every bill gets a fiscal note when drafted, and its money moves when enacted: spending costs its amount, a tax raises rate times base, an eligibility change moves enrollment times benefit, and a rule carries an enforcement cost for the government and a compliance cost for whoever it covers. This gives the 39 variants that had no effect a real one, which satisfies both "no effectless bill on the docket" (September 24) and "do not hide the 39 variants".
- **Six levers.** Every bill part becomes one of six levers: money, rate, who qualifies, rule (with penalty and enforcer), structure (create or abolish, local option, preemption) and process (reporting, oversight, deadlines, sunsets).
- **Measures that feed each other, not fixed rates.** Each subject menu tracks two to five measures a household can feel, and measures feed each other so loops create spirals up or down on their own (school quality, families moving, the tax base, school funding). Access (travel time to jobs, colleges and stores) and traffic are core measures that tie transit to schools, jobs and rents. Education gets a light world model: money per student, class size, teachers and pay, building condition, graduation rate.
- **Crises come from conditions that last.** A depression, civil conflict or recovery happens when real conditions stack up and persist, in stages that each re-check the world. Provisional numbers (DEPTH1 item 6) may set how long a condition must last, how bad counts as bad, and how much a reason weighs. They never set the chance that an outcome happens ("depression chance 0.01%", "85% of incumbents return").
- **Organized interests form from who loses money.** A bill's fiscal note says who pays; industries and groups that lose organize, lobby, donate, advertise and back opponents. This extends the personal-stakes rule for political people (September 26) to organizations.
- **Personality is the lens, not the side.** Personality sets what a person notices, how hard they react, whom they blame or credit, whose opinion moves them, whether they act, and how fixed they are. Which side they take comes from their life, values, family and experience. Consistent with D-093, a person's party follows from their views; party loyalty may be one reason behind a view, but party membership never assigns a person's positions at creation.
- **A view can have several reasons at once:** personal (what the law does to them), family, ideology or values, party loyalty, and friends. A person can hold mixed feelings, and the game can show the reasons.
- **Moving is a weighed decision.** Ties to stay (family, friends, job, a partner's job, the kids' school, a home they own) against pressures to leave (costs, taxes, crime, no work, disaster), weighted by the person's temperament. No flat rate per tax point.
- **Many ways to win.** Campaign support comes from the people a candidate actually reached, through the channels chosen (meeting people, speeches, a front porch, endorsements, news, ads, party). Named people count as themselves and modeled voter groups count as groups. Presidential races, especially the player's, are simulated in depth even if they take longer.
- **Ordinary readers.** Each person has a news habit shaped by age, interests and temperament, and news also spreads by word of mouth through relationships.

**Build order.** Four spines, each ending in something Lamontae can see in an ordinary game, and each connecting what exists before building new parts: (1) money: fiscal notes, everyone paid and taxed, pay and businesses by region; (2) measures: the six levers moving connected measures, starting with schools, access and traffic, housing and safety, with conditional crisis stages; (3) people: exposure, felt events, several-reason views through the personality lens, news and word of mouth, and actions including organized interests; (4) elections built from the people reached. Deferred: noise and pollution side effects, the full healthcare model, and defense and foreign policy depth.

Personality traits (September 26, evening). Lamontae approved the following. (1) One list of 97 traits: the 98 researched qualities plus the five core traits, minus six merges and cuts (Calm absorbs Even-tempered, Mischievous absorbs Cheeky, Daydreaming absorbs Distractible, Reserved and Gregarious versus Solitude-seeking are cut, and Responsible folds into the core 'follows through'). Five traits everyone has at some level; the other 92 are recorded only when notable. (2) Five strength levels: faint, a lean, clearly, strongly, defining. (3) For every person, not only the player: 1 to 3 inborn traits at random (strongly held one time in eight), and the rest from a generated upbringing and life. Adults usually hold 3 to 5 notable traits; children fewer, gaining more as they grow. (4) A trait is one reason among many in a decision. Its weight depends on how strongly it is held and how much the situation touches it, and close calls can go either way. A trait may hold in one part of life only (cruel in business, philanthropic in public). Opposite ends never pair, and a short reviewed list of clashing combinations is excluded. (5) Traits change through pushes that add up from different parts of a life over months and years, resisted by how strongly and how long a trait has been held. A rare severe event directly about a trait can change it at once. There is no timed fading; a trait fades only when life pushes against it. The game records why every change happened. (6) Others learn a person's traits from observed behavior; public figures' public traits are known from the start. (7) Encounters land according to both people's traits (two blunt people versus blunt with sensitive). (8) The player picks 2 or 3 traits in the intro survey or at creation, otherwise they emerge through play, and the player's traits unlock dialogue options in most circumstances. (9) The same system drives legislators, executives and ordinary people. (10) Every trait must be read by at least one decision, way of noticing, relationship effect or voice part; a check fails the build if any trait is read by nothing.

## The world outside your area (September 26)

Real-world statistics calibrate the simulation; the game generates its own world, and its values may go beyond anything seen in real life when the simulation leads there.

- **Your area** (your city and county, household, school and workplace): everyone is simulated each day.
- **Important people everywhere** (governors, state legislators, members of Congress, state and federal executives) are full people with personalities, histories and relationships, but they act when their institution acts: a session day, a hearing, a vote, a campaign event. Between those, institutions still move the way their people would. Bills keep advancing and are negotiated, but the simulation resolves them from the members' saved positions, stakes and relationships instead of playing each member's day.
- **Every other city and county** meets on its real schedule, usually twice a month. On a meeting date the game resolves that meeting: the agenda comes from that place's conditions and each member votes from their own saved record. Nothing there is computed between meetings, which is also why news from other towns arrives with natural lags. Laws they pass are real records with real effects.
- **Council members are real saved people,** thin at first (a name, views, a few traits and relationships) and filled in the first time the player looks closely, then saved permanently (D-005).
- **Personal stakes.** Every political person carries positions and also stakes from recorded events in their own life. When a stake's subject is on the table it can outweigh the general position; a fiscal hawk may protect one program because of something that happened to them. The player learns a stake only through a route the character could use (a conversation, a staffer, a news profile, research, the voting record) and can then use it.

## Politics is the main acceptance route, and laws must change the world (September 24)

The next proof has three gates, in order. First, federal, state, city and county measures with operative clauses are introduced and resolved under each level's actual authority; a city cannot amend a constitution. Second, the same seeded machinery produces distinct operative bills across all 50 states, D.C. and the five inhabited territories, using sourced baselines where they exist and clearly labeled fictional game profiles elsewhere. Third, enacted provisions change dated statistics for their jurisdiction through traceable typed effects. For spending, the first version may debit a simple jurisdiction balance and record the cost as a provisional model; a debit alone is never described as a delivered service. Person knowledge, attitudes, constituent feedback, detailed appropriations and rate polish follow these gates.

No effectless bill enters the normal docket: a subject offered to the player needs an operative consumer. Routine filings that do not become law may be summarized; enacted laws keep their individual text, procedure and traceable effects. Filing and passage rates follow saved conditions, institutional capacity and the election cycle, calibrated against official counts, not a fixed annual quota.

The full acceptance route Lamontae described: a candidate picks a real numbered seat, files, campaigns against the actual holder, and if elected replaces the correct member at the start of the term. The member drafts a multi-part operative bill, seeks support from actual colleagues, amends it, faces a subject committee and a floor vote, and receives the proper executive action and effective date. Enacted parts change the right tax, treasury or rule record; funded work needs a payment, possible delay or failure, a delivered service, bounded measures and resident exposure before any benefit is claimed. People learn and react through saved knowledge, interests and traits, and the next campaign can use that record. Save and reload keep every link.

## Every state legislature exists from the first day (September 26)

All 50 state legislatures are seated when a game begins, not only the home state's. Performance work must keep this and reduce the cost of a Day in other ways.

## Cities and counties have ordinary authority by default (September 24)

Cities and counties may propose ordinary taxes, spending and nonfiscal local rules under a broad default game authority. State law or a charter recorded for that place can narrow it. This replaces the blanket refusal of local instruments for game-profile cities and counties, without claiming every real municipality has home rule. Whether localities may change their own election and office rules: SETTLED September 28, 2026 by Claude CTO under default-to-realism. Yes, where the state's home-rule law or the place's charter allows it; no, where the state reserves it. An unread state starts from the most common rule among similar states.

## The wheel is a map of outcomes, not eight scores (September 24)

The U.S. News categories Lamontae shared (health, governance, economy, culture and global influence, civic health, opportunity, natural environment, infrastructure) are a coverage checklist for causal effects. One recorded cause, such as a law, court decision, executive action, employer change or project, may reach several domains with separate delayed, failed or limited outcomes. They are not eight disconnected score engines.

## A law changes the record first, and people feel it when the rule reaches them (September 22)

A passed bill first changes a concrete law, tax or budget record. Its effects on people, businesses and the economy occur when that rule reaches them: the next tax collection, an actual violation, a payment. When a city proposes a measure whose authority is disputed, the city attorney warns the player and a lawful challenge may proceed; a clear, settled prohibition blocks enactment. Which subjects a state preempts comes from that place's law and stays unknown until sourced; unknown is neither permission nor refusal.

## Taxes: assess everyone; zero can be lawful (September 22)

People and businesses are assessed under the taxes that apply to their income, wages, property, transactions or other taxable activity. A person may lawfully owe nothing. Liability and payment are separate records. When a tax cannot be paid, the unpaid balance stays in the world, and any notice, payment plan, penalty or enforcement follows that place's actual law. Routine cases may be summarized unless they affect the character. No one is charged a fixed tax simply for existing. Year-end settlement is automatic: a refund simply arrives, or a balance due is taken, with no return for the player to file (Lamontae, September 28, confirming this entry).

## Jobs reflect the place and make real offers (September 22)

Employers are generated to fit the place in kind and scale, and a real public body's name is used only when an official source verifies it; a verified name does not create a vacancy. Openings cover varied ordinary occupations, including work without advanced education, full-time work and credentialed roles. A listing shows the title, employer, work, expected hours, offered pay, qualifications and start date if known; a regional median sits in optional detail, never beside the offer as promised pay. Hourly work is quoted by the hour and salaried work by the year. A character applies or receives an opportunity through a relationship; a contact can recommend but not guarantee, and an employer may decline for a reason tied to the role and applicant. An accepted offer records an expected start; if the start passes, the employer may follow up or withdraw according to the situation. A listing is filled only by a represented hire.

## People, successors and contact (September 22)

A successor is an existing person with their own work, contacts and history. The handoff offers people connected to the current life and a searchable route to any eligible existing person, showing each person's own life before selection. They do not inherit the previous character's friendships, trust, spouse, job or knowledge. Playable younger people are eligible where their age has a genuine playable route.

A greeting is a dated contact that preserves familiarity and some existing trust; context can add a little trust when the person already admires the other or the encounter matters to them. A routine hello usually has little effect. A known person may initiate a call, invitation or request when their own schedule, relationship and reason support it. An unanswered invitation expires at its event time; whether the inviter minds depends on the relationship, and any consequence surfaces at the next interaction with that person, not as a separate notification.

When the player tells someone about a recorded event or plan, that person learns what was actually said. Whether they reply, help, keep quiet or pass it on follows their own knowledge, temperament, goals and relationship. The player learns of later sharing only through a route their character could access.

## Relationships and positions come from conduct (September 22; D-092 and D-093)

Being somewhere exposes a person to others; what they do and who they are makes the relationship, and anything conduct builds, conduct can damage. People decide their own political positions, positions change, and a party follows from a person's views rather than deciding them. Relationships may fade with absence, as a pace per bond, never a visible meter (OCD-LIFE-008).

## Childhood scenes and callbacks (September 22)

All eight earlier childhood follow-up drafts are rejected as game content. Effects of childhood choices vary by person and context: the recorded action and the other person's temperament and history decide the result, and a routine encounter may change no relationship. A later scene returns to an earlier choice only when it changes a relationship, creates a real opportunity or presents a meaningful new decision. Any replacement scene needs Lamontae's approval of its exact wording.

## First conversation family: a goal meets a real opportunity (September 23)

A person's aim changes which existing openings they notice, pursue or discuss. It never creates a vacancy, invitation or willing counterpart by itself. The initiator has their own aims and may decline; the controlled person is never auto-answered; a conversation can end with no relationship change; and with no real opportunity, no offer conversation appears.

## Journal voice (September 22 and 24)

The Journal has an expressive narrator whose statements about the character's inner life are grounded in recorded evidence; a feeling is never invented to make a sentence work. The main chronicle is titled "My life" and written in first person ("I"). It is selective: first job, major work changes, public office and similar milestones, with routine dated detail kept under Record. Ordinary scene narration elsewhere remains second person ("you").

## Storms come in four tiers (September 22)

Background weather, a storm felt in town, a damaging event, and a disaster. A storm reaches the character's town only if its footprint or effects do. Experienced severity and any emergency or disaster declaration are separate records; a declaration's area does not prove the town reached the highest tier.

## Press: fewer local reporters means fewer local stories (September 22)

A media owner may share stories across its outlets, crediting the originating newsroom, and may cut a newsroom. Lower local reporting capacity means fewer local meetings, investigations and everyday events covered, following the recorded staffing, beats and editors' response. Editors and staff may resist directives, with consequences.

## Statehood: local opposition shapes the fight (September 22)

Residents of D.C. and the inhabited territories hold individual, changeable views on statehood. Divided or opposed local opinion changes organizing, local votes where applicable, public arguments and congressional support; it does not end the effort automatically. A statehood drive runs through the institutions represented for that place. Statehood is a structural change in status and representation, so enacting it repeatedly without a change in D.C.'s status is a defect.

## Legislative elections use real dates (September 22)

Legislative elections follow the real election calendar of each jurisdiction, not a universal 28-day pacing rule.

## Time controls (September 24)

Day, Week and Calendar are the ways to move time. The separate "spend a little time on your own" button, its leisure choices, and the story-time skip controls are removed from ordinary navigation (\#606 closed, \#678 merged). A Day or Week stops at an accepted job's start.

## Wardrobe count (September 22)

Three formal and three everyday outfits for each presentation, masculine and feminine, each fitting all three bodies of that presentation: twelve authored outfits and thirty-six fitted combinations, with shoes, bottoms, hair and facial hair chosen independently. This replaces the earlier "nine things to test" wording.

## Only Lamontae approves art (September 20)

Only Lamontae makes human art decisions. No engineer, test script or agent may write a review under his identity or record approval on his behalf; a scripted test attribution is corrected through a traceable record, never by deleting history. A hash-valid image is not accepted intake or an installed asset. Every new exact asset version stays a candidate until he approves it.
