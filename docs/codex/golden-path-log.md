# The golden path, part 1: an eighteen-year-old files in person for the local board and meets 178 neighbors door to door

In 52 of the 56 places, a new life now goes from the eighteenth birthday to filing in person at a local counter. A second change sends filings in 33 states to the office each state's election code names, and dates every deadline before its own election. In Ardsley, Cynthia Lane's first campaign week met 178 neighbors at their doors, raising her unopposed share from 50% to 52.61%. Of 21 breaks, 14 are fixed here, 1 on main, 1 is designed behavior, 4 are open and 1 is unsupported. The largest open ones: a week-old save does not finish, and doors yield three to eight times the sourced conversation rate.

## The story, as first built

This is the path at the first change's final build. The second change, after the breaks, moves Ardsley's filing to the county board of elections and gives each deadline a year: August 16, 2027 for Trustee, and August 18, 2026 in Hopkinton. Hopkinton's counter becomes "Board of canvassers", its seat "Town council member", and its election November 3, 2026 instead of April 2.

**Ardsley, New York (seed golden-path-1).** Cynthia Lane is seventeen on January 5, 2026, living with both parents in the village. Her birthday is January 7. The player presses the next-day button twice and she turns eighteen. The Campaigns list now shows "Trustee" with "Eligible · Minimum age: 18 (estimated)".

Places offers "Westchester County", with "County clerk" beneath it. The player chooses it, and a visit goes on the calendar for 9:00 a.m. that day with the county clerk, Evelyn Sears. The player attends: Cynthia leaves at 8:40 a.m. and reaches the counter at 9:00. The player asks the counter's three questions in turn. The clerk answers for the eight seats her office takes filings for. The village's Trustee and the county's Commissioner ask 18. The village's Mayor and the county's Sheriff, District attorney, County clerk, County treasurer and Coroner ask 21. For Trustee the counter reads "November 2, 2027" for the election, "June 1 (estimated)" for the deadline and "$75.00 (estimated)" for the fee. Nobody has filed for any seat. The player presses "Trustee" and Cynthia is a candidate; the counter now lists her under Trustee, with the date she filed. Asking and filing take no time on the clock. She presses "Back" and is home at 9:20 a.m.

For the rest of the week the plan offers no shift. It reads "Host: none" beside a link to "Party and community work". On the same screen, each afternoon the player presses "Spend a session on the doors", whose note reads "An hour and a half of a day that has other things in it." Each session takes Cynthia to 22 doors. On Wednesday, January 7, 17 people come to the door; from Thursday to Sunday, 37 to 42 come each afternoon. By Sunday she has met 178 neighbors. Saturday, January 10, took 106 seconds to pass.

**Hope Valley, Rhode Island (seed golden-path-2).** Kian Haynes turns eighteen on January 7, 2026, in a village inside the town of Hopkinton. Rhode Island has no county governments. Places offers "Town of Hopkinton" with "Town clerk". At 9:00 a.m. the town clerk, Darius McDaniel, answers for the one seat his office takes, "Township board member". It asks 18; its election reads "April 2, 2026 (estimated)", its deadline "June 1 (estimated)" and its fee "$75.00 (estimated)". Kian presses "Township board member" and is a candidate. He is home at 9:20 a.m. Over five sessions on the doors he meets 176 neighbors, 21 on the first afternoon and 38 to 40 on each after it.

## What happens next

This package fixes breaks 4 to 10, 12, 16 to 19 and 21, with tests over all 56 places and generated-world tests of the counter and the doors. Its second change, below, fixes break 13 and, in 33 states, sends a filing to the office that state's election code names. Breaks 2, 3 and 15 and the world's growth go to the speed lane, break 20's missing jobs to JOBS-1, and the missing door-answer traits to the trait lane. The rules sweep takes the hand-written lines, the first four knock-ons and the second change's three estimates. Break 14 goes to the new TERR-LOCAL pool row.

## The second change: who takes the filing, and by when

In Ardsley, Places now offers "Westchester County" with "County board of elections" beneath it. At the counter is Rebecca Dalton, an election commissioner. Nobody held that office, so she was seated when the visit was arranged (measured). Trustee's election is still November 2, 2027, and its deadline is now August 16, 2027, marked as estimated (measured).

In Hope Valley, Places offers "Town of Hopkinton" with "Board of canvassers". The town clerk, Darius McDaniel, keeps its counter. The seat is now "Town council member", elected November 3, 2026, with a deadline of August 18, 2026, marked as estimated (measured).

For 33 states, the office that takes a local candidate's papers is now read from the state's election code (`filing-office.json`, read October 8, 2026). In the other 19 places with a local seat, the town's own clerk takes them, as before. The filing-office test checks that every state whose rules have a county office run local elections has its row (measured). In 10 of the 33 states the office is a board or commission. The person at its counter is the official the state's law puts there. South Carolina's municipal election commissions are the one board with no such official read, so a South Carolina town's own clerk keeps that counter. New York's county boards are two election commissioners (N.Y. Elec. Law § 3-200), and with none recorded the game seats one at the visit. A Rhode Island town clerk is by law the clerk of the town's canvassing board (R.I. Gen. Laws § 17-8-5).

A deadline is now the election date less the state's filing lead: the number of days between its 2026 candidate filing deadline and its 2026 congressional primary, from the Federal Election Commission's list of those dates. The lead is read for 52 of the 56 places, Guam among them. Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands use 85 days, the median of the leads read (measured).

Rhode Island had no town row. Each of Rhode Island's 31 towns now seats a five-member town council, as Hopkinton, Barrington and Smithfield do, read from the state's town directory and two town charters (inferred for the other 28 towns).

Three parts of this change are estimates. The counter officials for New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Rhode Island come from the statute that creates each post, not from a county's filing page. Georgia's comes from county acts and varies by county, while the game uses "Election supervisor" in every Georgia county. Every local deadline also uses the state's filing lead, or the median where none is read. All three go to the rules sweep.

## Method

The playthrough in `scripts/golden-path/` plays the path through the functions the shell's buttons call and prints each break; later parts add their steps after the campaign week. Each walk stays within seven simulated days, and places are drawn from all 56 by seed. The playthrough starts each life two days before its eighteenth birthday, so both characters turn eighteen on January 7, 2026. Each place was walked at least five times. The first walk came before any fix and the second after breaks 4 to 10 were fixed. A short third walk went to the clerk's visit, to read whether each clerk already held the office. A fourth walk measured the door sessions before the support fix (50.01%), and the final walk ran on the first change's last build (52.61% and 53.22%). The playthrough's own checks (a filing, support that moves, people met) find no break in Hope Valley's final week; breaks 13 and 20 there come from reading its results against the data and the sourced rates. The stories quote the final walks. The walk reads what each screen is given, not the rendered page, so wording such as "Host: none" is read from the screens' code. After the final walks, the counter's three question buttons became one "Continue" (break 21); the walk calls the same function either way. The final walks applied #3769's changes locally, uncommitted, so that the day after the birthday did not take more than 10 minutes; this branch no longer carries #3769. A probe run, seed gp-1, drew Bryant, Washington, and was used only to measure the crash and the day's length. Checked on main 03ffce4f, October 8, 2026. The second change was walked to the counter once more in each place, on main 74b777765 (after the first change merged) with the second change applied, the same day. The second change does not touch the doors (read from its diff), so the door and support figures stand; Hope Valley's 53.22% was measured while its seat was still named "Township board member".
