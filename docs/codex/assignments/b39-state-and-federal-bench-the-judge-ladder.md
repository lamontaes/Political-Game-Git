Verified against origin/main 40d67708d (Oct 6). Extends b13 (judge play, Session 42), reads b32 (court size and retirement rule fields) and b37 (confirmation scenes, reference only). One court engine under `src/simulation/judiciary` and `justice`.

## What the player experiences

You are a trial judge in a state that elects its judges. Your retention year comes, and the newspaper runs the lawyers' bar poll and the county's mood before the yes/no vote. You keep your seat, and three years later the governor names you to the intermediate appeals court from a commission's shortlist of three. Now you do not hear witnesses. You sit with two colleagues on a panel: briefs on the table, a case your old courtroom produced, the court's own earlier ruling on the law in question. Each of you says how you would rule. The presiding judge assigns who writes. A colleague disagrees and writes a dissent; you can join it, concur on narrower grounds, or sign the majority. The losing side asks the state supreme court to hear it and you learn the next week whether it did; the court's list of cases it takes comes from its own members' reasons, not a queue.

Years later you sit on the state's highest court when a statute the legislature passed last session reaches you. You vote to strike it. The opinion names the clause, the governor calls it an overreach, the author of the bill reads your dissent-minded colleague's line aloud at a hearing, and members decide between amending the bill, passing it again, or proposing a constitutional change. In a federal path the steps are district judge, circuit panel (with a vote on whether the full circuit rehears it), and the Supreme Court, where you may be asked for an emergency stay on a Friday night. Nobody gets a rank for free: each rung opens only through the real selection method of that court (election, retention, commission, governor, or President and Senate).

## Build steps (one PR each, in this order)

1. **Every vacancy gets filled, by the court's own method.** One function `fillJudicialVacancy(world, seatId)` read from the court's selection profile: governor from a commission shortlist, governor alone, governor plus confirming body, popular election (hand to Session 13 as a contest row), legislative election, judicial assignment. Writes `seatJudge` with the matching `JudicialSeatSelectionPath`. Wire `vacantSeatsAt` and the retirement/death/term-end exits (step 2) to it. `Replaces:` the Supreme-Court-only vacancy path's duplicate seating code in `supreme-court-appointments.ts` once b37's scenes call the shared function (delete only the duplicated part). Must not: a second nomination engine; the President and Senate steps stay in the existing functions.
2. **Judges leave by rule.** At `mandatoryRetirementAge` (court rule, via b32's field), `termEndsAt`, or a person's own choice (decided from their records), call `vacateJudicialSeat` with the existing end reasons. Set `termEndsAt` and `retentionDueAt` at every seating from the court's rules. `Replaces:` the null `retentionDueAt` literals at `supreme-court-appointments.ts:1002` and `opening.ts:212`.
3. **Retention and elections, as the reading side.** Write `retentionContests` rows for seats whose profile says retention; the vote itself is Session 13's contest. Build: the voter-facing reading (bar poll, rulings the town remembers) as considerations from records, and the result applied to the tenure (`election-loss` or a new term). Must not: write results here; a retention percent.
4. **Panels.** New `judiciary/panels.ts`: for an appellate case, the court's seated judges are drawn into a panel by the court's own rule (three for most circuits, all justices for a supreme court), without dice: rotate by seat order as `court-reasoning.ts:439 sentencingJudge` does and skip recused judges (b13's recusal). Each judge decides affirm, reverse, or send back through `evaluateDecision` (b13 step 4 owns the reasons: inside the law, precedent rows, outlook). The result record holds the vote, the author, and each separate writing. Extends b13's `justice/appeals.ts`; same module for federal and state.
5. **Opinions, dissents and assignment.** The senior judge in the majority assigns the opinion (chief or most senior; the player may assign when they are that judge). A writer writes through the English engine from their considerations and the case facts (`presentation/english-composition.ts:215 composeGroundedLine`; no authored lines). A dissent or concurrence is a recorded option for each judge; opinions write knowledge to the parties and the press desk (b13 step 5 channel). Cite precedent rows; the opinion becomes a new precedent row in the save (one table, `judiciary` records), read by `considerationsFor`.
6. **Control of the docket.** Higher courts choose which cases to hear: each member decides to take or deny a petition from the case's records (split between courts, size of the law's reach, a conflicting ruling in the record); a court's own number of cases taken is the result of members' reasons, not a quota. Federal circuits may rehear en banc (a member's request, then a majority). Emergency orders (stay, injunction pending appeal) run on a short clock through the same decision. Same function for state supreme courts where the profile gives discretionary review; courts without it hear every appeal.
7. **Law versus court versus legislature.** Extend `judicial-review.ts` so a law reaches the intermediate or federal court first where real procedure says so (`reviewingCourt` today skips them), the panel writes an opinion, and a struck law writes a public event the legislature's members read. What members do next is decided by the one vote engine (`governing/chamber-votes.ts decideChamberVote`) with their own reasons: amend, re-pass, appeal, or propose a constitutional change (instrument is b32's `ruleChangeAuthority`). Court size by law (b32): after `applyEnactedCourtSizes` adds seats, step 1 fills them through the court's own method. Must not: add reluctance numbers; a court-packing special case.
8. **The player's climb.** Make each rung reachable: a judge in a state with elected benches files through Session 13's contest; a commission state adds the commission step (applicants, shortlist, governor chooses) as scenes; federal: President's choice from the existing nominee pool (including the player; delete the `excluded` controlled-person filter at `supreme-court-appointments.ts:157` for a willing player). Scenes only through Session 4 rows; no authored lines.
