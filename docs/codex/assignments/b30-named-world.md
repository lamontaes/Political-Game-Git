# The named world: colleges as real places, every named person is a person, news read by habit (bank id b30, Session 54)

Phase "Living world" · Unlocks Ivy League and campus-politics paths, a college town that feels like itself, and a press you can click through to a person. Code checked at origin/main 2e7ac6cef (Oct 6).

## What the player experiences

The Ivy League schools, famous political-hotbed campuses and every state's flagship are real places with real names, with their campuses painted and their student bodies different from the community college down the road. Any person the game names, in the paper, in a speech, on a committee, in a dialogue line, is somebody: tap the name and see their record, with the same traits, relationships and history as anyone else. Each person reads their own mix of news from habit: a daily paper, a local weekly, a lighter outlet (sports, entertainment, lifestyle), talk radio or none, and that shapes what they know. Private businesses and outlets keep generated names; only public bodies, hospitals, colleges and banks carry real names.

## Build steps (one PR each)

1. **Colleges are places.** One record per IPEDS institution actually used in play (created on first need, never all 6,000 at open): id from IPEDS id, real name, kind (flagship, ivy-league, political-hotbed, regional-public, private, community) read from `art/campuses/manifest.json` where painted and from a data row `collegeKinds.json` otherwise. Ivy League = the 8 schools; flagship = each state's and territory's (`kind: "flagship"` rows exist); hotbed = a researched data list. Enrollment, study, tuition read the same catalog. `Replaces:` any college name string typed in play code.
2. **A campus gives a life.** A person studying at a college is placed in its town with roommates and classmates generated by the one people path; student politics (clubs, protests, College Democrats/Republicans chapters) come from the college's kind row through the existing scene and organization writers, not authored scenes. Alumni of a college tie to its records (donors, networks) through existing relationships.
3. **Every named person is a person.** Audit: grep play and presentation for names produced by string (news bylines, officials in text, "the senator") that have no person record. For each, create the person through `createLightweightPerson` + `ensurePeopleTraits` at the moment the name is first written, with the thin record filled on first look (D-005). Add a name-resolution test: every personal name in a rendered news story, speech or dialogue line resolves to a person id.
4. **Real names only where the owner allows.** A guard test: no real company, outlet, brand or national chain name appears in generated private names or outlet names; real names appear only for public bodies, hospitals, colleges, banks (the lists come from b23's reader and the IPEDS catalog).
5. **News by habit, including lighter outlets.** Add products `lighter-outlet`, `sports-and-entertainment`, `talk-radio` (data in `MEDIA_PRODUCTS` and a plan row each; fictional generated names per place). Each person gets a `newsHabit` record (which products, how often), derived from age, interests, temperament, and updated when life changes; exposure to a story happens through `recordStoryHeardExposure` when a habit reads it, not only through an explicit read. Word of mouth stays as is. Habit formation uses the one decision evaluator (`randomness: "none"`).
6. **All places.** Test loops 50 states, D.C. and territories: flagship exists, college record resolves, a named person resolves, a habit exists for sampled residents.

## Must not build

Hand-typed lists of famous people or colleges in code; a second person or organization writer; real news outlet or brand names; chain stores; a "politically active" stat on a person or college shown as a number; authored campus events; all colleges instantiated at start; any dice in habits.
