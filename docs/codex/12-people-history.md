# Job 12: people's history in the dossier

## Why

Lamontae, September 29: "I'd like to know why these people are bosses or something but no history pops up about anybody." In the playtest, the President's dossier was nearly empty, and "Full record" showed the same thin pop-up.

## Work

1. **The dossier and full record read the person's own records.**
   - Who they are: age, hometown and where they live now, family, party, faith.
   - Their career: every job with its employer and dates, every office run for and won, organizations they lead and how they came to lead them.
   - Their public record: votes, bills sponsored, positions, approval where it's measured.
   - Their relationships: allies, rivals, favors given and owed.
   - Money: income, property, and business ties, where the player could plausibly know them.
   - Recent events that involved them.
2. **The history before play fills it.** Lamontae approved D-9: the years before the game starts run the same hiring and favor rules as play. If that history isn't generated yet for officeholders and local leaders, generate it, deterministic for a seed. Then a boss is a boss for reasons the dossier can show, and nothing is labeled "machine" or "company town".
3. **What the player knows.** Show what the player could know through the knowledge system, and mark the rest as unknown to them, not as missing data.
4. **Text** comes from the English engine, from the records. No filler.

## Checks

- Open the dossiers of the President, the governor, the mayor and a council member, plus one ordinary neighbor, in a random place, with screenshots.
- Every line must trace to a record.
