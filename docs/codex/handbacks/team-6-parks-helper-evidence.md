## Idaho — positive dedicated gasoline-tax source, January predecessor reconstructed in part

Primary current code: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title63/T63CH24/SECT63-2412/ (`id63.html`, `id63.txt`). History ends with 2026 chapter 98 section 2, so current wording cannot be directly frozen at January 1, 2026.

Primary 2026 amendment: https://legislature.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/H0716.pdf (`idH716.pdf`, `idH716.txt`). Official enacted index https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/ (`id2026laws.html`, `id2026laws.txt`) identifies chapter 98, H716, approved 03/20/2026, effective 07/01/2026. H716 section 4 explicitly supplies the July 1 effective date. Amendment text supplies pre-amendment wording as well as new wording; do not mechanically concatenate strike/add text.

Section 63-2412(1)(f)1 and 2 EACH allocate 1.28% of the balance after paragraphs (a)-(e), and EACH sends 33% of that allocation to the park and recreation capital improvement account. Section (1)(f)3 adds .44% of that same remaining balance, restricted to developing, constructing, maintaining and repairing roads, bridges and parking areas within and leading to state parks/recreation areas. Exact combined park-account fraction is 33% × 1.28% twice, plus .44% = 1.2848% of this statutory remaining balance; this calculated sum is NOT a share of all gasoline-tax receipts. Section (2) makes this allocation apply only to 25 cents of each 32 cents of §63-2402(2) receipts. H716 pp. 3–4 leaves these park shares and 25/32 limitation unchanged.

January predecessor adjustments verified from H716: §(1)(d) bridge inspection deduction was $175,000, not the new $300,000; §(2) remaining seven cents previously went directly 60% state highway / 40% local distribution, rather than the new highway distribution account route. Other pre-share deductions include actual collection cost, sufficient refunds, $250,000 railroad crossing amount, and 7% state highway amount. Exact percentages must retain denominator and deduction conditions.

Account-purpose primary law: https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title57/T57CH18/SECT57-1801/ (`id57.html`, `id57.txt`), history latest 1988 chapter 253 section 2. Exact excerpt: “The purposes for which moneys in the account may be used shall be to acquire, purchase, maintain, improve, repair, furnish, and equip parks and recreation facilities and sites in the state of Idaho.” Department administers. This is positive qualifying tax evidence; do not substitute §67-4225 concession/rental fund (`id.html`, `id.txt`), which is a separate fee/receipts route.

## North Dakota — mixed conservation/parks tax source

Primary allocation: https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t57c51.pdf (`nd57.pdf`, `nd57.txt`), §57-51-15(1)(a), printed p.7: “The tax revenue collected under this chapter equal to one percent of the gross value at the well of the oil and one-fifth of the tax on gas must be deposited with the state treasurer.” First ordered allocation is “Eight percent of the amount available under this subsection to the North Dakota outdoor heritage fund, but not in an amount exceeding twenty million dollars per fiscal year.” Thus NOT 8% of all oil/gas revenues; preserve base and annual cap.

Primary purpose/continuing appropriation: https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t54c17-8.pdf (`nd.pdf`, `nd.txt`), §54-17.8-02 money continuously appropriated; §54-17.8-03(1)(d): “Conserving natural areas and creating other areas for recreation through the establishment and development of parks and other recreation areas.” Other grant purposes are sportsmen access, agriculture stewardship, habitat and conservation. Land acquisition/encumbrance over 20 years barred; maintenance and paving etc. require exceptional-circumstances finding.
