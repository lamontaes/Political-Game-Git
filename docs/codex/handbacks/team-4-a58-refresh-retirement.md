# Life refresh stops adding a second set of town businesses

Life refresh now keeps the town employers already saved during opening.
It no longer seats and settles a second, legacy set of businesses.
Five generated worlds retain their actual jobs, weekly pay contracts and
canonical reload behavior. Only the released import and call change.
Other legacy-business entry points and historical tests remain; the old module
is not deleted.

## 1. Why-chain

A normal opening now produces canonical town employers before adult hiring.
The later life refresh still called refreshLocalEconomy. That helper seated
legacy local-business organizations and settled their own business flows.
Thus a refresh created another set of employers even though the player's
actual employer already existed. The chain ended in a duplicate legacy engine.

The released caller now starts from the same world and keeps its existing
salary, job-market, mortgage, living-cost and opportunity sequence.
The terminal is the actual recorded employer and work contract, preserved
through those existing writers. No replacement sales or payment engine runs.
The removed call was src/simulation/life-opportunities.ts:451; its import
was at line 22. Those are the only production changes.

## 2. Research

Audit gap A58 authorizes retirement of the old business engine caller by caller.
Team7 and Team3 explicitly released this import and invocation.
No wage, sales amount, market share, paid-outcome rate or revenue assumption
is introduced. Existing business reviews and payroll remain their owners' paths.
Saved legacy compensation and remaining seating callers still need their own
contracts before the broader engine can be removed.

## 3. Revisions

Salary initialization and monthly scheduling are not edited. Neither are
compensation, job-market, mortgage, living-cost or opportunity writers.
The opening prerequisites remain separate exact published heads.
This removes one lifecycle entry into the legacy engine; it does not migrate
old organizations or contracts, delete helper exports, or create replacement
cash. The full saved world intentionally loses the duplicate legacy additions.

## 4. What gets built

1. Remove the sole production refreshLocalEconomy import and invocation.
2. Keep every following lifecycle writer in its existing order.
3. Run the unchanged five-place fixture before and after the candidate.
4. Preserve recorded employer, work, compensation and reload assertions.
5. Keep remaining legacy entry points and historical tests for later migration.

## 5. Simulated, records, world pieces, checks

Actual employers and paid staff roles already exist through the opening
prerequisite. The player's work and pay contract refer to one of those employers.
This repair is caller bookkeeping, not a new hiring or business decision.
If the existing world has no employer, this caller invents none.

Five places are scoped proof, not fifty-six populated worlds. The test checks
saved contract parity; it does not check business cash, a completed paycheck,
future employment or revenue. Payroll and quarterly business composition remain
with their existing owners. No second review or clock caller is introduced.
