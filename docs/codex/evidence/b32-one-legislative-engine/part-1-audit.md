## Shared steps and data seams

`legislative-clock.ts` owns measure progression for state and federal bills and for local bodies that call it: referral, hearing/report, calendar placement, floor stage, transmission/concurrence, enrollment, presentment, veto override, and enactment are derived from the measure phase and rule pack. `legislation.ts` owns append-only action recording and replay validation. Chamber rosters and decisions use the shared chamber vote engine. Conference is the exception: `ConferenceRule` exists in rule data/validation, but there is no conference action or `MeasureStepKey` in the clock. A second chamber that amends the bill reaches concurrence; there is no conference committee resolution path.

Data already has many of the intended variation points: seats, quorum, referral, committees, floor stages/amendments, chamber order, executive action/override, enactment and session calendar. It is not yet complete/used end to end for conference, all local presentment and effective-date handling, or a unified sitting calendar/handler.

## Duplicated orchestration to remove or reduce in Part 2

1. **Body-wide measure scans and sitting loops:** Congress `congressSittingHandler`; selected local city/county `moveOrdinances` within `localCouncilMeetingHandler`; and D.C. `moveActs` within `dcCouncilSittingHandler`. Each finds open measures, advances them, and reschedules its own body event. State progression instead schedules each measure through `scheduleInstitutionStep`; the new `state-legislature-queue.ts` is for dated state election/nomination/ballot/term wakes, not bill progression.
2. **Local council passage paths:** background councils call the clock with local-body context; player-facing `decideOrdinaryCouncilReading` / `recordCouncilReadingVote` and the D.C. `decideCouncilVote` / `moveActs` separately assemble the council question and roster. The record wrapper re-enters `applyInstitutionStep` for a floor vote, but the vote orchestration, admission, quorum, timing, amendment, and deadline checks remain in local adapters.
3. **Referral bypass:** D.C. directly places a measure on the calendar in `moveActs`; player municipal agenda placement directly calls `placeMeasureOnCalendar`. These are justified only where the body's compiled pack says no committee/referral step applies. The D.C. source comment still labels the no-committee assumption a placeholder.
4. **Executive desk plumbing:** state governing matters, the congressional President desk, and municipal executive deadlines each open/resolve their own office route. They share `evaluateGovernorBill` and the canonical executive action writer; retain the office/player differences as callback/data, not another bill state machine.
5. **Municipal post-passage branch:** `completeCouncilPassage` and `enactCouncilMeasure` own enrollment/presentment/deadline/effective-date follow-through and enactment effects for council measures instead of leaving all follow-through to the generic clock. The shared record/effect writers are already reused.
6. **Separate sitting calendars:** Congress, D.C. and local councils each have their own scheduling functions and due keys; state bills schedule measure-by-measure. Move policy into session/body rows and have the unified handler consume those rows, preserving distinct actual calendars as data.
7. **Term-limit law is a separate rule route:** `local-council-term-limits.ts` carries a hard-coded two-consecutive-term default, and `federal-reform.ts` includes an Article V proposal/vote path. Part 7 should route ordinary statutory limits through one `term.limit` value; retain Article V only when the instrument is constitutional.

## Part 2 deletion boundary

Delete the three existing body-wide sitting handlers after parity passes: `congressSittingHandler`, `localCouncilMeetingHandler`, and `dcCouncilSittingHandler`. State measures currently use per-measure `scheduleInstitutionStep`; the audit found no state body-wide sitting handler. Part 2 must give state sittings a `SessionRule`-driven body wake and route its measures through the same engine. Keep `state-legislature-queue.ts` for its election lifecycle; it is not a bill-sitting loop. The replacement must not branch on a level-name string: body/chamber/session rows and callbacks are inputs. Preserve the separate opening public meeting and local member-player interaction as producers/experience adapters, but have their legislative work enter the same sitting engine.
