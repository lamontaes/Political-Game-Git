# The federal budget has one live monthly settlement

Before: The monthly budget pass saved the federal account's actual payments
and also advanced a separate forecast treasury, including automatic deficit
borrowing.

After: The common government account is the only live federal settlement.
Legacy forecast records remain readable and unchanged. Without a saved cash
account, the pass records no federal cash month. It does not invent a loan.

## 1. Why-chain

The federal budget records actual payments because an appropriation is only
permission to spend. Why are projected outlays insufficient? They do not
identify a payer, recipient or completed transfer. Why must there be one
settler? Two monthly tallies can disagree while describing one government's
money. Why retain old forecast bytes? Old saves already contain them, and
changing those records would rewrite history. Why refuse a missing account?
Its cash cannot be established by a forecast. Bedrock: saved account positions
and resource-transfer outcomes. No person receives a ride or other service
solely because this accounting pass ran.

## 2. Research

No new rate or money level is introduced. The existing seven receipt and
thirteen outlay categories remain. Opening debt metadata remains the existing
sourced Treasury baseline or the saved legacy migration value. Actual cash
and partial payments come from canonical resource records. This change does
not infer a government borrowing event from an operating shortfall.

## 3. Revisions

The monthly caller stops invoking the legacy forecast calculator. The
calculator's exported compatibility API remains for isolated archive tests;
it has no live production caller. Both actual payment classification and
government settlement remain in their existing common implementations. The
earlier opening debt reader remains a one-time metadata migration when a
legacy save lacks the federal government row.

## 4. What gets built

1. Retire the monthly forecast call and preserve archived save bytes.
2. Keep partial outlays, exact source IDs and the same government cash result.
3. Preserve old-save account admission before consuming the payment cursor.
4. Prove missing-account refusal, repeat safety and canonical reload.
5. Keep historical forecast tests explicit about using the archived API.

## 5. Simulated, records, world pieces, checks

Simulated: no new official choice, person receipt, service attendance or loan.
Records: one federal budget row reads actual cash and payments, with existing
stamps and category mapping. World pieces: a saved national government account
and positive completed or partial transfers. Missing account evidence stays
unsupported. Checks: the common outlay equals the actual paid component, not
the legacy projected level; replay and reload do not count a dollar twice.

## 7. Worked example

In each of the 56 controlled jurisdiction contexts, the national account starts
with an authored $100.00. A saved $3.25 partial outlay leaves $96.75. The common
month names its actual flow and transfer IDs, including after canonical reload.
The legacy forecast does not gain a month. An old save lacking the federal
government row still records the same actual payment before the cursor moves.
These are authored cash fixtures; no citizen or service outcome is inferred.
