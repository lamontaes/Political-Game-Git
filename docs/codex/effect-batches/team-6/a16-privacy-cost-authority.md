# Privacy laws no longer create an invoice from population

Before: Passing or repealing a privacy law changed projected government
spending by population times another state's fiscal-note estimate. Separate
writers then attributed the same arithmetic to each law.

After: Those two price rows and duplicate writers are removed. A government
cost requires the law's own stated appropriation or staff actually hired and
paid through payroll. Existing saved attribution fields remain readable.

## 1. Why-chain

Government cost requires a recorded obligation or payment. Why does a fiscal
note not establish that cost elsewhere? It describes another government's
proposal, staffing assumptions and scale. Why can population not prove an
invoice? It names neither a payee nor a purchased input. Why is an
appropriation different? Its final enacted amount grants spending authority,
which an actual commitment and cash transfer must still use. Why keep payroll
separate from authority? An agency must actually hire someone before paying
that employee. Bedrock: final adopted appropriation records and actual saved
employment payments. No staff, firm invoice or person outcome is invented.

## 2. Research

The removed estimates were Colorado and Virginia consumer privacy fiscal
notes and Utah's age-verification fiscal note. Their numbers are not universal
prices. CTO's approved rule uses recordProgramAppropriation for a final stated
appropriation and the sole payroll for actual agency hires. This repair adds
no empirical rate, new population proxy or forecast employment.

## 3. Revisions

The monthly reader no longer invokes either duplicate attributor. Both
population-price rows are removed from SPENDING_QUESTION_EFFECTS. The
historical GovernmentLawCostAttribution shape moves to the budget store,
preserving its optional saved field. Other spending rows, cannabis behavior,
actual transfer classification and common settlement are unchanged.

## 4. What gets built

1. Remove the two fiscal-note invoice rows.
2. Remove both per-law cost modules and all production references.
3. Retain saved historical attribution bytes without creating new ones.
4. Prove lawful enactment and repeal do not fabricate government cost.
5. Preserve cannabis coexistence, missing context, replay and canonical reload.

## 5. Simulated, records, world pieces, checks

Simulated: no new staff decision, platform invoice, appropriation, commitment
or payment. Records: the monthly budget no longer attributes a nonexistent
cost. World pieces: an actual final enacted amount is required for the
existing appropriation path; a staff cost requires actual employment and
payroll. Their absence is unsupported authority, not measured zero cost.
Checks: enactment or repeal alone must not move spending or balance. Other
actual charges and saved historical records must remain intact.

## 7. Worked example

A controlled age-verification enactment in California is visible to the legal
reader. It has no stated appropriation or actual agency hires, so it creates
no extra administration spending or cost stamp. A separate cannabis repeal
still keeps its own revenue-loss stamp and identical financial result when
the age-verification measure is present. The privacy fixtures similarly
compare enactment or repeal with unchanged money, then reload the actual
saved government budget and refuse duplicate settlement.
