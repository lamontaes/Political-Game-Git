# Statehood leaves the law on people's voting-seat records

MERGED: not merged. This bounded federal stamp addition preserves the existing
statehood seating producer and records the enacted law behind each person's
new voting-seat tenure.

## 1. Why-chain

Admission creates congressional representation because the enacted federal law
adds voting seats. Each seat needs a person because a legislature's members cast
its votes. The producer records those people in public tenure events so Congress
can read their membership. Each event carries the operative law because that
law authorized the additional seat. Bedrock is the legal representation rule
and the person's saved office record. The current producer does not simulate
the first elections or presidential certification decisions.

## 2. Research

Existing source data cites H.R. 51 of the 119th Congress: sections 102 and 103
address elections and admission; section 403 bounds certification. It records
one House seat and two Senate seats. The inherited schedule adds statutory
maximum allowances of 60, 30 and 90 days; it is explicitly hardwired and omits
actual election timing. This patch introduces no empirical coefficient, cost,
price or new outcome draw. Research is preserved in
`data/research/congress/statehood-seats.json`.

## 3. Revisions

The stamp resolves the law separately at each recorded term's start, preserving
historical dates. No missing law is inferred. Existing delegate IDs, new member
IDs, seat records and chamber membership remain unchanged. Statehood's legal
irreversibility and the current repeal interpretation require a separate domain
contract; this change does not rewrite existing tenure history.
