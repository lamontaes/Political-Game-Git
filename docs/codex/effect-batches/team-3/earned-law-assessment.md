## 1. Why-chain (five whys, to bedrock)

Why save an obligation? The operative law can require more than the contract.
Why preserve the contract? It is the saved agreement under which this work was
completed. Why use the completion interval? Pay belongs to actual work, rather
than an assumed weekly schedule. Why bind its history frontier? Later records
must not change earlier work. Why require a validated transfer reference? A
caller cannot establish a lawful larger payment by supplying a larger amount.
The chain ends at the legal rule and the saved physical work interval.

This component does not model wage bargaining, employer compliance decisions or
every effect of the minimum-wage law. Those are separate existing mechanisms or
gaps, not outcomes inferred from this check.

## 2. Research

This implementation uses CTO-approved earned-work and statutory-pay contracts.
The $20 floor is an authored enacted test law, not a claim about Nebraska's real
starting rate. It adds no researched level, wage table or rate estimate. The
root-owned canonical dated wage rows remain separate sources.

1. The existing common pay writer validates completion, interval, earned terms

4. The focused producer check proves actual lineage and repeat dispatch. The

## 5. Simulated, records, world pieces, checks

SIMULATED: this check completes a real scheduled work session through the
existing life-path action. It does not claim an autonomous job or law decision.

RECORDS: the assessment carries the worker, employer, work, flow, original

CHECKS: no obligation is added when the saved contract already exceeds the
