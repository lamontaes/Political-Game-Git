## 1. Writing a bill

**Sept 21, 2026, the register recording his choice (REG:420):**

> "4. Legislation: BOTH objective-guided contextual draft/staff-proposal and direct modular provision construction/editing. Include multi-subject bills. The same compatible policy components produce initial law, new proposals, amendments and repeal; no fixed named-Act bank as the production generator. Text and consequences must be driven by the same structured meaning. OWNER REAFFIRMATION after the ordinary-life playtest: legislation must now reach implemented gameplay, not remain research or a headless compiler. ... A normal player must be able to pursue an actual measure, draft/edit its parts, negotiate actual provisions and commitments, move it through the applicable decision route and observe implemented consequences."

**Sept 25, 2026, his approval as the register records it (REG:422):**

> "LEGISLATION DRAFTING WORKFLOW — OWNER APPROVAL, SEPTEMBER 25, 2026: The current preferred workflow is a physical bill/working-document interface rather than a dashboard or gamified builder. A subject index or wheel may help the player enter a policy area, but it is navigation only and its exact visual treatment remains open; the owner specifically rejects the recent colored wheel treatment. Once a subject/current policy is selected, the bill itself becomes the primary workspace. Editable provisions or phrases are selected directly on the paper, with a compact contextual panel offering only supported operations such as changing eligibility, thresholds, funding, timing, reporting, replacement/repeal, or asking staff/counsel to prepare a revision. Parameter-specific micro-editors should match the change being made rather than use one generic form. Proposed wording is previewed on the paper and can be read as a clean copy or compared with current wording. Staff-prepared revisions are optional and use the same canonical bill meaning; direct editing remains available. "Add another section" reopens subject selection and adds another compatible typed component to the same bill, subject to jurisdiction/authority rules. The paper/current provision records remain the authoritative representation; side panels, notes, staff advice and navigation are projections over that same draft and must not drift into a second bill state. Preserve the current game's restrained office/paper visual language and existing compact navigation rather than replacing it with a new permanent menu or resource HUD. This approval is for the workflow; exact wheel styling, placement and final pixel treatment remain for prototype/playtest review."

## 7. Not described by the owner

Each of these is shown plainly in the approved look, and marked here so he can rule on it.

1. **What the subject index looks like.** He said only that it is "navigation only", that its look "remains open", and that he rejects the colored wheel. The example uses a plain list of the 18 subjects and the chosen one's topics.
2. **Which committee a bill goes to and who sits on it.** He described hearings with witnesses and questions (Oct 12), not referral or membership. The example sends the bond to the board's finance committee, which Marcus Hill chairs in the prototype.
3. **Markup.** The pool has the CTO's item `gov-committee-markup-trades`. He hasn't described a markup screen.
4. **How an amendment is shown.** He wants amendments, poison pills and riders, and a compare view (REG:422). He hasn't described an amendment screen. The example types the amendment as a strike and an insert, the way real amendments read.
5. **The floor vote layout.** He has said "cast your vote", that the old voting screen was terrible (Oct 29), and asked "When? How? Who else?" (Oct 26). The layout is mine.
6. **The executive's desk and the veto message.** He said the executive signs or vetoes everywhere. The desk screen and the typed veto message are mine.
7. **The override screen.** Beyond "feels like a real game" (Sept 26), it isn't described.
8. **Tracking a bill.** He hasn't described a page for following a bill. The example follows The Political Process's bill page (`refs/tpp/ss11`: sponsor, the bill, support in each chamber, a dated history), which he named as the systems model. Per the Oct 29 record it shows no exact support count before a vote.
9. **Who writes a town's estimate.** He said every bill gets a fiscal note (Sept 26) and that the estimate is "how the CBO would estimate". The example has the town clerk, Margaret Hale, sign Latimer's estimate, and Mississippi's Legislative Budget Office give the state's.
10. **Naming a bill.** On Oct 26 he complained "you can't change the bill name or anything". On Oct 14 he ruled that the player edits only the terms and the engine writes the rest. On Oct 15 he said a bill has a real name. The two rulings pull in different directions. The example's long title is the engine's. Its name (what the Ledger and the board call it) is the one place you can click to name it, so he can rule.
