Gracey Metrics Mock

Metrics from a played game

The history mostly exists: over six played years in Gracey, Kentucky, the world kept 87 of these 106 measures year by year, and the screen only ever asked for now and a year before. A year marked “not kept” is one the world does not hold.

The five biggest gaps

  • No year-by-year record of the town’s people and homes: population, rental homes, empty rentals, open jobs, job seekers and open claims are overwritten. The homeless count was written once, on February 1, 2025.
  • The state keeps three figures over the years: unemployment, pay and jobs. Its population, prices and sales, and the federal debt, are kept for now only. No state or country figure exists for schools, health, safety or roads.
  • Budgeted revenue never changes: the county’s, the state’s and the country’s were the same dollar figure in every fiscal year from 2025 to 2030.
  • Deaths have no date and no count: a named person’s death carries no date, and the record of counted births, deaths and illness stayed empty all six years.
  • Gracey has no government of its own in the records; its books are Christian County’s.

Odd figures, not yet traced

  • The state’s budgeted spending fell from $49.8 billion in 2029 to $37.3 billion in 2030, and the county’s from $288 million to $252 million.
  • The federal debt was exactly the same at the end of 2029 and 2030.
  • The town’s own school record shows spending per student of $344,636 for its one student in 2029, against $15,455 in the other count.
  • Town unemployment reached 15% in 2029, with 73 residents at work, while the state stood at 4.6%.
