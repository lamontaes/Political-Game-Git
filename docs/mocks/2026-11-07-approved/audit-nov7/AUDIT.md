# Every screen in the game, graded (Nov 7, 2026)

His ask, 8:50 p.m. (RULINGS-nov5.md item 57): "first, i want you to look through the game and look at things that dont have menus first. i know for sure legislation is one of them. i have descibed things multiple times. i want you to find all of the shitty ui and let's get everything up to speed first."

## What it comes to

- **69 player-facing surfaces checked.** None of them matches the look he approved this week. 9 are missing outright, 50 are crude and 10 are close.
- **The approved mocks aren't in the game.** You, the person card, Government and Budgets were approved today (items 53 to 56), but they exist only on this Mac (`cto-notes/menus/examples/`). The screens on main haven't changed since Nov 4. The one Nov 5 commit to the play screen, 9618bdca, only rewrote comments. Every screenshot below is still what a player sees.
- **Legislation is the worst gap.** He approved the bill-on-paper workflow on Sept 25 and said it again on Oct 14 and Oct 26. In the game, writing a bill is still one dropdown and one number box. Committee, amendments, state and federal floor votes, and veto overrides have no screen at all. The engine already has some of the parts (bill revision in `src/core2/modules/legislature.ts`, committee hearings in `src/core2/office-day-state.ts`), but no screen uses them.
- **His Nov 5 to 7 menu rulings aren't on main.** They are only in `cto-notes/menus/RULINGS-nov5.md` and the `menu-look` skill. On main, `docs/play-first/LAW.md` still lists the Oct 14 lines asking for "party split bar", "recent votes as yes/no bars" and "donuts, bars". A lane building from main today would build the bars he rejected on Nov 5.

### The 10 worst, in order

1. **Writing a bill** (Your office → Drafts) is one dropdown, one number box and a Draft button. There is no bill on paper, nothing to click inside the bill, no title, no sections to add, no staff draft and no real estimate. This is the opposite of what he approved on Sept 25 and repeated on Oct 14 and Oct 26.
2. **The bill paper** prints every member's vote as one paragraph. For a Congress bill that is 535 names, "Name · Yes · Name · Yes...", running down the window. The chamber names are blank (": 56 for, 44 against"). The estimate says "no pay effect". Sections are big buttons, and the numbers inside them can't be clicked.
3. **Committee, amendments, the state and federal floor, and veto overrides** have no screen at all. A member of Congress gets their vote cast for them ("I voted for H.R. 2", Oct 26 sitting).
4. **The council meeting and the governor's or mayor's desk** are a side panel of seven stacked buttons per bill: Read the bill, Speak for, Speak against, Yes, No, Abstain, Decide for me. Each bill also gets a green and red tally bar, round face rings and an empty chamber behind the panel. His Oct 5 ruling made a meeting a played scene.
5. **The campaign trail** is a wall of buttons and number boxes: "Ask for an interview · 1 hours", a box that says 50, Mail, a box that says 0, Put in your own money. The code itself marks it as a stopgap (`SG-PLAY-campaign-acts-buttons`). He ruled on Oct 20 that campaign acts are scenes you go to.
6. **Budgets** are two donut charts and a "books" table ("thje budget menus and stuff are disasters. just an excel sheet", Nov 7). A council member's own budget is a Cut 20% / Add 20% dropdown on each line. The approved Budgets mock (bud-strips) isn't built.
7. **Government** has green and red vote bars ("there's this green bar and stuff. No, I said that's not what I want", Nov 5) and a party bar. Members are boxes with small round face crops. There are no judges, and the executive is one face card. The approved Government mock isn't built.
8. **Who you are and the person card** show a full-body figure with a raised, waving hand, Home and Money fact boxes, and a timeline with icons. He rejected all of that on Nov 7 ("just an excel sheet of facts"; "no timeline"). Both approved mocks are waiting to be built.
9. **Election day** is a side panel of stacked full-width buttons from morning to night, with no information about the candidates. Election night is text rows over a lot of empty space. He asked for about five minutes of scenes and for election night as "its own pop-up menu kind of thing" (Oct 26).
10. **Journal and News** are bright cream paper sheets with page counters ("6 of 6") and large empty areas. The news reads like a list of records ("Local income tax authority became law on January 16, 2025."). He ruled on Nov 7 that paper "sticks out like a sore thumb", and on Nov 5 that he doesn't want "the page one and stuff".

Close behind: the Campaigns tab, which shows "$0.00 raised · $0.00 spent · 0 volunteers · 54 doorsWhere the race stands" (two lines run together); the ballot; and Parties, whose map is labeled "Meadow Lakes CDP" and "State Senate District N".

**Your call:** which one goes first. My pick is legislation, as one set: the bill paper, committee, the floor and the desk. You named it, and you've described it three times.

His standing description, Sept 25 (REG:422), restated Oct 14 (OD:695) and Oct 26 (OD:522): a subject index for navigation only, then "the bill itself becomes the primary workspace". None of this is on screen.
