Park Updates and Alerts

  • Do not bring firewood into Vermont state parks from outside the state, unless it is packaged, labeled, and heat-treated. The label must show it is certified by the USDA or another state’s agriculture department. To learn more, view our Camping Policies and Rules.
  • Black bears are active daily at Smugglers' Notch State Park. When staying at the park, you must securely stow your food, hygiene items and any other aromatic objects in your car or bear canister. Take all necessary precautions. Properly dispose of all trash immediately. There remains low risk of personal injury if proper precautions are used. For more information on camping in bear country, visit the BearWise camping tips page.

Welcome

Welcome to Smugglers’ Notch State Park. Smugglers’ Notch is a narrow mountain pass through the Green Mountains. Tall cliffs rise nearly 1,000 feet on each side of the winding road, which closes during the winter. In earlier times, only a footpath and a trail for horses passed through the Notch.

In 1807, President Thomas Jefferson signed the Embargo Act, which stopped American trade with Great Britain and Canada. This law was difficult for many northern Vermonters because Montreal was closer than other markets in the United States. Some local residents continued trading with Canada illegally, herding cattle and carrying goods through the Notch.

Later, freedom seekers escaping slavery used the Notch as part of their journey to Canada. During Prohibition in the 1920s, smugglers again used the route to bring liquor from Canada. The road through the Notch was improved in 1922.

Facilities/Amenities

Smugglers’ Notch State Park was relocated in 2003. Historic structures built by the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) were carefully moved and restored to preserve their craftsmanship. Learn more on the Vermont State Parks History page.

The new campground has sites that are more spread out, a restroom building that uses alternative energy, and easy access to Bingham Falls. The restrooms include flush toilets, hot and cold running water, and token-operated hot showers. Firewood and ice are available for sale.

Most campsites are walk-in and cannot be reached by vehicle. Because of this, and because Route 108 north of the campground is narrow, the park has very few sites that can accommodate RVs.

Day Use Area

Smugglers’ Notch State Park offers more than just the campground. Just outside the gates is a scenic corridor full of outdoor adventures.

Starting at the Barnes Camp Visitor Center, a 3.5-mile scenic highway winds through a steep mountain pass with beautiful views and many places to explore.

Visitors can walk along the wetlands boardwalk near the Barnes Camp Visitor Center or start a hike on the Long Trail. Farther up the road, the Smugglers’ Notch Picnic Area sits beside a beautiful stream.

History lovers can also see stone fireplaces built by the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC), a group of early conservation workers from the 1930s.

Other popular hikes include the challenging Hellbrook Trail and the well-known Sterling Pond Trail.

Activities in the notch include:

  • bouldering
  • ice climbing
  • cave exploring
  • biking

Park Interpreter

This park has a park interpreter who leads fun, hands-on activities. A park interpreter is a staff member who helps visitors learn about the park’s natural and cultural history.

Popular activities may include night hikes, nature crafts and games, campfire programs, and amphibian explorations.

Check out the events calendar to see some of the programs planned during your visit.
