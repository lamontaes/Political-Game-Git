# golden-rule.md — drawn or fixed outcome levels, and special cases

**Audited commit:** `ab4ac1b8839456a8d662b0e3c2da84288798bb47` (main, 2026-09-30 16:05 ET). **Written:** 2026-09-30 18:25 ET. Read-only; no tests run; every row cites `file:line`.

## Bottom line for the owner

**Rule 3 (levels must emerge).** 62 outcome levels are picked or drawn rather than produced by a mechanism: 30 drawn during play or at opening, 27 fixed literals, 5 drawn inside a research range. The ones that shape a whole game: each worker's wage percentile ±15 (`town-pay.ts:232, 715`); each lease's rent and each county's rent multiplier (`town-rent.ts:1347, 580`); every estimated opening budget line jittered ±5% (`public-budgets/opening.ts:544-556`); every outcome-web effect size drawn within its range (`outcome-web/index.ts:653-675`); a new program's appropriation picked from `[0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2]` × default (`automatic-legislation.ts:479-486`); campaign money raised `integer(85_000, 175_001)` (`campaigns.ts:1318`); town election turnout 12–32% and base support 0.6–1.4 drawn per race (`local-elections.ts:1071, 1077`); the national economy's monthly innovations (`macro-economy/kernel.ts:113-124`); every place measure's monthly drift (`place-outcomes.ts:71-88`); and the fixed levels — living costs $1,500/$900, mortgage $50,000/$1,200, office pay $60,000, public-body wage $21.64, federal minimum-wage raise $15, cannabis tax $40.70 per resident, rent-stabilization +5.1%, supply-law −0.146%/month, fairness-law +2.7%.

**Rule 6 (one rule, all states).** Named places in code branches: Charlottesville (`municipal-ordinance-procedure.ts:1278` `if (input.governmentKey !== "us-va-charlottesville")`, `CVILLE_2_98_AMENDED_ON :1255`, `municipal-public-work.ts:1550`); Washington DC (`CONGRESSIONAL_REVIEW :686`, `OVERRIDE_WINDOW_DAYS :727`, `municipal-procedure-placeholders.ts:36`, `question-authority.ts:149`, `presidential-turnover.ts:768`); Maine/Nebraska electors (`national-election-rules.ts:116` — this one is the real constitutional rule and belongs in data, not code); California (`constitutional-process.ts:184`). Institution short-circuits: 13 `US_CONGRESS_PACK_ID` / `isCongressMeasure` branches across 7 governing files; 21 `level === "state"` sites across 7 public-budgets files (county and city silently get 1/0); `judicial-review.ts:371` skips every federal law. Per-law special cases: 37 files hard-code a policy-question key; 20 modules implement one statute each; `TAX_QUESTION_EFFECTS`/`SPENDING_QUESTION_EFFECTS` apply Oklahoma, California, Utah and Virginia fiscal notes to every state. Nebraska's unicameral legislature is handled correctly through `pack.structure` (`legislature-rules.ts:516, 891, 1082`) — the pattern the rest should follow.

The full tables follow (sections 1–3 as produced by the sweep; each row was read in context).

---

Rule 3 restated: an outcome LEVEL (money, price, cost, count, rate, percentile) must emerge from a mechanism. A seeded draw "within a research range" is still a pick. A fixed literal is a pick. A research figure is a calibration check, not a source of the level.

Legend: **DRAWN** = a SeededRng draw sets the level; **FIXED** = a literal sets it; **DRAWN-IN-RANGE** = drawn between research bounds (the case the owner specifically forbids).

Three findings stand above the rest:

1. **outcome-web/index.ts:653-675 `drawnLinkSize`** is the rule-3 breach with the widest reach: it is the documented policy ("Research sizes are a baseline, not literal numbers (Lamontae, Sept. 28)") that every effect size is drawn inside a range, and teacher-salary-floor.ts:69 reuses it to draw a dollar floor. Every downstream level inherits a lottery.
2. **Estimated-from-average draws** (office-pay.ts:131, state-income-tax-law.ts:285, income-tax-withholding.ts:200, state-paid-leave-law.ts:176, paid-leave-benefits.ts:110, opening.ts:555, town-finances.ts:557) all follow one pattern: where a state's row was not read, draw around the mean of the states that were. The fix is the same everywhere: read the row.
3. **Per-law modules with a single hard-coded question key** are the dominant rule-6 shape. The state-name branches are few (Charlottesville, DC, ME/NE, CA) and mostly already sit behind `Record<string, …>` lookups, so moving the literal keys into data rows is mechanical; the twenty one-statute files are the larger refactor.
