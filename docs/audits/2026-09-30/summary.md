# Our Civic Duty — code audit, plain English

For Lamontae. Audited the game as it stood on the afternoon of September 30, 2026 (the merge of pull request #1294). Nothing was changed; this is a reading of the code. The five detailed reports sit next to this one; file names and line numbers are in their tables and in the appendix here, not on this page.

## What the game's engines should be

Six, not five. Your five are right in kind. One is missing.

1. **CLOCK** — the one place that says when anything happens: one way to move time, one list of things due, one calendar row per body and office. This is new. Today there is no owner of time: two clocks, the same list of fourteen daily steps written out twice, seven different legislative calendars, and the player's own money settling on a different clock from everyone else's. Every other merge below re-splits along that seam unless time is owned first.
2. **GOVERNMENT** — lawmaking, executives, courts. Right as proposed. Courts today are three unrelated pieces (constitutional review, criminal trials, clemency) and there are no civil cases at all; criminal trials are run by the newspaper's weekly sweep.
3. **ELECTIONS** — right as proposed.
4. **FINANCIAL** — right as proposed. The finding is not that it should split but that most of its jobs exist two to five times.
5. **SOCIAL** — right as proposed, including the English engine as its voice. That engine exists and is good; it reaches about a quarter of the game's prose.
6. **NARRATIVE** — right in intent (read only), wrong in ten places where the press decides money, support, relationships and court timing.

Cause and effect: you named the effects map as the one shared wiring. There are two wirings today; the second should be fed from the map or removed.

## Where the game stands against your rules

- **One engine per job.** Bills are moved by five separate drivers, filed by five, voted by five, signed by four kinds of executive (the President uses two party rules, the mayor always signs, the town has no mayor step at all). Government money lives in five records of three unrelated types. People are paid by five different payroll writers. Votes are counted nine ways. Term limits are counted by four engines in three different units. An opinion can form five ways.
- **One law system.** Laws already on the books at the start never go through the enactment path at all; they are read from a data file directly. About 45 separate code modules each recognize one law by name and apply their own size to it. Only appropriations, duties, eligibility and a linked tax proposal go through the shared path. So today, adding a law is adding code.
- **Golden rule.** 62 outcome levels are picked or drawn instead of produced: every worker's wage percentile, every lease's rent, every opening budget line, every effect size in the map, a new program's budget (picked from a list of five multipliers), campaign money raised, town turnout, the whole national economy month to month.
- **Zero dice.** 95 rolls decide outcomes during play; 24 more pick levels at world creation. The shared decision engine adds a −1/0/+1 roll to every option within two points of the leader, at 40 places (Supreme Court nominee, every appointee, job offers, couples, press stories, rival campaigns, belief formation). Who dies, who wins your election, who wins the presidency, every town election, births, break-ups, moves, crime victims and disasters are all rolled.
- **Bedrock.** The code is honest about it: hundreds of numbers are marked as placeholders, assumptions or estimates. Of 110 that matter, 14 are researched with a citation that measures the thing they set. The effects map has 220 links; 97 are sized; 41 are correctly zero with a study that found zero; **82 are named and cited but never wired, so in play they do nothing** — that is the biggest bedrock gap.
- **One rule, all states.** Charlottesville's charter sections and DC's congressional review are written as code branches; Congress is a special case inside the state legislature code; county and city budgets silently get "no effect" from most law readers because they only look at states; every federal law is skipped by judicial review.
- **Emergent, not authored.** Passed. No authored story branch that sets a world outcome was found; scene options only move what the player believes about themselves.
