# A birth outside town printed because the parent holds office (#9726), Words lead, Nov 16 14:20Z

Ruling: a story the paper prints because of who someone is must say who they are, from the term record. Without it the birth reads as a local birth. These play only when the parent holds an office on record and the birth is outside the played town; {office} is the office's own title from the record with its realm or district as the record names it.

Headline:
- "{office} {parent} has a child"
- "A child for {office} {parent}"
Lede:
- "{child} was born on {date} to {parent}, who serves as {office}."
- "{parent}, who serves as {office}, had a child on {date}. The child is named {child}."

Both parents: when the record holds two parents, the two-parent wording is used and names both; the office-holder is named with the office. The single-parent wording plays only when the record holds one parent.
Each line is shown on a printed page opened by presses at the final sha or comes out.

## Read at 6abef46a (Nov 16 15:00Z): two changes
1. The child Eden Howard has two parents on record (Alexandria Howard and Andrew Randolph); the new lines name only the office-holder. With two parents the ledes are:
- "{child} was born on {date}, to {other-parent} and {parent}, who serves as {office-a}."
- "{parent}, who serves as {office-a}, and {other-parent} had a child on {date}. The child is named {child}."
The one-parent ledes (same wording without {other-parent}) play only when the record holds one parent.
2. In a lede the office is a common noun with its article, lower case after the realm: "who serves as a Massachusetts state representative" ({office-a}). In a headline the title stands before the name and keeps its capitals: "Massachusetts State Representative Andrew Randolph has a child".
The comma after the date is right and stays.
