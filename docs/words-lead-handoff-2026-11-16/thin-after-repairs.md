## The list, in writing order

1. **Talk at home about the news.** After the repairs a housemate no longer opens on a story that has nothing to do with them, and the empty replies ("Oh, my.", "No kidding.", "I heard.") are gone: 17 news replies deleted, plus the "Did you hear about" raises. What is left is thin. To write: lines that raise a town story by what it does to the speaker (their job, rent, school, the seat a relative holds) or by the stand they have acted on, and replies that take a side or ask what the record can answer. This is first because it is the first talk of every new life. The conversation engine's counts at two places (`words-lead/builder-a-nov16-table-and-causes.md`) show how thin: a printed story was offered 10 to 19 times and refused 468 to 1,003 times, which is the tie rule working as intended, so news at home now waits on lines and ties, not on a fix. Two places only, so the kinds near zero (a shared school was offered 0 times at one place) are leads to check, not findings. Seen by the second reader at the tie-rule PR's head (#9678 c3916c81): two housemates clicked on opening day said nothing at all (Joshua Patton at Allison Gap, Virginia, seed 22; Kelly Chapman at Thomaston, Maine, seed 24). The conversation engine's trace across six places (`words-lead/tie-rule/housemates-silent.md`) found 8 of 19 people at home with nothing to say on a given day; they show as not clickable, which is the Nov 1 rule working, not a fault. Five are children, who have no lines at all. Three are adults: one housemate a day raises the address, and these three have no stand with a reason from their own life and no work day to talk about. No filler is being added. To write: what a child at home says, and what a jobless adult or a 17-year-old says from their own records (school, looking for work, the household's bills). This is the first place to point new scenes.

2. **Short answers about work, school and home.** 83 short pieces come out of everyday talk (list in `says-nothing-ruling.md`): "It's fine.", "Oh, okay.", "That's nice.", "Can't complain." Questions like "Still in school?" and "How's work?" will have fewer answers. To write: answers from the job, school and home records (what changed, who they work beside, how long they have been there), said as how it feels, never the figure. Seen in the second reader's played file for the deletion (84 talks, three places): asking how long someone has lived or worked somewhere now offers one reply every time, "That's a long time.", 16 times, even after "About 4 years."; it needs replies that fit a short stay and a long one. On the Train before the deletion, 14 talks already stop with nothing to press, 6 of them on the player's own "Where do you work?" with no answer; that is a fault for the conversation engine, filed apart from this list. The holes by move are in `story-builder/leftovers-thin-lists.md`: 19 moves with no piece and 15 answer moves with none. Three named holes from the conversation engine's dead-end fix (#9719): someone who works out of town cannot be asked "Where do you work?" because every answer needs an employer's name and theirs has none (lines written tonight in `words-lead/work-talk/out-of-town-work-lines.md`, not yet played; item words-out-of-town-work-has-a-true-answer); six child or retired people who ask the player about work have no line for where they stand themselves (item words-no-job-asker-says-where-they-stand, not written); and one reply, "You try living on {wage} an hour", is missing its wage. I am also waiting on the story builder's two lists (questions with fewer than two answers, moves with no piece) to know the exact holes.

3. **Opening and yearly addresses.** After the repairs an address no longer contradicts itself, moves between topics with a turn, names one thing that happened in its own place (a law, a disaster, a closing, an election), and the President does not name the town. Still thin: each address covers one or two issues in a few sentences, and two governors with the same top issue sound alike. To write: a second and third point per address from the speaker's own record (what they signed, vetoed, promised), a named person or place from the event's record, and more closes. Read by the second reader in six pressed addresses (Guam; Superior, Wyoming; Guilford Center, Connecticut) and still to fix by rewriting: a sentence that reads two ways ("I'll fight raising the debt limit only with spending cuts to match every step of the way."); points that open on "In fact," with nothing before them; one sentence that names its measure three times; and all three governors using a pay line, two the same one. Oath lines for an inaugural are owed too: no inaugural can say one yet. Also owed: the 1,080 "today" wordings need a read one by one so each is tied to a record or said only as belief; about 530 could not be placed by rule.

4. **A chair's reasons for sitting on a bill.** Four reasons play (own view, district, party, last vote). Four were deleted because no played world reached them (the next election, the sponsor, a group, temper). To write when a world can reach them: those four, and what a chair asks for in return.

5. **Election night beyond the one case.** The concession call now says the truth in one case (worse than the campaign's poll, not close). The other cases ship no line yet: counted again by law, close enough to ask for a recount, closer than the poll, about as polled, and no poll bought. To write as each is reached in play. The winner's side of the same night is thinner still.

6. **The Elections page's refusals.** Three reasons are shown (not your office, filing not open, filing closed). Four others have no sentence because no press reaches them yet (another district's seat, already filed elsewhere, not your town, one at-large filing). To write when the page can reach them.

7. **Journal.** 23 texts wait on being shown on an opened page; "landslide" and "From then on" come out or get a record rule. After that the Journal is thin on what a year meant to the people around the player (it lists events; it rarely says who was touched).

8. **Paper and newscast.** 117 mined lines stay unplayed. Closings and layoffs print the job count once; what they lack is the second-day story: who was let go that the player knows, what the town's body did next.

9. **Industries.** Lines for the six gaps the industries thread found (G1, G5, G4, G3, G2, G6 in that order) wait on its scene-first reports. Until then a law that reaches a kind of business is felt in the books but nobody says so.
