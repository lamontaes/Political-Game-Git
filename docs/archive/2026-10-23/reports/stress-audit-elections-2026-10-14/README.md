# Robbins votes, but no campaign gives anyone a reason to choose

Three calls are yours, set out under "For the owner": whether filing should cost a fee or petition; whether each resident's seeded starting politics is fair generation or a roll on their vote; and whether deciding to vote stays a plain yes-or-no. Elections run end to end, and winners take office on time. But no candidate raised or spent a dollar. Both congressional nominees gave identical answers, because a candidate's views never read their party. Named and counted voters decide by different rules: one lone council member drew 1 named vote and 2,565 counted ones. This audit confirmed 37 defects, one outside elections: a town of about 1,300 people holds about 6,700 counted adults.

## The run

The world was the played world as the player gets it: every module installed, two-level population on. It opened January 20, 2025, and ran to January 4, 2026 (349 game days).

- **The place:** Robbins, North Carolina (place GEOID 3757000, Moore County), drawn at random from all 56 states and territories.
- **The draw seed:** f97abfc4f65b8c53e16e2c2511d21701cd52bfee (place seed e9eb7c7e20abe0f864b7bdbb137141c99d3da1b2376495a0c1b839f093495c3e).
- **The code:** main at fd3574c9.
- **The time:** the town took 39 seconds to build and 144 seconds to run its 349 days, measured once on a cloud machine. That is over the two-minute limit for a town year; this audit did not look for the cause.

Two earlier draws held no election in the window, so each was set aside and another place drawn:

- Uehling, Nebraska (seed 05aa1ffb55a6ac392a5f84d5999aeed5aae4eea3): its first contests fall on November 3, 2026.
- Duncan, Mississippi (seed 446d064285d7d378e415d7c41c9d57b7718c4562): no 2025 town election, although Mississippi towns really voted on June 3, 2025. That is defect 21 below.

## The race

On April 1, 2025, the U.S. Representative for North Carolina's 9th district moved away. A special election was set for July 1, exactly three months later. The parties' nominees filed on May 17: Republican state senator Carrie Cook (district 21) and Democratic state senator Julie Thompson (district 32).

A statewide ranking picked each nominee: every legislator of the party, scored by ambition and by how well the legislator's lean fits the district (`src/core2/modules/officials.ts`, `officialCandidate`). Its candidate rows show Cook scored highest of 102 Republicans weighed and Thompson highest of 67 Democrats (measured). Neither nominee chose to run; the game chose them. The engine where people decide to run from their own drives, views and circumstances asked no one about this race (measured). Through late May and June, the town's newspaper, radio station and four television stations (a lot for a town this size; see defect 37) interviewed both candidates, 12 interviews in all, and one radio debate followed on June 23 (measured).

Both candidates gave the same three answers every time (measured):

- for "stand your ground"
- against shifting highway money to transit
- against automatic voter registration

Cook won 79,782 to 57,067, 58 percent (measured). Cook took the seat the same day. Cook's state senate seat went to an appointee, Brian Greer (measured).

In Robbins itself, all four seats on the November 4 ballot (mayor and three council seats) had exactly one candidate, the incumbent (measured from the filings). Mayor Rebecca Erickson, first elected in 2002, won a new term starting January 1, 2026, with 2,613 of the 2,657 ballots cast: 48 from named voters and 2,565 from counted residents (measured).

## The money

Not one dollar moved (measured). All six campaigns in the run, the two for Congress and the four in town, show $0 raised and $0 spent, with no volunteers, staff, doors knocked, ads or polls. The campaigns engine has no daily turn of its own, so donations, ads, polls and volunteers run only when the player presses them on the campaign screen (`src/core2/modules/campaigns.ts`, `CAMPAIGNS_MODULE`; inferred from the code). The routine written to run a rival candidate's daily canvassing, `rivalCampaignDay`, is called only by a test (inferred from the code).

Both state senators are full people in the world, so the vote count still docked each for having no campaign spending records (measured). The count lowered each candidate's expected share of the district vote by the same 4.5 percentage points (measured from the result's reasons). A candidate with no records is scored as having spent the minimum (inferred from the code). A modeled official who is not a full person gets no penalty at all (`src/core2/electorate-inputs.ts`, `candidateInputs`; inferred from the code).

## How people decided

**The named voter.** The named voter, a conservative Robbins adult the records hold as person_186c5380cb2e2cca, decided from their own record (measured from the ballot row):

- their views (lean +0.89 on a scale of −1 to +1)
- their Republican party tie
- their traits
- their work hours
- the election laws in force
- what reached them: 8 retellings of each candidate's interviews from family

Each part of a voter's pull toward a candidate runs on roughly a −1 to +1 scale. This voter's warmth toward the two candidates was identical (0.14), and so was their agreement with the platforms (0.58). Only party separated them: +0.89 for Cook, −0.89 for Thompson, plus 0.5 for their party tie. They voted for Cook (all measured from the ballot row). In July, 30 of the town's 72 named adults voted (measured). The town had 62 named adults in January, 72 in July and 71 in November (measured); why the count moved was not traced.

**The counted residents.** These are the town's adults the world keeps as counts and has not filled in as full people. Their number changed over the year: 6,410 in January, 6,556 at the July special and 6,728 in November (measured; why it grew was not traced). They decide by a second formula, which weighs their lean against the party label, how many named neighbors have heard of each candidate, and incumbency (`src/core2/modules/voting.ts`, `countedAppeal`; inferred from the code). In the special, 2,880 of them voted, 2,295 for Cook and 585 for Thompson (measured). The count also keeps their votes by age group: of the 1,253 counted voters aged 45 to 64 who chose a candidate, 1,006 chose Cook and 247 chose Thompson (measured). No screen or engine reads those age-group totals today (inferred from the code), so the player cannot see which ages carried the race.

**The district beyond the town.** Its 133,939 votes came from a third formula: seven party-identity groups sized from the district's 2024 vote (`src/core2/electorate-blocks.ts`, `countBlocks`; measured from the block row). Those votes, the town's 2,880 counted voters and its 30 named voters make up the 136,849 votes cast.

**The town's November races.** Here the named and counted formulas gave opposite answers:

- Named voters weigh an incumbent's voting record by adding up every recorded vote. For a voter near the center, each vote counts slightly against the incumbent (`src/core2/modules/record.ts`, `recordAppeal`; inferred from the code).
- Council member Kimberly Valdez had 56 votes on record (measured). Valdez's median record score with named voters was −8.2 (measured), against the −1 to +1 range of every other part of a voter's pull.
- 68 of the 69 named voters who went to the polls left the race blank, though Valdez was the only candidate (measured).
- Counted residents' formula has no record term (`countedAppeal`; inferred from the code), and 2,565 of them voted for Valdez (measured).
- A voter's interest in an election day is their strongest feeling about any race on the ballot (`voting.ts`, `atThePolls`; inferred from the code), so that −8.2 made named voters care a great deal. 69 of 71 named adults voted (97 percent); 2,588 of 6,728 counted adults did (38 percent) (measured).

## What the result changed

- **The winner took office.** Cook's House term began July 1, 2025, and runs to January 3, 2027 (measured).
- **The state senate seat changed hands.** Cook's old state senate seat was vacated "for higher office" and filled by appointment the same day (measured). The vacancy rule cited N.C. Gen. Stat. 163-11 and marked itself an estimate.
- **Cook votes in Congress.** Cook cast 24 recorded roll-call votes as a U.S. Representative between July 1 and December 15, 2025 (measured); how many votes the House held in that time was not checked.
- **The town got its stories.** The news engine printed an "election result" and a "took office" story for the town (measured).
- **No one's opinions moved.** The result event carries no topic, so the views engine skips it (`src/core2/modules/views.ts`, `onEvent`), and the outcome web never reads it (inferred from the code; defect 16).
- **The next election will forget the winner.** The 2026 contest for the House seat still points at the term of the representative who moved away (measured). When that election comes, Cook will not count as the incumbent, and voters will not weigh Cook's record (`voting.ts`, `candidateFacts` reads the contest's stored term; inferred, since no 2026 vote ran; defect 17).
- **Cook's identity changed.** Taking office overwrote the recorded gender, "female", with the placeholder "recorded" (measured).

## Paths that came back clean

- **The named voter's own decision.** Every input came from the voter's own record: views, impressions, party tie, traits, work hours and the election laws in force (`voting.ts`, `appealFor` and `turnout`; measured from the ballot row). No seeded roll or fixed percentage decided whether the voter voted or for whom. The flat party-tie bonus is defect 12.
- **No dice.** No path decided a candidacy, a vote, a donation or turnout with a random draw (inferred from the code of all ten paths). A tie by lot becomes a second election instead, under a registered stopgap.
- **The filing office.** North Carolina's municipal filing office ("County board of elections") is read from data with its statute cited (`elections.ts`, `filingRequirements`; measured from the mayor's filing).
- **Taking office, apart from defect 18.** Terms start on time, and a special-election winner is seated the same day (`voting.ts`, `takeOffice`; measured from the term rows). The vacancy the winner leaves is filled by the state's appointment rule.
- **No mechanic without a basis.** Exit polls, interviews, debates, endorsements, polls, recalls and ballot measures all trace to the golden path, _The Political Process_ baseline or a scheduled milestone (inferred from `docs/roadmap/political-process-baseline.md` and the milestone list).
- **Registered stopgaps.** All 14 stopgap IDs the election code calls have rows in the stopgap register (`src/core2/data/stopgaps.json`; inferred from the code).

## For the owner

1. **Filing fees and petitions.** The code says filing is free, citing a ruling of yours dated October 13, 2026. That ruling is not written in the docs, and the filing package still says candidates gather signatures. Which is right?
2. **Opening lean.** Each counted resident's political lean starts from a seeded draw (`src/core2/campaign-views.ts`, `normalOf`). Does that count as deciding who exists, which is allowed, or as a roll on how they vote?
3. **Voting is yes or no.** Whether a person votes is a hard comparison of duty plus interest against cost. A vote is a yes-or-no act, so this audit did not count it as a threshold defect. Say so if you want it continuous.

## What happens next

All 37 defects are open; this audit fixed nothing and changed no game code. They fall to the pillars that own the code, for the CTO to assign:

- **OPUS-PEOPLE** (views, the town's candidacy engine, the record): defects 3, 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11.
- **OPUS-GOV** (elections, campaigns, press, voting, offices, filing): defects 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 12 to 36.
- **OPUS-CORE** (population, speed): defect 37, and the run's 144 seconds for 349 days, which is over the two-minute limit for a town year.

The first three to take, by how much they change play: defect 1 (no one campaigns), defect 6 (a candidate's views ignore their party) and defect 3 (an incumbent's record always reads negative).

## What the agents could not reach

- **No 2026 election ran,** so defect 17 (the winner not treated as incumbent next time) comes from the contest rows, not a played race.
- **No tie occurred,** and no state or federal race away from the town fell in the window. The tie path and the far-off race engine were read in code only.
- On November 4, 2,588 counted residents voted: 2,565 chose Mayor Erickson and 2,565 chose Valdez (council seat 0), and 2,037 chose each of the other two council candidates. Why 23 and 551 left those races blank was not measured, because counted residents' single votes are not kept.
- **Most "heard it" rows for the result were pruned by the news engine before the dump.** How many townspeople learned who won was not measured.
- **The news engine's story choices were not traced through the run.** Read in code (`src/core2/modules/news.ts`, `onEvent`), it prints every public-record event.

## Method

- **The run:** a scratch script built the played world with every module installed (two-level population on) and stepped it one day at a time. It then dumped every module table and the durable log. The same seed reproduces this world.
- **The sub-agents:** ten sub-agents ran on Haiku, read-only, one path each:
  1. who runs
  2. the special nominees
  3. filing
  4. money
  5. press and events
  6. the named voter
  7. the counted public
  8. turnout, count and ties
  9. what read the result
  10. a code sweep for place names, duplicate models and stopgaps
- **Verification:** each agent traced one path end to end and returned a fixed-format report. Every defect above was checked by opening the code the agent named, and against the dumped records where the run reached it. Two claims did not hold and were dropped. One was that a House nominee must live in the district; the Constitution requires only that they live in the state. The other was a "moved away" event that belonged to the voter's relative.
