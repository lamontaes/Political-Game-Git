## Tonight's build (Oct 12)

Branch `opus-play` (draft PR #4002). Open a Codespace, run `npm run bench`, and open port 3000 **at `/play`** for the game screen. The plain page at `/` is the developer bench, with every record and "why".

**Start here: `/play`.** Type a town under Place (any of the 56; leave it empty for a random one) and press **New life**. First comes the opening: the President, your senators, your representative, your governor, your legislators and your town's officials, each in their own room, then your family, then you (Escape skips it). You start as the resident at or nearest 18 in a random town anywhere in the 56 places, at home with your family, in the approved Kit 12/13 look. Your card at bottom left has the clock and Day, Week and Until needed. Choices appear along the bottom. Click anyone in the picture to talk; the conversation runs across the bottom with the scales in its corner. **Calendar** (top left) shows the next two months: the November 3 general election, filing deadlines, laws taking effect and family birthdays. **News** opens with the economy (the nation's unemployment, inflation and rates, your region, jobs in town; it fills in after the first month), then public records as the world makes them. Once you're on a ballot, **Politics** shows your campaign: Knock doors, Volunteers, Donations, Endorsements, Ad, Poll, and Speech with a stance For or Against. **Politics** also has a button for each office that takes filings: press one to walk into that clerk's office, name the seat, hear the rules in the clerk's words, choose your platform and party, and file. **Politics** also shows who holds every seat over you, and Request signatures once you've filed. **Journal** is your life so far. People, Who you are, Money and property, and Jobs and study are at bottom right.

**What you can do tonight**

- In `/play` the town is full size (the whole place, about 10,000 people), with only your circle and the officials followed one by one. Building it takes about a minute. The developer bench at `/` still has a town-size field.
- Click anyone to see their record, household, job, relationships, money and story moments. Viewing changes nothing.
- Press **Play this resident** to take someone over. Move time by Day or Week, or click a later day on the Calendar to live until it. Pick from the choices the game offers; the buttons name what each does ("Go to work", "Get in touch").
- Press **Until needed**. Your character makes its own choices and goes to its own shifts; the skip stops only for a big story moment, a deadline, or new election results, and your card says which one stopped it.
- Switch to someone else at any time. The person you leave finishes their day on their own.
- Click any saved record to see why it happened.
- **See where they are.** Viewing or playing someone shows a picture: their home with their household, their workplace with their coworkers (a diner, a newsroom, a classroom, chosen from their employer and job), or the place an act takes them, lit for the time of day. People are drawn by the people engine on each picture's marked spots.

- **Talk to people.** Anyone in the picture with you has a Talk button. They open with something real ("Are you still living in East Thermopolis?"), and your choices answer and raise real subjects. The scales in the corner light up only when there's something about your own life you could lie about. Press them to lie: they tip, the line glows soft red, and the lie is remembered. **Leave** ends it.
- **Read "My life".** The journal shows your character's life as chapters: their schools, their children, their jobs.
- **See your government.** The Government panel shows the law where you live (minimum wage, taxes, SNAP, unemployment, Medicaid), who holds every seat over you, from the president to the city council, and the next elections with their filing deadlines and the office that takes filings.
- **File for office.** In Politics, press the office that takes filings to walk to its counter. Name a seat and a panel shows its deadline and each rule against your record, with For or Against on each platform question and your party. File there, then Request signatures to ask the people you know. Each person decides for themselves.
- The golden-path panel now shows step 3 (filing) reachable and the national economy running.

**What changed today**

- **This evening (from your notes and the CTO's playtest).** Loading names each step with a real progress bar, and the opening is fully prepared before it plays, so nobody pops in (measured: 6 shots, 0 figures late). Standing people are drawn in your approved whole-figure style, picked from each person's record, sized by depth, turned to the room and given a shadow (seated spots still use the old engine until seated cutouts exist). Politics has **Law here**, the law in force at each level. What changed lists laws a body enacted with the date they take effect. Click anyone for their card. The **Guide** explains terms. Homes come in apartment variants and regional house styles. Jobs and study lists your schools.
- **The audit fixes (this afternoon).** Filing happens only at the counter, with your platform and party. Who you are lists your schools. Family says "father" and "aunt". Choice buttons name what they do. You can jump to a Calendar day, and the card says what stopped time. The scales follow the approved design. Towns now open through the core's business-closures fix, so businesses don't fold in the first months.
- **Also new this afternoon.** **Travel** lists the town's businesses: press Go to look in at whoever is on shift. Clicking someone opens their card (only what you could know), with Talk. Officeholders have a **Chamber** button for their body's room. Speeches happen at a venue you pick, in front of the people they reach. **What changed** opens when a law takes effect (Connecticut's minimum wage went from $16.94 to $17.48 an hour on January 1). **Customize the world** on the title screen sets town size, wages, cash, outside demand, lean and turnout. Escape opens Options. Congress appears once in the opening. Choice buttons and policy questions use real names ("Take a break.", "Call Sarah Sykes.", "Limit groundwater withdrawal").
- The whole world runs in the bench town: people, families, drives, health and the story director (OPUS-PEOPLE), the national and regional economy, jobs, the bank and housing (OPUS-WORLD), and law, offices and elections (OPUS-GOV).
- Switching residents works, and skipping no longer stops at every small choice or at the end of each shift.
- The 25 new place pictures (county clerk offices, registrar, election warehouse, rallies, the convention, homes, schools) have their standing and seating spots.

- **Election night.** After an election day, the Election night sheet opens by itself: every race with each candidate's votes and party, the winner, turnout, the exit poll, and how you voted. Until needed stops when results are in.

- **Serve in office.** In Politics, any officeholder who lives in town has a **Play** button. Take over a council member or county commissioner, and their body appears with a bill paper. Pick a law amount (minimum wage, taxes and the rest, with today's amount shown) or a policy (handgun purchase age, anti-discrimination, paid family leave, right to counsel in eviction, bottle deposit). Draft it, **Ask** each colleague where they stand and why, offer a **Favor**, and **Schedule vote**. Two weeks later the body votes, each member for their own reasons.

- **Map.** Politics → Map draws your state: its counties, your districts and your town, with who holds each of your seats.
- **Family.** People → Family shows every relative in their own picture: where they are and what they're doing, as "mother" or "uncle", with their age.
- **Budgets.** Politics → Budgets shows every government over you and its budget lines. Play the mayor or a county executive to set priorities for the next budget vote. Money and property shows the taxes taken from your pay.

**Not in yet:** voting yourself (the game casts your ballot for you), the mayor deciding to sign or veto (the game decides for them), committee hearings, and the town visibly changing after a law passes. Filing and talking happen instantly for now; they don't spend the day's hours yet. Every home shows the same house interior until homes record what kind of dwelling they are. Children aren't drawn, because the people art is adults only.
