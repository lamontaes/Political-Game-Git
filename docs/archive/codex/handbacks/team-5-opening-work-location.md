# The first morning follows the player's work schedule

An employed player now starts at the job when their existing schedule says they are on shift. Otherwise, the world records why they are home: there is no shift at that hour. Today and the scene read that same location record. Earlier replay descriptors keep their original placement. This corrects the first morning without inventing a commute, a coworker, a day off, or weather.

## 1. Why-chain (five whys, to bedrock)

The earlier opening put an employed player at home because its initial location writer always used the primary household. That writer ignored work because orientation established residence without checking a shift. The shift already existed because an active employment relationship feeds the work schedule reader. The schedule gives a weekday and hour because the existing job category supplies a schedule pattern. That pattern is a game assumption, not a researched universal work pattern or a person's independently recorded decision. **Terminal: an existing generated schedule, a finding.** This piece reads that schedule rather than inventing another decision.

The chain omits actual employer rosters, holidays, sick leave, weather closures, remote work arrangements and the commute before the opening moment. It does not claim those are absent from real life.

## 2. Research

Source evidence is the existing active employment reader and work schedule reader. The opening already begins on Monday, January 5, 2026, at 9:10 a.m. local time. Schedule realism remains research work; no universal effect size or researched schedule distribution is claimed.

## 3. Revisions

Only new setup descriptors carrying `openingWorkLocationVersion: schedule-v1` use this placement. Descriptors missing the field retain home placement, including those with the current world-opening version. Legacy world openings retain their old placement even if the new field is present.

Only the controlled player's initial placement changes. Existing location records stop a second append. Later arrivals replace the initial location, and the Today reader stops using the opening reason on a later date. Existing scene and completed-activity projections take precedence. No schedule, travel, pay or meeting writer changes.
