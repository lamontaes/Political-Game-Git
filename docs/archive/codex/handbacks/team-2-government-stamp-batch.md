# Government events retain their governing law

The saved ward maps now retain the law that assigned the drawing power. Council retirement restrictions also retain their governing term-limit law. State candidacy restrictions retain both enacted and starting-law attribution. All fifteen controlled cases pass. The changes add attribution to existing consequences; they introduce no money, rate or decision rule.

MERGED: the shared history preservation fix is on main. This government writer batch is not merged.

WHAT EMERGED: HARDWIRED bookkeeping attaches the operative law to an existing saved consequence. The stamp does not decide who retires or how wards are drawn. Existing decisions and legal restrictions remain responsible for those outcomes.

## 1. Why-chain

A commission draws the map because the town's governing law assigns it that power. Why does a map get saved? The existing review invokes the ward writer. Why does that writer attach this law? Its canonical reader supplies the operative measure for the same town and question. Why does the record retain it? The shared event writer now clones the stamp. Why is that useful? The audit can identify the actual rule behind a saved consequence. The terminal is bookkeeping on an existing legal rule.

Council and state candidacy stamps follow the same chain. The existing reader first finds a real legal restriction. The event writer then records that restriction and supplies its actual governing law. Other retirement reasons receive no term-limit stamp. Constituents, party leaders and other political influences are outside this attribution change.

## 2. Research

This batch preserves existing ward and term-limit mechanisms. It introduces no estimate, cost or outcome level. Existing ward research remains in council-election-methods.json. Household ordering remains a stand-in for streets. Existing term-limit research and developer estimates remain unchanged; this patch does not certify their completeness.

## 3. Revisions

The stamp appears only when the operative law agrees with the actual consequence. A commission stamp requires a commission map. A repeal stamp requires a council map. Term-limit stamps require an operative yes answer and an actual saved restriction. No law produces no new attribution. A future legal authority cannot receive an earlier application date.

The fixture enacts laws through the canonical legislative procedure with explicitly supplied votes. Later-date unit contexts preserve all existing history and append cancellation records for skipped scheduled items. The batch helper validates the resulting world. These contexts are not ordinary multiyear simulations.
