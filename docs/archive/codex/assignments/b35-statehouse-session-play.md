# The statehouse session as the player lives it: calendar, hearings, floor sitting, other chamber, governor's desk (bank id b35, phase P4 every office, unlocks state legislator play in all 56 jurisdictions)

Verified against origin/main 40d67708d (Oct 6).

## What the player experiences

You are a state representative. Your phone shows the session: it opened in January, the bill-filing deadline is in three weeks, and the committee deadline is in March. You do not click through days. Time stops on what is yours. Your bill is referred to a committee and the chair posts a hearing date. On that morning you sit in the hearing room. The chair calls your bill, you present it, and people who really have a stake come to the table: a school superintendent from your district, a union steward, a trade association lobbyist, a retired teacher who signed up for public comment. Each is a real person in your world, speaking from what they own, owe and have lived. A member asks you a hard question. After testimony the committee marks up the bill: members offer amendments (some of them yours, after you asked them), and the committee votes whether to report it out.

Weeks later your chamber's calendar puts the bill on a floor day. The clerk reads the title, the sponsor presents, members rise to speak for and against, someone moves an amendment, and the roll is called with each member answering from their own reasons. If it passes, it crosses to the other chamber and the same thing starts over with people you may barely know. Then it goes to the governor, who signs, vetoes or waits for the clock; you may be called to the signing, or watch the veto override vote. If the session adjourns first, the bill dies and you see who ran out the clock. Between sessions, it is the interim: committees study, you work the district. A governor can call a special session and you are sent for.
