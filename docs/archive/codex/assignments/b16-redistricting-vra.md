## What the player experiences

Every ten years the game counts its own people. Seats move between states and every map is redrawn: council wards, state legislative districts, U.S. House districts. Whoever holds the pen draws, as that state's real law says: the legislature, a commission or the council. If you sit there, the map comes to you as 3 or 4 ready-made plans you compare on the map screen: per district, people, how far from even, how it has voted, and the neighborhood make-up as district totals, with who is paired in one district. You pick, and you bargain over the plans like any bill (amend, trade, pressure). Your own home can be drawn into a colleague's district. Afterward a group of residents can sue; judges weigh it as courts do (equal population; could a minority community form a majority district; does it vote together while being outvoted) and can throw the map out. Nobody's race is ever shown on a person; it exists only as Census counts for a neighborhood.

## Build steps (one PR each, in this order)

1. **Neighborhood make-up data (compiler, no person fields).** Source domain for Census P.L. 94-171 block-group counts (total and voting-age by race and Hispanic origin), ACS citizen voting-age population, and Section 203 language determinations by county. Per-state packs loaded on demand like map geometry; each unit carries its place, precinct and district joins. Unread units are estimated from similar units in the state and marked. Test: pack totals match the shipped place populations.
2. **The game takes its own census.** In years ending in 0 count each place from the world's own people (reference population plus recorded drift and flows); neighborhood shares drift with recorded moves, never re-read from 2020. In years ending in 1 call `apportionHouse` over the state counts then `electoralVotesFromSeats` and write a dated set `selectDatedSet` reads. Test: a growing state gains a seat in a 30-year run.
3. **One plan builder, a short list of real plans.** Generalize `drawWardCuts` into a builder over units (a town's units are block groups in geographic order, households assigned in proportion to their populations; state and House units are place and county pieces). It returns up to 4 plans, each from a distinct goal: even and compact; protect sitting members (today's council rule, kept); most seats for the drawer's party from recorded results; and a plan that meets the VRA test of step 5 where one can exist. Plans that come out identical are merged (the list may be 3, and says why). Deviation limits are data rows (see Research). `Replaces:` the single-plan body of `drawWardCuts` and the household-order "streets" in `town-wards.ts`; `redistrictAfterCensus` calls the builder and hands the list to step 4.
4. **Who picks, by law, and bargaining.** Read `independent-redistricting` through `lawInForce` as `wardDrawerInForce` does. A commission's seated members each pick a plan with `evaluateDecision` from recorded goals, party and the law's criteria. Where the legislature or council draws, the list becomes one measure per plan through the existing legislative engine and executive desk (amendable, tradeable, vetoable). If the player sits there, they pick and bargain through b08's one door (any measure on the body's agenda). Where the player has no seat, NPCs choose the same way. Test: commission and legislature in the same world produce different maps with recorded reasons. Must not: a second legislative path for map bills.
5. **Courts weigh it as courts do.** A challenge goes through a `fileJudicialChallenge`-style writer, filed by residents or a b18 group from an affected neighborhood. Federal maps go to the court `courtFor` returns, three-judge panel where federal law requires. Each judge's considerations are measured: equal-population deviation; Gingles 1 (could a compact district hold a minority citizen voting-age majority, measured from units); Gingles 2 and 3 (does the community vote together while bloc voting defeats its choices, measured by comparing b03's per-precinct results across neighborhoods of different make-up, never from a person's race); the judge's outlook (b13); precedent rows including Rucho for partisan federal claims and state constitution rows for state courts. Remedy: a redraw or a court-chosen plan from the builder.
6. **The player's screen.** Plan comparison on the political map: per district people, deviation, past vote, neighborhood make-up as district totals, incumbents paired. Choices go through the vote and bargaining surfaces.

## Must NOT build

Any race, ethnicity or language field on a person or person card; dice or a fixed share of districts that "must" be majority-minority; a hand-drawn map tool; a second legislative path; a second court engine or precinct module; per-state hand-coded maps; a yearly redraw tick (maps are drawn only in census years or when a law or ruling requires one); moving one neighborhood at a time (owner chose pick-from-plans).
