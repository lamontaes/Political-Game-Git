Decisions nobody has made yet

Each of these is a real gate. None of them was filled in by this run, and none may be assumed.

  1. Owner style acceptance. styleFamilyStatus is unassessed for both. The readiness report's standing action for both files was "judge it for style"; that judgement is yours and has not been made.
  2. Rights. rightsStatus is unknown for both and stays unknown. These are owner-generated inputs, but no rights declaration exists in the project for them, and visibility is not a licence.
  3. Native detail. nativeDetailState is unverified for both. Nothing in this project establishes whether the 5504px render carries native detail or an external enlargement. Until somebody vouches, every derived tier must report unverified detail rather than claim its pixel width.
  4. Environment family. Neither declares a family_id, because no family fits. art/manifest/environment_families.json describes no outdoor space at all, and no press or announcement room. Somebody has to decide whether a family is created or these stay unfamilied.
  5. Geometry. floorUsable, seatUsable, uiSafeRegions, the floor calibration, the safe and essential content areas, and every anchor are unmeasured. Both frames are dense with baked furniture — picnic tables on the pavilion slab, thirty chairs across the briefing room — so how much floor a person could actually stand on is a real open question, not a formality.
  6. Dynamic surface slots. Both plates contain the blank rectangles a declared slot would need — the park notice board, and the briefing room's wall display, lectern panel, door plate and corkboard sheets. No slot is declared. Until one is, nothing the simulation owns may be painted there.
  7. World identity. Neither picture names a place. What jurisdiction, park or press office either room stands for is canonical world truth supplied by a caller. It may never be inferred from the art, the filename, or the asset id.
  8. A gameplay consumer. Even a fully released plate would show nothing until a surface resolves a scene that uses it. That is travel, events, or a briefing capability — separate work, in a separate lane, not commissioned here.

What this page is not

  • It is not an approval. Nothing here records an approver, and none was invented.
  • It is not evidence that either room exists in the game world.
  • It is not a claim that any real park, press office, event or attendee is depicted. The art is generic and stays generic.
  • It is not a request for replacement art. Both files exist and are usable inputs; the open questions are acceptance and consumers, not generation.
